## Frame 15: A day in the soil  [fr_52]
NOTES: Solve it for a periodic surface swing (Cut). T′ = A e^(−z/d) cos(ωt − z/d): it fades with depth and lags, and d = √(2κ/ω).
- Diurnal: d = 9 / 12 / 17 cm for κ = 0.3 / 0.5 / 1 × 10⁻⁶ m²/s (✓). Below about three d, the day isn't felt.
- Curves are the formula at κ = 5 × 10⁻⁷, ±10 K at the surface (typical of a clear day, not a measurement).
- Hartmann's (4.10) leaves out the 2, so he gets ~10 cm and ~1.5 m. Same physics; his depth is ours ÷ √2.
  TEXT: A swing at the surface fades with depth, and lags. Over a day, it has gone within tens of centimetres.
  FIGURES: soil_base.svg, soil_day.svg, eq_sol.svg, eq_d.svg

## Frame 18: If water only conducted  [fr_64]
NOTES: Beat 6 (≈9 min, Smooth; back to the Arabian Sea). The hook. Water has a big ρc, so is that why the ocean is so sluggish?

## Frame 19: The mixed layer  [fr_68]
NOTES: What actually happens (Smooth). Wind stirs, waves break, and cooling at night and in winter makes surface water sink.
- A typical profile: a mixed layer, a thermocline, deep water (schematic after Stewart Fig 6.8).
- Eddy diffusivity is 10⁴-10⁶ × molecular (Stewart Ch. 8), so the layer is well mixed to its base, and its depth is set by stirring against stratification.
  TEXT: But the sea stirs: wind and waves from above, cooling water sinking at night and in winter. Eddies mix heat 10⁴–10⁶ times faster than molecules, so the top layer’s temperature is the same all the way down: the mixed layer.

## Frame 57: Wind, Coriolis, and the water between  [fr_231]
NOTES: Hold: the force balance on the whole stirred layer, in the steady state. Two forces on it: the wind's stress along the wind, and Coriolis at 90° to the motion. They can cancel only if the motion is 90° to the right of the wind. So the net transport is at right angles to the wind (NH). No spiral algebra. The surface water goes at an angle; averaged over the layer, it's 90°.
  TEXT: Ekman transport
  TEXT: Add up the whole stirred layer. Only two forces act, and they must cancel. So the water must move 90° to the right of the wind.
