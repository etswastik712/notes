Introduction to Climate Science

EC2113 · Semester 2 · Module 3 · Joy Monteiro

Lectures

  1. O1 · Climate science is accounting 28 October 2026 · Why a planet’s energy books must balance, why no single place on Earth is in balance, and how the atmosphere and ocean move energy, mass and momentum to keep them balanced.
  2. O2 · How much of the surface feels the weather? 5 November 2026 · Where sunlight goes in the ocean, how the weather pushes on the surface, and why only a thin layer of land but tens of metres of ocean feel it.
  3. O3 · A slow ocean that has to move 6 November 2026 · What a deep mixed layer does to weather noise, why the ocean gains heat where it is cold, who carries the heat, and what pushes the ocean.
  4. O4 · Nansen’s answer: what the wind does to a rotating ocean 11 November 2026 · The equations of fluid motion in words, why the wind moves the ocean’s top layer at right angles to itself, and how that brings cold water up and builds hills of water that the currents circle.
  5. O5 · The salt ledger: what makes seawater heavy 12 November 2026 · Why the sea is salty in some places and fresh in others, why the Bay of Bengal and the Arabian Sea differ, what makes seawater dense enough to sink, and how to read water masses.
  6. O6 · The overturning: the slow ocean, and can it switch off? 13 November 2026 · Why deep water forms in the North Atlantic but not the North Pacific, how the Southern Ocean’s winds bring it back up, why the round trip takes a thousand years, and how Stommel’s two boxes show that the overturning could switch off.
  7. O7 · Slow swings, and the ocean’s memory for carbon 18 November 2026 · How the tropical Pacific makes El Niño, why it grows and why it turns around, and how three boxes of air and ocean show where our carbon goes, how long it takes, and why CO₂ wouldn’t fall back to 280 ppm if we stopped.
