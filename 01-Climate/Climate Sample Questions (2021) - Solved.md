## Multiple choice
> [!example]- Answers
> 1. **(c) one positive and one negative coupling.** A loop's sign is the product of its couplings: one negative ⇒ negative (stabilising) feedback. Two negatives or none multiply to positive (destabilising). ([[CL04 Feedback and Multiple Steady States]])
> 2. **(b) less than the optimum temperature for daisy growth.** Kump Ch. 2 (read): the white-daisy loop (coverage → albedo → temperature) is negative; it closes into a **negative** feedback only where warming *increases* daisy growth, i.e. **below** the optimum: the stable state P1 is below the optimum, the unstable P2 above it ("the stability of P1 and instability of P2").
> 3. [!verify] The question merges two cores. **EPICA Dome C: ~800 000 years (c)**; Vostok alone: ~400 000 (420 000) years (a). Answer (c) if "Dome C" is meant, (a) for Vostok.
> 4. **(a) Ozone depletion in the Antarctic stratosphere** — caused by CFCs (chlorine chemistry on polar stratospheric clouds), not by global warming. (The sheet has a stray fifth option "d) It will remain the same", a copy-paste leftover.)
> 5. [!verify] Of the **orbital** changes, **precession (d)** has the highest frequency (periods ≈ 19–23 kyr, vs obliquity 41 kyr, eccentricity ~100/400 kyr). **Sunspot activity (b)** (~11 yr) is higher still but is not orbital. ([[CL17 Orbital Forcing and Glacial Cycles]])

## Short answers (2 marks each)
> [!example]- Answers
> 1. The daisies **halve** the response (5 K instead of 10 K): a **negative** feedback. Strength: feedback factor $f=\Delta T_{\text{with}}/\Delta T_{\text{without}}=5/10=0.5$ (Kump's measure: < 1 means negative); equivalently, in $\Delta T=\Delta T_0/(1-g)$, the loop gain is $g=1-10/5=-1$.
> 2. **Albedo**: the fraction of incident solar radiation reflected (0–1; Earth ≈ 0.3). Higher albedo → less absorbed sunlight → cooler: $T_e=[S(1-\alpha)/4\sigma]^{1/4}$; it enters the **ice-albedo feedback** (positive). ([[CL03 Radiation and Planetary Energy Balance]])
> 3. **Silicate weathering**: CO₂ dissolved in rain weathers silicate rocks (net $\mathrm{CaSiO_3+CO_2\to CaCO_3+SiO_2}$ after carbonate burial in the ocean). Weathering is faster when it is warmer and wetter, so warming → more CO₂ removed → less greenhouse → cooling; cooling slows it and volcanic CO₂ accumulates. A **negative feedback** on ~10⁵–10⁶-year timescales: the long-term thermostat. ([[CL16 Atmospheric Evolution Greenhouse Gases and the Carbon Cycle]])
> 4. Moving **north**, the current's **planetary vorticity $f$ increases**. To conserve potential vorticity $(f+\zeta)/H$, its relative vorticity would have to become more negative; instead, **friction with the western boundary** (the shear between the coast and the jet) supplies **positive (cyclonic) relative vorticity**, which balances the input of negative vorticity by the wind over the gyre — the reason the return flow is narrow, fast and on the **west** side. ([[CL19 Wind-Driven Ocean Circulation]], [[CL24 The Ocean's Ledger III - Wind and the Momentum Ledger]])
> 5. Large eruptions inject **SO₂ into the stratosphere**, which forms **sulfate aerosols** that reflect sunlight (raise the planetary albedo) for 1–3 years before settling: a temporary cooling of a few tenths of a kelvin (Pinatubo 1991 ≈ 0.5 K; general knowledge). Volcanic CO₂ warms only on geological timescales.
> 6. **Northern Hemisphere growing season**: from May to October photosynthesis by the NH land biosphere (most land is in the NH) exceeds respiration and draws CO₂ down; in winter respiration and decay dominate and CO₂ rises. ([[CL02 Observing Global Temperature]], [[CL16 Atmospheric Evolution Greenhouse Gases and the Carbon Cycle]])
> 7. A **perturbation** is a temporary disturbance of the system's *state* (it returns, or not, depending on stability); a **forcing** is a sustained change in a factor that *drives* the system, shifting the equilibrium itself (e.g. a change in solar luminosity) — Kump Ch. 2. ([[CL01 Earth as a System]])
> 8. Volume $=14\times10^6\ \text{m}^2\times5\ \text{m}=7\times10^7$ m³; residence time $=V/Q=7\times10^7/12=5.8\times10^6$ s $\approx$ **68 days**.
> 9. $T_e=\big[\frac{S(1-\alpha)}{4\sigma}\big]^{1/4}=\big[\frac{9121\times0.9}{4\times5.67\times10^{-8}}\big]^{1/4}=$ **436 K**, essentially the observed 440 K ⇒ **no significant greenhouse effect: Mercury has (almost) no atmosphere**. (The factor 4 assumes heat is spread over the whole sphere; with no redistribution the subsolar point would reach ~617 K — Mercury's real day/night contrast is huge.)
> 10. Loop: **temperature ↓ → ice/snow cover ↑ → albedo ↑ → absorbed sunlight ↓ → temperature ↓**: all couplings combine to a **positive** feedback. Once ice extends past a critical latitude (≈ 30° in simple energy-balance models), the loop runs away to a **snowball Earth**; escape needs volcanic CO₂ to build up (weathering stops under ice). ([[CL04 Feedback and Multiple Steady States]], [[CL17 Orbital Forcing and Glacial Cycles]])
