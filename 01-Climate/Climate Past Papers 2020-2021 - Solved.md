## 2020 end-sem: the official key, checked
> [!example]- Q1 (3.5 + 1.5) · NADW formation and properties ([[CL21 Thermohaline Circulation Water Masses and Sea Ice]], [[CL25 The Ocean's Ledger IV - Salt, Density and Water Masses]])
> **Key:** the Gulf Stream carries warm water north. It cools by losing heat to cold air, and evaporation makes it saltier. The North Atlantic and Norwegian currents carry the warm, salty water further north; upwelling in the subpolar gyre brings cool water up; brine rejection by sea-ice formation adds salt. Polar oceans have **no permanent thermocline**, so surface and deep water have about the same density. Cold, salty surface water therefore becomes denser than the water below and sinks.
> (b) NADW is cold, very saline and CO₂-rich (higher solubility when cold). T and S are quasi-conservative below the surface (no sources). CO₂ is not conservative: it converts to bicarbonate and carbonate and is changed by biology. ✓

> [!example]- Q2 (3.5 + 1.5) · Precession and synchronous glaciations ([[CL17 Orbital Forcing and Glacial Cycles]])
> **Key:**
> - **Axial precession**: torques from the Sun and Moon on the equatorial bulge make the axis wobble like a top, one cycle per ~25 700 yr. (The key also credits "other planets"; it is mainly the Sun and Moon.)
> - **Apsidal precession**: the orbital ellipse itself rotates, ~112 000 yr.
> - Combined, the **precession of the equinoxes** moves the solstices and equinoxes relative to perihelion, with a **~23 000 yr** cycle.
>
> (b) A globally synchronising feedback: lower NH summer insolation → ice sheets grow → planetary albedo ↑ → global cooling that reaches the SH too (the key also accepts a schematic). (CO₂ drawdown is another.) ✓ (with the one caveat on torques)

> [!example]- Q3 (3 + 2) · The narrow western boundary current keeps the SH subtropical gyre in vorticity balance ([[CL19 Wind-Driven Ocean Circulation]], [[CL24 The Ocean's Ledger III - Wind and the Momentum Ledger]])
> **Key:** the wind gives parcels negative relative vorticity.
> - Eastern side, equatorward flow: $f$ changes, so conservation of absolute vorticity adds positive relative vorticity, and so does friction with the coast. Together they balance the wind's input.
> - Western side, poleward flow: the latitude change adds negative relative vorticity. Only **strong frictional shear from a narrow, fast current** supplies enough positive vorticity to balance it.
>
> 2 marks for a schematic with the correct signs. [!verify] The key's sign language is copied from the NH case. In the SH, $f<0$ and the subtropical gyre is anticlockwise (anticyclonic for the SH), so "negative" and "positive" must be read relative to the hemisphere. The physics (β-effect plus wind torque balanced by western-boundary friction) is the same as [[Climate Sample Questions (2021) - Solved]] Q4.

> [!example]- Q4 (3 + 2) · Eastward NH winds deflect south; a missile at 50°N ([[CL12 Rotating Frames and the Coriolis Force]])
> **Key:** at rest, the poleward horizontal component of gravity (oblate Earth) balances the equatorward horizontal centrifugal component. An eastward wind spins *faster* than the Earth, so its centrifugal force is larger and the equatorward (southward) component wins.
> Missile: $t=10^6/10^3=1000$ s; $f=2\Omega\sin50°=1.117\times10^{-4}$ s⁻¹; $a=fu=0.112$ m s⁻²; deflection $\tfrac12at^2=$ **55.8 km** to the south ✓ (recomputed: 55.86 km).

## 2021 mid-sem (online; the questions in the screenshots)
> [!example]- Section A (MCQs)
> - **Daisyworld, luminosity decreases slightly:** **the overall temperature decrease is smaller than without the daisy feedback**: the feedback is negative (regulating). ([[CL04 Feedback and Multiple Steady States]], [[CL27 Tutorials I - Daisyworld with Land and Ocean]])
> - **Weathering of organic carbon in sedimentary rocks:** **an oxidation reaction**: $\mathrm{CH_2O+O_2\to CO_2+H_2O}$. It consumes O₂ and *adds* CO₂, so "removes CO₂" is false. ([[CL16 Atmospheric Evolution Greenhouse Gases and the Carbon Cycle]])
> - **Lapse rate 6.5 K/km, $T_s=288$, $T_e=255$:** emission from $(288-255)/6.5\approx$ **5 km**. ([[CL09 Radiative-Convective Equilibrium]])
> - **Cloud cover favours** **lower** daytime and **higher** nighttime surface temperatures (albedo by day, longwave trapping by night).
> - **North Pole at the summer solstice** (1368 W m⁻² at the Tropic of Cancer): sun elevation 23.5°, so $1368\sin23.5°=$ **545 W m⁻²**. ([[CL10 Global Energy Balance and Meridional Structure]])
> - **Which condition independently favours an NH glacial phase** (e = 0.05; NH summer at perihelion; tilt near 24.5°)? Strictly **none**: glaciation needs *cool NH summers*, i.e. summer at **aphelion** and **low** tilt (~22°). Summer at perihelion and high tilt both warm NH summers. High eccentricity alone barely changes annual insolation; it only amplifies precession. [!verify] The test probably expected the eccentricity option (the student chose it; its score is not shown).
> - **Hurricanes intensifying with warming:** the **water-vapour feedback**. A warmer ocean and air hold more vapour (~7 %/K), and latent heat is the hurricane's fuel. ([[CL04 Feedback and Multiple Steady States]])

> [!question] Section B · (a) Carbonate buffering reactions and the effect of anthropogenic CO₂ on ocean chemistry (3). (b) Why the ocean is not a permanent CO₂ sink (3).

> [!example]- Solution ([[CL16 Atmospheric Evolution Greenhouse Gases and the Carbon Cycle]], [[CL29 The Ocean's Ledger VI - Slow Swings and the Ocean's Memory for Carbon]], [[CL30 Tutorials III - Three Boxes of Carbon]])
> (a) $\mathrm{CO_2(g)\rightleftharpoons CO_2(aq)}$; $\mathrm{CO_2+H_2O\rightleftharpoons H_2CO_3\rightleftharpoons H^++HCO_3^-}$; $\mathrm{HCO_3^-\rightleftharpoons H^++CO_3^{2-}}$. Net uptake: $\mathrm{CO_2+CO_3^{2-}+H_2O\to2HCO_3^-}$. Effects: dissolved inorganic carbon and bicarbonate rise; **carbonate ion falls**; **pH falls** (ocean acidification); the CaCO₃ saturation state falls (harder for corals and shells).
> (b) Reasons:
> - Uptake consumes $\mathrm{CO_3^{2-}}$, so the **buffer capacity shrinks** (the Revelle factor rises): each extra unit of CO₂ is taken up less readily.
> - Only the **surface layer equilibrates quickly** (~1 yr). The deep ocean is reached only by slow overturning (centuries to millennia).
> - **Warming lowers CO₂ solubility** and stratification slows mixing.
> - The ocean exchanges with the atmosphere in both directions: carbon stored in water is returned at upwelling regions. The permanent removal is by **CaCO₃ burial and silicate weathering** (10⁴–10⁵ yr).

> [!question] Section B · Clouds: type 1 (albedo 0.2, top 220 K), type 2 (0.4, 250 K), type 3 (0.6, 280 K). (a) Which gives the surface maximum solar energy? (b) The largest greenhouse contribution? (c) Which certainly contains liquid water? (d) The net effect of type 2 ($S/4=342$ W m⁻², $T_s=288$ K).

> [!example]- Solution ([[CL07 Optical Properties of the Atmosphere]], [[CL08 Greenhouse Models]])
> (a) **Type 1**: lowest albedo, reflects least.
> (b) **Type 1**: coldest top (high cloud), emits least to space, so it traps the most.
> (c) **Type 3**: top at 280 K > 273 K, so it is liquid. The others could be ice or supercooled.
> (d) Albedo cooling $=0.4\times342=136.8$ W m⁻². Greenhouse warming = the reduction of OLR from surface emission to cloud-top emission $=\sigma(288^4-250^4)=390.1-221.5=168.6$ W m⁻². **Net +31.8 W m⁻²: warming.** (The same estimate gives type 1 +189 and type 3 −164 W m⁻²: high thin clouds warm, low bright clouds cool.)
