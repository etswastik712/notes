# Atmospheric Escape

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-beyond">extra · beyond</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** Neena Lecture 4, p. 11 (2025): escape if the mean molecular speed exceeds 1/6 of the escape velocity.
> 🔵 **extra · beyond:** numbers at an assumed 1000 K.

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Light molecules in a hot, weakly bound atmosphere move fast enough for the fast tail of their speed distribution to leave for good.

> [!key] Definition
> A gas escapes in significant amounts if $\bar v=\sqrt{8k_BT/\pi m}\gtrsim\tfrac16 v_\text{esc}$, $v_\text{esc}^2=2GM/R$. At 1000 K: H₂ 3241, He 2300, H₂O 1084, N₂ 869, CO₂ 694 m/s against $v_\text{esc}/6=1864$ (Earth), 838 (Mars): Earth loses H₂, He and keeps H₂O; Mars loses H₂O and keeps CO₂.

Sensitive to the exospheric temperature (CO₂ on Mars is marginal at 1500 K); only $M/R$ and $T$ matter.

Used in: [[CL16 Atmospheric Evolution Greenhouse Gases and the Carbon Cycle]]. Related: [[Atmospheric Evolution]] · [[Maxwell-Boltzmann Distribution]] · [[Standard Atmosphere]].
