# Snowball Earth

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span> <span class="scope-extra-beyond">extra · beyond</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** evidence, trigger, escape (Argha L4 sl. 48) · 🟡 **extra book:** Earth System Ch. 12 · 🔵 **extra:** hard vs slushball, escape CO₂

> [!intuition] In one picture
> A planet that has frozen into a white mirror can't warm up by ordinary means. Only a slow build-up of volcanic CO₂ (nothing is removing it) eventually overpowers the mirror.

> [!key] Hypothesis
> During the **Cryogenian Period** (~720–635 Ma, two glaciations: Sturtian and Marinoan) ice extended to the equator and the surface was globally frozen (a "snowball"). It is the end state of the [[Ice-Albedo Feedback]] runaway.

**Evidence** (Argha L4, slide 48): glacial sediments at paleolatitudes $<15^\circ$; **cap carbonates** on top; a negative $\delta^{13}$C excursion (productivity crash). **Trigger:** CO₂ drawdown (supercontinent break-up; a large igneous province exposing fresh basalt to weathering). **Escape:** ice stops silicate weathering while volcanism continues, so CO₂ builds up until the greenhouse effect exceeds the albedo effect.

> [!extra-beyond]- Extra (beyond)
> Debate: "hard snowball" (ice everywhere) vs "slushball" (open water near the equator). Geochemical data summarised by Hoffman et al. (2017, *Sci. Adv.* 3, e1600983) support global ice cover during both events. The coined term is due to Kirschvink (1992). Termination needs of order 0.1 bar CO₂ `[verify]` (Pierrehumbert 2004).
