# Insolation

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-beyond">extra · beyond</span>

> [!key] Definition
> **Insolation** is the incoming solar radiation per unit horizontal area at the top of the atmosphere (W m⁻²), averaged over a day, a season or a year. For an unrotated, untilted Earth the flux on a surface at latitude $\varphi$ is $S\cos\varphi$ at local noon, so the **daily mean** is $Q(\varphi)=(S/\pi)\cos\varphi$, largest at the equator, $S/\pi=433$ W m⁻² (435 with $S=1367$), zero at the poles.

- **Why $\cos\varphi$:** the beam has a fixed cross-section, but a tilted surface spreads it over a larger area ([[CL10 Global Energy Balance and Meridional Structure]] §2.1).
- **With tilt (23.4°):** the poles get sunlight in their summer half-year; at the solstice the *daily-mean* insolation is largest at the summer pole (559 W m⁻² at 90° S on 21 Dec) and zero in the polar night. Annual mean: 416 W m⁻² at the equator, 172 at the poles, **global mean $S/4$**.
- **Annual-mean shape:** $\bar Q(\varphi)\approx\tfrac S4\,[1-0.477\,P_2(\sin\varphi)]$, $P_2(x)=\tfrac12(3x^2-1)$ (North 1975; the coefficient is $\tfrac54\langle P_2(\sin\delta)\rangle$ with $\delta$ the Sun's declination).
- General exact formula: $Q=\frac S\pi\rho^2\,[h_0\sin\varphi\sin\delta+\cos\varphi\cos\delta\sin h_0]$ ([[CL10 Global Energy Balance and Meridional Structure#2.4 Daily-mean insolation derived|derivation in CL10]]).

Drives the temperature contrast that powers the circulation ([[CL11 Mean Meridional Circulation and Angular Momentum]]); global mean is the $S/4$ of [[CL03 Radiation and Planetary Energy Balance]]. See [[Solar Constant]], [[Seasons and Earth's Orbit]], [[Top-of-Atmosphere Radiation Budget]].
