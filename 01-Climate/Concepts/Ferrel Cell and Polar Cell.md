# Ferrel Cell and Polar Cell

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span> <span class="scope-extra-beyond">extra · beyond</span>

> [!key] Definition
> In mid-latitudes (≈30–60°) the zonal-mean circulation is a weak **Ferrel cell**: air *rises* at ~60° and *sinks* at ~30°, i.e. flows poleward near the ground and equatorward aloft. It is **thermally indirect**: warm air sinks and cold air rises, the opposite of a heat engine. Poleward of ~60° a weak **Polar cell** is again thermally direct: rising at ~60°, sinking over the pole, equatorward at the surface.

- **Surface winds:** the poleward surface branch of the Ferrel cell is turned east (westerlies); the equatorward branch of the Polar cell is turned west (polar easterlies).
- **Strength:** much weaker than the Hadley cell (Marshall & Plumb call them the "(much) weaker reverse cells"); named after William Ferrel (1817–1891).
- Mid-latitude flow is dominated by **eddies**, which carry most of the poleward heat and angular momentum there.

> [!extra-beyond]- Extra (beyond): why a *reverse* cell?
> The Ferrel cell is a feature of the Eulerian (zonal) mean; it is driven by the eddies' momentum and heat fluxes rather than by heating. In the transformed Eulerian mean (or isentropic) description the mid-latitude overturning is a single thermally direct cell, so the Ferrel cell is partly an artefact of averaging at fixed height/pressure (see Vallis, *Atmospheric and Oceanic Fluid Dynamics*, Ch. 11).
