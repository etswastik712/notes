# Foucault Pendulum

<span class="scope-extra-beyond">extra · beyond</span>

> [!syllabus] Scope of this note
> | Scope | What |
> |---|---|
> | 🔵 **Extra · beyond** | the whole note: a demonstration of the rotating frame, not in the course |

> [!key] Statement
> The plane of swing of a freely swinging pendulum rotates relative to the ground at the rate $\Omega\sin\varphi$ (clockwise in the Northern Hemisphere), completing one turn in $T=2\pi/(\Omega\sin\varphi)=23.93\ \mathrm h/\sin\varphi$. Léon Foucault demonstrated it in Paris in 1851.

> [!intuition] Fixed plane, turning floor
> At the **North Pole** the pendulum swings in a plane fixed in space (the stars) while the Earth turns beneath it once per sidereal day, so relative to the floor the plane turns once in 23.9 h. At the **equator** the floor does not turn about the local vertical, so there is no rotation. At latitude $\varphi$ only the vertical component $\Omega\sin\varphi$ of the rotation matters (as for the [[Coriolis Parameter]], whose $f/2$ is exactly this rate).

| place | latitude | period of one full turn |
|---|---|---|
| North Pole | 90° | 23.9 h |
| Paris | 48.9° | 31.8 h |
| Delhi | 28.6° | 50.0 h |
| Pune | 18.5° | 75.4 h |
| Equator | 0° | no rotation |

**Why.** For swing frequencies $\omega_0\gg f$ the horizontal Coriolis force acts, to leading order, like a slow rotation of the whole swing pattern at **half the Coriolis frequency**, $f/2=\Omega\sin\varphi$ (a Larmor-type result: see the magnetic analogy in [[Rotating Reference Frames]]), while the restoring force sets the fast swing. A *free parcel* with no restoring force instead turns at the full rate $f$ (inertial oscillation, [[CL12 Rotating Frames and the Coriolis Force]] §7).

See [[Coriolis Force]], [[Rotating Reference Frames]], [[Eotvos Effect]].
