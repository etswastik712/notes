# Infrared-Active Molecule

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** Neena Lecture 6, p. 2 (2025).

> [!intuition] In one picture
> A molecule can absorb infrared only if its motion makes an oscillating electric dipole to couple to the light.

> [!key] Definition
> IR absorption needs a **time-varying dipole moment**: vibrations of polar molecules; the asymmetric stretch and bend of CO₂; rotations of polar molecules. N₂, O₂ and the symmetric stretch of CO₂ have none: the atmosphere is transparent to sunlight, opaque to terrestrial IR (mainly H₂O).

The same symmetry argument classifies IR-active modes in chemistry.
