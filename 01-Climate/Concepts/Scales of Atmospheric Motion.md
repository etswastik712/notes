# Scales of Atmospheric Motion

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-beyond">extra · beyond</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** the table of horizontal scales (Suhas pre-midsem, AI notes p.14) · 🔵 **extra:** Rossby-number chart and discussion

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Like a zoom lens: at 10 cm you see molecules and dust devils, at 1000 km you see weather maps, at 10 000 km the planet's waves. Each scale is dominated by different forces, and the Earth's rotation only speaks up for the largest.

> [!key] Typical horizontal scales (m)
> molecular mean free path $10^{-7}$ · turbulent eddies $10^{-2}$–1 · dust devils 1–10 · gusts 10–$10^2$ · tornadoes $10^2$ · cumulonimbus $10^3$ · fronts and squall lines $10^4$–$10^5$ · hurricanes $10^5$ · synoptic cyclones $10^6$ · planetary waves $10^7$.

Rotation (Coriolis) matters only for scales $\gtrsim10^5$–$10^6$ m, where $Ro=U/fL\lesssim1$ ([[Rossby Number]]). Below that, pressure gradient, gravity and friction dominate (sea breezes, thunderstorms, tornadoes).

See [[CL13 Horizontal Motion - Geostrophic Balance and Friction]], [[Pressure Gradient Force]], [[Geostrophic Balance]].
