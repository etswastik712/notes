# Eotvos Effect

<span class="scope-extra-beyond">extra · beyond</span>

> [!syllabus] Scope of this note
> | Scope | What |
> |---|---|
> | 🔵 **Extra · beyond** | the whole note: a consequence of the vertical Coriolis component, not in the course (the underlying components are in [[CL12 Rotating Frames and the Coriolis Force]] §5) |

> [!key] Statement
> A body moving **east** at speed $u$ at latitude $\varphi$ feels an extra **upward** acceleration $2\Omega u\cos\varphi+u^2/a$ (it is lighter); moving **west** it is heavier. It comes from the vertical component of the Coriolis term, $+2\Omega\cos\varphi\,u$, plus the curvature term $u^2/a$.

> [!intuition] Spinning faster, flung harder
> Moving east you rotate about the axis faster than the ground, so the outward (centrifugal) push is bigger; its vertical part reduces your weight. Moving west reduces your rotation rate and increases your weight.

**Numbers.** Train east at 30 m s⁻¹ at the equator: $2\Omega u=4.4\times10^{-3}$ m s⁻² ($0.046$ % of $g$); jet at 250 m s⁻¹: $0.37$ %. Zero for north–south motion; zero effect at the poles (cos φ = 0). Used in marine and airborne gravimetry, where the speed of the ship or aircraft must be corrected for.

See [[Coriolis Force]], [[Centrifugal Force and Effective Gravity]], [[Coriolis Parameter]].
