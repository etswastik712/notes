# Cyclones and Anticyclones

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span> <span class="scope-extra-beyond">extra · beyond</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** cyclonic flow around lows, anticyclonic around highs, surface vs free atmosphere, NH vs SH, satellite images (22 Oct p19–24) · 🟡 **extra book:** gradient wind (W&H §7.2.6) · 🔵 **extra:** curvature numbers, cyclostrophic limit

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Water spiralling down a drain turns the same way as the Earth's rotation: **cyclonic** means 'in the sense of the planet's own spin' (anticlockwise in the NH, clockwise in the SH) and **anticyclonic** the opposite. Highs and lows are the hills and valleys of the pressure map; the wind circles them because it cannot flow straight downhill.

> [!key] Rules (geostrophic, then friction)
> - **Low (L):** NH anticlockwise, SH clockwise (cyclonic). **High (H):** NH clockwise, SH anticlockwise (anticyclonic).
> - **Free atmosphere:** the wind circles parallel to the isobars. **Surface:** friction turns it across the isobars: spiral **inward** at a low (convergence, rising air, cloud and rain) and **outward** at a high (divergence, sinking air, clear skies).
> - Large-scale lows and highs are in near-geostrophic balance ($Ro\sim0.1$); tropical cyclones are not ($Ro\gtrsim1$, so curvature matters).

**Gradient wind** (🟡/🔵): for circular flow of radius $R$, $V^2/R+fV=|\nabla p|/\rho$ at a low (slower than geostrophic) and $V^2/R-fV+|\nabla p|/\rho=0$ at a high (faster, and the gradient is capped at $f^2R/4$).

See [[CL13 Horizontal Motion - Geostrophic Balance and Friction]], [[Geostrophic Balance]], [[Planetary Boundary Layer and Friction]], [[Rossby Number]]; monsoon lows in [[CL15 Monsoons]].
