# Zonal-Mean Zonal Wind

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** the latitude–pressure section, annual/DJF/JJA, easterlies/westerlies, jets, ~30 m/s at ~200 hPa (21 Oct p7–12; notes p.2) · 🟡 **extra book:** M&P Fig. 5.20 as printed and footnote on instantaneous speeds

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Slice the atmosphere along a meridian like a layer cake and colour each point by the average eastward wind (average over all longitudes and many years). Two green mountains of westerlies (the jets) flank a brown plateau of weak easterlies (the tropics).

> [!key] Definition
> $\overline u(\varphi,p)=\dfrac1{2\pi}\displaystyle\oint u(\lambda,\varphi,p,t)\,\mathrm d\lambda$, further averaged in time: annual mean, December–February (DJF) or June–August (JJA). Observed pattern: easterly in the tropics, westerly in mid-latitudes, speed increasing with height away from the equator, maximum ≈ 30 m s⁻¹ near 200 hPa and 30–35° latitude, stronger in the winter hemisphere.

- **Use:** a *metric* to separate the tropical from the mid-latitude atmosphere (the zero contour near 25–30° at the surface).
- **Limitation:** hides longitude structure and the instantaneous peak (mean 30 vs ~50 m s⁻¹).
- **Interpretation:** thermal-wind balance with the zonal-mean temperature field ([[Thermal Wind]]); the maximum sits where $|\partial\overline T/\partial y|$ is largest. A computable illustration (idealised, not data) is Fig. CL14.1 of [[CL14 Observed Winds - Jets, Trades and the ITCZ]].
- **Weather vs climate:** the average is an expectation over longitude and time; departures are eddies ([[Storm Tracks and Eddy Fluxes]], [[Probability - Course Map]]).
