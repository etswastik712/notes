# Water-Vapour Feedback

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span> <span class="scope-extra-beyond">extra · beyond</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** positive feedback that roughly doubles the Planck-only response (Suhas notes p.18–19) · 🟡 **extra book:** Clausius–Clapeyron 7 % K⁻¹, $f\approx0.5$, runaway greenhouse (W&H §10.3.2) · 🔵 **extra:** layer-model estimate $f=0.4$–0.7

> [!intuition] In one picture
> A warmer atmosphere can hold more water vapour, a greenhouse gas, which warms it further: an amplifier wired in parallel with the thermostat. It is a feedback on the temperature fixed by the long-lived gases, not an independent driver.

> [!key] Statement
> The saturation vapour pressure rises about **7 % per kelvin** ([[Clausius-Clapeyron Relation]]). At roughly constant relative humidity the water-vapour content rises likewise and the longwave optical depth grows, so $\varepsilon$ increases with $T$: a **positive feedback**, $f_{\rm WV}\approx0.5$ (W&H), i.e. a **gain** $1/(1-f)=2$ on the Planck-only response ([[Climate Feedback Factor]]).

- **Layer model** ([[CL08 Greenhouse Models]]): $f=\dfrac{\mathrm dT_s}{\mathrm d\varepsilon}\dfrac{\mathrm d\varepsilon}{\mathrm dT}$ with $\mathrm dT_s/\mathrm d\varepsilon=59$ K and $\mathrm d\varepsilon/\mathrm dT=(1-\varepsilon)\times0.07\,s\,\tau$: $f=0.41$ for a water-vapour share $s=0.3$ of the optical depth, $0.69$ for $s=0.5$.
- **Numbers:** CO₂ doubling $F=3.7$ W m⁻²: 1.1 K without feedback, **2.2 K** with $f=0.5$.
- **Nonlinearity:** the feedback strengthens with temperature (Clausius–Clapeyron is exponential); if tropical SST passed ~60 °C the factor would approach 1: **runaway greenhouse** (possibly Venus).
- Fast (days): vapour responds almost instantly, so it acts even in transient responses. Controlled by evaporation and condensation, hence a **feedback**, unlike the long-lived gases that set the temperature ([[Greenhouse Gases and Absorption Bands]]).

See [[CL04 Feedback and Multiple Steady States]], [[CL07 Optical Properties of the Atmosphere]], [[Greenhouse Effect]]; thermodynamics: [[Thermodynamics - Course Map]].
