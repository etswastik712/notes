# Intertropical Convergence Zone

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-beyond">extra · beyond</span>

> [!key] Definition
> The **ITCZ** is the band near the equator where the trade winds of the two hemispheres **converge**, air rises in deep convection, and rainfall is heavy: the **ascending branch of the Hadley cells**. On satellite images it is the narrow line of bright cloud girdling the tropical oceans.

Suhas (14 Oct): the tropics are *defined by the dynamics of the air*, and the ITCZ is the signature of that definition.

- **Position:** follows the warmest surface and the overhead Sun, shifting to the summer hemisphere at the solstices (DJF: just south of the equator; JJA: north); the annual mean lies slightly north of the equator.
- **Link to the wet tropics:** mean ascent coincides with the cold cloud tops in outgoing longwave radiation; regions of mean descent are dry and cloud-free (deserts).
- **Why convergence:** the near-surface equatorward flow of each Hadley cell meets at the equator; mass must go up.

> [!extra-beyond]- Extra (beyond)
> Over the Asian sector the seasonal migration is so large that the ITCZ becomes the **monsoon trough** ([[CL15 Monsoons]]). Heating by latent release in the ITCZ is what drives the Hadley circulation ([[Thermodynamics - Course Map]]: heat engine).
