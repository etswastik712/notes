# Storm Tracks and Eddy Fluxes

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span> <span class="scope-extra-beyond">extra · beyond</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** eddies as the key agency of mid–high-latitude heat and moisture transport; localized highs and lows (notes pp.2–3) · 🟡 **extra book:** storm tracks and eddy heat-flux maps (Hartmann §6.5, Figs 6.17–6.18) · 🔵 **extra:** link to the eddy-driven jet

> [!intuition] In one picture
> The jet is a river; the storms (cyclones and anticyclones) are the whirlpools that form where the river is fast and the temperature contrast across it is sharp. Whirlpools mix water between the banks: eddies mix heat poleward.

> [!key] Definition
> **Eddies** are departures from the zonal and time mean (the lows and highs of an instantaneous weather map). **Storm tracks** are the regions where transient eddies (periods shorter than ~1 week) are most active, i.e. where mid-latitude cyclones most frequently pass; their **eddy heat flux** $\overline{v'T'}$ carries heat poleward.

- **Location:** downstream of the Tibetan Plateau and the Rocky Mountains, over the northern Pacific and Atlantic (northern winter); in the SH, a ring near 50–60° S, strongest in the Atlantic and Indian sectors (Hartmann §6.5).
- **Role in the budget:** the key agency of poleward heat, moisture and (via momentum flux) angular-momentum transport at mid–high latitudes: they carry angular momentum poleward and downward to the surface westerlies ([[CL11 Mean Meridional Circulation and Angular Momentum]], [[Poleward Energy Transport]]).
- **Link to jets:** eddies grow on the strong temperature gradient under the jet (baroclinic instability) and, in turn, drive the eddy-driven jet ([[Jet Streams]]).
