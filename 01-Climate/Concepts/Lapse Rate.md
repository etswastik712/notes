# Lapse Rate

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-beyond">extra · beyond</span>

> [!key] Definition and sign convention
> The **lapse rate** is the rate of decrease of temperature with height, $\Gamma\equiv-\mathrm dT/\mathrm dz$ (K km⁻¹). It is *positive* when temperature falls with height; a layer where $T$ increases with height ($\Gamma<0$) is an **inversion**.

| lapse rate | meaning | value |
|---|---|---|
| environmental $\Gamma$ | what a balloon sounding measures | 6–7 K km⁻¹ mean in the troposphere; wide range locally |
| dry adiabatic $\Gamma_d$ | an unsaturated parcel's cooling | 9.8 ([[Dry Adiabatic Lapse Rate]]) |
| saturated $\Gamma_s$ | a saturated parcel's cooling | 4–9 (smaller in warm humid air) |
| radiative equilibrium $\Gamma_{RE}$ | with no motion, grey-gas example | ≈ 23 near the ground ([[CL09 Radiative-Convective Equilibrium]]) |

> [!extra-beyond]- 🔵 Extra (beyond): the standard atmosphere
> The International Standard Atmosphere uses $\Gamma=6.5$ K km⁻¹ from the ground (288.15 K) to 11 km (216.65 K), then an isothermal layer to 20 km ($\Gamma=0$), then a layer where $T$ *increases* by 1 K km⁻¹ to 32 km ($\Gamma=-1$: ozone heating).

Comparing $\Gamma$ with $\Gamma_d$ or $\Gamma_s$ classifies a layer as stable, neutral or unstable ([[Atmospheric Stability]]); convection limits $\Gamma$ ([[Convective Adjustment]]). The surface temperature follows from $T_s\approx T_e+\Gamma z_e$ (CL09 §3). Related: [[CL06 Hydrostatic Balance and Vertical Structure]] (vertical structure), [[Greenhouse Effect]].
