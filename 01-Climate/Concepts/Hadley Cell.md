# Hadley Cell

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span> <span class="scope-extra-beyond">extra · beyond</span>

> [!key] Definition
> The **Hadley cell** is the thermally direct overturning circulation of the tropics: air **rises** near the equator (in the [[Intertropical Convergence Zone]]), flows **poleward aloft**, **sinks** in the subtropics (~30°) and returns **equatorward near the surface** as the [[Trade Winds and Westerlies|trade winds]]. There is one cell in each hemisphere; in the annual mean ascent is a little north of the equator, but at the solstices ascent shifts to the summer hemisphere and the winter-hemisphere cell dominates.

**Why it exists.** Tropical air columns are warm and therefore *thick*, so pressure surfaces slope down toward the pole aloft and drive a poleward pressure-gradient force (Suhas, 8 Oct). **Why it stops at ~30°.** A ring of air carried poleward conserves absolute angular momentum ([[Absolute Angular Momentum of Air]]) and would need $u=\Omega a\sin^2\varphi/\cos\varphi$: 14, 58, 134 m s⁻¹ at 10°, 20°, 30°, impossible beyond that, so the cell must end; the large westerlies it builds at its poleward edge are the [[Subtropical Jet]].

**Numbers.** Upper-branch meridional wind up to ~3 m s⁻¹; mass overturning of order $10^{11}$ kg s⁻¹ ($10^2$ on the $10^9$ kg s⁻¹ scale of Marshall & Plumb Fig. 5.21), see [[Mass Stream Function]]. Descent is warm and dry (adiabatic compression), giving the subtropical deserts and the *trade inversion*; horizontal temperature gradients inside the cell are very weak (efficient transport).

> [!extra-beyond]- Extra (beyond): theory of its width
> The Held & Hou (1980) model gives the edge $\varphi_H=(5R/3)^{1/2}$ with $R=gH\Delta\theta/(\theta_0\Omega^2a^2)$: 20° for Earth-like parameters and $\propto1/\Omega$. See [[CL11 Mean Meridional Circulation and Angular Momentum]].
