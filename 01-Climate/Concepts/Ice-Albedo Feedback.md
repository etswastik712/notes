# Ice-Albedo Feedback

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** mechanism, $f_\alpha$, multiple states (Argha L4) · 🔵 **extra:** present-day magnitude of $f_\alpha$

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Ice is a mirror: cold makes more ice, more ice reflects more sun, less sun makes it colder. A self-reinforcing loop, like a snowball rolling downhill gathering more snow.

> [!key] Mechanism
> Cooling → more snow and ice → higher albedo → less absorbed sunlight → more cooling (and the reverse for warming). A **positive** feedback: $f_\alpha=a\,\dfrac{S}{16\sigma T^{3}}>0$ with $a=-\mathrm d\alpha/\mathrm dT$.

![[Ice-Albedo Feedback Loop.svg|520]]

- For the slide-45 ramp ($a=0.0125$ K⁻¹), $f_\alpha\approx1.5>1$ in the transition zone: instability, hence three steady states and hysteresis ([[CL04 Feedback and Multiple Steady States]]).
- In the present climate the *global* $f_\alpha$ is only $\sim0.1$ because ice covers a small area; it matters most at high latitude (Arctic amplification).
- Large enough, it permits [[Snowball Earth]]; a tipping element in [[Bistability and Tipping Points]].

See [[Albedo]], [[Climate Feedback Factor]].
