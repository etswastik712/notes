# Top-of-Atmosphere Radiation Budget

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span>

> [!key] Definition
> The net radiation at the top of the atmosphere is $R_{TOA}=Q_{abs}-\mathrm{OLR}$, with absorbed solar radiation $Q_{abs}=S_{TOA}(1-\alpha)$ (Hartmann Eqs. 2.19–2.20). It is purely radiative and can be measured from satellites (ERBE, CERES). $R_{TOA}>0$: the column gains energy.

**Global mean:** 340 W m⁻² incident, ≈ 100 reflected (planetary albedo ≈ 0.29), ≈ 240 W m⁻² absorbed and emitted: $\langle R_{TOA}\rangle\approx0$ (any non-zero value heats or cools the planet).

**Geography of the three terms** (Hartmann §2.8):

| term | largest | smallest |
|---|---|---|
| albedo $\alpha$ | polar regions (cloud, snow, large zenith angle); secondary maxima under thick tropical clouds and over the Sahara | tropical oceans with few clouds (8–10 %) |
| [[Outgoing Longwave Radiation]] | warm deserts and cloud-free tropical oceans | poles; regions of high, cold tropical clouds |
| net $R_{TOA}$ | subtropical summer-hemisphere oceans (~140 W m⁻²) | winter polar night |

Zonal mean: $R_{TOA}>0$ equatorward of ≈ 35–40°, $<0$ poleward; the pattern implies the [[Poleward Energy Transport]]. Quantity feeding [[CL03 Radiation and Planetary Energy Balance]] (global mean) and [[CL10 Global Energy Balance and Meridional Structure]] (latitude dependence). See [[Albedo]], [[Insolation]].
