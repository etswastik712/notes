# Subtropical Jet

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span> <span class="scope-extra-beyond">extra · beyond</span>

> [!key] Definition
> The **subtropical jet** is a narrow band of strong westerly wind near **30° latitude** at **~200 hPa (~10 km)**, on the poleward flank of the Hadley cell. It is strongest in the **winter** hemisphere.

- **Strength:** zonal-mean speed ~30 m s⁻¹; instantaneous local maxima ~50 m s⁻¹ (the jet meanders in longitude and time, so averaging smooths it). A weaker jet (~20 m s⁻¹) lies near 45° in the summer hemisphere.
- **Origin:** poleward-moving upper-level air conserves absolute angular momentum and is turned east by Coriolis ([[Absolute Angular Momentum of Air]]): the jet is "driven in large part by the advection from the equator of large values of absolute angular momentum by the Hadley circulation" (M&P §8.2.1). Equivalent view: the sharp temperature gradient at the poleward edge of the cell implies, via thermal wind balance, westerlies increasing with height.
- **The ring-of-air wind overshoots:** conservation from the equator would give 134 m s⁻¹ at 30°; eddy mixing and friction reduce it to the observed ~30–50 m s⁻¹.

> [!extra-beyond]- Extra (beyond): two jets
> A second, **eddy-driven** (polar-front) jet sits near 50–60°; in some seasons and longitudes the two merge. The Held–Hou model predicts a wind $u_M=\Omega a\varphi_H^2\approx59$ m s⁻¹ at its Hadley edge (20°).
