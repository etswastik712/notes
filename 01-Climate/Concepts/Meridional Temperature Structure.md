# Meridional Temperature Structure

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span> <span class="scope-extra-beyond">extra · beyond</span>

> [!key] Summary
> In the annual and zonal mean the **temperature** falls with height and (in the troposphere) poleward: the annual-mean surface temperature is below 0 °C poleward of ≈ 60° and peaks near 27 °C just north of the equator; the pole-to-equator difference through the troposphere is ≈ 40 °C (M&P §5.1.4).

- **Potential temperature** $\theta=T(p_0/p)^{R/c_p}$ (conserved in adiabatic motion): surfaces of constant $\theta$ (**isentropes**; $\mathrm d\theta=0\Rightarrow\mathrm dS=0$) slope **upward toward the pole** in the troposphere; $\theta$ (unlike $T$) **always increases with height**, reflecting the stability of the atmosphere to dry processes.
- Widely spaced $\theta$ contours = troposphere, closely spaced = stratosphere; the **tropopause** is higher in the tropics (🟡) than over the pole.
- The horizontal temperature gradient implies, through hydrostatic balance, a horizontal pressure gradient aloft: the engine of [[CL11 Mean Meridional Circulation and Angular Momentum]] and (with [[Coriolis Force]]) of the westerly jets ([[CL13 Horizontal Motion - Geostrophic Balance and Friction]]).

Details, an idealised cross-section figure and the derivation of $\theta$: [[CL10 Global Energy Balance and Meridional Structure#5. Meridional structure of temperature|CL10 §5]]. See [[Ideal Gas Law]], [[CL06 Hydrostatic Balance and Vertical Structure]].
