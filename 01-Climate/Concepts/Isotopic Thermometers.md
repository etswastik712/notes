# Isotopic Thermometers

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span> <span class="scope-extra-beyond">extra · beyond</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** $\delta^{18}$O definition, why polar ice is light, ice sheets and foraminifera, calibration and depth→time (Argha L2 sl. 21–23) · 🟡 **extra book:** ice-layer counting (Kump Ch. 1) · 🔵 **extra:** Rayleigh model, fractionation factors, caveats

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Heavy raindrops fall first. Each time a cooling air mass rains, the heavy isotope leaves preferentially, so what is left (and the snow that falls later, farther and colder) is lighter and lighter. The lightness of the snow therefore records how cold the journey was.

> [!key] Definition
> An **isotopic thermometer** infers past temperature from the **stable isotope ratio** of a preserved material, usually
> $$\delta^{18}\mathrm O=\Big(\frac{(^{18}\mathrm O/^{16}\mathrm O)_{\rm sample}}{(^{18}\mathrm O/^{16}\mathrm O)_{\rm standard}}-1\Big)\times1000\ \text{‰}$$
> measured in **ice cores** (the ice itself) or in **foraminiferal CaCO₃** in ocean sediment. Standard VSMOW: $2005.2\times10^{-6}$.

- **Ice:** evaporation prefers $^{16}$O; $^{18}$O rains out first on the way to the pole ⇒ colder site ⇒ lighter ice ($\delta\approx-50$ ‰ at the poles). Empirical spatial calibration: $\delta^{18}\mathrm O=0.695\,T_{\rm ann}-13.6$ ‰ (Dansgaard 1964).
- **Ocean/forams:** growing light ice sheets leave the ocean heavy; shells record ice volume and temperature (colder ⇒ heavier, about 0.23 ‰ per °C `[verify]`).
- **Model:** Rayleigh distillation $R=R_0F^{\alpha-1}$ with $\alpha(T)$ from Majoube; idealised slope 1.6 ‰ per °C, about twice the empirical one.
- **Caveats:** calibration is empirical; spatial slope ≠ temporal slope; mixed signals in ocean records; dating by layer counting, ash layers, ¹⁴C, magnetic reversals.
