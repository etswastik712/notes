# Atmospheric Composition

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span> <span class="scope-extra-beyond">extra · beyond</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** composition of dry air, abundance measures, well-mixed vs short-lived gases (Suhas; Argha L5 sl. 55) · 🟡 **extra book:** W&H Table 1.1 values · 🔵 **extra:** updated CO₂/CH₄/N₂O

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Almost the whole atmosphere is two inert gases; the gases that run the climate are a few hundred parts per million. Abundance and climatic importance are anti-correlated.

> [!key] Dry air by volume (mole fraction)
> N₂ 78.08 %, O₂ 20.95 %, Ar 0.93 %; CO₂ ≈ 420 ppm (380 ppm in the 2006 table), Ne 18 ppm, He 5 ppm, CH₄ ≈ 1.9 ppm, Kr 1 ppm, H₂ 0.5 ppm, N₂O ≈ 0.34 ppm, O₃ 0–0.1 ppm. Water vapour: 0.001–5 % (highly variable; ~0.25 % of the mass).

- $M_d=\sum x_iM_i=28.97$ g mol⁻¹ ⇒ $R_d=287.0$ J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹ ([[Ideal Gas Law]]).
- **Mixing ratios** are conserved by an expanding parcel; concentrations are not ([[Mixing Ratio and Humidity]]).
- **Long-lived** gases (N₂, O₂, Ar, CO₂, CH₄) are well mixed up to the turbopause (~105 km); **short-lived** gases (H₂O, O₃, NO₂) stay near their sources.
- The infrared-active gases are all trace (H₂O, CO₂, O₃, CH₄, N₂O): diatomic N₂, O₂ do not absorb in the thermal infrared ([[Greenhouse Effect]], [[CL07 Optical Properties of the Atmosphere]]).
