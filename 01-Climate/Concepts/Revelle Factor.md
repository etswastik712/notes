# Revelle Factor

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** Joy O7 notes §7; carbon-boxes handout §2.2

> [!intuition] In one picture
> The ocean's appetite for CO₂ falls as it eats: each tonne absorbed makes the water more acidic and less willing to take the next.

> [!key] Definition
> Seawater holds carbon mostly as HCO₃⁻ and CO₃²⁻: $1/B=1+k_1/[\mathrm H^+]+k_1k_2/[\mathrm H^+]^2\approx170$ pre-industrially (why the ocean holds ~50–60× the air's carbon). Absorbing CO₂ releases H⁺, lowering pH and $1/B$. Consequences: ~22 % of our carbon stays in the air once the ocean catches up; 24 % of a 1000 GtC pulse but 42 % of 5000 GtC; without it (a linear model) only 1.6 %.
