# Jet Streams

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span> <span class="scope-extra-beyond">extra · beyond</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** definition, both hemispheres all seasons, stronger in winter, strength not equal at all longitudes, 200 hPa ~30 m/s (21 Oct p11–12, p18; notes p.2) · 🟡 **extra book:** local maxima downstream of the Tibetan Plateau and Rockies (Hartmann §6.5) · 🔵 **extra:** two kinds of jet, thermal-wind scaling

> [!intuition] In one picture
> A jet stream is a high-altitude river of air: a narrow ribbon of fast westerlies, a few hundred kilometres wide and a few kilometres deep, that meanders around the hemisphere. It sits above the place where the temperature changes fastest from warm to cold.

> [!key] Definition
> **Jet streams** are narrow regions of strong winds (the slide's wording) found in both hemispheres in all seasons, strongest in the winter hemisphere. In the zonal mean the **subtropical jet** peaks near 30–35° at ≈ 200 hPa with ≈ 30 m s⁻¹ (instantaneous maxima ≈ 50 m s⁻¹); a weaker one (~20 m s⁻¹) lies near 45° in the summer hemisphere.

- **Why they exist:** a strong latitudinal temperature gradient ⇒ [[Thermal Wind]]; plus poleward transport of angular momentum by the Hadley cell ([[Absolute Angular Momentum of Air]]). Jet speed $\propto\Delta T$.
- **Two kinds:** the **subtropical jet** (≈ 30°, at the Hadley-cell edge, [[Subtropical Jet]]) and the **polar-front / eddy-driven jet** (≈ 50–60°, maintained by eddy momentum fluxes, [[Storm Tracks and Eddy Fluxes]]).
- **Not zonally symmetric:** local maxima over East Asia/West Pacific and North America/Atlantic in northern winter; the zonal mean smooths them.
- **Why it matters:** jets steer storms, set the position of the storm tracks, and (with the monsoon) shape regional rainfall.
