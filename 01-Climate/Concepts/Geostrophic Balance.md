# Geostrophic Balance

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** $v_g=\frac1{\rho f}\frac{\partial p}{\partial x}$, $u_g=-\frac1{\rho f}\frac{\partial p}{\partial y}$, flow parallel to isobars (22 Oct p16–17, 19–22) · 🟡 **extra book:** Buys-Ballot law, pressure-coordinate form, non-divergence (M&P §7.1)

> [!intuition] In one picture
> A hiker who refuses to lose height walks along a contour line of the hill. The air wants to run "downhill" from high to low pressure, but the Coriolis force turns it to the right until it runs *along* the contours (isobars): the pressure push and the Coriolis turn cancel.

> [!key] Definition
> **Geostrophic balance** is the steady, frictionless balance between the pressure gradient force and the Coriolis force: $f\,\hat{\mathbf z}\times\mathbf u_g=-\frac1\rho\nabla p$, i.e.
> $$u_g=-\frac{1}{\rho f}\frac{\partial p}{\partial y},\qquad v_g=\frac{1}{\rho f}\frac{\partial p}{\partial x},\qquad|\mathbf u_g|=\frac{|\nabla p|}{\rho|f|}.$$

- Wind blows **parallel to the isobars**, with **low pressure on the left in the NH** (right in the SH): Buys-Ballot's law. Speed $\propto$ gradient and $\propto1/f$.
- Valid when the Rossby number is small, away from the ground and from the equator ([[Rossby Number]]).
- Example: 4 hPa per 300 km at 45° N gives 10.8 m s⁻¹.
