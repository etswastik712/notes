# Standard Atmosphere

<span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span> <span class="scope-extra-beyond">extra · beyond</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟡 **extra book:** the idea and layers (W&H Ch. 1, Figs. 1.8–1.9) · 🔵 **extra:** the layer-by-layer computation and table (computed here). Not shown in class

> [!intuition] In one picture
> One tidy average profile, 'the atmosphere' as a function of height only, so that pilots, modellers and students agree on typical numbers.

> [!key] Definition
> The **US Standard Atmosphere 1976** prescribes $T(Z)$ in layers of constant lapse rate; $p(Z)$ follows from hydrostatic balance and the gas law. Surface values: 1013.25 hPa, 288.15 K, 1.225 kg m⁻³.

| layer base (km) | $T$ (K) | lapse (K km⁻¹) | $p$ (hPa) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 288.15 | −6.5 | 1013.25 |
| 11 (tropopause) | 216.65 | 0 | 226.32 |
| 20 | 216.65 | +1.0 | 54.75 |
| 32 | 228.65 | +2.8 | 8.68 |
| 47 (stratopause) | 270.65 | 0 | 1.109 |
| 51 | 270.65 | −2.8 | 0.669 |
| 71 | 214.65 | −2.0 | 0.0396 |

- Facts: 50 % of the mass below 5.5 km, 78 % below 11 km, 95 % below 20 km; 540 hPa at 5 km; 314 hPa at Everest.
- Real tropopause: ~16–17 km in the tropics, ~8–9 km at the poles (the standard 11 km is mid-latitude).
- The tropospheric lapse rate is set by convection ([[Lapse Rate]], [[CL09 Radiative-Convective Equilibrium]]); the stratospheric warming by ozone absorption.
