# Greenhouse Gases and Absorption Bands

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span> <span class="scope-extra-beyond">extra · beyond</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** N₂/O₂ vs asymmetric greenhouse molecules, transitions between energy levels (Argha L5 sl. 57–59) · 🟡 **extra book:** $E=E_o+E_v+E_r$, line broadening (W&H §4.4.3) · 🔵 **extra:** mode table, dipole rule, symmetry, attribution shares

> [!intuition] In one picture
> To absorb light a molecule needs a resonant 'note' and a 'handle': a vibration or rotation that makes its electric dipole oscillate so the light wave can grab it. Symmetric N₂ and O₂ have no handle; bent H₂O and the bending or asymmetric-stretching CO₂ do.

> [!key] Rule
> A photon is absorbed only if $h\nu=\Delta E$ between two allowed molecular states **and** the transition changes the dipole moment ($\partial\mu/\partial Q\ne0$ for vibrations; a permanent dipole for pure rotation).

| gas | main infrared bands (µm) | notes |
|---|---|---|
| **H₂O** | 2.7, 6.3 (bend), > 17 (rotation) | all three modes active; dominates the effect, responds to temperature ([[Water-Vapour Feedback]]) |
| **CO₂** | 4.26 (asymmetric stretch), **15.0 (bend)** | symmetric stretch (7.2 µm) is IR-inactive; no pure rotation |
| **O₃** | 9.6, UV < 0.31 | sits in the window; absorbs UV |
| **CH₄** | 3.3, 7.7 | |
| **N₂O** | 4.5, 7.8, 17 | |
| **N₂, O₂** | essentially none in vis/IR | symmetric diatomics: no dipole |

Photon energies: 15 µm = 0.083 eV (vibrational quantum); rotational quanta are ~10⁻³–10⁻² eV (far IR); electronic transitions are UV/visible. A "trace" gas matters through the **number of absorbers × cross-section at the wavelengths where Earth emits** (CO₂ column: 6.6 kg m⁻²).
*Extra:* single-gas attribution (Schmidt et al. 2010, `[verify]`): water vapour ≈ 50 %, clouds ≈ 25 %, CO₂ ≈ 19 %, rest ≈ 6 % of the greenhouse effect.
