# Convection

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span>

> [!key] Definition
> **Convection** is vertical transport of heat (and moisture, momentum) by buoyancy-driven fluid motion: warm, light parcels rise and cool, heavy parcels sink. It occurs when the environment is statically unstable ([[Atmospheric Stability]]).

- **Dry** convection (unsaturated; limit $\Gamma_d$) vs **moist** convection (condensing; limit $\Gamma_s$, much stronger because of latent heat); **shallow** (cumulus, boundary layer) vs **deep** (cumulonimbus reaching the tropopause: heavy rain, thunderstorms).
- **Two ways to start it** (taught): warm the surface (afternoon heating, tropics, summer) or cool the air aloft (cold fronts, mid-latitude thunderstorms) ([[CL09 Radiative-Convective Equilibrium]], §6).
- **Effect:** it moves heat up from the ground, so it *cools the surface and warms the free troposphere* at unchanged OLR; it limits the lapse rate ([[Convective Adjustment]]); with radiation it defines **radiative–convective equilibrium**.
- **Size of the flux:** the global mean surface heat flux carried by evaporation is ~80 W m⁻² (latent) and ~20 W m⁻² (sensible) `[verify]` against a budget table, compared with 240 W m⁻² absorbed sunlight: the energy balance of [[Zero-Dimensional Energy Balance Model]] hides this vertical conveyor belt.
- **Diagnostic:** deep convection shows as low OLR ([[Outgoing Longwave Radiation]], [[Intertropical Convergence Zone]]).

> [!intuition] A pot of water on the hob
> Heat from below makes the bottom water light; it rises, cool water sinks, and the whole pot mixes. The atmosphere is a pot heated from below by sunlight on the ground; the tropics are the part of the pot that is boiling.

In the laboratory the mixed layer burrows into a stratified tank as $\sqrt t$ (CL09 §8). Large-scale consequence: tropical convection feeds the [[Hadley Cell]].
