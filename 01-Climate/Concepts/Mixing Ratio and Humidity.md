# Mixing Ratio and Humidity

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span> <span class="scope-extra-beyond">extra · beyond</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** mass mixing ratio, mole fraction, conserved tracers, vapour pressure (Suhas pre-midsem) · 🟡 **extra book:** RH, dew point (W&H §3.5.1) · 🔵 **extra:** Bolton's formula

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Concentration is 'grams per cubic metre' and changes when a parcel is stretched; a mixing ratio is 'grams per gram of dry air' and travels with the parcel. Relative humidity is how full the air's sponge is, and the sponge doubles in size every 10 K.

> [!key] Definitions
> Mass mixing ratio $r_i=m_i/m_d$; for water vapour $w=m_v/m_d=\varepsilon e/(p-e)$ and specific humidity $q=m_v/(m_v+m_d)=w/(1+w)$. Mole fraction $x_i=r_iM_d/M_i$. Relative humidity $\mathrm{RH}=e/e_s(T)\approx w/w_s$; dew point $T_d$: $e_s(T_d)=e$.

- Saturation vapour pressure (Bolton): $e_s=6.112\exp[17.67T_c/(T_c+243.5)]$ hPa; 1.26, 6.11, 12.27, 23.37, 42.46 hPa at −20, 0, 10, 20, 30 °C: about +7 % per K ([[Clausius-Clapeyron Relation]]).
- Example: 25 °C, RH 60 %, 1000 hPa ⇒ $e=19.0$ hPa, $w=12.0$ g kg⁻¹, $T_d=16.7$ °C, $T_v-T=2.2$ K.
- CO₂ 420 ppmv ⇒ 638 ppm by mass.
- $w$ and $q$ are conserved in a rising parcel until condensation; they are the variables of the water budget.
