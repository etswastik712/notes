# Outgoing Longwave Radiation

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span>

> [!key] Definition
> **OLR** is the thermal (longwave) radiation emitted by the Earth–atmosphere system to space, measured at the top of the atmosphere. Globally $\approx240$ W m⁻² $=\sigma T_e^4$ with $T_e=255$ K ([[Effective Emission Temperature]]).

**What controls it.** The *temperature of the emitting substance*: OLR is large where a warm surface radiates through a dry, cloudless atmosphere (subtropical deserts, cloud-free tropical oceans) and small where the emitter is cold: poles, and the tops of tall, cold tropical clouds (Hartmann §2.8; [[Stefan-Boltzmann Law]] $\propto T^4$).

- Zonal mean: varies much less with latitude than absorbed sunlight (≈ 245–265 W m⁻² in the tropics, ≈ 150–190 at the poles), because heat transport smooths temperature.
- Slight dip at the equator: cold tops of deep convective clouds (M&P Fig. 5.5 caption, Fig. 4.26) 🟡.
- Observations of OLR are used as a proxy for tropical convection (low OLR = high cloud = rising air).

Related: [[Top-of-Atmosphere Radiation Budget]], [[Greenhouse Effect]] (the gap between surface emission and OLR), [[CL07 Optical Properties of the Atmosphere]], [[CL09 Radiative-Convective Equilibrium]].
