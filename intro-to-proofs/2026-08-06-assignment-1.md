# Assignment 1

## Covered
Concepts: mathematical statements, truth tables, conjunction, disjunction, negation, logical equivalence, conditional statements, tautologies and contradictions.

Notation (P, Q, R are statements): $\lnot P$ (not P), $P\land Q$ (and), $P\lor Q$ (or), $P\Rightarrow Q$ (if P then Q), $P\Leftrightarrow Q$ (iff), $P\sim Q$ (logically equivalent). The symbol $=$ is for equality of mathematical objects; $\sim$ or $\equiv$ is for logical equivalence.

Definition given: an integer $p\ge 2$ is prime if its only divisors are $1$ and $p$.

Problems and what each practises:
1. Truth tables for $P\lor Q$, $\lnot P$, $\lnot(P\land Q)$, $(P\lor Q)\land(P\lor R)$, $\lnot(\lnot P)$: building tables, including a three-variable one.
2. Negate "2 is an integer" and "$x$ is a real number and $x>0$": negation of simple and compound statements.
3. Verify by truth table: De Morgan's laws $\lnot(P\lor Q)\sim\lnot P\land\lnot Q$ and $\lnot(P\land Q)\sim\lnot P\lor\lnot Q$; $P\land(\text{tautology})\sim P$; $P\lor(\text{contradiction})\sim P$; $(P\Rightarrow Q)\sim(\lnot P\lor Q)$; $P\lor Q\sim\lnot(\lnot P\land\lnot Q)$: proving equivalences via tables.
4. Classify as tautology, contradiction or contingency: $P\lor\lnot P$, $P\land\lnot P$, $(P\land Q)\Rightarrow P$, $P\Rightarrow(P\land Q)$.
5. Symbolise: "P true and Q false", "either P or Q", "if P then Q is false", "P and Q have the same truth value": translation English to logic.
6. True or false: (a) whenever $p$ is prime, $2p-1$ is prime; (b) infinitely many primes; (c) every prime ends in 1, 3, 7 or 9 (if false, give and prove the correct statement): counterexamples and a short proof.
7. State the Goldbach and twin prime conjectures; are they proved, what is their status: awareness of open problems.
