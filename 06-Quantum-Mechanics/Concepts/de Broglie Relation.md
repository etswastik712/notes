# de Broglie Relation

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** [[PH2113_Wavefunction_to_Uncertainty (1).pdf#page=1|WU §3]]: $p=h/\lambda=\hbar k$. · 🟡 **extra · book:** Davisson–Germer and group velocity (AI monograph §3).

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Every moving thing has a wavelength; for anything big it is absurdly short.

> [!key] Definition
> $\lambda=h/p$, equivalently $p=\hbar k$ with $k=2\pi/\lambda$ (and $E=\hbar\omega$).

## Numbers
$\lambda=h/\sqrt{2meV}$ for an electron through $V$: $0.1669$ nm at 54 V, $0.1226$ nm at 100 V, $0.1001$ nm at 150 V. Neutron at 2200 m/s: $0.180$ nm. Baseball: $1.1\times10^{-34}$ m. Davisson and Germer saw 54 V electrons diffract from nickel (quoted $0.165$ nm).

> [!extra-book]- 🟡 Extra (book): phase and group velocity
> For $E=p^2/2m$, $\omega=\hbar k^2/2m$: $v_{\rm phase}=\omega/k=v/2$ but $v_{\rm group}=\mathrm d\omega/\mathrm dk=v$: a packet travels at the classical speed.

> [!pitfall] Wavelength is not size
> $\lambda$ says when wave effects matter (compare with slit or lattice spacing), not how big the particle is.
