# Zeeman Effect

<span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span> <span class="scope-extra-beyond">extra · beyond</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟡 **Extra · book:** Sakurai §5.3, §5.5.4 (spin energies in $B$); Griffiths §4.4.2. 🔵 **Extra · beyond:** the scales ([[QM09 The Stern-Gerlach Experiment and Spin|QM09]] §6, §13).

> [!intuition] In one picture
> A magnetic field splits every level with magnetic quantum number $m$ by $g\mu_BBm$: a spin-½ becomes a two-level system.

> [!key] Definition and key results
> $E=-\boldsymbol\mu\cdot\mathbf B=g_J\mu_B\,m_J\,B$; spin-½ ($g\approx2$): $E_\pm=\pm\mu_BB$, splitting $2\mu_BB=115.8\ \mu$eV/T $=27.99$ GHz/T; at 1 T $h\nu/k_B=1.35$ K. Thermal population ratio $e^{-2\mu B/k_BT}$; $P=\tanh(\mu B/k_BT)$.

The Zeeman splitting is what an SG magnet converts into a *force*, since $\mathbf F=-\nabla E$; and what resonance spectroscopy reads out. The SG magnet makes the splitting position-dependent: $E_\pm(z)=\pm\mu(B_0+B'z)$.

> [!pitfall] Pitfall
> Do not confuse the Zeeman splitting (field) with the fine and hyperfine splittings (internal fields): the $21$ cm line exists at $B=0$.

Used in: [[QM09 The Stern-Gerlach Experiment and Spin|QM09]] §2, §6, §7, §13. Related: [[Larmor Precession]] · [[Bohr Magneton]] · [[Magnetic Resonance]] · [[Degeneracy]].
