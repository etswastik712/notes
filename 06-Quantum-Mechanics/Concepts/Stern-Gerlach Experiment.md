# Stern-Gerlach Experiment

<span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span> <span class="scope-extra-beyond">extra · beyond</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟡 **Extra · book:** Sakurai §1.1 ([[modern-quantum-mechanics-3nbsped-9781108473224-1108473229_compress.pdf#page=21|PDF p.21 ff.]]); Griffiths §4.4 ([[Griffiths - Introduction to quantum mechanics.pdf#page=177|p.177 ff.]]). 🔵 **Extra · beyond:** the silver-beam numbers ([[QM09 The Stern-Gerlach Experiment and Spin|QM09]] §2).

> [!intuition] In one picture
> A magnet whose field changes across the beam sorts atoms by the orientation of their magnetic moment: classically a smear, in fact **two spots**.

> [!key] Definition and key results
> $\bar F_z=\mu_z\,\partial_zB_z$ (the transverse force $F_x=-\alpha\mu_x$ is averaged away by [[Larmor Precession]]). A spin-$j$ atom gives $2j+1$ beams, $S_z=m\hbar$. A magnet followed by a block is a **filter** (prepares $|{\pm\mathbf n}\rangle$); a magnet followed by a plate is a **measurement** of $S_{\mathbf n}$.

Silver ($m=107.87$ u, $v=548$ m/s), $\partial_zB=10$ T/cm, 3.5 cm of magnet and 1 cm of drift: $F=9.3\times10^{-21}$ N ($5.3\times10^3$ times the weight), deflection $0.166$ mm each way, **0.33 mm between the spots**; $1.8\times10^5$ Larmor turns during the flight. Heavy and neutral atoms are essential; for free electrons the Lorentz force swamps the spin force ([[Von Neumann Measurement Model]]).

> [!pitfall] Pitfall
> Two spots do **not** mean "the spin is either up or down in space": the same atoms are 50:50 along a perpendicular axis ([[Sequential Stern-Gerlach Experiments]]). The experiment was made to test orbital "space quantisation"; silver has $l=0$ and the two beams are spin.

Used in: [[QM09 The Stern-Gerlach Experiment and Spin|QM09]] §1–§2, §5, §8. Related: [[Sequential Stern-Gerlach Experiments]] · [[Spin]] · [[Bohr Magneton]] · [[Measurement Postulate]] · [[Von Neumann Measurement Model]].
