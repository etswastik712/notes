# Spin

<span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span> <span class="scope-extra-beyond">extra · beyond</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟡 **Extra · book:** Sakurai §1.1, §3.2; Griffiths §4.4. 🔵 **Extra · beyond:** the superluminal-surface estimate ([[QM09 The Stern-Gerlach Experiment and Spin|QM09]] §3, §9.4).

> [!intuition] In one picture
> An angular momentum that belongs to the particle itself, with no position-space picture: a two-state internal degree of freedom for the electron.

> [!key] Definition and key results
> Spin-½: $\mathbf S=\frac\hbar2\boldsymbol\sigma$, $[S_i,S_j]=i\hbar\epsilon_{ijk}S_k$, eigenvalues $\pm\hbar/2$ along every axis; magnetic moment $\boldsymbol\mu=-g_s\mu_B\mathbf S/\hbar$, $g_s=2.0023$. Half-integer $j$ cannot be written as $-i\hbar\,\mathbf r\times\nabla$ (single-valuedness forces integer $l$).

A classical ball of the classical electron radius ($2.82\times10^{-15}$ m) carrying $\hbar/2$ would spin with surface speed $\hbar/2m_er=2.05\times10^{10}$ m/s $=68c$. Fermions have half-integer spin and obey the Pauli principle; the ground state of silver is $5s$ with $l=0$, so its two SG beams are pure spin.

> [!pitfall] Pitfall
> Spin is not a rotating ball, and "spin up" always refers to an axis: $|{+z}\rangle$ is 50:50 along $x$.

Used in: [[QM09 The Stern-Gerlach Experiment and Spin|QM09]] §3–§4, §9. Related: [[Spinor]] · [[Angular Momentum Algebra]] · [[Pauli Matrices]] · [[Gyromagnetic Ratio and g-Factor]] · [[Stern-Gerlach Experiment]].
