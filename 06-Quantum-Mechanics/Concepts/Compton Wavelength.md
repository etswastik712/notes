# Compton Wavelength

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-beyond">extra · beyond</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** [[PH2113_Lecture8_Uncertainty_Wavefunctions_Schrodinger (1).pdf#page=2|L8 §2]]. · 🔵 **extra · beyond:** the matching time scale.

> [!intuition] In one picture
> The length below which a particle cannot be treated as an ordinary non-relativistic object: squeezing it there costs rest-mass energy.

> [!key] Definition
> $\lambda_C=h/mc=2.426$ pm and the **reduced** wavelength $\hbar/mc=3.86\times10^{-13}$ m for the electron. Localising to $\Delta x\sim\hbar/mc$ needs $\Delta p\sim mc$.

## Why this length
The only length from $\hbar$, $c$ and $m$ is $\propto\hbar/mc$ ([[Dimensional Analysis]]: $a=1$, $b=-1$, $d=-1$). Non-relativistic quantum mechanics needs $p\ll mc$, i.e. $\Delta x\gg\hbar/mc$.

> [!pitfall] A validity threshold, not a size
> The electron is not that large: scattering experiments (as stated in the lecture) reveal no structure far below it. Below $\hbar/mc$ one needs relativistic quantum theory, which is outside the course. The matching energy is $mc^2=511$ keV and the matching time $\hbar/mc^2=1.29\times10^{-21}$ s.
