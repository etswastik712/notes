# Photon Polarisation

<span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span> <span class="scope-extra-beyond">extra · beyond</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟡 **Extra · book:** Sakurai §1.1.3 ([[modern-quantum-mechanics-3nbsped-9781108473224-1108473229_compress.pdf#page=26|p.6 ff.]]). 🔵 **Extra · beyond:** the dictionary and the polariser chain ([[QM09 The Stern-Gerlach Experiment and Spin|QM09]] §12.1).

> [!intuition] In one picture
> The classical shadow of the spin-½: a polariser is an SG analyser for the two-level system $\{H,V\}$.

> [!key] Definition and key results
> Jones vector $(\cos\alpha,\sin\alpha)$ for linear polarisation at angle $\alpha$; $H/V\leftrightarrow\pm z$, $\pm45^\circ\leftrightarrow\pm x$, circular $\leftrightarrow\pm y$ (complex). Malus: $I=I_0\cos^2\alpha$ ($=\cos^2\frac\theta2$ with Bloch angle $\theta=2\alpha$). Three polarisers $\frac12\cos^2\alpha\sin^2\alpha=\frac18\sin^22\alpha$: $0.125$ at $45^\circ$.

Angles double because a photon has helicity $\pm1$, a spin-½ has $\pm\frac12$. $N$ polarisers turned by $90^\circ/N$ transmit $\cos^{2N}(\pi/2N)$ ($0.78$ for 10, $0.976$ for 100). Mach–Zehnder = SG interferometer; Stokes operators = Schwinger bosons with two modes.

> [!pitfall] Pitfall
> Complex amplitudes are indispensable even for light: circular polarisation is an equal mixture of $H$ and $V$ with a $90^\circ$ phase.
