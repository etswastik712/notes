# Bohr Magneton

<span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟡 **Extra · book:** Sakurai §1.1.1 ([[modern-quantum-mechanics-3nbsped-9781108473224-1108473229_compress.pdf#page=23|p.3]]); values in [[QM09 The Stern-Gerlach Experiment and Spin|QM09]] §3.

> [!intuition] In one picture
> The natural unit of atomic magnetic moments: the orbital moment of an electron with one unit $\hbar$ of angular momentum.

> [!key] Definition and key results
> $\mu_B=\dfrac{e\hbar}{2m_e}=9.274\times10^{-24}$ J/T $=5.788\times10^{-5}$ eV/T $=13.996$ GHz/T; $\mu_B/k_B=0.6717$ K/T. Nuclear magneton $\mu_N=\mu_B\,m_e/m_p=5.051\times10^{-27}$ J/T ($1/1836$ of $\mu_B$); proton $\mu_p=2.793\,\mu_N$.

Silver in an SG magnet sees $\mu_z=\pm\mu_B$ (for $g=2$, $m_s=\pm\frac12$). Energy of a spin in 1 T: $\pm5.8\times10^{-5}$ eV, i.e. a level splitting of $2\mu_BB=115.8\ \mu$eV $=28$ GHz $=1.35$ K.

> [!pitfall] Pitfall
> The moment is $g\mu_B m_s$, so spin gives $\mu_B$ (not $\mu_B/2$): $g_s\approx2$ compensates $m_s=\frac12$.

Used in: [[QM09 The Stern-Gerlach Experiment and Spin|QM09]] §2–§3, §6. Related: [[Gyromagnetic Ratio and g-Factor]] · [[Zeeman Effect]] · [[Stern-Gerlach Experiment]] · [[Spin-Only Magnetic Moment]].
