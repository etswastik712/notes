# Spin-Only Magnetic Moment

<span class="scope-extra-beyond">extra · beyond</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🔵 **Extra · beyond:** [[QM09 The Stern-Gerlach Experiment and Spin|QM09]] §12.4; the $\mathrm{O_2}$ triplet is in [[CH02 Molecular Orbital Theory Diatomics]].

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Count the unpaired electrons and the moment of a compound follows: the Stern–Gerlach magnetism of bulk matter.

> [!key] Definition and key results
> $\mu_{\rm eff}=g\sqrt{S(S+1)}\,\mu_B=\sqrt{n(n+2)}\,\mu_B$ with $S=n/2$, $g=2$: $n=1,2,3,4,5\Rightarrow1.73,\ 2.83,\ 3.87,\ 4.90,\ 5.92\ \mu_B$.

$\mathrm{O_2}$ (two unpaired $\pi^*$ electrons): $2.83\ \mu_B$; high-spin Fe$^{3+}$, Mn$^{2+}$ ($d^5$): $5.92$; Fe$^{2+}$ high-spin: $4.90$; Co$^{2+}$ high-spin: $3.87$. A low-spin complex has a *smaller* moment. A bulk paramagnet is attracted into a field gradient ($\mathbf F=\nabla(\boldsymbol\mu\cdot\mathbf B)$ on the thermally aligned moments).

> [!pitfall] Pitfall
> Orbital contributions and spin–orbit coupling shift real moments (heavier metals); the formula is a first estimate.

> [!connection] In crystal field theory (added 2026-11-04, [[CH10 Crystal Field Theory]] §5; S&A pp.478–479, Table 20.3)
> A measured moment fixes the number of unpaired electrons $N$ and hence the **spin state** of an octahedral complex: high-spin $d^6$ gives $4.90\ \mu_B$ ($N=4$), low-spin $d^6$ is diamagnetic; octahedral Co(II) with $\mu=4.0\ \mu_B$ is high spin ($3.87$, not $1.73$). Orbital angular momentum is quenched in most complexes but **not** for low-spin $d^5$ ($\mathrm{K_3[Fe(CN)_6]}$: $2.3\ \mu_B$, between $N=1$ and $N=2$) and high-spin $d^6$, $d^7$, where a degenerate orbital lets the field circulate the electrons (S&A Fig. 20.6).
