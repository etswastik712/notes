# Parity Theorem

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** the lecture's remark on [[Finite_well.pdf#page=3|p.3]]: *"we can solve only for $x>0$"*.

> [!intuition] In one picture
> If the potential looks the same reflected in the origin, every non-degenerate eigenfunction must be either exactly even or exactly odd.

> [!key] Definition and key results
> $V(-x)=V(x)\Rightarrow[\hat P,\hat H]=0$; non-degenerate bound states satisfy $\psi(-x)=\pm\psi(x)$. The ground state is even, then they alternate even, odd, even, ...

It halves the work: solve on $x>0$ with $\psi'(0)=0$ (even) or $\psi(0)=0$ (odd). This is why the finite well gives two separate quantisation equations.

Used in: [[QM04 The Particle in a Box]] §6, [[QM07 The Finite Square Well]] §2, §9. Related: [[Parity Operator]] · [[Eigenstate and Eigenvalue]] · [[Node of a Wavefunction]].
