# Centrifugal Barrier

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** [[Angular_momentum.pdf#page=6|Angular_momentum.pdf p.6]] (2D); [[Hydrogen_atom.pdf#page=4|hydrogen notes eq. 25]] (3D)

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Angular momentum costs kinetic energy that grows as the particle approaches the axis, acting like a repulsive wall.

> [!key] Definition
> Radial equations contain $\frac{\hbar^2l^2}{2mr^2}$ (2D) or $\frac{\hbar^2l(l+1)}{2mr^2}$ (3D) added to $V(r)$: an effective potential. It forces $R\propto r^{|l|}$ (2D) or $r^l$ (3D) at the origin and makes energies depend on $l$, except for the special $1/r$ and $r^2$ potentials. Classical analogue $L^2/2mr^2$.
