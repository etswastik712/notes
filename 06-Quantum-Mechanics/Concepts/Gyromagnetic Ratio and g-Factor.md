# Gyromagnetic Ratio and g-Factor

<span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span> <span class="scope-extra-beyond">extra · beyond</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟡 **Extra · book:** Sakurai §1.1.1, §3.2.3 (neutron $g_n\simeq-1.91$); Griffiths §4.4.2. 🔵 **Extra · beyond:** Schwinger correction check ([[QM09 The Stern-Gerlach Experiment and Spin|QM09]] §3).

> [!intuition] In one picture
> How big a magnetic moment per unit angular momentum a particle has: the factor that turns spin into a precession frequency.

> [!key] Definition and key results
> $\boldsymbol\mu=\gamma\mathbf S$, $\gamma=g\,\dfrac{q}{2m}$. Orbital $g=1$; electron spin $g_s=2.0023$ (Dirac: exactly 2; Schwinger: $g/2-1=\alpha/2\pi=1.1614\times10^{-3}$ against $1.15965\times10^{-3}$ measured, $0.15\,\%$); $\gamma_e/2\pi=-28.025$ GHz/T. Proton $g_p=5.586$ ($\gamma_p/2\pi=42.577$ MHz/T); neutron $\mu_n=-1.913\mu_N$ ($g=-3.826$, $\gamma_n/2\pi=-29.165$ MHz/T).

$\gamma_e/\gamma_p=(g_e/g_p)(\mu_B/\mu_N)=658.2$. A neutron has a moment although it is neutral: evidence for quark substructure. For a level of total angular momentum $j$ built from $l$ and $s$, the weak-field Landé factor is $g_J=1+\frac{j(j+1)+s(s+1)-l(l+1)}{2j(j+1)}$ ($=2$ for $^2S_{1/2}$).

> [!pitfall] Pitfall
> Sign matters: $\gamma<0$ for electron and neutron (the moment is antiparallel to the spin), so they precess counter-clockwise about $\mathbf B$.

Used in: [[QM09 The Stern-Gerlach Experiment and Spin|QM09]] §3, §7.1. Related: [[Bohr Magneton]] · [[Larmor Precession]] · [[Zeeman Effect]] · [[Spin]].
