# Modern Synthesis

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** [[Evolution_06-Oct-2026.pdf#page=56|EE08 slides 53–58]]; Futuyma & Kirkpatrick Ch. 1 pp. 16–18 (mandatory)

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Darwin's selection plus Mendel's genes, made quantitative by Fisher, Haldane and Wright and tested by naturalists and palaeontologists.

> [!key] Definition
> The 1930s–50s consensus that **adaptive evolution is caused by natural selection acting on particulate (Mendelian) genetic variation**; mutation is selection's raw material; mutation, gene flow, selection and drift cause microevolution and, over long times, speciation and macroevolution (Futuyma p. 16). Named after Julian Huxley's 1942 book; 'the embryologists were the only people missing' (slide 56).

Futuyma's Box 1A lists 14 principles; those marked * (acquired characters not inherited, gradual species differences, the species concept, allopatric speciation) are qualified by later research. Kimura's neutral theory challenged the 'almost everything is adaptive' view.

Used in: [[EE08 Evolution - An Introduction]], [[EE09 Hardy-Weinberg Equilibrium]]. Related: [[Extended Evolutionary Synthesis]] · [[Blending Inheritance]] · [[Natural Selection]] · [[Hardy-Weinberg Equilibrium]].
