# Reserve Design and Management

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** the slide activities: core and buffer areas, corridors, halting cattle and forest harvesting, patrols; plus Hunter and Gibbs Ch. 11 (mandatory reading) ([[07_Conservation Biology.pdf#page=21|slide 21]])

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Protect the middle completely, soften the edge, and connect the patches so animals can move between them.

> [!key] Definition
> A **reserve** has a protected **core**, a **buffer** zone of regulated use, and **corridors** connecting habitat. Project Tiger site criteria: little disturbance, high prey and predator numbers, no drilling or mining or timber ventures, distribution across states.

## Principles and a worked example
Diamond's (1975) six principles from island biogeography: larger is better; one large reserve beats several small of equal area (the SLOSS debate); small ones close together; clustered rather than in a line; connected by corridors; as circular as possible (least edge). Hunter and Gibbs (Ch. 11) add **minimum dynamic area** (large enough to hold patches of all disturbance stages), a buffered **landscape context** (matrix of seminatural land; boundaries are permeable), **connectivity** for four kinds of movement (daily, annual migration, dispersal, range shifts) and **management**: people management (visitors, exploitation, local goodwill), natural disturbances (fire, floods), water regimes, invasive exotics and overabundant natives. IUCN categories: Ia strict nature reserve, Ib wilderness area, II national park, III natural monument, IV habitat/species management area, V protected landscape, VI managed resource protected area.

> [!example] SLOSS with money (Box 11.1) and species-area
> Land costs \$5,000 per ha, budget \$1.2 million: one 240-ha reserve. If species lists are **nested**, it holds 224 species against 199 for three smaller reserves of the same total cost. If lists are **un-nested**, five small reserves (\$750,000) hold 709 species against 224 for the large one. With $S=cA^{0.25}$ a tenfold area increase raises species number by $10^{0.25}=1.78$, i.e. 78 %, and halving the area loses 16 %.
> **Project Tiger as design:** core and buffer zones, corridors, no cattle and logging (principles above); the Tanzanian link between Lake Manyara and Tarangire parks protects the zebra and wildebeest dry-season movement ([[Serengeti Wildebeest Migration]]).

> [!pitfall] Reserves are not islands
> Edges, poachers, livestock and pollution cross boundaries; reserves also export water and animals (marine reserves seed fisheries). In developing countries local people bear the costs while most tourism income leaves the area, so reserves often meet hostility unless limited use is permitted (Chitwan: grass cutting ten days a year). Two-way flow and size effects: [[Metapopulation]], [[Minimum Viable Population]]; case study: [[Project Tiger]].
