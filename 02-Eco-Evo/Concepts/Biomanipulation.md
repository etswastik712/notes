# Biomanipulation

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-beyond">extra · beyond</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** 'Reduce the number of fishes!': removal of fish and the 18-lake Dutch study (Meijer et al. 1999), where transparency increased ([[06_04-Sep-2026_Biotic-Abiotic Interactions.pdf#page=20|slide 20]])  ·  🔵 **extra · beyond:** the food-web chain behind it

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Fewer small fish means more water fleas, which eat more algae; the water clears.

> [!key] Definition
> **Biomanipulation** changes the food web to restore a lake: remove zooplankton-eating (and bottom-feeding) fish, so large zooplankton (*Daphnia*) graze more phytoplankton and transparency increases. Meijer et al. (1999) studied 18 Dutch lakes of various shapes and sizes for ten years after biomanipulation.

## The chain, with a clearance estimate
Fewer zooplankton-eating (and bottom-feeding) fish $\xrightarrow{-}$ more large zooplankton (*Daphnia*) $\xrightarrow{-}$ less phytoplankton $\xrightarrow{+}$ lower turbidity: sign of the whole chain $(-)(-)(+)=+$ for "fish removal improves transparency". Clearer water then favours plants, which reinforce clarity ([[Shallow Lake Regime Shift]]).

> [!example] How much can grazers clear? (illustrative)
> If *Daphnia* occur at 10 per litre and each clears 2 mL of water per hour, the water cleared per day is $10\times2\times24=480$ mL per litre: the grazers filter **about half the lake volume per day**. A fish population that holds *Daphnia* at 0.5 per litre clears only 2.4 % per day. Meijer et al. (1999): 18 Dutch lakes of different shapes and sizes followed for ten years after biomanipulation (the slide's transparency curves rise after the intervention); Lake Washington's 1976 jump to 10 m after *Daphnia* returned is the same mechanism arising naturally.

**Conditions.** Works best in shallow lakes where the nutrient level is not too high (Scheffer et al.: "shock therapy" can give a permanent clear state); may need repeated removal; fish and predatory fish stocking are two versions ([[Lake Restoration Methods]]).

> [!pitfall] A temporary fix if the state is not flipped
> If nutrient loading is above the point where the clear state exists, the effect fades as fish recolonise; the slide's table says permanent effects "may be difficult to obtain". Biomanipulation is a test of the bistable picture: it succeeds when it moves the lake across the unstable threshold ([[Alternative Stable States]]).
