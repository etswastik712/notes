# Serengeti Wildebeest Migration

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** the running example of the whole first lecture ([[01-IntroToBiology_-07-Aug-2026.pdf#page=3|slides 3–11]]): ~1500 km per year, muscle efficiency, synchronous births, precocial calves, deaths at river crossings, fire · 🟡 **extra · book:** the numbers and mechanisms in Holdo et al. (2009) and Hopcraft et al. (2015).

> [!key] Fact sheet (*Connochaetes taurinus*)
> - **Population:** about 1.2 million, regulated around that value (growth positive below it, negative above); the highest recruitment, about 10 % a year, followed recovery from rinderpest. About 52 animals km⁻² over their range (Hopcraft et al.).
> - **Movement:** about 4.25 km a day, so ≈ 1,550 km a year; the loop plains → Mara → plains is about 650 km; the record is 58 km in a day.
> - **Landscape:** ecosystem ≈ 30,700 km² (Maddock 1979); rainfall rises from < 400 mm in the south-east plains to > 1,200 mm near Lake Victoria; plains soils are shallow, nutrient-rich volcanic ash; northern woodland soils are sandier and poorer ([[Holdo_2009_Opposing rainfall and nutritional gradients best explain the wildebeest migration in the Serengeti.pdf#page=2|Holdo et al., p.432–433]]).
> - **Year:** wet season on the plains from about December to April; calving from mid-February; dry-season range in the north and west.
> - **Calves:** stand in about 6 minutes, walk in 30; outrun hyena within a day.

> [!intuition] Following the best grass, not the most grass
> Rain makes grass grow everywhere, but the rain-rich north and west grow tall, lean grass on poor soil, while the rain-poor south-east plains grow short, protein-rich grass on volcanic ash, though only for a few months. The herd moves onto the plains when the rains make that rich grass available, and back north and west when the plains dry out and green grass survives only there.
