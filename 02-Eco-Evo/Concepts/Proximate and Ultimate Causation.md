# Proximate and Ultimate Causation

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** the two classes of question about the wildebeest, *how* (mechanism) and *why* (benefit): [[01-IntroToBiology_-07-Aug-2026.pdf#page=5|slides 5]] and [[01-IntroToBiology_-07-Aug-2026.pdf#page=7|7]] · 🟡 **extra · book:** Mayr's names for them, *proximate* and *ultimate*, and the warbler example ([[Extract from Growth of Biological Thought_Mayr.pdf#page=47|Mayr, PDF p.47–48]]).

> [!intuition] Two honest answers to "why does the kettle whistle?"
> Because steam is forced through a narrow slot (how it works now). And because somebody designed it to signal that the water has boiled (why it exists). A living thing has both kinds of answer because it carries a **genetic program** that was shaped by selection over many generations and is *read out* in each individual's lifetime.

> [!key] Definition
> **Proximate causes** explain how a trait works or is triggered in an individual *now*: physiology, development, the immediate environmental cue. **Ultimate (evolutionary) causes** explain why the trait exists: the selective advantage that shaped the genetic program in earlier generations (Mayr 1961, 1982). Every biological phenomenon has both, and the two answers do not compete.

| | asks | warbler leaves on 25 Aug | wildebeest |
|---|---|---|---|
| **proximate** | *How?* | day length has dropped below a threshold; weather favours departure | muscle efficiency; movement tracks green-grass intake and plant nitrogen; the diestrus cycle synchronises ovulation |
| **ultimate** | *Why?* | insect-eaters that stayed would starve in winter, so selection favoured migration | predator swamping by synchronous calving; access to a nutrient-rich pulse on the plains |

> [!pitfall] Do not set the two against each other
> "Birds migrate because the days get shorter" and "birds migrate because food disappears" are both true: the first names the trigger, the second the reason the trigger is wired to that behaviour. An exam answer that gives only one half is incomplete.

> [!verify] Terminology in Hopcraft et al.
> [[Hopcraft+_Why are Wildebeest the most abundant herbivore.pdf#page=7|Hopcraft et al. (2015, p.131)]] call the population-wide fall in forage protein "the ultimate cause" of synchronous breeding and the diestrus cycle "the proximate cause". In Mayr's strict sense both are proximate (a cue and the physiology that responds to it); the *ultimate* cause would be the advantage that selection acted on, such as predator swamping. This vault uses Mayr's sense.

> [!extra-beyond]- 🔵 Extra (beyond): Tinbergen's four questions
> Tinbergen (1963) split the same idea into four: **mechanism**, **ontogeny** (development) on the proximate side, and **function** (survival value) and **phylogeny** (history) on the ultimate side.
