# Ecological Niche

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** the n-dimensional hypervolume with the 1-D, 2-D, 3-D examples, and the two definitions (population-persistence; resource-utilisation) from Schoener ([[04-Competition_25-Aug_2026.pdf#page=19|slide 19]])

> [!intuition] In one picture
> A niche is not a place but a list of conditions and resources a species needs: temperature, salinity, food size, time of day. With enough axes it is a shape in n-dimensional space.

> [!key] Definition
> G. E. Hutchinson's **niche** is an $n$-dimensional hypervolume of environmental conditions and resources. Schoener distinguishes the **population-persistence niche** (the conditions under which a population has positive growth; Hutchinson) from the **resource-utilisation niche** (how members actually use resources; MacArthur, Levins). Typical axes: habitat, food type and size, time of day or year.

## Two definitions, fundamental versus realised
Hutchinson's niche is a hypervolume in the space of environmental variables and resources. The **fundamental niche** is where a species could persist in the absence of competitors and enemies; the **realised niche** is the part it actually occupies. Schoener's two senses (slides 20–21): the *population-persistence niche* (conditions with positive growth: $\lambda>1$) and the *resource-utilisation niche* (what individuals do, with axes habitat, food and time).

> [!example] Connell's barnacles
> *Chthamalus* occupies the upper intertidal zone; *Balanus* the middle and (as the slide says) is limited below by predation. Where *Balanus* survives it outcompetes *Chthamalus* for space, so *Chthamalus*' realised niche is a slice of its fundamental niche, bounded above by its own tolerance and below by competition. Removal experiments (Connell 1961) reveal the difference. The one-dimensional example on slide 19 is the range of temperature at which Alpine plants photosynthesise at low light (a *persistence* niche on one axis); the sand shrimp *Crangon septemspinosa* has a two-axis niche of temperature and salinity.

**Overlap and competition.** If two utilisation curves overlap strongly, $\alpha$ is large ([[Lotka-Volterra Competition Model]]); width and overlap determine how many species can fit on an axis (MacArthur and Levins' limiting similarity).

> [!pitfall] Hairston's "love-hate" remark
> The niche is neither a place (habitat) nor a job (role) alone, and the two Schoener senses need not coincide. Practical uses (predicting invasions, partitioning) work best when the axes are specified. See [[Niche Partitioning]], [[Competitive Exclusion Principle]].
