# Lake Washington Recovery

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** the slide story: 1940s oligotrophic, sewage in the 1950s-60s, diversion, recovery, Daphnia return, detergent phosphate cut ([[06_04-Sep-2026_Biotic-Abiotic Interactions.pdf#page=11|slide 11]])  ·  🟡 **extra · book:** 'Rescue of Lake Stinko' (the University of Washington story; paper tier)

> [!intuition] In one picture
> The textbook case of a lake brought back from the brink by cutting the nutrient supply and by a wider community effort.

> [!key] Definition
> Lake Washington (Seattle): oligotrophic in the 1940s; receiving sewage in the 1950s-60s it turned eutrophic ('Lake Stinko'); W. T. Edmondson (1970) implicated the sewage; the sewage was diverted to the sea; as P fell the lake improved, further when *Daphnia* returned after the cyanobacteria disappeared; detergent makers reduced phosphate over time.

> [!verify] Check
> The slide text reads 'Sewage disposal in the lake -> Oligotrophy -> Lake Stinko'; eutrophication, not oligotrophy, is what produces 'Lake Stinko'. Treat 'Oligotrophy' there as a slip.

## The numbers and the lesson
Sequence (Lampert and Sommer; *Rescue of Lake Stinko*): raw sewage from about 1900; the last Seattle outfall removed in 1941; ten suburban treatment plants added 1941–53 (80 million litres a day); 1955 *Oscillatoria rubescens* new to the lake; 1958 referendum for "Metro" (58 % Seattle, 67 % elsewhere) after a defeat in March; early 1960s transparency about 1 m, "Lake Stinko"; **diversion to Puget Sound completed in 1968**; within 2 years phosphorus fell to a new low equilibrium; transparency 3–4 m until 1975; **1976: large *Daphnia* returned and transparency doubled to up to 10 m**.

> [!example] Two kinds of control in one lake
> A *bottom-up* step (lower phosphorus: 1 m to 3–4 m) and a *top-down* step (grazers: to 10 m). The return of *Daphnia* is credited to the disappearance of filamentous cyanobacteria that clog their filters (bottom-up) and to reduced predation by mysid shrimp (top-down). Without the second step the lake would have been clear but not as clear as it became. Edmondson's approach: show that algal mass tracked nutrient input, summarise it in a nine-page question-and-answer for policy makers.

> [!pitfall] Which direction does "reversal" run?
> Recovery followed a *different path* from the decline; the lake did not simply retrace its states (hysteresis in a mild form). For shallow lakes the difference is large and can mean no recovery at all without extra measures ([[Shallow Lake Regime Shift]], [[Biomanipulation]]).
