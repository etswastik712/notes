# Shallow Lake Regime Shift

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** the vegetation-turbidity interaction (Scheffer 1998) and the model's S-shaped curve; 'making it more realistic does not change the qualitative conclusions' ([[06_04-Sep-2026_Biotic-Abiotic Interactions.pdf#page=16|slide 16]])  ·  🟡 **extra · book:** Scheffer and van Nes (2007) *Hydrobiologia* 584: 455-466 (effects of depth, lake size, climate, nutrients)

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Plants keep shallow water clear, and clear water lets plants grow; algae make water turbid, and murky water kills plants. Each state defends itself.

> [!key] Definition
> In shallow lakes vegetation and turbidity affect each other negatively: plants reduce turbidity (less resuspension, nutrient uptake, shelter for zooplankton), turbidity shades out plants. Two negatives multiply to a positive: a **reinforcing loop** ([[Causal Loop Diagram]]). Over a range of nutrient loading both a clear (vegetated) and a turbid (algal) state are stable.

## The graphical model and its three assumptions
Scheffer et al. (Fig. 5): (1) turbidity rises with the nutrient level; (2) submerged vegetation reduces turbidity; (3) vegetation disappears when a critical turbidity is exceeded. Assumptions 1–2 give two equilibrium-turbidity curves (with and without vegetation); 3 decides which applies, producing a window of nutrient levels with two stable states and an unstable one between. Mechanisms by which plants clear the water: nutrient uptake, protection of *Daphnia* from fish, and prevention of resuspension. Fish keep the turbid state by controlling *Daphnia* and stirring sediment.

> [!example] Lake Veluwe and the effect of size
> Charophyte cover collapsed as phosphorus rose (late 1960s–early 1970s) and came back only after loading had been reduced for two decades, at much lower P: forward and backward switches at different nutrient levels. Scheffer and van Nes (2007): the critical nutrient level for turbidity is **higher in smaller lakes**; climate and depth shift it; different plant groups (charophytes, submerged angiosperms, free-floating plants, green algae, cyanobacteria) can each be a stable state.
> **Shock therapy.** A temporary strong fish reduction can tip a lake to the clear state "if the nutrient level is not too high" ([[Biomanipulation]]).

> [!pitfall] Cure and cause differ
> Phosphorus *caused* the shift, but reducing it is often not enough to reverse it ([[Alternative Stable States]]). Where lake depth is large the response is smooth (no hysteresis). The loop that holds each state: vegetation clears water, clear water favours vegetation; sign product $(-)(+)(-)(+)=+$ ([[Causal Loop Diagram]]).
