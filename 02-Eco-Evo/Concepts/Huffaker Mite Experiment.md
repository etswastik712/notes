# Huffaker Mite Experiment

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** the mite experiment on spatially arranged oranges and its conclusions ([[05_01_Sep_2026_Predation.pdf#page=11|slide 11]])

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Give the prey many scattered refuges and the predators have to keep finding them; prey go extinct locally and recolonise elsewhere, so the whole system lasts longer than any one patch.

> [!key] Definition
> Prey mite *Eotetranychus sexmaculatus* and predator mite *Typhlodromus occidentalis* on oranges arranged in space. Gause's system was too simple (no environmental heterogeneity). Oscillations arose only with a complicated spatial arrangement of patches; metapopulations promote prey survival through extinction and recolonisation; with an efficient predator the prey survive only in a patchy environment.

## The design and why it worked
Prey: the herbivorous six-spotted mite *Eotetranychus sexmaculatus* feeding on oranges; predator: *Typhlodromus occidentalis*. Huffaker built "universes" of oranges (sometimes with rubber balls), with only about 5 % of each orange's area exposed, and put **partial barriers of petroleum jelly** between them; the predator had to crawl, the prey could also **balloon** on silk threads (wooden posts and a fan helped). Small set-ups failed to give oscillations; the 120-orange universe gave **three oscillations over about six months**.

> [!example] A metapopulation reading
> Let $p$ be the fraction of oranges occupied by prey. Prey colonise empty oranges at rate $c$ and go locally extinct (eaten) at rate $e$: $\dot p=cp(1-p)-ep$, $p^*=1-e/c$ (see [[Metapopulation]]). Predators colonise slowly (they crawl), so the prey are always a step ahead: a "hide-and-seek". Prey persistence needs $c>e$; making the predator more efficient raises $e$ until the prey survive only if the patch network is large and well connected, as the slide says.

**Conclusions on the slides.** Oscillations only with a complicated spatial arrangement of patches; distribution and relative abundance of mites change over time; prey need some advantage to survive; metapopulations promote prey survival through extinction and recolonisation; if the predator is efficient the prey survive only in a patchy environment; predators can sometimes reduce prey more effectively than pesticides.

> [!pitfall] Huffaker did not manipulate dispersal directly
> Both mites moved under their own power; the barriers changed the *difficulty* of dispersal. The lesson is qualitative: space can stabilise an interaction that is unstable in a well-mixed system. Contrast [[Gause Predator-Prey Experiments]] and the homogeneity assumption of [[Lotka-Volterra Predator-Prey Model]].
