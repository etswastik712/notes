# Extinction Rates

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-beyond">extra · beyond</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** the motivating statement: contemporary extinction rates are 100-1000 times typical geological rates and projected to rise ([[07_Conservation Biology.pdf#page=2|slide 2]])  ·  🔵 **extra · beyond:** mass extinctions in the geological record

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Species have always vanished, but now at a pace set by human activity rather than by geology.

> [!key] Definition
> Contemporary rates of extinction are vastly greater (100-1000 times) than the rates typical of the geological past, and they are projected to get worse (slide). Background rates are measured in extinctions per million species-years.

## What the factor means, and the species-area link
Extinction rates are quoted as extinctions per million species-years (E/MSY): the background rate in the fossil record is of order 1 E/MSY (a group of $10^6$ species loses about one a year). The slide's "100–1000 times" therefore means $10^2$–$10^3$ E/MSY, i.e. 100–1000 species a year per million, and "projected to get worse". The five mass extinctions of the Phanerozoic (end-Ordovician, late Devonian, end-Permian, end-Triassic, end-Cretaceous) lost a large fraction of species within geologically short times; the present comparison is with those, not with the background ([[Evolution of Earth and Life - Course Map]]).

> [!example] How habitat loss translates into species loss (🔵)
> The species-area relation $S=cA^{z}$ with $z\approx0.25$ (typical range 0.15–0.35): destroying 90 % of a habitat leaves $0.1^{0.25}=56\,\%$ of the species *eventually*, i.e. 44 % are lost, and halving the area loses $1-0.5^{0.25}=16\,\%$. The loss is delayed (an **extinction debt**): the species that will go extinct are still present, in populations too small to persist ([[Minimum Viable Population]]).

**Drivers.** Habitat loss and fragmentation, overexploitation, invasive species ([[Invasive Species]]), pollution and climate change; for tigers poaching and prey loss ([[Project Tiger]]).

> [!pitfall] Rates are hard to measure
> Species go unnoticed before they vanish; baselines differ by group; "extinct" needs long searches. The statement is a *ratio of rates* with a wide range, not a precise count. Reasons to care are the subject of [[Conservation Ethics]].
