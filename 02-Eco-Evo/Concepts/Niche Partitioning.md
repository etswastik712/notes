# Niche Partitioning

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** spatial (Gause's *Paramecium*, Connell's barnacles), temporal (spiny mice), morphological (character displacement) and conditional (Park's *Tribolium*) niche separation ([[04-Competition_25-Aug_2026.pdf#page=25|slide 25]])

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Species with similar needs can live together if they differ along at least one axis: where they feed, when they feed, what size of food, or which conditions favour them.

> [!key] Definition
> Coexistence of similar species through **resource partitioning**: *spatial* (*P. caudatum* feeds in the upper layers, *P. bursaria* in the lower), *temporal* (common spiny mouse *Acomys cahirinus* nocturnal, golden spiny mouse *A. russatus* diurnal), *morphological* ([[Character Displacement]]) and **conditional** (the winner depends on physical conditions: Park's flour beetles *Tribolium confusum* and *T. castaneum*). Higher equilibrium size does not guarantee winning.

## How partitioning makes coexistence possible
If species use a resource axis with centres $d$ apart and widths $\sigma$, $\alpha=\exp(-d^2/4\sigma^2)$ falls quickly with separation; partitioning is anything that lowers $\alpha$ until $\alpha_{12}\alpha_{21}<1$ and the thresholds $K_1/K_2$, $K_2/K_1$ hold ([[Lotka-Volterra Competition Model]]).

> [!example] Park's flour beetles (slide 30), and why $K$ is not enough
> Alone, in cool-moist conditions *T. castaneum* reaches 45.2 against 28.2 for *T. confusum*, yet *T. confusum* wins 71 % of contests; in warm-moist conditions *T. confusum* is larger alone (41.2 against 38.3) and *T. castaneum* wins all (100 %). Cool-dry: 100 % *confusum*; warm-dry: 90 % *confusum*; moderate-moist: 86 % *castaneum*. The **winner depends on the physical conditions**, so in a fluctuating environment neither is permanently excluded.
> **Spatial:** *P. caudatum* in the upper layers, *P. bursaria* the lower; barnacle zonation. **Temporal:** common spiny mouse nocturnal, golden spiny mouse diurnal; after removal of the common mouse the golden one became nocturnal. **Morphological:** beak size of Galápagos finches ([[Character Displacement]]).

> [!pitfall] Is partitioning the cause or the result?
> Partitioning seen today can be the "ghost of competition past": species that overlapped were excluded and the survivors differ. The spiny-mouse removal shows *induced* niche shifts; the finch pattern suggests *evolved* ones. Distinguishing needs experiments, not only patterns.
