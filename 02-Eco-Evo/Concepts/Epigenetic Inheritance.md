# Epigenetic Inheritance

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** [[Evolution_06-Oct-2026.pdf#page=64|EE08 slides 61–79]]
> 🟡 **extra · book:** Weaver et al. 2004 (abstract)

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Marks on DNA (such as methylation) change which genes are read, can be set by the environment, and are sometimes passed on.

> [!key] Definition
> **Within a lifetime**: rat mothers' licking and grooming demethylates the hippocampal glucocorticoid-receptor promoter in week 1, raising GR and calming the adult; reversed by cross-fostering and by a histone-deacetylase inhibitor (Weaver et al. 2004); abused humans show more GR-promoter methylation. **Across generations**: vinclozolin in a pregnant rat → disease in male offspring for ≥ 4 generations; toadflax peloric form = heritable *Lcyc* methylation with no DNA difference (Cubas et al. 1999).

Compared with genetic change: faster, more dynamic, can arise in many individuals at once (slide 84).
