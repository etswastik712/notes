# Character Displacement

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** the definition on the slide ([[04-Competition_25-Aug_2026.pdf#page=29|slide 29]])

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Where two similar species live together, natural selection pushes them apart in size or shape, so they differ more in sympatry than where each lives alone.

> [!key] Definition
> **Character displacement:** the phenomenon by which character traits of two or more closely related species differ more when they occur together than when they occur in different geographical areas.

## Evidence and what must be shown
When two close relatives share a place they often differ more than where each lives alone. The classic case is the Galápagos ground finches: beak depth distributions of *Geospiza fuliginosa*, *G. fortis* and *G. magnirostris* differ between islands depending on which species are present (Daphne, Los Hermanos, Floreana and San Cristóbal, Pinta and Marchena on slide 29). Beak depth sets the seed size a bird can handle, so differences reduce overlap in diet and thus the competition coefficient ([[Lotka-Volterra Competition Model]]).

> [!example] How much difference is enough?
> A rule of thumb (Hutchinson 1959): coexisting related species in a guild often differ by a ratio of about **1.3 in a linear dimension** (about 2.2 in mass, since $1.3^3=2.2$). With resource use of width $\sigma$, a separation $d=\sigma$ gives $\alpha=0.78$ and $d=2\sigma$ gives $0.37$; character displacement shifts $d$ upward in sympatry.

**Two readings.** *Evolutionary displacement*: selection against competitors favours extreme phenotypes in sympatry. *Ecological sorting*: only species that already differ are able to coexist, so the "displacement" is a sampling effect. The slide gives the definition; evidence for selection needs heritable trait change and ruling out environmental differences between islands.

> [!pitfall] A definition, not a proof
> The definition describes a *pattern* (more different in sympatry than in allopatry). It is a case of the more general link between ecology and microevolution drawn on the "domain of BI2113" slide ([[Levels of Biological Organisation]]), and a mechanism for [[Niche Partitioning]].
