# Trophic State of Lakes

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** the classical Naumann-Thienemann types, the modern continuum bounded by total phosphorus at spring circulation, and the factors that set trophic status ([[06_04-Sep-2026_Biotic-Abiotic Interactions.pdf#page=2|slide 2]])

> [!intuition] In one picture
> A lake's 'diet': how much nutrient it receives sets how much algae it grows, hence how clear and productive it is.

> [!key] Definition
> **Classical view** (Naumann, Thienemann): *oligotrophic* (little productivity, clear water), *eutrophic* (high productivity, greenish water from phytoplankton), *dystrophic* (low conductivity, brown water from humic substances), then extinction (infilling). **Modern view:** oligotrophic and eutrophic are regions of a **trophic continuum** separated by limits such as the total phosphorus $P_{tot}$ at spring circulation; the condition is biological, so several criteria are used.

## Classes, the retention-time relation and an example
Quantitative classes from total phosphorus at spring circulation (Lampert and Sommer): ultra-oligotrophic $<5$, oligotrophic 5–10, mesotrophic 10–30, eutrophic 30–100, hypereutrophic $>100$ µg L⁻¹. The phosphorus a lake holds follows the inflow concentration and the hydraulic retention time $\tau_w$: $P_L=1.55\,[P_{in}/(1+\sqrt{\tau_w})]^{0.82}$ (OECD, $n=87$, $r^2=0.86$).

> [!example] Same inflow, three lakes
> $P_{in}=50$ µg L⁻¹. $\tau_w=0.5$ yr: $P_L=24.7$ (mesotrophic); 10 yr: 11.9 (mesotrophic); 100 yr: 5.4 (oligotrophic). **Lake Constance**: 6 µg L⁻¹ in 1950 (oligotrophic), 10 in 1957, 87 in the early 1980s (eutrophic), 8 in 2006 (oligotrophic): the lake crossed three classes and came back.
> **One number can mislead.** Schöhsee (mean depth 13 m, $P_{tot}\approx20$) is mesotrophic by P but has an anoxic hypolimnion, eutrophic in the older sense; Lake Constance (mean depth 100 m, $P\approx100$ in 1980) was eutrophic by P but only about 50 % oxygen-saturated at the bottom. The explanation is **morphometry**: the epilimnion/hypolimnion volume ratio (above 1: tends to eutrophy).

**Classical typology.** Naumann and Thienemann: oligotrophic (clear; orthograde oxygen curve; *Tanytarsus* larvae; coregonids), eutrophic (green; clinograde curve; *Chironomus*; cyprinids), dystrophic (brown, humic, low conductivity), then infilling. Primary production and chlorophyll rise along the series (50–300 vs $>1000$ mg C m⁻² d⁻¹; 0.3–3 vs 10–500 µg chlorophyll *a* L⁻¹).

> [!pitfall] A continuum, not boxes
> "Trophic state" is partly a biological property and cannot be read from one factor ([[Eutrophication_Limnoecology.pdf#page=2|Lampert and Sommer, Box 8.1]]). It also changes with time: natural eutrophication is slow (silting up) while human eutrophication can take decades ([[Cultural Eutrophication]]).
