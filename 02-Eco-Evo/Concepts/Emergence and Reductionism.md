# Emergence and Reductionism

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** "Emergence: reductionism often fails" as the fourth feature of biological systems ([[01-IntroToBiology_-07-Aug-2026.pdf#page=15|slide 15]]) · 🟡 **extra · book:** Mayr's three kinds of reductionism and his account of emergence ([[Extract from Growth of Biological Thought_Mayr.pdf#page=39|Mayr, PDF p.39–44]]).

> [!intuition] A traffic jam has no cause in any single car
> Every car obeys the same simple rules, yet a jam travels *backwards* along the road at a speed no car has. You cannot find it by dissecting one car; you can only see it when many interacting cars are studied together. That is an emergent property: a feature of the whole that is not present in, and not deducible by looking at, the parts taken separately.

> [!key] Definitions
> **Emergence:** characteristics of a whole that cannot be deduced, "not even in theory" in Mayr's words, from the most complete knowledge of its components taken separately or in partial combinations (Mayr 1982, after Lloyd Morgan). Two things follow: a whole can become a part of a still higher whole, and a whole can affect its own components ("downward causation").
> **Reductionism** (three meanings): **constitutive**, organisms are made of the same matter and obey the same physical laws (accepted by all biologists); **explanatory**, understand a whole by dissecting it into parts (useful but limited); **theory**, biology's laws are special cases of physics (Mayr: never achieved).

- *Why explanatory reduction fails:* components behave differently inside the ensemble than in isolation, and processes at a higher level are often largely independent of the details below. A knee joint works the same if the cartilage is replaced by plastic.
- Emergence is **not** vitalism: modern emergentists accept constitutive reduction without reservation. It is also not inherently biological: Huxley's "aquosity" of water cannot be deduced from hydrogen and oxygen alone.

> [!pitfall] "Emergent" is a description, not an explanation
> Saying a property is "due to complexity" does not explain it (Mayr). The useful question is: *at which level must this be studied?* For the wildebeest, a migration emerges from individual movement rules plus a landscape ([[Serengeti Wildebeest Migration]]), and the model of Holdo et al. shows it vanishes when the tracking neighbourhood is too small.
