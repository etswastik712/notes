# Levels of Biological Organisation

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** the ladder biomolecule → … → biosphere, the time-scale picture and the domain of BI2113 ([[01-IntroToBiology_-07-Aug-2026.pdf#page=13|slides 13–14]]) · 🟡 **extra · book:** Mayr's distinction between *integrated* and *aggregational* hierarchies ([[Extract from Growth of Biological Thought_Mayr.pdf#page=44|Mayr, PDF p.44–45]]).

> [!intuition] A city seen from different heights
> From the street you meet individuals; from a rooftop, neighbourhoods; from a plane, the whole metropolis and the roads that tie it to others. Each height shows patterns invisible from the one below (traffic jams exist only at the scale of the road network), and each has its own natural clock: a person's day, a neighbourhood's decade, a city's century.

> [!key] The ladder (slide 13)
> biomolecules → cell → tissue → organ → organ system → **individual → population → community → ecosystem → biome → biosphere**.
> - **Population:** individuals of one species living together.
> - **Community:** all the populations of different species that co-occur.
> - **Ecosystem:** the community together with its abiotic environment.
> - **Biome:** a large region defined by its climate and vegetation. **Biosphere:** all the ecosystems on Earth.

![[fig_ee01_levels.png|520]]
*The domain of BI2113 is the individual and everything above it. Population-and-above levels change on ecological time scales; changed trait values in a population are **microevolution**, and the accumulation of speciation events is **macroevolution** (longer time scales).*

- The **life-cycle loop** on the slide closes the ladder: an individual makes gametes by reduction division and gene re-arrangement; fertilisation produces a cell that develops into the next individual.
- Different questions live at different levels, which is why the course moves up the ladder: one population ([[EE02 Structured Population Models]], [[EE03 Population Dynamics and Chaos]]), two interacting populations ([[EE04 Competition and Invasions]], [[EE05 Predation]]), ecosystems ([[EE06 Eutrophication and Alternative Stable States]]).

> [!extra-book]+ 🟡 Extra (book): two kinds of hierarchy
> In an **integrated** hierarchy the lower units combine through interaction into a new higher unit (cells → tissue → organ). In an **aggregational** hierarchy such as the Linnaean ranks (species → genus → family) the higher category is just a classifier's grouping, with no new interacting whole. Only the first kind is a *level of organisation* in the sense of the slide (Mayr 1982).

Used in: [[EE01 Introduction to Biology and Ecological Thinking]].
Connections: the same scale-separation idea organises the atmosphere ([[Scales of Atmospheric Motion]]) and Earth's subsystems ([[Earth System]]).
