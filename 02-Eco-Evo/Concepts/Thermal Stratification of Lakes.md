# Thermal Stratification of Lakes

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-beyond">extra · beyond</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** the words hypolimnion and spring circulation as used on the slides ([[06_04-Sep-2026_Biotic-Abiotic Interactions.pdf#page=7|slide 7]])  ·  🔵 **extra · beyond:** the physics of stratification and turnover

> [!intuition] In one picture
> In summer the warm, light surface water floats on a cold, dense bottom layer that gets no oxygen from the air. Wind and cooling remix them in spring and autumn.

> [!key] Definition
> A deep lake in summer separates into a warm, mixed, lit **epilimnion**, a **metalimnion** (thermocline) with a steep temperature gradient, and a cold, dark **hypolimnion**. Density differences suppress mixing, so oxygen used up in the hypolimnion is not replaced. Water is densest at about 4 °C; spring and autumn **circulation** mix the column.

## Why layers form, and what they do to oxygen
Water is densest near 4 °C ($\rho\approx1000$, 999.7 at 10 °C, 998.2 at 20 °C, 997.0 at 25 °C kg m⁻³). In summer the surface warms; the warm layer floats on the cold one and wind mixes only the top: **epilimnion** (warm, mixed, lit, in contact with air), **metalimnion** with the steep **thermocline**, **hypolimnion** (cold, dark, cut off from the atmosphere). Spring (and autumn) **circulation** mixes the column when temperatures equalise. Lampert and Sommer use $P_{tot}$ at spring circulation as the standard measure for exactly this reason.

> [!example] Stability and oxygen (illustrative numbers)
> Temperatures 20 °C above, 8 °C below across 5 m: $\Delta\rho\approx1.6$ kg m⁻³, so the buoyancy frequency squared is $N^2=g\Delta\rho/(\rho\Delta z)\approx3\times10^{-3}\ \text{s}^{-2}$ (period about 2 minutes): wind cannot mix across it. A hypolimnion that starts the summer with $10$ mg O₂ L⁻¹ and loses $0.1$ mg L⁻¹ day⁻¹ to decomposing algae is anoxic after 100 days; a deep lake with a large hypolimnion has far more oxygen to spend (Lake Constance), a small one runs out (Schöhsee).

**Lake types by mixing.** Dimictic (mix in spring and autumn), monomictic (warm lakes), polymictic (shallow lakes that mix often). Mixing is also what returns hypolimnetic phosphorus to the surface, so stratification and internal loading go together ([[Internal Phosphorus Loading]]).

> [!pitfall] Warming changes the clock
> A warmer climate generally lengthens summer stratification and strengthens it, extending the period of oxygen depletion (general expectation; see [[Earth System]] for the climate side). The physics is the hydrostatic and buoyancy balance of [[Hydrostatic Balance]]; for the trophic consequences see [[Trophic State of Lakes]].
