# Nutrient Limitation

<span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span> <span class="scope-extra-beyond">extra · beyond</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟡 **extra · book:** Lampert and Sommer (mandatory reading) discuss phosphorus as the limiting nutrient; Liebig and Redfield are standard background  ·  🔵 **extra · beyond:** Redfield ratio as a check on N versus P limitation

> [!intuition] In one picture
> A plant (or alga) grows only as fast as its scarcest ingredient allows, like a barrel that leaks at its shortest stave.

> [!key] Definition
> **Liebig's law of the minimum:** growth is limited by the resource in shortest supply relative to need. In most temperate lakes this is phosphorus, which is why P control restores lakes. Algae take up nutrients in roughly the **Redfield ratio** C:N:P $\approx$ 106:16:1 (by atoms), so N:P above about 16 suggests P limitation.

## Liebig, Redfield and a ratio test
Growth is limited by the resource that is scarcest *relative to need* (Liebig's law of the minimum). Phytoplankton take up elements near the **Redfield ratio** C:N:P $=106:16:1$ by atoms. In mass terms N:P $=16\times14.01/30.97=7.2$. A rule of thumb: N:P (mass) above about 7 suggests phosphorus limitation, below it nitrogen limitation, with the usual caveats about the *available* fractions.

> [!example] Which nutrient limits?
> A lake has 200 µg N L⁻¹ and 10 µg P L⁻¹: N:P $=20$ by mass ($20\times30.97/14.01=44$ by atoms), far above the Redfield value: **phosphorus-limited**, so cutting P is the lever. Domestic sewage (N:P $\approx4:1$, basis not stated in the reading) lowers the water's ratio; if the ratio falls below 16 nitrogen can limit, and **nitrogen-fixing cyanobacteria** (*Anabaena*, *Aphanizomenon*) can supply their own N: a reason why controlling P rather than N is the standard strategy in freshwater (the reading's emphasis).

**Competition angle.** With one limiting resource the species with the lowest $R^*$ wins ([[Tilman R-Star Rule]]); with two, species limited by different resources can coexist: the phytoplankton community changes along the nutrient gradient (Scheffer and van Nes: a continuum of species replacements interrupted by dramatic shifts). In terms of [[Carrying Capacity]], $K$ for algae is set by the *limiting* nutrient supply.

> [!pitfall] Limitation can switch with time and place
> Seasonal and spatial switching (P in spring, N or light in late summer, Fe or Si for some groups); at the peak of eutrophication light or nitrogen can be limiting, which delays the response to P control ([[Cultural Eutrophication]]). Chemistry of the cycles: [[Inorganic Chemistry - Course Map]].
