# Crypsis

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** the examples on the slide: dead-leaf mantis, tawny frogmouth, leaf-mimicking mantis, lantern fly ([[05_01_Sep_2026_Predation.pdf#page=18|slide 18]])

> [!intuition] In one picture
> The opposite of advertising: look like part of the background so the predator never notices.

> [!key] Definition
> **Crypsis:** avoiding detection by resembling the background or an inedible object (leaf, bark, twig). The slide's examples are the dead-leaf mantis, the tawny frogmouth, the leaf-mimicking mantis *Choeradodis rhomboidea* and a lantern fly.

## Kinds of concealment and a cost
**Background matching** (colour and pattern like the substrate), **disruptive coloration** (patches that break up the outline), **countershading** (dark above, light below, cancelling shadows) and **masquerade** (resembling an object a predator ignores, such as a leaf or twig). The slide's examples: dead-leaf mantis, tawny frogmouth (bark), leaf-mimicking mantis *Choeradodis rhomboidea*, lantern fly.

> [!example] Detection as a time budget
> If a predator detects a visible prey on each look with probability 1 and a cryptic prey with probability $q=0.05$, the expected number of looks before detection is $1/q=20$. A predator with a fixed foraging time and alternative prey ignores the cryptic prey in favour of conspicuous ones: concealment is a *relative* defence that lowers the **functional response** to that prey ([[Functional and Numerical Responses]]) and, as a result, raises the prey's equilibrium density in a model like [[Lotka-Volterra Predator-Prey Model]] (smaller capture coefficient $p$, larger $H^*=m/ap$).

**Trade-offs.** Concealment constrains behaviour (staying still), limits mating displays and does not help once found; hence the alternative strategy of advertising ([[Aposematism]]). The same body can combine them: cryptic at rest, warning colours flashed when disturbed (intimidation, [[Anti-Predator Behaviour and Chemical Defence]]).

> [!pitfall] Match depends on the observer
> What is cryptic depends on the predator's vision and on the background it is seen against; a moth matched to bark is conspicuous on a different tree. Crypsis, mimicry and aposematism lie on a spectrum of signal design aimed at a specific receiver ([[Mimicry]]).
