# Rabinowitz Rarity Classification

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** the classification and the placement of species on it ([[07_Conservation Biology.pdf#page=9|slide 9]])

> [!intuition] In one picture
> A species can be rare for three different reasons: it lives in few places, it tolerates few habitats, or it is always sparse where it lives. Different mixes need different conservation.

> [!key] Definition
> Rabinowitz crosses **geographic range** (extensive/restricted), **habitat tolerance** (broad/narrow) and **local population size** (large/small): $2^3=8$ combinations, of which one (extensive, broad, large) is common and the other **seven are forms of rarity**.

## A scoring view, and what each cell needs
Count the "rare" properties (restricted range, narrow habitat tolerance, small local population) to get a rarity score 0–3. From slide 10:

| species | range / tolerance / local population | score |
|---|---|---|
| house sparrow, dandelion | extensive / broad / large | 0 (common) |
| Galápagos medium ground finch | restricted / broad / large | 1 |
| California grey whale; **passenger pigeon** | extensive / narrow / large | 1 |
| **tiger** | extensive / broad / small | 1 |
| fish crow; Tasmanian devil; northern spotted owl | restricted-narrow-large; restricted-broad-small; extensive-narrow-small | 2 |
| mountain gorilla | restricted / narrow / small | 3 (rarest) |

> [!example] What follows for action
> A score-1 species is not safe: the pigeon (narrow tolerance for mast forest, huge numbers) and the tiger (broad tolerance, few per area) show opposite *reasons* for risk. **Large-but-narrow** species need the key habitat protected across the range (the pigeon's mast forests); **small-but-widespread** species need low human pressure and prey over large areas (tiger: density at best 12–18 per 100 km², one tiger per 500 prey); **restricted and small** species need protected sites and monitoring (gorilla).

> [!pitfall] Scale, time and threats
> "Large local population" and "extensive range" depend on the scale of the survey and the time (a species can move cells as it declines: the pigeon went from a huge population to none within decades). The grid is a *state description*, not a threat assessment; the dynamics are in [[Demographic Genetic and Environmental Stochasticity]] and [[Allee Effect]].
