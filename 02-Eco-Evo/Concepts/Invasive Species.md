# Invasive Species

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** the invasive-species section: the threat, Kerala's 2018 and 2019 floods, the 2021 survey, control (water-hyacinth crafts) and self-study cases (Asian carps, *Caulerpa taxifolia*) ([[04-Competition_25-Aug_2026.pdf#page=13|slide 13]])

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Competitive exclusion at human speed: a species moved by people into a place where its enemies are absent and the locals have no defence.

> [!key] Definition
> An **invasive species** is a non-native species that establishes, spreads and harms native biodiversity, economy or health. Kerala: after the 2018 floods arapaima (up to 200 kg) and alligator gar (up to 137 kg) appeared in rivers; the 2021 survey of 44 rivers and 53 reservoirs found 28 alien fish and 4 aquatic weeds, 7 with established populations, mainly via the aquarium trade and aquaculture (Raj et al. 2021).

## Stages, rates and the Kerala case
An invasion passes through **transport**, **introduction**, **establishment** and **spread**, with a filter at each step. A rule of thumb (Williamson's "tens rule"): roughly 10 % of introduced species appear in the wild, about 10 % of those establish, and about 10 % of the established become pests; the figures vary by group. Kerala's 2021 survey of 44 rivers and 53 reservoirs fits the filter idea: 28 alien fish and 4 alien macrophytes were found, **7 with established populations**, and the dominant routes were the aquarium trade and aquaculture. After the 2018 and 2019 floods arapaima (to 200 kg) and alligator gar (to 137 kg) turned up in rivers.

> [!example] In the model's language
> An invader that arrives without its enemies and can use the resource at a lower level than natives has a large $\alpha_{\text{invader}\to\text{native}}$ and a smaller reverse term; with a lower $R^*$ it excludes the native ([[Tilman R-Star Rule]]). A small founding population, however, faces demographic accidents and Allee effects, which is why most introductions fail and why repeated introductions (propagule pressure) matter ([[Population Biology of Invasions]]).

**Use and control.** Water hyacinth is turned into livelihood products: handicrafts by 300 women trained by Kottapuram Integrated Development Society (Kerala), mats and baskets (Assam State Rural Livelihoods Mission), and a 30:70 hyacinth-cotton mix for sarees (Shantipur, West Bengal). Self-study cases: Asian carps, the alga *Caulerpa taxifolia*. Restoration also uses invaders: the zebra mussel is a biological filter that is itself invasive ([[Lake Restoration Methods]]).

> [!pitfall] Exotic is not the same as invasive
> Most alien species do not establish, and not all established ones are harmful; the label depends on impact. Climate and trade move many more species than before; conservation consequences for natives: [[EE07 Conservation Biology]].
