# Hardy-Weinberg Equilibrium

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** [[09_Hardy-Weinberg.pdf#page=7|EE09 slides 6–11]]; Templeton Ch. 2, Hartl & Clark pp. 74–80

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Random pairing of gametes from a gene pool gives genotypes p² : 2pq : q², and Mendelian inheritance alone never changes the pool.

> [!key] Definition
> In a large randomly mating population without selection, mutation or migration, allele and genotype frequencies are constant and $A_1A_1:A_1A_2:A_2A_2=p^2:2pq:q^2$. Reached in **one** generation (two if the sexes differ), **neutrally stable**, depends only on allele frequencies; $2pq\le\tfrac12$; rare alleles sit mostly in heterozygotes.

A null model: every evolutionary force is a broken assumption. Importance: preserves variation (answers [[Blending Inheritance]]), carrier estimates, the reference for χ² tests ([[Chi-Square Goodness-of-Fit Test]]).

Used in: [[EE09 Hardy-Weinberg Equilibrium]], [[EE10 Natural Selection]]. Related: [[Allele and Genotype Frequencies]] · [[Genetic Drift and Inbreeding Depression]] · [[Blending Inheritance]].
