# Lake Restoration Methods

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** the three tables (physical, chemical, biological) of Søndergaard et al. (2012), pp. 455-458, mandatory reading ([[06_04-Sep-2026_Biotic-Abiotic Interactions.pdf#page=22|slide 22]])

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Cut the supply, remove what is already in the mud, or rearrange the living community.

> [!key] Definition
> **Physical:** sediment dredging, dilution/flushing, water-level manipulation, hypolimnetic withdrawal. **Chemical:** hypolimnetic oxygenation, phosphorus inactivation (aluminium, iron, calcium salts). **Biological:** removal of zooplankton-eating fish, stocking predatory fish, introducing mussels (zebra mussel *Dreissena polymorpha*, itself invasive). Each method comes with limits stated on the slides (cost, disposal, aluminium toxicity, timing, permanence).

## A decision logic over the three tables
Methods target one of three things: the **external load** (sewage diversion, treatment; Lake Washington, Lake Constance: the reading's "best method is prevention of nutrient addition"), the **internal load** (dredging, hypolimnetic oxygenation or withdrawal, phosphorus inactivation) and the **food web** (fish removal, predator stocking, mussels).

> [!example] Choosing a method (scenario)
> Shallow, unstratified lake; sewage already diverted; high P in the mud; turbid; many bream. *Do not choose* hypolimnetic oxygenation or withdrawal (no hypolimnion). *Do choose:* sediment dredging (expensive; needs disposal sites) or aluminium (check pH: toxic in soft-water or high-pH lakes) for the stock; **fish removal** to flip the state; a **lower water level** to help plants; **flushing** if nutrient-poor water is plentiful. Combine: cut the stock and push the state. A deep stratified lake with an anoxic hypolimnion: oxygenation (more than 10 years, permanence uncertain) or withdrawal (the downstream water is nutrient-rich, perhaps anoxic).
> **Drawbacks as listed:** dredging expensive; flushing needs clean water; water-level change may not be allowed; withdrawal harms downstream; oxygenation long-term; aluminium pH toxicity; fish removal and stocking effects temporary or timing-dependent; zebra mussels are invasive and can reach very high densities.

**Scale.** About 1.5 Tg P a year reaches fresh water; in many places there is not enough water to assimilate the load. "Tackling this problem requires inputs from scientists, politicians and above all the local people" (slide 25).

> [!pitfall] Expect a lag and a changed lake
> Restoration is judged over years: water renewal, sediment release and food-web adjustment mean a lag of several years (Schlachtensee, Berlin, in the reading). Mandatory source: Søndergaard et al. (2012), pp. 455–458 (tables on slides 22–24). Related: [[Internal Phosphorus Loading]], [[Biomanipulation]], [[Invasive Species]] (the mussel option).
