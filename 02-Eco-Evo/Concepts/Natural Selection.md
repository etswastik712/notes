# Natural Selection

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** [[Selection_Class Lecture.pdf#page=1|EE10 slides 1, 8–15]]; Freeman & Herron Ch. 3 (mandatory)

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Some heritable variants survive and reproduce better than others, so the next generation is made more of them.

> [!key] Definition
> **Differential survival and reproduction of individuals due to differences in phenotype** (Zimmer & Emlen 2013). Darwin's postulates: variation, heritability, differential success, non-random success → the population changes. Acts on individuals and phenotypes; evolution is the change in populations and allele frequencies. Always behind, not progressive, works with what is available, yet mutation + recombination let new traits evolve.

'Natural selection is not evolution' (wrap-up lecture): it needs heritable variation, and drift, mutation, migration and non-random mating also change allele frequencies.

Used in: [[EE10 Natural Selection]], [[EE08 Evolution - An Introduction]]. Related: [[Modes of Selection]] · [[Heritability]] · [[Fitness and Relative Fitness]] · [[Darwin's Finches on Daphne Major]].
