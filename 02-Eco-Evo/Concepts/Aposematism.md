# Aposematism

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** the definition and the yellow-winged darter example ([[05_01_Sep_2026_Predation.pdf#page=17|slide 17]])

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Advertising: a dangerous or foul-tasting animal makes itself conspicuous so predators learn once and remember.

> [!key] Definition
> **Aposematic (warning) coloration:** markings that make a dangerous, poisonous or foul-tasting animal particularly conspicuous and recognisable to a predator.

## Why conspicuousness can pay
A warning signal works because predators **learn**: a predator that tastes one noxious prey avoids look-alikes afterwards. The cost of educating predators is borne by the population carrying the signal, which makes sharing attractive.

> [!example] Müller's arithmetic
> Suppose $M=100$ naive predators each sample $k=3$ signal-bearers before learning: 300 prey lost per generation. If the signal belongs to one species with $n=10{,}000$ individuals, each individual risks $300/10{,}000=3\,\%$; if two species of the same size share the signal the risk is $300/20{,}000=1.5\,\%$ each. This is the advantage of **Müllerian mimicry** ([[Mimicry]]). A cryptic palatable species has no education cost but is eaten whenever detected ([[Crypsis]]).

The slide's definition: markings that make a dangerous, poisonous or foul-tasting animal conspicuous and recognisable. Slide 17's example is the yellow-winged darter *Sympetrum flaveolum*; the unpalatable *Heliconius* and the bold black-and-yellow of bees are classic cases.

> [!pitfall] Conspicuousness must be paired with a defence
> The slide on chemical defence stresses that toxins often do not save the individual that is eaten; the signal is what protects the *population*. Warning colouring must also be *recognisable* and *memorable* (simple, contrasting patterns). How such a trait can arise when rare is itself a puzzle (a conspicuous mutant is eaten before predators learn); explanations include gregariousness and kin selection, and starting from a mildly conspicuous, distasteful form.
