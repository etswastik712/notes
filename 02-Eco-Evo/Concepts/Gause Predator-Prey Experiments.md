# Gause Predator-Prey Experiments

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** Gause's *Paramecium* (prey) and *Didinium* (predator) experiments: no refuge, refuge, and immigration ([[05_01_Sep_2026_Predation.pdf#page=10|slide 10]])

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Put a predator in a jar with its prey and it eats them all and starves. Only an outside rescue, a new prey and predator every third day, keeps the cycle going.

> [!key] Definition
> Gause's microcosms: (a) no refuge: extinction; (b) with refuge: extinction; (c) one prey and one predator added every third day: **sustained oscillations**. Conclusion on the slide: left to itself the system is driven to extinction; oscillations need repeated outside interference.

> [!provenance]- Resolved question
> Resolved in [[EE05 Predation]] §5.1: Molles (Ch. 14) says that with the sediment refuge *Didinium* starved after eating the unprotected *Paramecium* and became extinct while the prey recovered, so the slide's 'extinction' in (b) is the predator's.

## The three outcomes
| set-up | outcome |
|---|---|
| (a) no refuge | the predator (*Didinium*) eats all the prey (*Paramecium*), then starves: both extinct |
| (b) with sediment refuge | predators eat the unprotected prey, starve and go extinct; the prey recover |
| (c) one prey and one predator added every third day | sustained oscillations |

Molles adds that in an earlier successful system Gause obtained cycles with *Paramecium aurelia* feeding on the yeast *Saccharomyces exiguus*. His conclusion on the slide: left to itself the system goes extinct; oscillations need constant interference from outside.

> [!example] Reading it through the model
> The Lotka-Volterra equations predict cycles forever; Gause saw that a *closed* microcosm cannot do that: stochastic extinction of the small predator population, and a prey refuge that stops the prey from ever being limited. With immigration the system is "rescued" at each low, which in the equations is the same as adding constant terms $+I_H$, $+I_P$ and so changes the extinction-prone orbit into a persistent one. Lotka (1932) already noted that refuges lower the capture coefficient $p$, raising $H^*=m/ap$.

> [!pitfall] Which extinction?
> Slide 10 says only "extinction" for (b); Molles' text says the predator. In (a) *both* go extinct. Lesson for conservation: small closed populations rarely sustain interactions; compare [[Metapopulation]], [[Demographic Genetic and Environmental Stochasticity]]. Gause also did the competition experiments of [[Competitive Exclusion Principle]].
