# Modes of Selection

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** [[Selection_Class Lecture.pdf#page=4|EE10 slides 4–7]]

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Selection can push a trait one way, squeeze it toward the middle, or split it toward both extremes.

> [!key] Definition
> **Directional**: one extreme favoured, the mean moves (finch beaks 1977 deeper, 1983/2004 smaller). **Stabilizing**: intermediates favoured, variance shrinks. **Disruptive**: both extremes favoured, variance grows and the distribution may split. One-locus analogues: directional (dominance sets the speed), heterozygote advantage (balancing, $p^*=t/(s+t)$), heterozygote disadvantage (unstable threshold).
