# Deccan Next field guide (northern leg only) (2026-11-02)

Field guide from a 4 and 5 October 2023 trip, compiled by Makarand Bodas, Gauri Dole, Poushali Chatterjee and Vivek S. Kale. The teacher says it was written for a different event, so some of it is irrelevant. Only the **northern leg** applies to the 7 Nov trip. (Classroom: Deccan Next_Field Guide.pdf)

## Covered

**Setting (background slides)**
- The route lies on the main Deccan Plateau, at about 550 to 1200 m above msl. Kalsubai (1648 m in this guide) is the thickest continuous pile of Trap basalts but is not visited.
- Three planation surfaces carry mature, meandering river valleys: Pune-Indore (Upper Plateau) at 550 ± 50 m, Ahmednagar at 700 ± 50 m, and the highland surface (Bhimashankar about 1000 m in the north, Mahabaleshwar about 1200 m in the south).
- Drainage across the plateau is entirely eastward: Godavari (north) and Bhima-Krishna (south).
- The region was once taken as stable craton interior. The Koyna and Latur earthquakes showed otherwise, and both lie on concealed rifts. Structural disturbance (pre-, syn- and post-volcanic) follows narrow linear zones with Precambrian heritage: the Central Indian Tectonic Zone (Son-Narmada and Tapi lineaments), the Western Coastal Belt, the Kurduwadi and Chiplun-Koyna lineaments, and the Pranhita-Godavari rift.
- The Sahyadri Group is the "type section" of Deccan stratigraphy. No fossiliferous inter-flow sediments occur in the more than 3000 m composite sequence of this region.

**Internal configuration of a lava**
- Lava erupts at about 1100 to 800 °C onto a surface at about 30 to 40 °C. The features seen in the frozen basalt formed after emplacement, except phenocrysts, which started to grow before eruption.
- Features: glassy (tachylitic) rinds from instant chilling of the surface, vesicles (trails of escaping vapour), and layering from cooling downward from the top and upward from the base.
- Crystallinity increases downward to the medial cooling isotherm, then decreases toward the base. This gives three layers in a frozen lobe: crust, core and base.
- Terminology: each pulse of lava becomes a lava flow, made of many lobes. Exposures are 2D sections, so they are described as lobes. A single vent active over time gives many flows, together a flow-field.
- Stacking architecture of a flow-field is braided, ponded or tabular. It is controlled by (a) volumetric emplacement rate, (b) substrate topography and structure, (c) viscosity and internal strain of each flow.
- Morphology: Walker (1971) recognised two basic types (pāhoehoe and ʻaʻā). Most flood-basalt lavas are pāhoehoe types. Lobe morphology results from a continuous variation in emplacement rate, vapour-loss rate and mode, lateral movement rate and cooling rate, not from different "lava types". Lateral transition between morphologies is therefore normal.

**Stratigraphy (Sahyadri Group, Western DVP; Pattabhiram et al. 2022)**
- Subgroups, bottom to top: Kalsubai (Salher, Nashik, Malshej formations), Lonavala (Indrayani, Bushe), Wai (Poladpur, Purandargarh, Mahabaleshwar), with Bombay above (Borivali). The Kalsubai and Lonavala subgroups are separated by the Giravali GPB (M3).
- Four regionally continuous GPB (giant plagioclase basalt, megacryst) marker horizons are the most reliable field markers: Thalghat (M1), Ghoti (M2), Giravali (M3, with Manchar GPB M3a below it and the Bhimashankar Member between), Rajgad (M4).
- Where the Bhimashankar Member pinches out (south and east), the Giravali GPB is the marker between the Kalsubai and Lonavala subgroups.
- Average ages and polarity from the table: Salher 66.4 ± 0.3 Ma, Nashik and Malshej 66.3 ± 0.2 Ma (chron C29r), Indrayani 66.1 ± 0.2, Bushe 66.0 ± 0.1, Wai (Poladpur to Purandargarh) 65.7 ± 0.2, Mahabaleshwar 65.4 ± 0.2 Ma (C29n, normal polarity).
- Dominant morphology: Salher, Nashik and Malshej are hummocky and sheet pāhoehoe lobate flows (Salher with rare rubbly ʻaʻā, Nashik and Malshej with some slabby flows); Indrayani is sheet and rubbly tabular; Bushe hummocky pāhoehoe lobate; Poladpur, Purandargarh and Mahabaleshwar are rubbly and slabby tabular flows.
- Chemostratigraphic equivalents (Beane et al. 1986, Subbarao and Hooper 1988): Malshej corresponds to Thakurwadi/Bhimashankar, Indrayani to Khandala, Bushe, Poladpur, Ambenali, Mahabaleshwar. The Rajgad GPB is not recognised in the chemical stratigraphy.
- Members marked with an asterisk (Jawhar, Igatpuri, Thakurwadi, Bhimashankar) were Formations in earlier schemes; the guide says reducing their rank breaks the Code of stratigraphic nomenclature and may be revised.

**Northern leg: stops and key features** (start 06:30 from SPPU, return about 20:00)
- NL1, Rajgurunagar Ghat (NH50, about 740 m): Manchar GPB (M3a) and the following Malshej (Bhimashankar) Formation. Megacryst basalt, cross section of a tabular sheet flow, incipient flow-top breccia (FTB) capped by a glassy vesicular crust, a trapped vesicular lobe in the core, gas escape features, vesicularity and joints in crust, core and base, and the contrast between structural fracture zones and cooling joints. An intrusive dyke at the base of the ghat if time allows (some trekking).
- Journey NL1 to NL2: the wide flat Kukdi valley near Belhe is an anomalous, beheaded river valley under the steep Harishchandragad escarpment. The escarpment is inferred to follow a NW-SE strike-slip fault (Ghod Fault) of the Kurduwadi Lineament Zone (Kale et al. 2017).
- NL2a, Ane Ghat base (SH61, about 760 m): braided lobate flow section of the upper Malshej Formation, made of many pāhoehoe lobes or sheet lobes. Look for chilled (tachylitic) rinds, vesicular crust, compact core and a base with pipe vesicles, trapped vesicular lobes, gas escape features and local incipient FTB.
- NL2b, Ane Ghat top: Giravali GPB (M3). Megacryst flow with variably distributed plagioclase phenocrysts, synvolcanic faulting of lobe boundaries, axial clefts and injection features of hummocky lobes, and a superimposed tectonic shear zone. A knick zone on an entrenched meander has produced a natural arch (stone bridge) by differential weathering and removal of an entire lobe.
- NL3, Mandhol Dam, Kanhoor Plateau: upper Malshej flows with a thin Giravali GPB cap. Entrenched, gently meandering rejuvenated channel below the dam, with a rectilinear fracture-controlled drainage pattern and hanging tributaries joining at right angles. The flow is crossed by a dyke swarm and rectilinear fracture sets. Lobe stacks show tripartite (crust, core, base) layering. The guide treats the channel features as evidence of recent uplift of this block relative to the neighbouring Godavari (Pravara) valley block to the north, where thick Quaternary alluvium has accumulated.
- NL4, Kukdi river bed near Nighoj, about 600 m ("Ranjan Khalge"): bedrock channel beside a roughly 10 m cascade with potholes more than 10 m across, like limestone sink-holes but uncommon in basalt. Lower Malshej flows. The linear channel runs parallel to the inferred Ghod Fault of the Kurduwadi Lineament; potholes and cascades indicate recent rejuvenation.

**Field-trip practicalities from the guide** (these were for 2023; the 7 Nov trip rules are in [[landscape-evolution/2026-10-30-field-trip-7-nov-safety-and-declaration]])
- Expect heat and humidity, about 200 to 300 m walking from bus to exposures, shoes and cap. GPS, Brunton compass, hammer and sample bags are optional personal equipment.

**Southern leg** (Jambhulwadi, Khandala Pargaon, Mandhardev Ghat, Karad borehole lab) is not part of the 7 Nov trip per the teacher, so it is not summarised. The guide's southern-leg point about box-shaped flat-bottomed steep-sided valleys with 5 to 10 m of Quaternary alluvium and colluvium is listed in the exam prep as extra context.
