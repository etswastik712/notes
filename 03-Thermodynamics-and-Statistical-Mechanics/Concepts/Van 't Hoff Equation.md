# Van 't Hoff Equation

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** Follows from [[TD2213_Tutorial1.pdf#page=1|Tutorial 1]] Q2 (Gibbs–Helmholtz) with $\Delta G^\circ=-RT\ln K$; [[TH08 Gibbs Energy Equilibrium Constants and Phase Boundaries]] §4.2.
> 🟡 **extra · book:** Schroeder Problem 5.85 (p. 214) and Problem 5.86; the ammonia value $K(500\ ^\circ\mathrm C)=6.9\times10^{-5}$ is quoted on p. 213.

> [!intuition] In one picture
> How fast $\ln K$ changes with $1/T$ is the reaction enthalpy: a straight-line plot whose slope is $-\Delta H^\circ/R$.

> [!key] Key facts
> $\mathrm d\ln K/\mathrm dT=\Delta_rH^\circ/RT^2$; $\ln(K_2/K_1)=-(\Delta H^\circ/R)(1/T_2-1/T_1)$ (constant $\Delta H$); $\ln K=-\Delta H^\circ/RT+\Delta S^\circ/R$. Le Chatelier: endothermic: $K$ rises with $T$.

> [!example] Ammonia (recomputed)
> $K(298)=5.8\times10^5$; $K(773\ \mathrm K)=6.9\times10^{-5}$ (constant $\Delta H$) or $5.5\times10^{-5}$ with $\Delta C_p=-5.48$ J K$^{-1}$.
