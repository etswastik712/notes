# Hess's Law

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** [[Thermodynamics_Tutorial_2_Questions.pdf#page=1|Tutorial 2]], Q3 (hydration of Mg$^{2+}$, a closed cycle); [[TH07 Enthalpy Thermochemistry and Calorimetry]] §3.
> 🟡 **extra · book:** Schroeder §1.6 and the data table, pp. 404–405 (djvu page = printed + 10).

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Enthalpy is a state function, so the heat of a reaction at constant pressure does not depend on the route: add reactions, add enthalpies.

> [!key] Key facts
> $\Delta H_{\rm total}=\sum\Delta H_{\rm steps}$; reverse a step: change sign; multiply by $k$: multiply $\Delta H$. $\Delta_rH^\circ=\sum\nu\Delta_fH^\circ$ (products) $-\sum|\nu|\Delta_fH^\circ$ (reactants).

> [!example] Hydration of Mg$^{2+}$ (Tutorial 2, Q3)
> $\mathrm{MgCl_2(s)}\to\mathrm{Mg^{2+}(aq)}+2\mathrm{Cl^-(aq)}$ directly ($-155$) or via the gas-phase ions ($+2526$, then $x$, then $2(-381)$): $x=-155-2526+762=-1919$ kJ mol$^{-1}$. Check: the loop sums to zero.

> [!pitfall] Signs
> Write each step in the direction you traverse it; quoted lattice *dissociation* enthalpies are positive, lattice *formation* enthalpies negative.
