# Entropy Production

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** [[TD2213_Tutorial1.pdf#page=1|Tutorial 1]], Q4 (prove that entropy generation is positive when body A loses heat to body B); [[TH08 Gibbs Energy Equilibrium Constants and Phase Boundaries]] §2 and §6; microscopic version in [[TH09 Microstates Macrostates and Multiplicity]] §4.3.
> 🟡 **extra · book:** Koks §3.8, §3.13 (relation of entropy increase to interaction direction); Schroeder §3.2.

> [!intuition] In one picture
> The entropy created inside an isolated pair by an irreversible process: never negative, zero only for reversible processes.

> [!key] Key facts
> Heat $\delta Q$ from $T_A$ to $T_B$: $\delta S_{\rm gen}=\delta Q(1/T_B-1/T_A)>0$ for $T_A>T_B$. Finite bodies: $S_{\rm gen}=C_A\ln(T_f/T_A)+C_B\ln(T_f/T_B)\ge0$ (Jensen). Constant $T,P$ process: $S_{\rm gen}=-\Delta G/T$. Lost work $=T_0S_{\rm gen}$.

> [!example] Numbers (recomputed)
> 1000 J from 500 K to 300 K: $1.333$ J K$^{-1}$ (lost work $400$ J). Two $1000$ J K$^{-1}$ bodies, 500 K and 300 K: $64.5$ J K$^{-1}$. Water-gas reaction at 1000 K: $10.55$ J K$^{-1}$ per mole.
