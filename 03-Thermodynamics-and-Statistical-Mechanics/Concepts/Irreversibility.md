# Irreversibility

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-beyond">extra · beyond</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** [[1a131e225246d9749a298f49675c5fe5_MIT8_333F13_Lec2.pdf#page=2|MIT L2 (reversible vs quasi-static)]]. · 🔵 **extra · beyond:** lost work (Gouy–Stodola).

> [!intuition] In one picture
> A process that cannot be undone without leaving a trace somewhere else.

> [!key] Definition
> A **reversible** process can be run backward in time by reversing inputs and outputs; it is quasi-static and dissipation-free. Any process with friction, heat flow across a finite $\Delta T$, or free expansion is irreversible and generates entropy: $\Delta S_{\rm total}>0$.

## Lost work
$1000$ J flowing from $500$ to $300$ K could have yielded $400$ J reversibly; it yields $0$ and $T_C\,\Delta S_{\rm gen}=300\times1.333=400$ J: lost work equals sink temperature times entropy produced.

> [!pitfall] Reversible $\ne$ quasi-static
> Reversible implies quasi-static; the converse fails (friction).
