# Second Law of Thermodynamics

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** [[1a131e225246d9749a298f49675c5fe5_MIT8_333F13_Lec2.pdf#page=1|MIT L2 §I.D]].

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Heat runs downhill by itself; going uphill costs work. Everything else (engine limits, entropy) follows.

> [!key] Definition
> Kelvin: no process has as its sole result the complete conversion of heat into work. Clausius: none has as sole result the transfer of heat from a colder to a hotter body. Equivalent forms: $\oint\bar\mathrm dQ/T\le0$; $\mathrm dS\ge\bar\mathrm dQ/T$; $\delta S\ge0$ for isolated systems.

## What it gives
A temperature scale independent of any substance ($\eta=1-T_C/T_H$), the entropy $S$, the arrow of time and the equilibrium criteria of [[TH05 Potentials Maxwell Relations Stability and the Third Law]]. Statistical meaning: the macrostate with most microstates is overwhelmingly probable.

> [!pitfall] Statistical, not absolute
> For $N\sim10^{23}$ the probability of an entropy-decreasing fluctuation (all molecules in one half) is $2^{-N}$: never seen, not forbidden.

Used in: [[TH04 The Second Law Carnot Engines and Entropy]]. Related: [[Kelvin and Clausius Statements]] · [[Carnot Theorem]] · [[Entropy]] · [[Irreversibility]].
