# Quasi-Static Process

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** [[0d0ec730e8e02ed0399d39ff05054a72_MIT8_333F13_Lec1.pdf#page=4|MIT L1 §I.C]].

> [!intuition] In one picture
> So slow that the system never leaves equilibrium, so its coordinates are defined at every stage.

> [!key] Definition
> A **quasi-static** transformation is slow enough that the system is always in equilibrium; then $\bar\mathrm dW=\sum J_i\mathrm dx_i$ and the path is a curve in the coordinate plane. **Reversible** processes are quasi-static, but a slow process with friction is quasi-static yet irreversible.

## Note
For a gas, $W_{\rm by}=\int P\,\mathrm dV$ only along a quasi-static path (otherwise $P$ is not defined for the whole system). Free expansion is neither quasi-static nor reversible.

> [!pitfall] Slow is not enough
> Friction or heat flowing across a finite temperature difference make a slow process irreversible ([[Irreversibility]]).
