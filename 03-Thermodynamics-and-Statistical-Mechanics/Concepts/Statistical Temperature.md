# Statistical Temperature

<span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟡 **extra · book:** Koks §3.5 (p. 148); Schroeder §3.1 (p. 85).
> 🟢 **taught (pointer only):** [[TH09 Microstates Macrostates and Multiplicity]] §4.2 (Tutorial 1 Q4 is the macroscopic consequence).

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Temperature is the exchange rate between energy and the logarithm of the number of microstates; equal temperatures are the condition for the most probable split of energy.

> [!key] Key facts
> $1/T=(\partial S/\partial U)_{V,N}$, $S=k_B\ln\Omega$; maximising $\Omega_A\Omega_B$ gives $\partial S_A/\partial U_A=\partial S_B/\partial U_B$. Einstein solid: $k_BT/\varepsilon=1/\ln(1+N/q)$ (0.417, 0.910, 1.443, 2.466, 5.485 at $q/N=0.1,0.5,1,2,5$). Likewise $P/T=\partial S/\partial V$, $-\mu/T=\partial S/\partial N$.

> [!example] Heat flow at $q_A=80$
> $k_BT_A/\varepsilon=0.642>k_BT_B/\varepsilon=0.417$: A is hotter; one quantum A→B gains $0.794\,k_B$ (exact), the reverse loses $0.848\,k_B$.
