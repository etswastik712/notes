# Gibbs-Helmholtz Equation

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** [[TD2213_Tutorial1.pdf#page=1|Tutorial 1]], Q2 ($\Delta G^\circ/T$ at 500 °C from 25 °C with constant $\Delta H^\circ$); [[TH08 Gibbs Energy Equilibrium Constants and Phase Boundaries]] §4.1.
> 🟡 **extra · book:** Schroeder §5.6 (Problem 5.85: van 't Hoff, a consequence).

> [!intuition] In one picture
> $G/T$ changes with temperature at a rate set only by the enthalpy: $-H/T^2$.

> [!key] Key facts
> $[\partial(G/T)/\partial T]_P=-H/T^2$; for reactions $\Delta_rG/T$ at $T_2$ $=\Delta_rG/T$ at $T_1+\Delta_rH(1/T_2-1/T_1)$ (constant $\Delta H$). Derivation: $\partial_T(G/T)=-S/T-G/T^2=-H/T^2$ with $S=-\partial G/\partial T$.

> [!example] Tutorial 1 Q2 (recomputed)
> $-50\,000/298.15+(-80\,000)(1/773.15-1/298.15)=-167.70+164.85=-2.85$ J K$^{-1}$mol$^{-1}$; $\Delta G^\circ(773\ \mathrm K)=-2.2$ kJ mol$^{-1}$, $K=1.41$ (against $5.7\times10^8$ at 25 °C).
