# Calorimetry

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** [[Thermodynamics_Tutorial_2_Questions.pdf#page=1|Tutorial 2]], Q5 (bomb calorimeter, benzoic acid) and Q6 (neutralisation in a cup); [[TH07 Enthalpy Thermochemistry and Calorimetry]] §5.
> 🟡 **extra · book:** Schroeder §1.6 (heat capacities, calorimetry data).

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Measure a temperature rise in a vessel of known heat capacity: $q=C\Delta T$. Constant volume gives $\Delta U$, constant pressure gives $\Delta H$.

> [!key] Key facts
> $q=C\Delta T$; bomb: $\Delta_cU=-q/n$ ($q_V=\Delta U$); cup: $\Delta H=-q/n$ ($q_P=\Delta H$), $q=mc\Delta T$. Calibrate with electrical heating $IVt$ or a standard (benzoic acid). Correct for heat loss by extrapolating the pre- and post-reaction drift to the ignition time.

> [!example] Tutorial 2 (recomputed)
> Bomb: $q=10.0\times1.50=15.0$ kJ, $n=4.094$ mmol, $\Delta_cU=-3.66\times10^3$ kJ mol$^{-1}$ ($\Delta_cH=-3664.8$). Cup: $q=100\times4.18\times6.7=2.80$ kJ, $n=0.0500$ mol, $\Delta H=-56.0$ kJ mol$^{-1}$.
