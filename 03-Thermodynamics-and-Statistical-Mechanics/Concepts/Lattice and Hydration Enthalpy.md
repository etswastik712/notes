# Lattice and Hydration Enthalpy

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** [[Thermodynamics_Tutorial_2_Questions.pdf#page=1|Tutorial 2]], Q3 (hydration of Mg$^{2+}$ from the lattice dissociation, solution and Cl$^-$ hydration enthalpies); [[TH07 Enthalpy Thermochemistry and Calorimetry]] §3.1.
> 🟡 **extra · book:** Inorganic chemistry (Shriver and Atkins, Born-Haber cycles), cross-link.

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Dissolving a salt is a competition: pulling the lattice apart costs a huge enthalpy that the hydration of the ions almost entirely repays.

> [!key] Key facts
> $\Delta_{\rm sol}H=\Delta_{\rm lat}H(\text{dissociation})+\Delta_{\rm hyd}H(\text{cation})+\sum\Delta_{\rm hyd}H(\text{anions})$. MgCl$_2$: $-155=2526+x+2(-381)\Rightarrow x=-1919$ kJ mol$^{-1}$.

> [!pitfall] Which sign?
> Lattice *dissociation* is endothermic; lattice *formation* has the opposite sign. A closed cycle must sum to zero: $+2526-1919-762+155=0$.
