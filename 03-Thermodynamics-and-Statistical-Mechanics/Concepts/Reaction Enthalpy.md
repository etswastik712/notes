# Reaction Enthalpy

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** [[TD2213_Tutorial1.pdf#page=1|Tutorial 1]], Q1 ($\Delta H=\Delta U+P\Delta V$) and [[Thermodynamics_Tutorial_2_Questions.pdf#page=1|Tutorial 2]], Q5 ($\Delta_cU$ from the bomb, with $\Delta n_g=-0.5$); [[TH07 Enthalpy Thermochemistry and Calorimetry]] §1–§2.
> 🟡 **extra · book:** Schroeder §1.6 (enthalpy), Ch. 5.

> [!intuition] In one picture
> The heat taken up by a reaction at constant pressure; it differs from the energy change by the work of making or removing gas.

> [!key] Key facts
> $\Delta_rH=\Delta_rU+\Delta n_gRT$ (ideal gases; $RT=2.479$ kJ mol$^{-1}$ at 298.15 K). $\Delta H=\Delta U+P\Delta V$ at constant $P$; Tutorial 1 Q1: $2.50+2.0\times10^5\times5.0\times10^{-3}=3.50$ kJ.

> [!example] Differences (recomputed)
> N$_2$ + 3H$_2$ → 2NH$_3$: $\Delta H-\Delta U=-4.96$ kJ; benzoic acid combustion $-1.24$ kJ; vaporising water at 373 K: $+3.10$ kJ (so $\Delta_{\rm vap}U=37.56$ kJ at $\Delta_{\rm vap}H=40.66$).
