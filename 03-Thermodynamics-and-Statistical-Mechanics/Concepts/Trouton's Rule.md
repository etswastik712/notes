# Trouton's Rule

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** Applied to [[TD2213_Tutorial1.pdf#page=1|Tutorial 1]], Q3 (the liquid with $\Delta_{\rm vap}H=40$ kJ mol$^{-1}$ at 80 °C); [[TH08 Gibbs Energy Equilibrium Constants and Phase Boundaries]] §5.
> 🟡 **extra · book:** Standard physical chemistry (the range is recalled).

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Most simple liquids gain about the same entropy, roughly $10.5R$, when they boil at their normal boiling point.

> [!key] Key facts
> $\Delta_{\rm vap}S=\Delta_{\rm vap}H/T_b\approx85$–$88$ J K$^{-1}$mol$^{-1}$ (recalled); Tutorial 1 Q3 liquid: $113.3$; water: $109.0$ (associated liquids are higher). At $T_b$, $\Delta_{\rm vap}G^\circ=0$.

> [!pitfall] A rule, not a law
> Associated (hydrogen-bonded) liquids and very light or very cold ones deviate; the rule is a statement about the entropy of the *vapour* at 1 atm.
