# Ideal Gas Temperature Scale

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** [[0d0ec730e8e02ed0399d39ff05054a72_MIT8_333F13_Lec1.pdf#page=3|MIT L1 eq. I.10]]. · 🟡 **extra · book:** constant-volume gas thermometer (Zemansky Ch. 1).

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Any dilute gas gives the same $PV$ along an isotherm, so a gas thermometer needs no calibration by substance.

> [!key] Definition
> $T(\mathrm K)=273.16\times\lim_{P\to0}(PV)_{\rm system}\big/\lim_{P\to0}(PV)_{\rm triple\ point}$. For a constant-volume thermometer $T=273.16\,P/P_{\rm tp}$.

## Notes
Example: $P_{\rm tp}=80$ kPa, reading $109.3$ kPa gives $373.2$ K. The thermodynamic (Carnot) scale coincides with it. Since 2019 the Boltzmann constant is exact and the triple point of water is measured ($273.16$ K to about 0.1 mK).

> [!pitfall] The limit matters
> Real gases differ at finite pressure; one extrapolates $P\to0$ (or uses a very dilute gas) to remove the dependence on the gas.
