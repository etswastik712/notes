# Kirchhoff's Law

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** [[Thermodynamics_Tutorial_2_Questions.pdf#page=1|Tutorial 2]], Q1 (HCl) and Q4 (water-gas reaction at 298 and 378 K); [[TH07 Enthalpy Thermochemistry and Calorimetry]] §4.
> 🟡 **extra · book:** Schroeder §1.6 ($C_p$) and Problem 3.31 (the $C_p(T)$ formula for graphite).

> [!intuition] In one picture
> The enthalpy of reaction changes with temperature at the rate $\Delta C_p$: products and reactants warm up at different speeds.

> [!key] Key facts
> $\mathrm d\Delta_rH/\mathrm dT=\Delta_rC_p$; $\Delta_rH(T_2)=\Delta_rH(T_1)+\int_{T_1}^{T_2}\Delta_rC_p\,\mathrm dT$; constant $\Delta C_p$: $\Delta_rH(T_2)=\Delta_rH(T_1)+\Delta_rC_p(T_2-T_1)$. Valid if no phase change occurs between $T_1$ and $T_2$.

> [!example] Numbers (recomputed)
> HCl formation: $-120+25\times52\times10^{-3}=-118.70$ kJ mol$^{-1}$. Water-gas reaction: $\Delta_rC_p=29.14+28.82-8.53-33.58=+15.85$ J K$^{-1}$; $\Delta_rH(378)=131.29+0.01585\times80=132.56$ kJ mol$^{-1}$. Graphite heated 298→500 K: $2632$ J (variable $C_p$) against $1723$ J (constant): constant $C_p$ is $35\%$ low over 200 K.
