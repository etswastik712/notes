# Equilibrium Constant

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** [[Thermodynamics_Tutorial_2_Questions.pdf#page=1|Tutorial 2]], Q2 (ATPase: $K=0.295$ and $\Delta_rS^\circ$ give $\Delta_rH^\circ=-10.4$ kJ mol$^{-1}$); [[TH08 Gibbs Energy Equilibrium Constants and Phase Boundaries]] §3.
> 🟡 **extra · book:** Schroeder §5.6 (pp. 208–219); statistical derivation in [[SM05 The Grand Canonical Ensemble and Chemical Equilibrium]] §7.

> [!intuition] In one picture
> The value of the reaction quotient at which the Gibbs energy is stationary; large $K$ means a very negative standard Gibbs energy.

> [!key] Key facts
> $\Delta_rG=\Delta_rG^\circ+RT\ln Q$; at equilibrium $Q=K$ and $\Delta_rG^\circ=-RT\ln K$; $\Delta_rG=RT\ln(Q/K)$. $\Delta G^\circ=-5.71$ kJ mol$^{-1}$ at 298.15 K is $K=10$; $-50$ kJ is $K=5.7\times10^8$. ATPase: $\Delta G^\circ=+3.02$ kJ mol$^{-1}$, $\Delta H^\circ=-10.4$; at pH 7, $K'=2.95\times10^6$, $\Delta G^{\circ\prime}=-36.9$ kJ mol$^{-1}$.

> [!pitfall] The standard state is part of the answer
> $\Delta G^\circ$ refers to unit activity of every species (H$^+$ at pH 0); biochemists use pH 7, shifting $\Delta G^\circ$ by $RT\ln10^7=40.0$ kJ mol$^{-1}$ for one H$^+$.
