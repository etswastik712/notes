# Macrostate

<span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟡 **extra · book:** Koks §2.1; Reif §2.1–2.2; Blundell §4.3.
> 🟢 **taught (pointer only):** [[TH09 Microstates Macrostates and Multiplicity]] §1 (pointer: MIT L4 §I.J).

> [!intuition] In one picture
> What you specify when you give only a few macroscopic variables: all microstates that agree with them count as one macrostate.

> [!key] Key facts
> Dice: the sum; coins: the number of heads; solid: $q$ (energy); gas: $(U,V,N)$ or the number in the left half. $P(M)=\Omega(M)/\Omega_{\rm tot}$. The macrostate is a property of what you measure: coarsening it merges macrostates and raises $\Omega$ and $S=k_B\ln\Omega$.

> [!example] How sharp is it?
> Coins: the macrostate "within 1% of half heads" has probability $0.236$ ($N=100$), $0.493$ ($10^3$), $0.956$ ($10^4$).
