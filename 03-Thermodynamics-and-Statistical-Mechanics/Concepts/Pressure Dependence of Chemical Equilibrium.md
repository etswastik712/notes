# Pressure Dependence of Chemical Equilibrium

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-beyond">extra · beyond</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** [[Bhagwat.pdf#page=1|Bhagwat notes]], pp. 4–5: if $\sum\nu_i=0$ the pressure factors cancel; $\sum\nu_i=-\tfrac12$: $K\propto P^-0.5$, higher $P$ pushes the reaction forward.
> 🔵 **extra · beyond:** inert gas at constant $P$ versus constant $V$, the $\Delta\nu=0,+1$ examples, tables (mine).

> [!intuition] In one picture
> $K$ depends on temperature only, but the **composition** that satisfies it depends on pressure through the number of moles of gas: squeezing favours the side with fewer moles.

> [!key] Definition
> $K=\prod_i(x_iP/P^\circ)^{\nu_i}=\big[\prod x_i^{\nu_i}\big](P/P^\circ)^{\sum\nu_i}$. $\Sigma\nu<0$: higher $P$ favours products; $\Sigma\nu>0$: reactants; $\Sigma\nu=0$: no effect. Inert gas at constant total $P$ lowers all partial pressures (acts like lower $P$); at constant $V$ and $T$ it changes nothing.

$K=10$ (illustrative), SO$_2$/SO$_3$: $\varepsilon_{\rm eq}=0.5707,\ 0.7645,\ 0.8819,\ 0.9547$ at $0.1,\ 1,\ 10,\ 200$ bar; with $3$ mol inert at 1 bar: $0.6667$. N$_2$O$_4\rightleftharpoons2$NO$_2$ ($K=0.15$): $\varepsilon=\sqrt{K/(K+4P)}=0.522,\ 0.190,\ 0.061$ at $0.1,\ 1,\ 10$ bar.
