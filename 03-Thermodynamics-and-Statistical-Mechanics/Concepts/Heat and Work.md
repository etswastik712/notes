# Heat and Work

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** [[0d0ec730e8e02ed0399d39ff05054a72_MIT8_333F13_Lec1.pdf#page=4|MIT L1 eq. I.12]]. · 🟡 **extra · book:** sign conventions (Zemansky Ch. 3–4).

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Two ways to deposit energy: by organised pushing (work) or by random molecular jostling across a temperature difference (heat).

> [!key] Definition
> Work $\bar\mathrm dW=\sum J_i\mathrm dx_i$; heat $\Delta Q\equiv\Delta E-\Delta W$ is the remainder in a non-adiabatic process. Neither is a state function. MIT: $W$ on the system; Zemansky: $W$ by the system.

## Example
$A=(2\text{ atm},10\text{ L})\to B=(1\text{ atm},20\text{ L})$ for a monatomic gas ($\Delta E=0$): isochoric-then-isobaric $W_{\rm by}=Q=1013$ J; isobaric-then-isochoric $2026$ J; straight line $1520$ J.

> [!pitfall] Sign of $W$
> Same experiment, opposite sign of the symbol: $\mathrm dE=\bar\mathrm dQ+\bar\mathrm dW$ (MIT) versus $\mathrm dU=\bar\mathrm dQ-\bar\mathrm dW$ (Zemansky).
