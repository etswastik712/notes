# Thermodynamic Temperature

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** [[1a131e225246d9749a298f49675c5fe5_MIT8_333F13_Lec2.pdf#page=3|MIT L2 eqs. I.21–I.22]].

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Define temperature by what a perfect engine does, not by any substance.

> [!key] Definition
> $Q_C/Q_H=T_C/T_H$ for a Carnot engine, from $1-\eta(T_1,T_3)=[1-\eta(T_1,T_2)][1-\eta(T_2,T_3)]$ (with $f(T)=T$); scale fixed by the triple point $273.16$ K. All thermodynamic temperatures are positive.

## Properties
It coincides with the ideal-gas scale. $(\partial S/\partial E)_x=1/T$. A negative $T$ would let an engine take heat from both reservoirs and convert it all to work (Kelvin violation).

> [!pitfall] Negative temperatures
> Statistical systems with a bounded energy spectrum (e.g. spin systems) can have $T<0$ in the statistical sense (hotter than $T=\infty$); that is outside this classical argument, which assumes Carnot engines between reservoirs with unbounded energy.
