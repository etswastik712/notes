# Storm Surge

<span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟡 **Extra · book:** A&A = Anderson and Anderson, *Geomorphology* (PDF page = printed page + 18). Ch. 16, printed pp.511-512 ([[LS26 Sea Level and Ocean Waves]] §4.2).

> [!intuition] In one picture
> A storm raises the sea twice over: the low pressure sucks it up and the wind shoves it against the coast.

> [!key] Definition and key results
> Inverse barometer $\Delta z=\Delta P/\rho g$ ($\approx1.1$ m for a 113 hPa deficit; the atmosphere is 10.3 m of fresh water). Wind setup $\Delta\eta=\tau_wL/\rho gh$ with $\tau_w=\rho_aC_dU^2$: 3.7 m for $U=50$ m/s, a 100 km wide, 20 m deep shelf. Wide, shallow shelves are dangerous; the wind term dominates a 10 m surge. On a delta slope of $10^{-4}$ a 5 m surge reaches 50 km inland.

> [!pitfall] Pitfall
> Surge is not the same as the waves riding on it; at high tide both add.
