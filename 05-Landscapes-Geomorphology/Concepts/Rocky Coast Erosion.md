# Rocky Coast Erosion

<span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟡 **Extra · book:** A&A = Anderson and Anderson, *Geomorphology* (PDF page = printed page + 18). Ch. 16, printed pp.520-526 ([[LS27 Coasts Beaches Deltas and Rocky Shores]] §6-§7).

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Waves work on a cliff like a river on bedrock: abrasion and quarrying, only the power comes from breaking waves and the tide decides how much reaches the rock.

> [!key] Definition and key results
> Abrasion, quarrying (hydraulic wedging), little dissolution (salt weathering above the waves). Wave power $\rho g^2H^2T/32\pi$; energy at the cliff base $\propto H^2$ with $H_{max}\approx0.78h$ (tide changes it by $(5/3)^2=2.8$). Seismometers: each wave sways the cliff by $\sim10\ \mu$m; a cliff retreating 10 cm/yr is cycled $\sim10^9$ times. Arctic ice-cemented bluffs retreat tens of m/yr as sea ice disappears.

> [!pitfall] Pitfall
> Rocking of the cliff occurs with or without a beach; abrasion needs bare rock.
