# Overland Flow and Runoff Mechanisms

<span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟡 **Extra · book:** A&A = Anderson and Anderson, *Geomorphology* (PDF page = printed page + 18). Ch. 11, printed pp.363–370 ([[LS25 Water in the Landscape]] §6-§7).

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Rain reaches the channel by the surface (fast), by shallow soil flow (slower) or by groundwater (slowest); which one dominates sets how flashy the river is.

> [!key] Definition and key results
> HOF when $R>I$; SOF where the water table reaches the surface (a variable source area that grows with the storm); SSSF through conductive soil layers. Speeds $\sim10^{-1}$, $10^{-4}$, $10^{-7}$ m/s. Sheetwash: $q=(R-I)x$, $h\propto x^{2/3}$, $\tau_b=\rho ghS$; belt of no erosion $X_c=\frac{\tau_c}{\rho gS(R-I)}\sqrt{8\tau_c/\rho f}$ (271 m for 5/2 mm/h rain, 18 m for 50/5 mm/h). Kinematic-wave time $h(L)/(R-I)$.

> [!pitfall] Pitfall
> Horton overland flow is rarely observed in vegetated terrain; the belt-of-no-erosion theory now describes bare badlands and pavement.
