# Hyperpycnal Flow

<span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟡 **Extra · book:** A&A = Anderson and Anderson, *Geomorphology* (PDF page = printed page + 18). Ch. 16, printed pp.518-519 ([[LS27 Coasts Beaches Deltas and Rocky Shores]] §5.3).

> [!intuition] In one picture
> If the river's muddy water is denser than the basin's water it dives and runs along the bed as a turbidity current.

> [!key] Definition and key results
> Bulk density $\rho_w+C(1-\rho_w/\rho_s)$; plunging needs $C>(\rho_{amb}-\rho_{riv})/(1-\rho_w/\rho_s)$: 40 kg/m³ into seawater, 16 kg/m³ into a 1010 kg/m³ estuary; cold river into a warm lake plunges at any $C$. Hypopycnal plumes spread over a salt wedge.

> [!pitfall] Pitfall
> Hyperpycnal flow is rare in the sea but common in lakes (Lake Mead's foreslope is far gentler than the angle of repose).
