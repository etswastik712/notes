# Drainage Density and Horton's Laws

<span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟡 **Extra · book:** A&A = Anderson and Anderson, *Geomorphology* (PDF page = printed page + 18). Ch. 11, printed pp.350–351 ([[LS25 Water in the Landscape]] §1).

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Little streams join to make big ones in a branching tree whose number of branches falls by a fixed factor with each order; how closely the channels are spaced sets the length of the hillslopes.

> [!key] Definition and key results
> Strahler order $j$; $N_j=N_1R_b^{-(j-1)}$ with bifurcation ratio $R_b\approx3$–$4$ (random-topology networks give $3.99$). Drainage density $D_d=\sum L_i/A$ (1/length). For parallel channels $w=1/D_d$ apart: hillslope (divide-to-channel) length $=1/2D_d$, mean distance to the nearest channel $=1/4D_d$. High $D_d$ means short hillslopes and flashy rivers.

> [!pitfall] Pitfall
> $R_b$ is nearly universal because random branching gives it, not because rivers are 'tuned'; drainage density, not $R_b$, distinguishes landscapes.

Used in: [[LS25 Water in the Landscape]], [[LS16 Fluvial Landscapes]] (Hack's law). Related: [[Channel Head]] · [[Overland Flow and Runoff Mechanisms]] · [[Water Balance]].
