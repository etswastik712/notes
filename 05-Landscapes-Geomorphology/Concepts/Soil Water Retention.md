# Soil Water Retention

<span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟡 **Extra · book:** A&A = Anderson and Anderson, *Geomorphology* (PDF page = printed page + 18). Ch. 11, printed pp.353–355 ([[LS25 Water in the Landscape]] §3).

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Small pores hold water against gravity by surface tension; the drier the soil, the harder it is to pull more out.

> [!key] Definition and key results
> Suction head $h=2\gamma\cos\vartheta/\rho gr$ ($\gamma=0.072$ N/m): 3.4 m ($-33$ kPa, field capacity) for $r=4.3$ µm, 153 m ($-1.5$ MPa, wilting point) for $r=0.1$ µm. Above a water table in equilibrium $\psi=z$, so $\theta(z)$ is the retention curve on its side. Capillary fringe $\approx2\gamma/\rho gr_{max}$: 0.3 m (sand), 3 m (silt), 30 m (clay). Available water $=\theta_{fc}-\theta_{pwp}$, largest in loam.

> [!pitfall] Pitfall
> Clays hold the most water but also release the least to plants.
