# Darcy's Law and Hydraulic Conductivity

<span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟡 **Extra · book:** A&A = Anderson and Anderson, *Geomorphology* (PDF page = printed page + 18). Ch. 11, Boxes 11.1 and 11.3, printed pp.356 and 362 ([[LS25 Water in the Landscape]] §3.4).

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Water seeps through the pores at a rate proportional to the head gradient, with a constant that depends on both the fluid and how big and connected the pores are.

> [!key] Definition and key results
> $Q=-KA\,dh/dl$, $K=k\rho g/\mu$ (m/s), $k$ the intrinsic permeability (m²). $K$ spans 13 orders of magnitude: $\sim1$ m/s gravel to $10^{-13}$ m/s shale. Pipe-lattice model: $k=\phi d^2/96$ (Poiseuille: one pipe carries $\pi d^4\Delta p/128\mu\ell$, the fourth-power law). Medium sand ($D=0.3$ mm): $K\approx6\times10^{-4}$ m/s.

> [!pitfall] Pitfall
> Darcy's $q$ is a flux per total area, not the speed of the water in the pores (divide by porosity).
