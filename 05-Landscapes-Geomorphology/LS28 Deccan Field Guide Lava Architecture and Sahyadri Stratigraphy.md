## 1. The setting
<span class="scope-taught">taught</span> *guide, background slides (as reported)*
- The route lies on the main **Deccan Plateau**, about **550–1200 m** above sea level; **Kalsubai** (1648 m in this guide) is the thickest continuous pile of flood basalt but is not visited.
- **Three planation surfaces** carry mature meandering river valleys: **Pune–Indore** (Upper Plateau, $550\pm50$ m), **Ahmednagar** ($700\pm50$ m) and the **highland surface** (Bhimashankar $\sim$1000 m in the north, Mahabaleshwar $\sim$1200 m in the south) ([[planation-surfaces]]; the lecture lists six levels, 1330–600 m: `[!verify]` which scheme the exam uses).
- Drainage across the plateau is **entirely eastward**: Godavari (north) and Bhima–Krishna (south) ([[LS18 Deccan Traps Terrain]] §6).
- The region was once taken as **stable craton interior**; the **Koyna and Latur earthquakes** showed otherwise, both on **concealed rifts**. Structural disturbance (pre-, syn- and post-volcanic) follows **narrow linear zones with Precambrian heritage**: the Central Indian Tectonic Zone (Son–Narmada and Tapi lineaments), the Western Coastal Belt, the **Kurduwadi** and **Chiplun–Koyna** lineaments and others.
- The **Sahyadri Group** is the "type section" of Deccan stratigraphy; no fossiliferous inter-flow sediments occur in its $>3000$ m composite sequence here ([[intertrappean-beds]] elsewhere).

## 2. Anatomy of a lava flow
<span class="scope-taught">taught</span>
- Lava erupts at about **1100–800 °C** onto a surface at **30–40 °C**. Most features in the frozen basalt formed **after emplacement**; **phenocrysts** are the exception (they began growing before eruption).
- Features: **glassy (tachylitic) rinds** from instant chilling of the surface, **vesicles** (trails of escaping vapour) and **layering** from cooling downward from the top and upward from the base. **Crystallinity increases downward** to the **medial cooling isotherm** and then decreases toward the base: hence three layers in a frozen lobe, **crust, core and base** ([[lava-flow-internal-structure]]). The base may carry **pipe vesicles**; the crust a vesicular glassy skin and, in places, **flow-top breccia (FTB)**; the core is massive and jointed ([[columnar-jointing]]).
- **Terminology:** each pulse of lava is a **lava flow**, made of many **lobes**; exposures are 2-D sections, so they are described as lobes; one vent active over time gives many flows, together a **flow-field**.
- **Stacking architecture** of a flow-field is **braided, ponded or tabular**, controlled by **(a)** the volumetric emplacement rate, **(b)** the substrate topography and structure, **(c)** the viscosity and internal strain of each flow.
- **Morphology:** Walker (1971) recognised pāhoehoe and ʻaʻā; most flood-basalt lavas are **pāhoehoe types**. Lobe morphology (hummocky, sheet, slabby, rubbly) results from a **continuous variation** in emplacement rate, vapour-loss rate and mode, lateral movement rate and cooling rate, **not from different "lava types"**; lateral transitions between morphologies are normal. (Lecture link: effusion rate → landform, [[LS18 Deccan Traps Terrain]] §4.)
- 🔵 *Why the layers form:* a flow of thickness $d$ loses heat mainly by conduction through the top and base, so the cooling time scales as $d^2/\kappa$ ($\kappa\approx10^{-6}$ m² s⁻¹): a $10$ m lobe needs of order $10^8$ s $\approx3$ yr to solidify through, a $50$ m sheet of order $80$ yr (scaling only; LS18 has the detailed numbers). The crust, which cools first, is chilled glassy and vesicle-rich; the core cools slowest and crystallises most.

## 3. Sahyadri Group stratigraphy (Western Deccan Volcanic Province)
<span class="scope-taught">taught</span> *after Pattabhiram et al. 2022 as reported in the guide*

| subgroup | formations (bottom → top) | dominant morphology | age |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kalsubai** | Salher, Nashik, Malshej | Salher: hummocky and sheet pāhoehoe lobate flows (rare rubbly ʻaʻā); Nashik, Malshej: hummocky/sheet pāhoehoe, some slabby | Salher $66.4\pm0.3$ Ma; Nashik and Malshej $66.3\pm0.2$ Ma (chron C29r) |
| **Lonavala** | Indrayani, Bushe | Indrayani: sheet and **rubbly tabular**; Bushe: hummocky pāhoehoe lobate | $66.1\pm0.2$; $66.0\pm0.1$ Ma |
| **Wai** | Poladpur, Purandargarh, Mahabaleshwar | rubbly and **slabby tabular** flows | Wai (Poladpur–Purandargarh) $65.7\pm0.2$; Mahabaleshwar $65.4\pm0.2$ Ma (C29n, **normal** polarity) |
| Bombay (above) | Borivali | | |

- The Kalsubai and Lonavala subgroups are separated by the **Giravali GPB (M3)**.
- **Four regionally continuous GPB (giant plagioclase basalt, "megacryst") marker horizons** are the most reliable field markers: **Thalghat (M1)**, **Ghoti (M2)**, **Giravali (M3**, with the **Manchar GPB M3a** below it and the **Bhimashankar Member** between**)**, **Rajgad (M4)**. Where the Bhimashankar Member pinches out (south and east) the Giravali GPB marks the Kalsubai–Lonavala boundary.
- **Chemostratigraphic equivalents** (Beane et al. 1986; Subbarao and Hooper 1988): Malshej ↔ Thakurwadi/Bhimashankar, Indrayani ↔ Khandala, then Bushe, Poladpur, Ambenali, Mahabaleshwar; the Rajgad GPB is not recognised in the chemical stratigraphy. Members with an asterisk (Jawhar, Igatpuri, Thakurwadi, Bhimashankar) were Formations in earlier schemes; the guide says demoting them breaks the stratigraphic Code.
- 🔵 *Rates:* from $66.4$ Ma to $65.4$ Ma is about $1.0$ Myr for more than $3000$ m: an average accumulation of **$\approx3$ mm yr⁻¹**, i.e. about **one 10–50 m flow every 3–17 kyr** if the pile were made of single flows of that thickness (arithmetic on the guide's numbers; the true eruptive history is episodic, with pulses and quiet intervals).

## 5. A field checklist
1. **Which lobe, which layer?** Crust (vesicles, glassy rind, FTB), core (massive, columnar or entablature jointing), base (pipe vesicles). Is the section tabular, braided or ponded?
2. **Which morphology?** Hummocky, sheet, slabby, rubbly: remember it is a **continuum** (emplacement rate, vapour loss, lateral movement, cooling).
3. **Which marker?** A megacryst horizon: GPB (M1–M4); Giravali separates Kalsubai from Lonavala.
4. **Fracture or joint?** Cooling joints bound columns; **structural fracture zones** and dykes steer drainage (right-angle tributaries).
5. **Rejuvenation?** Knick zones, entrenched meanders, natural arch, potholes and cascades: recent incision into a rigid bedrock ([[LS23 Bedrock Channels and River Incision]]).

## Summary
> [!summary] Cheat-sheet
> - **Lobe:** crust (glassy rind, vesicles, FTB) / core (massive, most crystalline at the medial cooling isotherm) / base (pipe vesicles); **flow** = many lobes; **flow-field** = many flows from a vent; architecture **braided / ponded / tabular** (emplacement rate, substrate, viscosity and strain); morphologies are a **continuum**.
> - **Sahyadri Group:** Kalsubai (Salher, Nashik, Malshej) → Lonavala (Indrayani, Bushe) → Wai (Poladpur, Purandargarh, Mahabaleshwar); 66.4 → 65.4 Ma; **GPB markers** Thalghat M1, Ghoti M2, Giravali M3 (Manchar M3a below, Bhimashankar Member between), Rajgad M4.
> - **Stops:** NL1 Rajgurunagar Ghat (lobe anatomy, M3a), NL2 Ane Ghat (braided lobes; M3; natural arch on an entrenched meander), NL3 Mandhol Dam (rejuvenated channel, fracture-controlled drainage, dykes), NL4 Kukdi bed near Nighoj (potholes, cascade, Ghod Fault).

## Practice
1. Sketch a lava lobe and label crust, core and base with two features of each. Why does crystallinity peak below the middle?
2. A flow-field is "braided". Name the three controls and say how a higher emplacement rate would change the architecture.
3. Give the order of the nine Sahyadri formations and the position of the four GPB markers.
4. The Kalsubai–Lonavala boundary is hard to find where the Bhimashankar Member is absent. What do you use?
5. From the guide's ages and the $>3000$ m thickness estimate the mean accumulation rate. What does a $20$ m flow thickness imply for the recurrence of flows?
6. Why would a river parallel to a strike-slip fault show potholes and a cascade (NL4)?

> [!example]- Solutions
> 1. Crust: tachylitic rind, vesicles (flow-top breccia); core: massive, columnar jointing; base: pipe vesicles. Cooling from top and base meets at the medial isotherm, which cools last, so crystals grow longest there.
> 2. Emplacement rate, substrate topography/structure, viscosity and internal strain; a high rate favours tabular sheets that bury topography, a low rate favours braided or ponded lobes controlled by the substrate.
> 3. Salher, Nashik, Malshej (Kalsubai); Indrayani, Bushe (Lonavala); Poladpur, Purandargarh, Mahabaleshwar (Wai); GPB M1 (Thalghat), M2 (Ghoti), M3 (Giravali; M3a Manchar below, Bhimashankar Member between), M4 (Rajgad).
> 4. The Giravali GPB (M3).
> 5. $3000$ m/$1.0$ Myr $=3$ mm/yr; a $20$ m flow every $20/3=6.7$ kyr on average.
> 6. The fault-controlled channel is straight and rigid; rejuvenation (base-level fall) makes it incise a jointed basalt, and joint-bounded blocks plucked by eddies make potholes and cascades (abrasion and quarrying, [[LS23 Bedrock Channels and River Incision]]).
