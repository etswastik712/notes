# November 16-20 (2026-11-18)

Slide DG34 was read by OCR and is heavily garbled (many symbols lost). Statements below are reconstructed from the legible fragments plus the surrounding lectures; where I filled a gap it says so. Check against the PDF.

## Covered

### Thm 20.28 (ideals <-> normal subgroups)
- Let $G$ be a connected Lie group and $H\subset G$ a connected Lie subgroup, with Lie algebras $\mathfrak h\subset\mathfrak g$. Then $H$ is normal in $G$ iff $\mathfrak h$ is an ideal in $\mathfrak g$ (a subspace $L$ with $[X,Y]\in L$ for all $X\in\mathfrak g$, $Y\in L$).
- Remark on the slide: $\exp:\mathfrak g\to G$ need not be surjective, so the proof works with products of exponentials and the Lemma below.

### Lemma 20.23 (normality tested on exponentials)
- $G$ connected Lie group, $H$ connected Lie subgroup. Then $H$ is normal in $G$ iff $\exp(X)\exp(Y)\exp(-X)\in H$ for all $X\in\mathfrak g$, $Y\in\mathfrak h$.
- Proof, ($\Rightarrow$): $\exp(Y)\in H$ and $H$ is normal, so the conjugate lies in $H$.
- Proof, ($\Leftarrow$) (garbled; reading): choose an open $V\ni0$ in $\mathfrak g$ on which $\exp$ is a diffeomorphism onto $U=\exp(V)$; so each $g\in U$ is $\exp X$ with $X\in V$, and $gHg^{-1}\subset H$ by hypothesis (as $H$ is generated by $\exp(\mathfrak h)$). A general $g\in G$ is a product $g_1\cdots g_k$ of elements of $U$ (a neighbourhood of $e$ generates the connected group $G$); induction on $k$ gives $gHg^{-1}\subset H$.

### Adjoint representation (recalled)
- $C_g(h)=ghg^{-1}$ is a Lie group homomorphism $G\to G$; $\mathrm{Ad}(g)=(C_g)_*:\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak g$. $\mathrm{Ad}:G\to GL(\mathfrak g)$ is a Lie group homomorphism, the adjoint representation of $G$.
- $\mathrm{Ad}_*=\mathrm{ad}$ where $\mathrm{ad}(X)Y=[X,Y]$; $\mathrm{ad}:\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak g)$ is the adjoint representation of $\mathfrak g$.
- Prop (stated on the slide): for a Lie subgroup $H\subset G$, $\mathfrak h=\{X\in\mathfrak g:\exp(tX)\in H\ \forall t\in\mathbb R\}$.
- Naturality of $\exp$ applied to $\mathrm{Ad}$ (reading of the proof): $\mathrm{Ad}(\exp X)=\exp(\mathrm{ad}X)=\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{(\mathrm{ad}X)^k}{k!}$, where the right-hand $\exp$ is the matrix exponential on $GL(\mathfrak g)$.

### Proof of Thm 20.28
- ($\Leftarrow$) Suppose $\mathfrak h$ is an ideal. For $X\in\mathfrak g$, $Y\in\mathfrak h$: $(\mathrm{ad}X)^kY\in\mathfrak h$ for all $k$ (induction), so $\mathrm{Ad}(\exp X)Y=\sum_k\frac{(\mathrm{ad}X)^kY}{k!}\in\mathfrak h$ ($\mathfrak h$ is a closed subspace). Hence $\exp(X)\exp(Y)\exp(-X)=\exp(\mathrm{Ad}(\exp X)Y)\in\exp(\mathfrak h)\subset H$ (naturality of $\exp$ for $C_{\exp X}$). By Lemma 20.23, $H$ is normal.
- ($\Rightarrow$) Suppose $H$ is normal. For $X\in\mathfrak g$, $Y\in\mathfrak h$, $s,t\in\mathbb R$: $\exp(X)\exp(tY)\exp(-X)=\exp(t\,\mathrm{Ad}(\exp X)Y)\in H$ for all $t$. By the Prop above, $\mathrm{Ad}(\exp X)Y\in\mathfrak h$. Replace $X$ by $sX$: $\mathrm{Ad}(\exp sX)Y\in\mathfrak h$ for all $s$, and differentiating at $s=0$ (a curve in a subspace has derivative in the subspace) gives $\mathrm{ad}(X)Y=[X,Y]\in\mathfrak h$. So $\mathfrak h$ is an ideal.

### Example: $\exp$ need not be surjective
- $\exp:\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb R)\to SL(2,\mathbb R)$ is not surjective. The slide argues with a matrix $A\in SL(2,\mathbb R)$ (the entries are illegible in the OCR) assumed to equal $e^B$ with $B\in\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb R)$, looks at the eigenvalues of $B$ (trace zero, so $\pm\lambda$) and gets a contradiction with $\det=1$/the eigenvalues of $A$. I could not recover the exact matrix; the usual choice is $A=\begin{pmatrix}-1&1\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}$ (not diagonalizable, eigenvalue $-1$ twice, while $e^B$ with $\pm\lambda$, $\lambda\ne0$ would be diagonalizable); treat this as my reconstruction.

### Question on the slide (HW9-style): $G$ connected, $X,Y\in\mathfrak g$
- $[X,Y]=0$ iff $\exp(sX)\exp(tY)=\exp(tY)\exp(sX)$ for all $s,t$. ($\Rightarrow$) $\mathrm{Ad}(\exp sX)Y=e^{s\,\mathrm{ad}X}Y=Y$, so $\exp(sX)\exp(tY)\exp(-sX)=\exp(tY)$. ($\Leftarrow$) commuting gives $\mathrm{Ad}(\exp sX)Y=Y$ for all $s$; differentiate at $s=0$ to get $[X,Y]=0$.
- $G$ abelian iff $\mathfrak g$ abelian. ($\Rightarrow$) $C_g=\mathrm{id}$, so $\mathrm{Ad}$ is constant $=I$, $\mathrm{ad}=\mathrm{Ad}_*=0$. ($\Leftarrow$) $[X,Y]=0$ for all $X,Y$ gives $\mathrm{ad}=\mathrm{Ad}_*=0$, so (as $G$ is connected) $\mathrm{Ad}$ is the constant map $I$; then $(C_g)_*=\mathrm{id}$ for every $g$, so $C_g=\mathrm{id}_G$ (two homomorphisms from a connected group with equal differentials agree, HW10 Q1), i.e. $gh=hg$.
