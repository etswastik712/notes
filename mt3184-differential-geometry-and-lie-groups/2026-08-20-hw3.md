# HW3 (2026-08-20)

## Covered
Homework 3 relates to lectures up to August 20th. $M$ is an $m$-dimensional and $N$ an $n$-dimensional smooth manifold. Notation: $\alpha'(s)=\alpha_*\big(\tfrac{d}{dt}\big|_s\big)$. (The text of the PDF was extracted by OCR; the formulas below were checked for consistency, e.g. $\alpha(t)$ and $p$ lie on $S^2$.)

Problems and what each practises:
1. Let $\alpha:(-\tfrac1{\sqrt5},\tfrac1{\sqrt5})\to S^2\subset\mathbb R^3$, $\alpha(t)=(t,\,2t,\,\sqrt{1-5t^2})$; $p=(\tfrac14,\tfrac12,\tfrac{\sqrt{11}}4)=\alpha(\tfrac14)$; $F:S^2\to S^2$, $F(x,y,z)=\big(\tfrac12(x-\sqrt3\,y),\ \tfrac12(\sqrt3\,x+y),\ z\big)$ (a rotation about the $z$-axis); $\mathcal A=\{(U_i^\pm,\varphi_i^\pm)\}_{i=1}^3$ the atlas of $S^2$ given by projection maps.
   - (a) Fix the basis of $T_pS^2$ from the chart $(U_3^+,\varphi_3^+)$ and compute $\alpha'(\tfrac14)$ in it. Practises: velocity of a curve in chart coordinates.
   - (b) Compute $DF(p)(\alpha'(\tfrac14))$ in the basis from $(U_3^+,\varphi_3^+)$, for both domain and codomain. Practises: the differential of a smooth map as a Jacobian in local coordinates (compare [[mt3184-differential-geometry-and-lie-groups/2026-08-14-hw2]] Q4).
   - (c) Express $\alpha'(\tfrac14)$ in the basis from $(U_2^+,\varphi_2^+)$ by applying the change-of-basis formula for tangent vectors. Practises: change of basis between charts (compare [[mt3184-differential-geometry-and-lie-groups/2026-08-14-hw2]] Q3).
2. Use the smooth manifold construction lemma (Lemma 1.35) to prove that $TM$ is a smooth manifold and that the projection $\pi:TM\to M$ is smooth. Practises: the Chart Lemma and the charts $\tilde\varphi$ on $TM$ (Prop 3.18, see [[mt3184-differential-geometry-and-lie-groups/2026-08-20-august-17-21]]).
3. A vector field on $M$ is a smooth map $v:M\to TM$ with $\pi\circ v=\mathrm{id}_M$. Let $\{(U,\varphi),(V,\psi)\}$ be the stereographic atlas of $S^2$, $U=S^2\setminus(0,0,1)$, $V=S^2\setminus(0,0,-1)$. Construct a vector field on $S^2$ vanishing at exactly one point:
   - (a) Give a non-vanishing vector field $X$ on $\mathbb R^2$ that becomes a constant vector field on identifying $T_p\mathbb R^2$ with $\mathbb R^2$ in the obvious way, for all $p$. Practises: vector fields on open subsets of $\mathbb R^n$.
   - (b) Let $Y:=(\varphi^{-1})_*(X)$, a smooth vector field on $U$. Express $Y$ in the coordinates given by $\psi$ on $U\cap V$. Practises: pushing a field forward by a diffeomorphism and changing coordinates ($F$-related fields, Prop 8.19 in [[mt3184-differential-geometry-and-lie-groups/2026-08-27-august-24-28]]).
   - (c) Show that extending $Y$ to all of $S^2$ by $Y_{(0,0,1)}=0$ gives a smooth vector field with exactly one zero. Hint: check that the coordinate functions of $Y$ with respect to the basis from $\psi$ have a smooth extension by $0$ at $(0,0,1)$. Practises: smoothness of a vector field via its component functions (Prop 8.14); contrast with the Hairy Ball Theorem, which forbids a nowhere-vanishing field on $S^2$.
