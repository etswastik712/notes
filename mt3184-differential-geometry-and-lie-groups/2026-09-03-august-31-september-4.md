**Consequences of the rank theorem (DG12)**
- Global Rank Theorem (restated): $F: M\to N$ smooth of constant rank. $F$ surjective ⇒ submersion; $F$ injective ⇒ immersion; $F$ bijective ⇒ diffeomorphism.
- A submersion is an open map. Proof outline: by the rank theorem, around each $p$ there are charts in which $F(x^1,\dots,x^m)=(x^1,\dots,x^n)$; the projection is an open map, so $F(U)$ is open. Hence $F(M)$ is an open submanifold of $N$ if $F$ is a submersion (the slide leaves the finish as an exercise).
- An injective immersion need not have an embedded image. Example: $F:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R^2$ with a figure-eight image (sine-type curve). A second example: $F:\mathbb R\to T^2=S^1\times S^1$, $t\mapsto(e^{it},e^{i\alpha t})$ with $\alpha\in\mathbb R\setminus\mathbb Q$, is an injective immersion (the image is dense in $T^2$).
- Definition: an immersed submanifold of $M$ is a subset $S\subset M$ with a topology (not necessarily the subspace topology) and a smooth structure such that the inclusion $S\hookrightarrow M$ is a smooth immersion.
- Prop 5.18: let $F: S\to M$ be an injective immersion. Then $F(S)$ with the topology "$U\subset F(S)$ open iff $F^{-1}(U)$ open in $S$" and the atlas induced from $S$ is an immersed submanifold of $M$, and $F: S\to F(S)$ is a diffeomorphism.
- HW (set in class): immersed submanifolds are locally embedded, i.e. if $F: S\to M$ is a smooth immersion then each $p\in S$ has an open $U\subset S$ with $F(U)$ a submanifold.

**Lie group homomorphisms and Lie subgroups (DG12, DG13)**
- A Lie group homomorphism has constant rank (Thm 7.5), so by the global rank theorem a smooth bijective homomorphism is an isomorphism.
- Definition: a Lie subgroup $H$ of $G$ is a subgroup that is an immersed submanifold and a Lie group (the group operations of $H$ are smooth, i.e. $m_H: H\times H\to H$ and $i_H: H\to H$ are smooth).
- Prop 7.17: if $F: G\to H$ is an injective Lie group homomorphism, then $F(G)$ is a Lie subgroup of $H$ and $F: G\to F(G)$ is a Lie group isomorphism. Proof: $F$ is injective of constant rank, so an immersion; hence $F(G)$ is an immersed submanifold; the topology on $F(G)$ makes it diffeomorphic to $G$, a Lie group.
- Example: $F:\mathbb R\to T^2$, $t\mapsto(e^{2\pi it},e^{2\pi i\alpha t})$, so $F(\mathbb R)$ is a Lie subgroup of $T^2$ for irrational $\alpha$.
- $GL(n,\mathbb R)$ is an open Lie subgroup of $M(n,\mathbb R)$ (viewed under multiplication it sits in $GL(n,\mathbb R)$ itself).
- Lemma 7.12: let $H$ be a Lie subgroup of $G$. Then $H$ is open ⇒ $H$ is closed. Proof: $H$ open ⇒ each coset $gH$ is open (as $L_g$ is a diffeomorphism) ⇒ $G\setminus H=\bigcup_{g\notin H}gH$ is open ⇒ $H$ closed. If $H$ is an open Lie subgroup of $G$, it is a union of connected components of $G$.
- Subgroup generated by $S\subset G$, written $\langle S\rangle$: the smallest subgroup of $G$ containing $S$. If $S=S^{-1}$ and $S_k=\{s_1\cdots s_k\}$, then $\langle S\rangle=\bigcup_k S_k$.
- Prop 7.14: let $W$ be a neighbourhood of $e$ in $G$ with $W=W^{-1}$ (the slide is partly illegible). Then (a) if $W$ is open, $\langle W\rangle$ is open; (b) if $W$ is connected, $\langle W\rangle$ is connected; (c) if $G$ is connected, $\langle W\rangle=G$. Proof of (a): $W^k$ is open because $W^k=\bigcup_{w}L_{w}(W^{k-1})$ with each $L_w$ a diffeomorphism. Proof of (b): $W^k$ is a continuous image of $W\times\dots\times W$, which is connected; the $W^k$ all contain $e$, so their union is connected. (c) is set as homework.
- Prop 7.15: let $G_0$ be the identity component of $G$. Then $G_0$ is a normal subgroup of $G$ and it is the only connected open subgroup; every connected component of $G$ is a coset of $G_0$ (since $L_g$ and $L_{g^{-1}}$ are diffeomorphisms, $gG_0$ is the component containing $g$).
- Prop 7.16: let $F: G\to H$ be a Lie group homomorphism. Then $\ker F$ is an embedded Lie subgroup of $G$ of dimension $\dim G-\mathrm{rank}\,dF$. Proof: $F$ has constant rank, so $F^{-1}(e)$ is a submanifold of dimension $\dim G-\mathrm{rank}\,F$. Example: $\det: GL(n,\mathbb R)\to\mathbb R^*$ is a surjective Lie group homomorphism of rank 1, so $SL(n,\mathbb R)$ is an embedded Lie subgroup of dimension $n^2-1$.
- Immersed submanifold vs closed vs embedded: Thm 7.21: a Lie subgroup $H\le G$ is closed iff it is embedded. Thm 20.12 (Closed Subgroup Theorem): a subgroup $H\le G$ is closed iff it is an embedded Lie subgroup (proof starts on DG14).
