**Properties of exp (DG27, DG28)**
- Prop 20.10 (garbled): for $X,Y\in\mathfrak g$ there are $\varepsilon>0$ and a smooth map $\zeta:(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\to\mathfrak g$ such that $\exp(tX)\exp(tY)=\exp\big(t(X+Y)+t^2\zeta(t)\big)$ for $|t|<\varepsilon$. Proof idea: $\exp$ is a diffeomorphism from a neighbourhood of $0$ in $\mathfrak g$ onto a neighbourhood of $e$, so $\varphi(t)=\exp^{-1}(\exp(tX)\exp(tY))$ is smooth near $0$; $\varphi(0)=0$ and $\varphi'(0)=d\exp_0^{-1}\big(dm_{(e,e)}(X,Y)\big)=X+Y$ (since $d\exp_0=\mathrm{id}$ and $dm_{(e,e)}(X,Y)=X+Y$); Taylor's theorem then gives the form above.
- Corollary 20.11: $\exp(X+Y)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\big(\exp(X/n)\exp(Y/n)\big)^n$. Proof: for $n$ large, apply 20.10 with $t=1/n$ to get $\exp(X/n)\exp(Y/n)=\exp\big((X+Y)/n+\zeta_n/n^2\big)$; take $n$-th powers (using $(\exp Z)^n=\exp(nZ)$), giving $\exp(X+Y+\zeta_n/n)$, and let $n\to\infty$ by continuity of $\exp$.
- Naturality (DG28): for a Lie group homomorphism $\Phi:G\to H$, $\Phi\circ\exp_G=\exp_H\circ\Phi_*$. Proof: $\Phi$ sends integral curves of the left-invariant field $X$ to integral curves of the left-invariant field $\Phi_*X$ (they are $\Phi$-related), and $t\mapsto\Phi(\exp tX)$ is a one-parameter subgroup of $H$ with initial velocity $\Phi_*X$, so it equals $\exp(t\Phi_*X)$.
- Prop (DG28): if $H$ is a Lie subgroup of $G$ with Lie algebra $\mathfrak h\subset\mathfrak g$ (identified with $T_eH$), then $\mathfrak h=\{X\in\mathfrak g:\exp(tX)\in H\ \forall t\in\mathbb R\}$. Proof ($\subseteq$): for $X\in\mathfrak h$, $t\mapsto\exp(tX)$ is the one-parameter subgroup of $H$ generated by $X$, hence lies in $H$ (Prop 20.3). ($\supseteq$): if $\exp(tX)\in H$ for all $t$, then $\gamma(t)=\exp(tX)$ is a smooth map into $G$ with image in $H$; $H$ is weakly embedded (below), so $\gamma:\mathbb R\to H$ is smooth, hence a Lie group homomorphism into $H$ with $\gamma'(0)=X\in T_eH$. (This last step is only partly legible.)

**Smooth maps into embedded submanifolds (DG28)**
- Lemma: let $S\subset M$ be an embedded submanifold and $F:N\to M$ smooth with $F(N)\subset S$. Then $F:N\to S$ is smooth. Proof: at $p\in N$ take a slice chart $(V,\psi)$ of $S$ around $F(p)$ and a chart $(U,\varphi)$ of $N$ at $p$ with $F(U)\subset V$; then the last $n-k$ coordinates of $\psi\circ F\circ\varphi^{-1}$ vanish and the first $k$ are smooth.
- Counterexample for immersed submanifolds: the figure-eight curve $\alpha(t)=(\sin 2t,\sin t)$ (reconstructed from a garbled slide). Let $S=\mathrm{Im}\,\alpha$ as an immersed submanifold; the map $F:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R^2$ with image in $S$ (a reparametrisation of the curve through $[0,2\pi)$) is smooth into $\mathbb R^2$ but not smooth into $S$.
- Definition: an immersed submanifold $S\subset M$ is **weakly embedded** if for every smooth $F:N\to M$ with $F(N)\subset S$, the map $F:N\to S$ is smooth. So embedded ⇒ weakly embedded.
- Lie subgroups are weakly embedded: they are integral manifolds of the involutive distribution obtained by left-translating $T_eH$ (proved in [[mt3184-differential-geometry-and-lie-groups/2026-11-06-november-2-6]], Thm 19.25 and its corollary).

**Closed Subgroup Theorem (Thm 20.12; DG27, DG28)**
- Statement: let $G$ be a Lie group and $H\le G$ a subgroup. Then $H$ is a closed subset of $G$ iff $H$ is an embedded Lie subgroup.
- Proof ($\Rightarrow$): let $H$ be closed. Define $\mathfrak h=\{X\in\mathfrak g:\exp(tX)\in H\ \forall t\in\mathbb R\}$.
  1. *Claim: $\mathfrak h$ is a vector subspace.* If $X\in\mathfrak h$ then $tX\in\mathfrak h$ for all $t\in\mathbb R$ (by definition). If $X,Y\in\mathfrak h$ then $\exp(t(X+Y))=\lim_n\big(\exp(tX/n)\exp(tY/n)\big)^n$ (Cor 20.11), each term is in $H$, and $H$ is closed, so $\exp(t(X+Y))\in H$.
  2. *Claim: there is a neighbourhood $U$ of $0$ in $\mathfrak g$ on which $\exp$ is a diffeomorphism and $\exp(U)\cap H=\exp(U\cap\mathfrak h)$.* (Proof below.)
  3. *Slice charts.* Granting the claim, choose a linear isomorphism $\mathfrak g\to\mathbb R^n$ sending $\mathfrak h$ to $\mathbb R^k\times\{0\}$. Then $\varphi=(\exp|_U)^{-1}$ followed by this isomorphism is a chart for $G$ at $e$ in which $H\cap\exp(U)$ is $\{x^{k+1}=\dots=x^n=0\}$: a slice chart of $H$ at $e$. For $h\in H$, left translation $L_h$ is a diffeomorphism of $G$ preserving $H$ ($H$ is a subgroup), so $L_h\circ\exp$ gives a slice chart at $h$. Hence $H$ is an embedded submanifold, and a Lie subgroup.
- Proof of claim 2: $\exp(U\cap\mathfrak h)\subseteq\exp(U)\cap H$ is clear. For the converse suppose no such $U$ exists. Choose a complementary subspace $S$ with $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak h\oplus S$ and define $\Phi:\mathfrak h\oplus S\to G$, $\Phi(x,y)=\exp(x)\exp(y)$. Then $d\Phi_0=\mathrm{id}$, so $\Phi$ is a diffeomorphism near $0$; take neighbourhoods $U_i\downarrow 0$ with $\exp$ and $\Phi$ diffeomorphisms on them (slide: after discarding initial terms). By assumption there are $h_i\in\exp(U_i)\cap H$ not in $\exp(U_i\cap\mathfrak h)$; write $h_i=\exp(z_i)=\Phi(x_i,y_i)=\exp(x_i)\exp(y_i)$ with $x_i\in\mathfrak h$, $y_i\in S$. Since $\exp(x_i)\in H$, $\exp(y_i)=\exp(x_i)^{-1}h_i\in H$. Also $y_i\neq0$ (otherwise $h_i=\exp(x_i)$ and injectivity of $\exp$ on $U_i$ gives $z_i=x_i\in\mathfrak h$, a contradiction), and $y_i\to0$. Fix an inner product on $\mathfrak g$ with norm $\|\cdot\|$; the unit vectors $y_i/\|y_i\|$ lie on the unit sphere of $S$, so by compactness a subsequence converges to some $Y\in S$ with $\|Y\|=1$; in particular $Y\ne0$. For $t\in\mathbb R$ choose integers $n_i$ with $n_i\|y_i\|\to t$ in the sense $n_i y_i\to tY$ (take $n_i=\lfloor t/\|y_i\|\rfloor$). Then $\exp(y_i)^{n_i}=\exp(n_iy_i)\in H$ and $\exp(n_iy_i)\to\exp(tY)$; $H$ is closed, so $\exp(tY)\in H$ for all $t$. Thus $Y\in\mathfrak h\cap S=\{0\}$, a contradiction.
- Proof ($\Leftarrow$): an embedded Lie subgroup is closed. Let $h_i\in H$ with $h_i\to g\in G$. Take a slice chart domain $U$ of $H$ at $e$ and a smaller open $W\ni e$ with $\overline W\subset U$ compact (slide: "there exists $W$ open with $\overline W\subset U$"); choose an open $V\ni e$ with $V^{-1}V\subset W$ (using continuity of $(g,h)\mapsto g^{-1}h$). For large $i,j$, $h_i,h_j\in gV$, so $h_i^{-1}h_j\in V^{-1}g^{-1}gV\subset W$, i.e. $h_i^{-1}h_j\in H\cap\overline W$. $H\cap U$ is closed in $U$ (slice), so $H\cap\overline W$ is closed; fixing $i$ and letting $j\to\infty$, $h_i^{-1}h_j\to h_i^{-1}g\in\overline W\cap H$. Hence $g=h_i(h_i^{-1}g)\in H$, so $H$ is closed. (The final part is reconstructed from a garbled slide; check the PDF.)
