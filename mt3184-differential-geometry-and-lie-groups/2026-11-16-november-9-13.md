## Covered

### DG31: infinitesimal generator of an action; end of Thm 19.26
- Thm 19.26 (recalled as the opening outline): for a Lie subalgebra $\mathfrak h\subset\mathfrak g$ there is a unique connected Lie subgroup $H\subset G$ with $\mathrm{Lie}(H)=\mathfrak h$. Outline: $D_g=\{X_g: X\in\mathfrak h\}$ is a left-invariant involutive distribution; by Frobenius it has a foliation; the leaf through $e$ is $H$. This gives a bijection between connected Lie subgroups and Lie subalgebras.
- A complete vector field $V$ has a global flow $\theta:\mathbb R\times M\to M$, i.e. an $\mathbb R$-action, $\theta(t,p)=\gamma_p(t)$ with $\gamma_p$ the integral curve of $V$ starting at $p$. Its infinitesimal generator is $V$: $\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\big|_{0}\theta(t,p)=V_p$.
- Goal of the lecture: if $G$ is a simply connected Lie group with Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$ and $\hat\theta:\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak X(M)$ is a Lie algebra homomorphism such that every $\hat\theta(X)$ is a complete vector field, then there is a unique right $G$-action on $M$ whose infinitesimal generator is $\hat\theta$.
- Left-invariant fields on $G$: the flow of $X\in\mathrm{Lie}(G)$ through $p$ is $t\mapsto p\exp(tX)$, because $L_p$ takes integral curves of $X$ starting at $e$ to integral curves of $X$ starting at $p$ (as $X$ is left-invariant).
- **Infinitesimal generator of a Lie group action.** Let $\theta:M\times G\to M$, $(p,g)\mapsto p\cdot g$, be a right action. For each $X\in\mathrm{Lie}(G)$, $\varphi_t(p)=p\cdot\exp(tX)$ is an $\mathbb R$-action (a flow) on $M$ (from $\exp((s+t)X)=\exp(sX)\exp(tX)$). Let $\hat\theta(X)$ be its infinitesimal generator: $\hat\theta(X)_p=\frac{d}{dt}\big|_{0}\,p\cdot\exp(tX)$. This defines $\hat\theta:\mathrm{Lie}(G)\to\mathfrak X(M)$.
- **Thm 20.15.** $\hat\theta:\mathrm{Lie}(G)\to\mathfrak X(M)$ is a Lie algebra homomorphism. Proof as on the slide: for $p\in M$ let $\theta^{(p)}:G\to M$, $g\mapsto p\cdot g$ be the orbit map. For all $X\in\mathrm{Lie}(G)$ and $p\in M$, $X$ and $\hat\theta(X)$ are $\theta^{(p)}$-related, i.e. $d\theta^{(p)}_g(X_g)=\hat\theta(X)_{p\cdot g}$ (the check is a derivative of $t\mapsto p\cdot g\exp(tX)$ at $t=0$). Then $\hat\theta$ is linear; since $X,\hat\theta(X)$ and $Y,\hat\theta(Y)$ are $\theta^{(p)}$-related, so are $[X,Y]$ and $[\hat\theta X,\hat\theta Y]$. But $[X,Y]$ and $\hat\theta([X,Y])$ are also $\theta^{(p)}$-related, so $\hat\theta([X,Y])=[\hat\theta X,\hat\theta Y]$ at every point $p\cdot g$, hence everywhere.
- More generally, any Lie algebra homomorphism $\hat\theta:\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak X(M)$ is called a $\mathfrak g$-action on $M$. It is **complete** if $\hat\theta(X)$ is a complete vector field for every $X\in\mathfrak g$.
- **Thm 20.16 (Fundamental Theorem on Lie Algebra Actions).** Let $M$ be a smooth manifold and $G$ a simply connected Lie group with Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$. Suppose $\hat\theta:\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak X(M)$ is a complete $\mathfrak g$-action on $M$. Then there is a unique smooth right $G$-action on $M$ whose infinitesimal generator is $\hat\theta$.

### DG32: universal cover; the Lie correspondence
- A space $X$ is **simply connected** if it is connected and every loop is homotopic to a constant (slide: for a loop $\alpha:S^1\to X$ there is a homotopy $H:S^1\times[0,1]\to X$ with $H_0=\alpha$ and $H_1$ constant).
- Universal cover: for a connected Lie group $G$ there is a simply connected Lie group $\widetilde G$ and a surjective Lie group homomorphism $p:\widetilde G\to G$ such that every $g\in G$ has an open neighbourhood $U$ with $p^{-1}(U)=\bigsqcup_i V_i$ (disjoint open sets) and $p|_{V_i}:V_i\to U$ a diffeomorphism. If $G$ is already simply connected, any such $p$ is a diffeomorphism. (The slide's wording of the hypothesis "connected" is garbled; I have taken it from the usual statement.)
- **Lie correspondence (as stated):** $S:\{\text{simply connected Lie groups}\}\to\{\text{finite-dimensional Lie algebras}\}$, $G\mapsto\mathrm{Lie}(G)$, is a bijection (on isomorphism classes). $S$ is well defined: $\mathrm{Lie}(G)$ is finite-dimensional, and $G_1\cong G_2\Rightarrow\mathrm{Lie}(G_1)\cong\mathrm{Lie}(G_2)$ (via $\beta\circ\alpha$-type compositions of $\Phi_*$).
- The slide notes that simple connectivity is needed: $\mathbb R$ and $S^1$ have isomorphic Lie algebras but are not isomorphic as Lie groups (my reading of the garbled lines); $S^1$ is not simply connected.
- **Thm 20.19.** Let $G$ be a simply connected Lie group, $H$ a Lie group, and $\varphi:\mathrm{Lie}(G)\to\mathrm{Lie}(H)$ a Lie algebra homomorphism. Then there is a unique Lie group homomorphism $\Phi:G\to H$ with $\Phi_*=\varphi$.
  - Proof idea (slide): each $\varphi(X)$ is left-invariant on $H$, hence complete, so $\varphi:\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak X(H)$ is a complete $\mathfrak g$-action. Since $G$ is simply connected, Thm 20.16 gives a right action $\theta:H\times G\to H$ with infinitesimal generator $\varphi$. Define $\Phi(g)=\theta(e,g)=e\cdot g$.
  - Claim: $\Phi$ is a homomorphism. For $h\in H$, $X$ and $\varphi(X)$ are $\theta^{(h)}$-related ($\theta^{(h)}(g)=h\cdot g$). So $\theta^{(h)}$ takes integral curves of $X$ to integral curves of $\varphi(X)$. Since $t\mapsto h\exp(t\varphi(X))$ is the integral curve of the left-invariant field $\varphi(X)$ starting at $h$, one gets $h\cdot\exp(tX)=h\exp(t\varphi(X))$. For $h=e$: $\Phi(\exp tX)=\exp(t\varphi(X))$. For general $g=\exp X_1\cdots\exp X_n$ (such products give all of $G$ since $\exp(U)$ generates the connected group $G$, $U$ a neighbourhood of $0$ on which $\exp$ is a diffeomorphism), induction with the action property gives $h\cdot g=h\,\Phi(g)$, hence $\Phi(g_1g_2)=\Phi(g_1)\Phi(g_2)$. Also $\Phi_*=\varphi$ because $\frac{d}{dt}\big|_0\Phi(\exp tX)=\varphi(X)_e$.
  - Uniqueness: if $\Psi_*=\varphi$ too, $\Phi=\Psi$ (HW10 Q1: homomorphisms from a connected group with the same differential agree; see [[mt3184-differential-geometry-and-lie-groups/2026-11-06-hw10]]).
- **Cor 20.20.** $S$ is injective: if $G,H$ are simply connected and $\mathrm{Lie}(G)\cong\mathrm{Lie}(H)$ then $G\cong H$. Proof: an isomorphism $\varphi:\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak h$ gives $\Phi:G\to H$ with $\Phi_*=\varphi$ and $\Psi:H\to G$ with $\Psi_*=\varphi^{-1}$. Then $(\Psi\circ\Phi)_*=\mathrm{id}_{\mathfrak g}$, and $\mathrm{id}_G$ also induces $\mathrm{id}_{\mathfrak g}$, so by uniqueness $\Psi\circ\Phi=\mathrm{id}_G$; likewise $\Phi\circ\Psi=\mathrm{id}_H$.
- **Thm 20.21.** $S$ is surjective: for every finite-dimensional Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$ there is a simply connected Lie group $G$ with $\mathrm{Lie}(G)\cong\mathfrak g$. Proof starts here (continued in DG33): by Ado's Theorem $\mathfrak g$ is isomorphic to a subalgebra $\mathfrak g_0\subset\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb R)$.

### DG33: finishing the correspondence; normal subgroups; adjoint representation
- Statement (Lie correspondence): there is a bijection between isomorphism classes of simply connected Lie groups and isomorphism classes of finite-dimensional Lie algebras, given by $G\mapsto\mathrm{Lie}(G)$.
- Proof of surjectivity (finished): by Thm 19.26 there is a connected Lie subgroup $G_0\subset GL(n,\mathbb R)$ with $\mathrm{Lie}(G_0)=\mathfrak g_0$. Let $p:\widetilde G_0\to G_0$ be the universal cover. $\widetilde G_0$ is a simply connected Lie group, and since $p$ is a local diffeomorphism at $e$, $dp_e$ is an isomorphism, so $\mathrm{Lie}(\widetilde G_0)\cong\mathrm{Lie}(G_0)=\mathfrak g_0\cong\mathfrak g$.
- Outline of the existence of $\Phi$ (Thm 20.19) is repeated on the slide, including: use the local diffeomorphism of $\exp$ at $0$ and the fact that a neighbourhood of $e$ generates $G$ to get $\Phi(g\exp(tX))=\Phi(g)\exp(t\varphi(X))$ for all $g$.
- **Thm 21.26.** If $K\subset G$ is a closed normal subgroup, then $G/K$ is a Lie group (slide text is garbled; this is my reading).
- **Thm 21.32** (my reading of garbled text). Let $\mathfrak g$ be a finite-dimensional Lie algebra and $\widetilde G$ the simply connected Lie group with $\mathrm{Lie}(\widetilde G)=\mathfrak g$. Then $G$ is a connected Lie group with Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$ iff $G\cong\widetilde G/\Gamma$, where $\Gamma$ is a discrete normal subgroup of $\widetilde G$.
- If $\varphi:\mathfrak g_1\to\mathfrak g_2$ is a Lie algebra homomorphism then $\ker\varphi$ is an ideal: if $\varphi(Y)=0$ then $\varphi([X,Y])=[\varphi X,\varphi Y]=0$.
- **Ideal:** a subspace $L\subset\mathfrak g$ with $[X,Y]\in L$ for all $X\in\mathfrak g$, $Y\in L$. Ideals are kernels of Lie algebra homomorphisms, just as normal subgroups are kernels of Lie group homomorphisms.
- **Thm 20.28.** Let $G$ be a connected Lie group and $H\subset G$ a connected Lie subgroup. Then $H$ is normal in $G$ iff $\mathrm{Lie}(H)$ is an ideal in $\mathrm{Lie}(G)$.
- **Adjoint representation.** For $g\in G$ let $C_g:G\to G$, $C_g(h)=ghg^{-1}$, a Lie group homomorphism. Define $\mathrm{Ad}(g)=(C_g)_*:\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak g$, a Lie algebra homomorphism (in fact an automorphism). Then $\mathrm{Ad}:G\to GL(\mathfrak g)$ is a Lie group homomorphism, the **adjoint representation of $G$**.
- **Prop 20.24.** $\mathrm{Ad}:G\to GL(\mathfrak g)$ is a Lie group homomorphism. Proof: $C_{g_1g_2}=C_{g_1}\circ C_{g_2}$, so $\mathrm{Ad}(g_1g_2)=\mathrm{Ad}(g_1)\mathrm{Ad}(g_2)$; smoothness of $\mathrm{Ad}$ is marked "later" on the slide.
- Let $\mathrm{Ad}_*:\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak g)$ be the induced Lie algebra homomorphism. Define $\mathrm{ad}:\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak g)$ by $\mathrm{ad}(X)Y=[X,Y]$. That $\mathrm{ad}$ is a Lie algebra homomorphism is homework (HW11 Q2). The map $\mathrm{ad}$ is the **adjoint representation of $\mathfrak g$**.
- **Thm 20.27.** $\mathrm{Ad}_*=\mathrm{ad}$, i.e. $\frac{d}{dt}\big|_{0}\mathrm{Ad}(\exp tX)Y=[X,Y]$. Proof sketch (legible part): $\mathrm{Ad}(\exp tX)=d(C_{\exp tX})=d(R_{\exp(-tX)})\circ d(L_{\exp tX})$; $Y$ is left-invariant so $L$ leaves it unchanged. The flow of the left-invariant field $X$ is $\theta_t=R_{\exp tX}$, so $\mathrm{Ad}(\exp tX)Y=(\theta_{-t})_*Y$ and $\frac{d}{dt}\big|_{0}$ of this is $\mathcal L_XY=[X,Y]$.
