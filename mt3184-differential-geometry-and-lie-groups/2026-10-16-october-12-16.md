**Integral curves and flows (DG22)**
- Proposition 9.6 (naturality of integral curves): let $F:M\to N$ be smooth, $X\in\mathfrak X(M)$, $Y\in\mathfrak X(N)$ $F$-related. Then $F$ takes integral curves of $X$ to integral curves of $Y$: if $\gamma$ is an integral curve of $X$ then $F\circ\gamma$ is an integral curve of $Y$, and if $\gamma$ starts at $p$, then $F\circ\gamma$ starts at $F(p)$. Proof: $(F\circ\gamma)'(t)=dF(\gamma'(t))=dF(X_{\gamma(t)})=Y_{F(\gamma(t))}$.
- Flow domain, flow and infinitesimal generator as in [[mt3184-differential-geometry-and-lie-groups/2026-10-10-october-5-9]]; $\theta_0=\mathrm{id}$, $\theta(s,\theta(t,p))=\theta(s+t,p)$ when defined; the generator is $V_p=\partial\theta/\partial t|_{t=0}$.
- Fundamental Theorem on Flows (Thm 9.12): each $V\in\mathfrak X(M)$ has a unique maximal flow $\theta:D\to M$ with generator $V$; $\theta^{(p)}$ is the unique maximal integral curve starting at $p$; $\theta_t:M_t\to M_{-t}$ is a diffeomorphism.
- Proposition 9.13 (naturality of flows; proof outline only, homework): if $F:M\to N$ is smooth, $X\in\mathfrak X(M)$ and $Y\in\mathfrak X(N)$ are $F$-related with flows $\theta,\eta$, then $M_t\subset$ the domain on which $F\circ\theta_t=\eta_t\circ F$ (i.e. $F(M_t)\subset N_t$ and $F\circ\theta_t=\eta_t\circ F$ on $M_t$). Outline: naturality of integral curves.

**Complete vector fields (DG22, DG23)**
- $V\in\mathfrak X(M)$ is complete if it generates a global flow ($D=\mathbb R\times M$), i.e. every maximal integral curve is defined on all of $\mathbb R$.
- Lemma 9.15 (Uniform Time Lemma): let $V\in\mathfrak X(M)$ with flow $\theta:D\to M$. If there is $\varepsilon>0$ such that $(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\subset D^{(p)}$ for every $p\in M$, then $V$ is complete. Proof idea: if $b=\sup D^{(p)}<\infty$ pick $t_0$ with $b-t_0<\varepsilon$, set $q=\theta(t_0,p)$ and extend $\gamma$ beyond $b$ by following the integral curve from $q$ for time $\varepsilon$, contradicting maximality.
- Theorem 9.16: every compactly supported vector field is complete (HW8 Q2). Theorem 9.18: every $X\in\mathrm{Lie}(G)$ (left-invariant field on a Lie group) is complete. Proof sketch: let $\gamma:(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\to G$ be the integral curve of $X$ starting at $e$; then $L_g\circ\gamma$ is an integral curve starting at $g$ defined on the same interval ($X$ is $L_g$-related to itself, Prop 9.6), and the uniform time lemma applies.
- Theorem 9.20 (Flowout Theorem): let $S\subset M$ be an embedded submanifold of codimension $\ge1$ (the slide says "codimension" with the exact condition cut off; check), $V\in\mathfrak X(M)$ nowhere tangent to $S$ ($V_p\notin T_pS$ for all $p\in S$), $\theta:D\to M$ its flow, $D_S=D\cap(\mathbb R\times S)$ and $\Phi:D_S\to M$ the restriction of $\theta$. Then $\Phi$ is an immersion; $\partial\Phi/\partial t=V\circ\Phi$; there is a smooth $\delta:S\to(0,\infty)$ such that $\Phi$ restricted to $\{(t,p):|t|<\delta(p)\}$ is injective, hence $\Phi(D_S)$ is an immersed $(\dim S+1)$-dimensional submanifold containing $S$ to which $V$ is tangent. If $S$ has codimension 1, then $\Phi|_{\ldots}$ is a diffeomorphism onto an open submanifold of $M$.
- Proof pattern of the Flowout Theorem (DG23): (a) $d\Phi$ at $(0,p)$ maps a basis of $T_{(0,p)}(\mathbb R\times S)$ to $\{V_p,v_1,\dots,v_k\}$, linearly independent since $V_p\notin T_pS$, so $\Phi$ is an immersion at $t=0$; at general $(t_0,p)$ use $\Phi=\theta_{t_0}\circ\Phi_0$ with $\theta_{t_0}$ a diffeomorphism, so the rank is preserved. (b) Injectivity for small times: a local statement in a slice chart plus a partition of unity to choose $\delta$ smoothly. (c) The codimension 1 case: an injective immersion between manifolds of the same dimension is a diffeomorphism onto an open submanifold (rank/inverse function theorem).
- Example (DG23): the helicoidal vector field on $\mathbb R^3$ with $S$ the $x$-axis and $V=\partial_z+(\cos\ldots)$ type field (garbled; check); the flowout gives a helicoid.

**Singular and regular points; straightening (DG24)**
- $p\in M$ is a singular point of $V$ if $V_p=0$, regular if $V_p\neq0$.
- Proposition 9.21: let $\theta:D\to M$ be the flow of $V$. If $p$ is a singular point then $D^{(p)}=\mathbb R$ and $\theta^{(p)}\equiv p$. If $p$ is a regular point then $\theta^{(p)}$ is an immersion (so it is injective or periodic; compare HW7 Q4). Proof: if $V_p=0$ the constant curve $t\mapsto p$ is an integral curve, so by uniqueness of maximal integral curves $\theta^{(p)}$ is constant; for the immersion statement, a non-immersion would mean $V_{\theta(t,p)}=0$ for some $t$, then $\theta^{(p)}$ would be constant by the previous case, contradicting $V_p\neq0$.
- Theorem 9.22 (Canonical form near a regular point): if $p$ is a regular point of $V$ there is a chart $(W,\psi)$ centered at $p$ with coordinates $(s,x^2,\dots,x^n)$ such that $V=\partial/\partial s^1$ on $W$. Proof: choose a chart $(x^i)$ with $V^j(p)\neq0$, shrink so that $\partial/\partial x^j$-component is nonzero, and let $S=\{x^j=0\}$ (codimension 1, so $V$ is transverse near $p$). By the Flowout Theorem $\Phi$ restricted to $(-\delta,\delta)\times S$ is a diffeomorphism onto an open $W\ni p$; composing with a chart $\psi$ of $S$ gives the chart $(t,\psi_2,\dots,\psi_n)\circ\Phi^{-1}$ in which $\Phi$ becomes the product coordinates and $V=\partial_t$.

**Lie derivatives (DG24)**
- On $\mathbb R^n$: for $V\in T_p\mathbb R^n$ and $f$, $D_Vf(p)=\frac{d}{dt}|_{0}f(p+tV)$; vectors at different points are identified canonically via $T_p\mathbb R^n\cong\mathbb R^n$. On an arbitrary manifold there is no coordinate-independent way to identify $T_pM$ with $T_qM$, so one uses the flow.
- Definition: for $V\in\mathfrak X(M)$ with flow $\theta$ and a smooth covariant tensor/vector field $W$ (the slide writes $\omega$), the Lie derivative is $(\mathcal L_VW)_p=\frac{d}{dt}\big|_{t=0}\,d(\theta_{-t})_{\theta_t(p)}\big(W_{\theta_t(p)}\big)=\lim_{t\to0}\frac{d(\theta_{-t})_{\theta_t(p)}(W_{\theta_t(p)})-W_p}{t}$. It is defined because $(t,p)\in D$ for small $|t|$ (the slide's formula is partly illegible; check).
- Lemma 9.36: $\mathcal L_VW$ exists at each $p$ and depends smoothly on $p$. Proof: in a chart $U\ni p$ with $(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\times U\subset D$, write $\theta_t(x)$ with components; the expression $d(\theta_{-t})(W)$ has entries smooth in $(t,x)$, so the derivative in $t$ at $t=0$ exists and is smooth in $x$.
- Theorem 9.38: for $V,W\in\mathfrak X(M)$, $\mathcal L_VW=[V,W]$ (the OCR cuts off after the statement; this is the standard Lie-derivative theorem and matches the slide's heading "Thm 9.38"; check).
