# HW9

## Covered
Problem sheet on one-parameter subgroups and the exponential map; it relates to lectures up to October 24th ([[mt3184-differential-geometry-and-lie-groups/2026-10-24-october-19-24]]). Not graded (homework is for self-study). The statements below are as printed; $G$ is a Lie group with Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$.

1. Let $H$ be the image of a one-parameter subgroup of $G$. Show that $H$ is isomorphic as a Lie group to $\mathbb R$, $S^1$ or the trivial group $\{e\}$.
   Practises: one-parameter subgroups as homomorphisms $\mathbb R\to G$ (Thm 20.1); the trichotomy constant / injective / periodic for maximal integral curves ([[mt3184-differential-geometry-and-lie-groups/2026-10-10-hw7]] Q4).
2. Compute the exponential maps of the abelian Lie groups $\mathbb R^n$ and $T^n$.
   Practises: finding one-parameter subgroups explicitly and applying $\exp X=\gamma_X(1)$.
3. Let $A,B\subset\mathfrak g$ with $A\oplus B=\mathfrak g$. Show that $\Phi:A\oplus B\to G$, $\Phi(X,Y)=\exp X\exp Y$, is a diffeomorphism on some neighbourhood of $(0,0)$.
   Practises: $d\exp_0=\mathrm{id}$ and the Inverse Function Theorem (compute $d\Phi_{(0,0)}$).
4. Let $G$ be connected. (a) For $X,Y\in\mathfrak g$, show $[X,Y]=0$ iff $\exp tX\exp sY=\exp sY\exp tX$ for all $s,t\in\mathbb R$. (b) Show that $G$ is abelian iff $\mathfrak g$ is abelian.
   Practises: relation between the bracket and commuting flows (Lie derivative, $\mathcal L_VW=[V,W]$); $\exp$ generating a connected group near $e$; compare [[mt3184-differential-geometry-and-lie-groups/2026-09-11-hw6]] Q6 (abelian Lie group ⇒ abelian Lie algebra).
5. Prove that $\exp(X+Y)=(\exp X)(\exp Y)$ for all $X,Y\in\mathfrak g$ iff the identity component of $G$ is abelian. Hint for "if": prove that $t\mapsto(\exp tX)(\exp tY)$ is a 1-parameter subgroup.
   Practises: uniqueness of one-parameter subgroups with given initial velocity ($d/dt$ at $0$ gives $X+Y$ by $dm_{(e,e)}(X,Y)=X+Y$, [[mt3184-differential-geometry-and-lie-groups/2026-08-27-hw4]] Q6); Q4.
6. Let $H$ be a Lie subgroup of $G$. Show that the closure of $S$ is also a Lie subgroup. Conclude that every Lie subgroup of $G$ is either an embedded submanifold of $G$ or a dense subset of an embedded submanifold.
   Practises: Closed Subgroup Theorem (Thm 20.12). Note: the sheet writes "$S$" where $H$ is presumably meant (typo in the sheet).
