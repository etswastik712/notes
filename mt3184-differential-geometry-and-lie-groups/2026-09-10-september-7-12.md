**Lie subgroups and the Closed Subgroup Theorem (DG14)**
- A Lie subgroup $H$ of $G$ is a subgroup that is an immersed submanifold and a Lie group. Open ⇒ closed (see [[mt3184-differential-geometry-and-lie-groups/2026-09-03-august-31-september-4]]).
- If $\omega$ is an open set in $G$ then $L_\omega$-type products $\omega\cdot S$ are open; if $G$ is connected, $G_0=G$; $G_0$ is a normal subgroup.
- If $F: G\to H$ is a Lie group homomorphism, then $F(G)$ is a Lie subgroup of $H$ (when injective), and $\ker F$ is an embedded Lie subgroup of $G$ of dimension $\dim G-\mathrm{rank}\,dF$. Example: $SL(n,\mathbb R)\le GL(n,\mathbb R)$.
- Thm 20.12 (Closed Subgroup Theorem): let $H\le G$ be a subgroup. Then $H$ is closed iff $H$ is an embedded Lie subgroup. Proof outline (⇒ of closedness direction as on the slide): take a slice chart $(U,\varphi)$ for $H$ at $e$; there is an open $V\ni e$ with $\bar V\subset U$ and $g h^{-1}\in U$ for $g,h\in V$. Since $H\cap U$ is a slice, it is closed in $U$. If $h_i\in H$ converge to $g\in G$, then $h_i^{-1}h_j\to e$ eventually lies in $H\cap U$-closed, hence $g\in H$.
- Exercise set: show $\mathbb R_{>0}^n$ is an embedded Lie subgroup of $GL(n,\mathbb R)$ of dimension $n$ (slide marked "LATER", partly illegible).

**Group actions (DG14, DG15)**
- A smooth left action of $G$ on $M$ is a smooth map $\theta: G\times M\to M$, $(g,p)\mapsto g\cdot p$, with $g_1\cdot(g_2\cdot p)=(g_1g_2)\cdot p$ and $e\cdot p=p$.
- Examples: $GL(n,\mathbb R)$ on $\mathbb R^n$ by $A\cdot x=Ax$ (smooth, as each component is polynomial in $a_{ij}$ and $x_j$); $G$ on itself by left multiplication.
- For $g\in G$, $\theta_g: M\to M$, $p\mapsto g\cdot p$, is a diffeomorphism. For $p\in M$, the orbit map $\theta^{(p)}: G\to M$, $g\mapsto g\cdot p$.
- Orbit of $p$: $\mathcal O_p=G\cdot p$. Stabilizer of $p$: $G_p=\{g\in G: g\cdot p=p\}$. An action is transitive if for all $p,q$ there is $g$ with $g\cdot p=q$; free if $g\cdot p=p$ implies $g=e$.
- Example: $GL(n,\mathbb R)$ acting on $\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\}$ is transitive; orbits of $GL(n,\mathbb R)$ acting by $A\cdot X=AXA^T$ on $M(n,\mathbb R)$ (congruence): the stabilizer of $I$ is $O(n)$.
- Equivariant maps: for actions $\theta$ of $G$ on $M$ and $\varphi$ of $G$ on $N$, a smooth $F: M\to N$ is equivariant if $F(g\cdot p)=g\cdot F(p)$, i.e. $F\circ\theta_g=\varphi_g\circ F$.
- Example: a Lie group homomorphism $F: G\to H$ is equivariant when $G$ acts on itself by left multiplication and on $H$ by $g\cdot h=F(g)h$. Another: for any action, each orbit map $\theta^{(p)}$ is equivariant with respect to left multiplication on $G$ and $\theta$ on $M$ (set as HW).
- Equivariant Rank Theorem: if $G$ acts transitively on $M$, $G$ acts on $N$ and $F: M\to N$ is equivariant, then $F$ has constant rank.

**Orbits, stabilizers, semidirect products (DG15, DG16)**
- Consequence: the orbit map $\theta^{(p)}$ has constant rank (left multiplication is transitive). Hence the stabilizer $G_p=(\theta^{(p)})^{-1}(p)$ is an embedded Lie subgroup of $G$ of dimension $\dim G-\mathrm{rank}\,\theta^{(p)}$. The orbit $\mathcal O_p=G\cdot p$ is an immersed submanifold of $M$ (when $G_p$ is trivial, $\theta^{(p)}$ is injective, so by the global rank theorem it is an injective immersion).
- Example: $GL(n,\mathbb R)$ acting on $M(n,\mathbb R)$ by $A\cdot X=AXA^T$: the stabilizer of $I$ is $O(n)$, an embedded Lie subgroup; the differential of the orbit map at $I$ is $B\mapsto B+B^T$, so the image is the space of symmetric matrices, rank $n(n+1)/2$, and $\dim O(n)=n^2-n(n+1)/2=n(n-1)/2$ (the slide's final figure is garbled; this value is computed from the printed rank). $SO(n)$ is an open subgroup of $O(n)$.
- Semidirect products: for a smooth action $\theta: H\times N\to N$ by group automorphisms ($\theta_h: N\to N$ a Lie group isomorphism), $N\rtimes_\theta H$ is the Lie group with underlying manifold $N\times H$ and group operation $(n_1,h_1)(n_2,h_2)=(n_1\,\theta_{h_1}(n_2),\,h_1h_2)$ (see [[mt3184-differential-geometry-and-lie-groups/2026-09-11-hw6]] and the semidirect product notes).

**Lie algebras (DG17)**
- A Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$ is a real vector space with a function $[\cdot,\cdot]:\mathfrak g\times\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak g$ that is (i) bilinear, (ii) antisymmetric: $[X,Y]=-[Y,X]$, (iii) satisfies the Jacobi identity: $[X,[Y,Z]]+[Y,[Z,X]]+[Z,[X,Y]]=0$.
- A Lie subalgebra is a vector subspace closed under brackets. A Lie algebra homomorphism is a linear map $A:\mathfrak g_1\to\mathfrak g_2$ with $A[X,Y]=[AX,AY]$.
- Examples: $\mathbb R^n$ with $[X,Y]=0$ (abelian); $\mathfrak X(M)$ with $[X,Y]=XY-YX$; $M(n,\mathbb R)$ with $[A,B]=AB-BA$, denoted $\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb R)$.
- If $F: M\to N$ is a diffeomorphism, then for $X\in\mathfrak X(M)$ the field $F_*X$ is the unique $Y$ that is $F$-related to $X$; the map $X\mapsto F_*X$ preserves brackets.
- Theorem: $G$ connected: $G$ abelian ⇔ $\mathfrak g$ abelian. Lie's Third Theorem: every finite-dimensional real Lie algebra is the Lie algebra of some simply connected Lie group.
- Lie($G$) is the space of left-invariant vector fields on $G$ (those $X$ with $X$ $L_g$-related to itself for all $g$); it is a Lie subalgebra of $\mathfrak X(G)$.
- Thm 8.37: the evaluation map $\varepsilon:\mathrm{Lie}(G)\to T_eG$, $X\mapsto X_e$, is a vector space isomorphism. Proof: linear; injective since $X_g=d(L_g)_e(X_e)$; surjective: for $v\in T_eG$ define $V_g=d(L_g)_e(v)$, show $V$ is smooth (via $Vf$ smooth, using a curve $\gamma$ with $\gamma'(0)=v$) and left-invariant.
- Thm 8.44 (induced Lie algebra homomorphism): let $F: G\to H$ be a Lie group homomorphism. For each $X\in\mathfrak g$ there is a unique $Y\in\mathfrak h$ that is $F$-related to $X$; the map $F_*:\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak h$, $X\mapsto Y$, is a Lie algebra homomorphism. Proof idea: $Y$ is determined by $Y_e=dF_e(X_e)$; check $Y$ is $F$-related to $X$ using $F\circ L_g=L_{F(g)}\circ F$; $F$-relatedness of brackets gives the homomorphism property.
