**Submanifolds and their tangent spaces (post, DG8)**
- For a submanifold $S\subset M$ of dimension $k$ with a slice chart around $p$ (coordinates $x^1,\dots,x^n$, with $S\cap U=\{x^{k+1}=\dots=x^n=0\}$), the inclusion $i: S\to M$ has an injective differential $di_p: T_pS\to T_pM$. So $T_pS$ is identified with a $k$-dimensional subspace of $T_pM$, with $v=\sum_{i\le k}v^i\partial/\partial x^i|_p$. The proof of injectivity uses a slice chart.
- Homework-style exercise: for the sphere $S^2\subset\mathbb R^3$ and $p\in S^2$, identify $T_pS^2$ with the affine plane orthogonal to $p$ (the slide compares two 2-dimensional subspaces of $\mathbb R^3$; the details are not legible).

**Vector fields on submanifolds of $\mathbb R^n$ (DG8)**
- A vector field on a submanifold $S\subset\mathbb R^n$ is a smooth map $v: S\to\mathbb R^n$ with $v(p)\in T_pS$ for all $p$.
- Hairy Ball Theorem (stated): $S^n$ has a nowhere-vanishing vector field iff $n$ is odd.

**The tangent bundle (DG8)**
- To define a vector field on a manifold $M$ one needs a smooth map $v: M\to\coprod_{p\in M}T_pM$ with $v(p)\in T_pM$; so $TM=\coprod_pT_pM$ must itself be a manifold.
- For a chart $(U,\varphi)$, define $\tilde\varphi: \pi^{-1}(U)\to\varphi(U)\times\mathbb R^n$, $\sum v^i\,\partial/\partial x^i|_p\mapsto(\varphi(p),v^1,\dots,v^n)$. The set-level definition on all of $M$ via a single chart is not well defined in general; it is well defined when $M$ is an open subset of $\mathbb R^n$.
- Lemma 1.35 (Smooth Manifold Chart Lemma): let $M$ be a set and $\{U_\alpha\}$ a collection of subsets with maps $\varphi_\alpha: U_\alpha\to\mathbb R^n$ such that (i) each $\varphi_\alpha$ is a bijection onto an open subset of $\mathbb R^n$; (ii) each $\varphi_\alpha(U_\alpha\cap U_\beta)$ is open in $\mathbb R^n$; (iii) if $U_\alpha\cap U_\beta\ne\varnothing$, then $\varphi_\beta\circ\varphi_\alpha^{-1}$ is smooth; (iv) countably many $U_\alpha$ cover $M$; (v) for $p\neq q$ either both lie in some $U_\alpha$ or there are disjoint $U_\alpha\ni p$, $U_\beta\ni q$. Then $M$ has a unique smooth structure in which each $(U_\alpha,\varphi_\alpha)$ is a chart. Proof outline: take the topology generated by the basis of sets $\varphi_\alpha^{-1}(V)$, $V$ open; each $\varphi_\alpha$ is then a homeomorphism; (iii) makes the family an atlas; (iv) gives second countability and (v) the Hausdorff property.
- Prop 3.18: $TM=\coprod_pT_pM$ is a $2n$-dimensional smooth manifold, and the projection $\pi: TM\to M$ is smooth. Proof outline with the chart lemma: for an atlas $(U_\alpha,\varphi_\alpha)$ define $\tilde\varphi_\alpha$ on $\pi^{-1}(U_\alpha)$ as above; check it is a bijection onto the open set $\varphi_\alpha(U_\alpha)\times\mathbb R^n$; the overlap maps $\tilde\varphi_\beta\circ\tilde\varphi_\alpha^{-1}$ are smooth (they are given by $(x,v)\mapsto(\varphi_\beta\circ\varphi_\alpha^{-1}(x),\,D(\varphi_\beta\circ\varphi_\alpha^{-1})_x v)$); choose a countable atlas for second countability. In these coordinates $\pi$ is $(x,v)\mapsto x$, so it is smooth.
- Definition: a vector field on $M$ is a smooth map $v: M\to TM$ with $\pi\circ v=\mathrm{id}_M$, i.e. $v(p)\in T_pM$.
