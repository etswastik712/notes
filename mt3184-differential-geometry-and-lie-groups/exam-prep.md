## Must know
- Smooth structure: two smooth atlases induce the same smooth structure iff their union is a smooth atlas; every smooth atlas lies in a unique maximal one ([[mt3184-differential-geometry-and-lie-groups/2026-08-07-hw1]] Q1).
- Smoothness of a continuous map $F$ at $p$ can be checked in one pair of charts: $\psi\circ F\circ\varphi^{-1}$ smooth ([[mt3184-differential-geometry-and-lie-groups/2026-08-07-hw1]] Q3).
- Tangent vector at $p$ (midsem Q1): a linear map $v:C^\infty(M)\to\mathbb R$ with $v(fg)=f(p)v(g)+g(p)v(f)$. Partition of unity subordinate to a cover $\{U_\alpha\}$: smooth $\psi_\alpha:M\to[0,1]$ with $\operatorname{supp}\psi_\alpha\subset U_\alpha$, locally finite supports, $\sum\psi_\alpha\equiv1$ ([[mt3184-differential-geometry-and-lie-groups/2026-11-16-midsem-exam]]).
- Tangent vectors are local; $T_{(p,q)}(M\times N)\cong T_pM\oplus T_qN$ ([[mt3184-differential-geometry-and-lie-groups/2026-08-14-hw2]]).
- Smooth Manifold Chart Lemma (Lemma 1.35): bijective charts onto open sets, open overlaps, smooth transitions, countably many charts covering, separation of points give a unique smooth structure making them charts. $TM$ is a $2n$-manifold with $\pi$ smooth (Prop 3.18); chart $\tilde\varphi:\sum v^i\partial_i|_p\mapsto(\varphi(p),v)$ ([[mt3184-differential-geometry-and-lie-groups/2026-08-20-august-17-21]]).
- Vector field: smooth section of $TM$; smooth iff components smooth in every chart iff $Xf$ smooth for all smooth $f$ (Prop 8.14). Derivations of $C^\infty(M)$ correspond to vector fields. $F$-related fields: $dF_p(X_p)=Y_{F(p)}$ (Prop 8.19).
- Rank Theorem: constant rank $r$ ⇒ charts with $\psi\circ F\circ\varphi^{-1}(x)=(x^1,\dots,x^r,0,\dots,0)$. Global Rank Theorem: surjective ⇒ submersion; injective ⇒ immersion; bijective ⇒ diffeomorphism.
- Lie group homomorphisms have constant rank (Thm 7.5). Immersed submanifold: inclusion is an immersion. Lie subgroup: subgroup that is an immersed submanifold and a Lie group. $\ker F$ is an embedded Lie subgroup of dimension $\dim G-\mathrm{rank}\,dF$ (Prop 7.16). $G_0$ is the unique normal, open, connected subgroup (Prop 7.15).
- A compact immersed submanifold is embedded (midsem Q3): the inclusion is an injective immersion, and a continuous injection from a compact space to a Hausdorff space is a closed map, hence a topological embedding; immersion + topological embedding = embedding.
- A smooth map equivariant for a transitive $G$-action on the domain (any $G$-action on the target) has constant rank, since $dF_{gx}\circ d(\theta_g)_x=d(\psi_g)_{F(x)}\circ dF_x$ with $\theta_g,\psi_g$ diffeomorphisms (midsem Q4). Equivariant Rank Theorem (Thm 7.25); stabilizers are embedded Lie subgroups, orbits immersed submanifolds.
- Two Lie group homomorphisms from a connected $G$ that agree on a nonempty open set agree everywhere: they agree on a neighbourhood of $e$ (translate by $g\in U$), and such a neighbourhood generates $G$ (midsem Q5).
- Non-abelian 2-dimensional Lie algebras: $[X,Y]=c\,[V_1,V_2]$ for any basis (the bracket is determined by $[V_1,V_2]$ via the determinant of the coordinates); there is a basis with $[W_1,W_2]=W_2$, and so any two such algebras are isomorphic (midsem Q6).
- **Closed Subgroup Theorem (Thm 20.12)**: a subgroup $H\le G$ is closed iff it is an embedded Lie subgroup. Proof outline: $\mathfrak h=\{X:\exp(tX)\in H\ \forall t\}$ is a subspace (by the product formula), and a neighbourhood $U$ of $0$ has $\exp(U)\cap H=\exp(U\cap\mathfrak h)$ (by contradiction with a complement $S$, compactness of the unit sphere in $S$ and $H$ closed), giving slice charts; left translation moves them around.
- $\mathrm{Lie}(G)$: left-invariant vector fields; $\varepsilon:\mathrm{Lie}(G)\to T_eG$ is an isomorphism (Thm 8.37); $F_*$ is a Lie algebra homomorphism (Thm 8.44). Homomorphism Theorem: $G$ simply connected, $\varphi:\mathrm{Lie}(G)\to\mathrm{Lie}(H)$ a homomorphism ⇒ unique $F$ with $F_*=\varphi$. Lie's Third Theorem. $\mathrm{Lie}(GL(n,\mathbb R))=\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb R)$ with $[A,B]=AB-BA$; $\mathfrak{so}(n)=\{A:A+A^T=0\}$. Ado's Theorem.
- Fundamental Theorem on Flows (Thm 9.12): unique maximal flow $\theta:D\to M$, $\theta_t:M_t\to M_{-t}$ diffeomorphism with inverse $\theta_{-t}$. Naturality: $F\circ\theta_t=\eta_t\circ F$ for $F$-related fields (Prop 9.13). Uniform Time Lemma 9.15; compactly supported and left-invariant fields are complete. Flowout Theorem 9.20. Regular points: $\theta^{(p)}$ immersion, straightening chart (Thm 9.22). $\mathcal L_VW=[V,W]$ (Thm 9.38).
- One-parameter subgroup: Lie group homomorphism $\mathbb R\to G$; exactly the maximal integral curves through $e$ of left-invariant fields (Thm 20.1). $e^A=\sum A^k/k!$ converges and $t\mapsto e^{tA}$ is the one-parameter subgroup of $A$ (Prop 20.2); those of a Lie subgroup $H$ are those of $G$ with initial velocity in $T_eH$ (Prop 20.3).
- $\exp:\mathfrak g\to G$, $\exp X=\gamma_X(1)$: smooth; $\exp(sX)$ is the one-parameter subgroup (Prop 20.5); $\exp((s+t)X)=\exp(sX)\exp(tX)$; $\exp(-X)=(\exp X)^{-1}$; $(\exp X)^n=\exp(nX)$; $d\exp_0=\mathrm{id}$ so $\exp$ is a local diffeomorphism at $0$; for $GL(n,\mathbb R)$, $\exp A=e^A$.
- Naturality of $\exp$: $\Phi\circ\exp_G=\exp_H\circ\Phi_*$ for a Lie group homomorphism $\Phi$. For a Lie subgroup $H$, $\mathfrak h=\{X:\exp(tX)\in H\ \forall t\}$ (check slide DG28 for the exact hypotheses).
- Prop 20.10 / Cor 20.11: $\exp(tX)\exp(tY)=\exp(t(X+Y)+O(t^2))$ and $\exp(X+Y)=\lim_n(\exp(X/n)\exp(Y/n))^n$.
- Weakly embedded: an immersed $S\subset M$ such that every smooth $F:N\to M$ with image in $S$ is smooth into $S$. Embedded ⇒ weakly embedded; integral manifolds of involutive distributions are weakly embedded (Thm 19.17); Lie subgroups are weakly embedded.
- Distribution of rank $k$: smooth rank-$k$ subbundle $D\subset TM$ (local frames $V_1,\dots,V_k$). Integral manifold: immersed $S$ with $T_pS=D_p$; integrable: through every point. Involutive: $[X,Y]\in D$ whenever $X,Y\in D$. Integrable ⇒ involutive (Prop 19.3). Flat chart: $D=\mathrm{span}(\partial_1,\dots,\partial_k)$; completely integrable: flat charts around every point.
- **Local Frobenius Theorem**: every involutive distribution is completely integrable. **Frobenius Theorem**: for involutive $D$, the maximal connected integral manifolds form a foliation of $M$.
- Foliation of dimension $k$: partition into connected immersed $k$-dimensional leaves with charts flat for it (each leaf meets the chart in countably many slices). Example: irrational-slope lines on $T^2$ have dense leaves.
- $D$ is $\Phi$-invariant iff its foliation is $\Phi$-invariant (Prop 19.23).
- Lemma 19.24: a Lie subalgebra $\mathfrak h\subset\mathfrak g$ gives a left-invariant involutive distribution $D_g=\{X_g:X\in\mathfrak h\}$. Thm 19.25: every Lie subgroup is an integral manifold of such a distribution. **Thm 19.26**: for each Lie subalgebra $\mathfrak h\subset\mathfrak g$ there is a unique connected Lie subgroup of $G$ with Lie algebra $\mathfrak h$ (the leaf through $e$).
- **Infinitesimal generator.** For a right action $\theta:M\times G\to M$ and $X\in\mathrm{Lie}(G)$, $p\cdot\exp(tX)$ is an $\mathbb R$-action; $\hat\theta(X)_p=\frac{d}{dt}|_0\,p\cdot\exp(tX)$. For each $p$, $X$ and $\hat\theta(X)$ are $\theta^{(p)}$-related ($\theta^{(p)}(g)=p\cdot g$), and **Thm 20.15**: $\hat\theta:\mathrm{Lie}(G)\to\mathfrak X(M)$ is a Lie algebra homomorphism ([[mt3184-differential-geometry-and-lie-groups/2026-11-16-november-9-13]]).
- **Thm 20.16 (Fundamental Theorem on Lie Algebra Actions).** $G$ simply connected with Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$, $\hat\theta:\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak X(M)$ a complete $\mathfrak g$-action (Lie algebra homomorphism with every $\hat\theta(X)$ complete) ⇒ a unique smooth right $G$-action on $M$ with infinitesimal generator $\hat\theta$. Know the statement; its proof is not in the final exam.
- **Thm 20.19.** $G$ simply connected, $H$ a Lie group, $\varphi:\mathrm{Lie}(G)\to\mathrm{Lie}(H)$ a Lie algebra homomorphism ⇒ unique Lie group homomorphism $\Phi:G\to H$ with $\Phi_*=\varphi$. Proof: apply Thm 20.16 to the action of $G$ on $H$ with generator $\varphi$, set $\Phi(g)=e\cdot g$; uniqueness by HW10 Q1.
- **Cor 20.20 / Thm 20.21 (Lie correspondence).** $G\mapsto\mathrm{Lie}(G)$ is a bijection between isomorphism classes of simply connected Lie groups and of finite-dimensional Lie algebras. Injective: Cor 20.20 (compose the two homomorphisms, uniqueness gives identity). Surjective: Ado gives $\mathfrak g\cong\mathfrak g_0\subset\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb R)$, Thm 19.26 gives a connected $G_0\subset GL(n,\mathbb R)$, take the universal cover. Simple connectivity is essential ($\mathbb R$ and $S^1$ have isomorphic Lie algebras).
- Universal cover: a connected Lie group has a simply connected Lie group $\widetilde G$ with a surjective, locally diffeomorphic covering homomorphism $p:\widetilde G\to G$; if $G$ is simply connected, $p$ is a diffeomorphism. Connected Lie groups with Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$ are $\widetilde G/\Gamma$, $\Gamma$ discrete normal (Thm 21.32, slide garbled). $G/K$ is a Lie group for $K$ closed normal (Thm 21.26, slide garbled).
- Ideal: subspace $L\subset\mathfrak g$ with $[X,Y]\in L$ for $X\in\mathfrak g$, $Y\in L$; kernels of Lie algebra homomorphisms are ideals. **Thm 20.28**: for $G$ connected and $H$ a connected Lie subgroup, $H$ is normal iff $\mathrm{Lie}(H)$ is an ideal in $\mathrm{Lie}(G)$.
- Adjoint representation: $C_g(h)=ghg^{-1}$, $\mathrm{Ad}(g)=(C_g)_*$; $\mathrm{Ad}:G\to GL(\mathfrak g)$ is a Lie group homomorphism (Prop 20.24, since $C_{g_1g_2}=C_{g_1}C_{g_2}$). $\mathrm{ad}(X)Y=[X,Y]$ is a Lie algebra representation (HW11 Q2), and **Thm 20.27**: $\mathrm{Ad}_*=\mathrm{ad}$, from $\mathrm{Ad}(\exp tX)Y=(\theta_{-t})_*Y$ with $\theta_t=R_{\exp tX}$ the flow of $X$.
- HW9 statements to be able to prove: image of a one-parameter subgroup is $\mathbb R$, $S^1$ or trivial; for connected $G$, $[X,Y]=0$ iff the one-parameter subgroups commute, and $G$ abelian iff $\mathfrak g$ abelian; $\exp(X+Y)=\exp X\exp Y$ for all $X,Y$ iff $G_0$ abelian; the closure of a Lie subgroup is a Lie subgroup ([[mt3184-differential-geometry-and-lie-groups/2026-10-26-hw9]]).
