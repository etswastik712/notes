## Covered

**Distributions (DG29, DG30)**
- Recall: $TM=\coprod_{p\in M}T_pM$ with the topology and smooth structure from the chart $\tilde\varphi:\sum v^i\partial_i|_p\mapsto(\varphi(p),v)$; $\pi:TM\to M$ is smooth.
- A **distribution** of rank $k$ on $M$ is a subset $D\subset TM$, $D=\coprod_pD_p$, where each $D_p\subset T_pM$ is a $k$-dimensional subspace, and which is smooth: each point has a neighbourhood $U$ and smooth vector fields $V_1,\dots,V_k$ on $U$ with $\langle V_1|_q,\dots,V_k|_q\rangle$ a basis of $D_q$ for all $q\in U$ (a tangent subbundle).
- Examples: (i) $V\in\mathfrak X(M)$ nowhere zero: $D_p=\mathrm{span}(V_p)$; the integral curves are integral manifolds, so $D$ is integrable. (ii) $M=\mathbb R^n$, $D_p=\mathrm{span}(\partial_1,\dots,\partial_k)$: the slices $\{x^{k+1}=c_{k+1},\dots,x^n=c_n\}$ are integral manifolds. (iii) A rank-2 distribution on $\mathbb R^3$ spanned by two fields $X,Y$ (one involving $y\partial_z$; the formula is illegible) that is **not** integrable: if $S$ were an integral submanifold through $0$, then $X,Y$ would be tangent to $S$, so their integral curves through points of $S$ stay in $S$, and the slide derives a contradiction from the resulting surface (details illegible).
- An immersed submanifold $S\subset M$ is an **integral manifold** of $D$ if $T_pS=D_p$ for every $p\in S$. $D$ is **integrable** if every point of $M$ lies in an integral manifold of $D$.
- Prop 19.3: let $D$ be integrable, $U\subset M$ open and $X,Y\in\mathfrak X(U)$ with $X_p,Y_p\in D_p$ for all $p\in U$. Then $[X,Y]_p\in D_p$ for all $p\in U$. Proof: let $S$ be the integral manifold through $p$; $X|_S,Y|_S$ are tangent to $S$, so $[X|_S,Y|_S]$ is a vector field on $S$ and is $\iota$-related to $[X,Y]$ ($\iota:S\hookrightarrow M$); hence $[X,Y]_p\in T_pS=D_p$.
- $D$ is **involutive** if for every open $U\subset M$ and $X,Y\in\mathfrak X(U)$ with $X_p,Y_p\in D_p$ for all $p\in U$, we have $[X,Y]_p\in D_p$ for all $p\in U$. So integrable ⇒ involutive.
- **Flat chart**: a chart $(U,\varphi)$, $\varphi=(x^1,\dots,x^n)$, is flat for $D$ if $D_q=\mathrm{span}(\partial_1|_q,\dots,\partial_k|_q)$ for all $q\in U$. $D$ is **completely integrable** if every $p\in M$ lies in a chart flat for $D$. Completely integrable ⇒ integrable (the slices $\{x^{k+1}=c^{k+1},\dots\}$ are integral manifolds).
- **Local Frobenius Theorem**: every involutive distribution is completely integrable. (So involutive ⇔ integrable ⇔ completely integrable.) Only the statement appears in the OCR.

**Foliations (DG29, DG30)**
- Let $\mathcal F$ be a collection of disjoint connected immersed $k$-dimensional submanifolds ("leaves") whose union is $M$. A chart $(U,\varphi)$ is **flat for $\mathcal F$** if for each leaf $L$, $\varphi(L\cap U)$ is empty or a countable union of $k$-slices $\{x^{k+1}=c^{k+1},\dots,x^n=c^n\}$ (i.e. $\mathbb R^k\times\{c\}$ with $c$ in a countable subset of $\mathbb R^{n-k}$). $\mathcal F$ is a **$k$-dimensional foliation** of $M$ if $M$ is covered by charts flat for $\mathcal F$.
- A distribution $D$ **induces** (determines) a foliation $\mathcal F$ if $D_p=T_pL$ for each $p$, $L$ the leaf through $p$.
- Example: the lines of slope $\theta$ in the torus $T^2=\mathbb R^2/\mathbb Z^2$, $\gamma(t)=(e^{2\pi it},e^{2\pi i\theta t})$; if $\theta\notin\mathbb Q$ every leaf is dense. A second example on the slide is illegible.
- **Frobenius Theorem (global)**: let $D$ be an involutive distribution on $M$. The collection of all maximal connected integral manifolds of $D$ forms a foliation of $M$.
- Thm 19.17 (DG30): every integral manifold of an involutive distribution is weakly embedded. Immersed submanifold $S\subset M$ is weakly embedded if for every smooth $F:N\to M$ with $F(N)\subset S$, $F:N\to S$ is smooth ([[mt3184-differential-geometry-and-lie-groups/2026-11-06-october-26-30]]).
- **Invariance.** For a diffeomorphism $\Phi:M\to M$, $D$ is **$\Phi$-invariant** if $d\Phi_p(D_p)=D_{\Phi(p)}$ for all $p$; a foliation $\mathcal F$ is $\Phi$-invariant if $\Phi(L)$ is a leaf for every leaf $L$. Prop 19.23: for an involutive $D$ with induced foliation $\mathcal F$, $D$ is $\Phi$-invariant iff $\mathcal F$ is $\Phi$-invariant (proof left as homework: HW10 Q3).

**Lie subalgebras and Lie subgroups (DG30)**
- Lemma 19.24: let $\mathfrak h$ be a Lie subalgebra of $\mathrm{Lie}(G)$ and $D_g=\{X_g:X\in\mathfrak h\}$. Then $D$ is an involutive, left-invariant distribution. Proof: $D_g=d(L_g)_e(\mathfrak h_e)$, so all $D_g$ have the same dimension and $D$ is left-invariant ($dL_h(D_g)=D_{hg}$). If $X_1,\dots,X_k$ is a basis of $\mathfrak h$, then $\langle X_1,\dots,X_k\rangle$ is a smooth global frame for $D$, so $D$ is smooth. Involutive: for $X_j,X_l$ the brackets $[X_j,X_l]\in\mathfrak h$ (subalgebra), so any two local sections in $D$, written $\sum f^jX_j$, have bracket in $D$ by the product rule.
- Thm 19.25: every Lie subgroup $H\le G$ is an integral manifold of an involutive distribution (the left-invariant one defined by $\mathfrak h=\mathrm{Lie}(H)$, with $D_h=dL_h(T_eH)=T_hH$ for $h\in H$). Corollary: Lie subgroups are weakly embedded (Thm 19.17).
- **Thm 19.26 (Lie subgroups ↔ Lie subalgebras)**: let $G$ be a Lie group with Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$ and $\mathfrak h\subset\mathfrak g$ a Lie subalgebra. Then there is a unique connected Lie subgroup $H$ of $G$ with $\mathrm{Lie}(H)=\mathfrak h$.
  - Existence: let $D$ be the left-invariant involutive distribution given by $\mathfrak h$, $\mathcal F$ the foliation it determines (Frobenius), and $H=L_e$ the leaf through $e$. Since $D$ is left-invariant, $\mathcal F$ is left-invariant (Prop 19.23), so for $h\in H$, $L_{h^{-1}}(H)$ is the leaf through $h^{-1}$... The slide shows that $h\in H\Rightarrow h\,L_e$ is the leaf through $h$, hence $hH=H$ and $h^{-1}\in H$ (so $H$ is a subgroup; the argument is garbled). The multiplication map $H\times H\to G$ is smooth into $G$ with image in $H$; as $H$ is weakly embedded, $H\times H\to H$ is smooth, and likewise inversion, so $H$ is a Lie subgroup with $T_eH=D_e=\mathfrak h$.
  - Uniqueness (the claim, partly illegible): suppose $\tilde H$ is another connected Lie subgroup with $\mathrm{Lie}(\tilde H)=\mathfrak h$. Then $T_h\tilde H=dL_h(\mathfrak h_e)=D_h$, so $\tilde H$ is a connected integral manifold of $D$ through $e$, hence $\tilde H\subseteq H$ by maximality of $H$. Conversely, taking a flat chart $U$ of $\mathcal F$ at $e$, $H\cap U$ is a countable union of slices, and the slice through $e$ lies in $\tilde H$, giving an open subset of $H$ inside $\tilde H$; since an open neighbourhood of $e$ generates a connected group, $H\subseteq\tilde H$. So $H=\tilde H$.
