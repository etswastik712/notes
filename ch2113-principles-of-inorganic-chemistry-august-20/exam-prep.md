## Must know
- Arrhenius: an acid raises $[\mathrm{H_3O^+}]$, a base raises $[\mathrm{OH^-}]$; fails for CO2, NH3, CaO and non-neutral salts (Classroom: Acids and Bases Lecture 1).
- Brønsted–Lowry: acid = proton donor, base = proton acceptor; conjugate base = what remains after loss of H⁺.
- Lux–Flood: base = O²⁻ donor (basic anhydride), acid = O²⁻ acceptor (acid anhydride), e.g. CaO + SiO2 → CaSiO3.
- Lewis: acid = e⁻-pair acceptor, base = e⁻-pair donor.
- Aqua, hydroxo, oxo acids: where the acidic proton sits; oxo-acid strength rises with central-atom electronegativity and electron-withdrawing substituents (CCl3COOH > CH3COOH; O2SF(OH) > O2S(NH2)(OH)).
- BF3 < BCl3 < BBr3 in Lewis acidity, because of F→B 2p–2p π back-donation; SiI4 < SiBr4 < SiCl4 < SiF4.
- HSAB: hard acids bind F⁻ > Cl⁻ > Br⁻ > I⁻, soft acids the reverse; hard binds hard, soft binds soft; hard–hard is electrostatic (Madelung), soft–soft is covalent/dispersion.
- Diborane: 12 valence electrons, 4 terminal 2c–2e⁻ B–H bonds plus 2 bridging 3c–2e⁻ B–H–B bonds.
- Wade's rules: closo $n+1$, nido $n+2$, arachno $n+3$, hypho $n+4$ skeletal electron pairs; each BH gives 2 electrons, each extra H gives 1, charges counted; for ligand-free clusters skeleton electrons = $g - 2n$.
- Borazine B3N3H6: "inorganic benzene", B–N 1.436 Å, prone to addition (e.g. 3 Br2); dehydropolymerizes to BN on pyrolysis at 1200 °C.
- Cyclophosphazenes $(\mathrm{NPX_2})_n$: pπ–dπ (Dewar "island") bonding; heating (NPCl2)3 at 210–250 °C gives polyphosphazenes $[\mathrm{N{=}PR_2}]_n$.
- Midsem Q2(i) point: when HSAB is not named in the question, CH3NH2 was also accepted for 1 mark (Classroom: announcement 2026-10-08).
