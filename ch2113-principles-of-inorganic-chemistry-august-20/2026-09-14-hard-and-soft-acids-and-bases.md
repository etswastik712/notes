# Hard and Soft Acids and Bases (HSAB)

## Covered
- Hard/soft classification: introduced by Pearson, a generalization of the Class A / Class B distinction (Ahrland, Chatt, Davis). The classes are identified empirically by opposite orders of complex stability ($K_f$) (Classroom: HSAB Principle).
- With halide bases: hard acids bind in the order I⁻ < Br⁻ < Cl⁻ < F⁻; soft acids in the order F⁻ < Cl⁻ < Br⁻ < I⁻.
- Rule: hard acids bind hard bases, soft acids bind soft bases.
- Neutral molecules: the Lewis acid phenol forms a more stable complex (H-bonding) with Et2O than with Et2S, while I2 binds Et2S more strongly. So phenol is hard and I2 is soft.
- Hard acids bind in the order R3P << R3N, R2S << R2O. Soft acids bind in the reverse order: R2O << R2S, R3N << R3P.
- Interpretation: hard–hard bonding is ionic or dipole–dipole. Soft species are more polarizable and show more covalent character.
- Other contributions to the Gibbs energy of the complex: (1) rearrangement of the substituents of the acid and base needed to form the complex; (2) steric repulsion between substituents; (3) competition with the solvent in solution.
- Consequences: rationalizes organic chemistry, helps choose preparative conditions and predict reaction directions, and explains the distribution of ores and minerals in the crust (Goldschmidt classification).
  - Lithophile elements: found with oxide/silicate; Li, Mg, Al, Cr are hard cations with hard bases in their ores.
  - Chalcophile elements (e.g. Hg): found as sulfide, selenide, telluride. Cd, Pb, Sb, Sn, Zn are borderline but softer than Al³⁺, hence sulfide ores.
