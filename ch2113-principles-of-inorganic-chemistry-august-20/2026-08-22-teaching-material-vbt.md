# Teaching Material: VBT

## Covered
From Lecture Notes_VB_theory_2026.pdf (Classroom).
- VB theory was the first quantum mechanical theory of bonding and expresses Lewis's ideas as wavefunctions; MO theory largely superseded it.
- H2: for widely separated atoms $\psi = \chi_A(1)\chi_B(2)$. Close together, either electron may be on either atom, so the state is the superposition $\psi = \chi_A(1)\chi_B(2) + \chi_A(2)\chi_B(1)$ (un-normalised wavefunction of the H–H bond). By the Pauli principle only paired spins can be described by this function and contribute to the σ bond.
- Potential energy curve: $E = 0$ is the free-atom energy; energy falls as the atoms approach, then rises rapidly from nuclear repulsion at short distance. A deeper minimum means a stronger bond.
- A σ bond has cylindrical symmetry about the internuclear axis; its electrons have zero orbital angular momentum about that axis.
- N2: each N is 2s² 2px¹ 2py¹ 2pz¹ with z the internuclear axis. The σ bond comes from spin-pairing of the two facing 2pz electrons; the 2px and 2py electrons approach side by side and form two π bonds.
- Polyatomics: each σ bond is spin-pairing of electrons in orbitals with cylindrical symmetry about that internuclear axis; π bonds use orbitals of appropriate symmetry.
- Problem: using O 2py and 2pz would give H2O a 90° angle, and NH3 likewise, but the observed angles are 104.5° and 107°.
- Promotion: moving an electron to a higher orbital is worthwhile if the bonds it allows recover the energy. Carbon 2s²2p² promoted to 2s¹2px¹2py¹2pz¹ gains four bonds (CH4) and relieves 2s electron repulsion.
- Hybridization (AB4, CH4): the four tetrahedral sp³ hybrids are $h_1 = s + p_x + p_y + p_z$, $h_2 = s - p_x - p_y + p_z$, $h_3 = s - p_x + p_y - p_z$, $h_4 = s + p_x - p_y - p_z$. The angle between them is $\cos^{-1}(-1/3) = 109.47^\circ$. Hybrids are directional with enhanced amplitude in the internuclear region, so bonds are stronger than from pure s and p.
- Hybrid sets by coordination number: 2 linear sp, pd, sd; 2 angular sp, sd; 3 trigonal planar sp², p²d; 3 trigonal pyramidal sp², pd²; 4 tetrahedral sp³, sd³; 4 square planar p²d², sp²d; 5 TBP sp³d, spd³; 6 octahedral sp³d²; 6 trigonal prismatic spd⁴, pd⁵ (as listed on the slide).
- Steric-number method: $Z = \dfrac{\text{valence electrons} + \text{monovalent atoms} + \text{charge}}{2}$ (written on the slide with the form "6+0+2−0 over 2" for $\mathrm{SO_4^{2-}}$): $\mathrm{SO_4^{2-}}$ Z = 4, sp³; $\mathrm{CO_3^{2-}}$ Z = 3, sp²; H2O has 2 bond pairs and 2 lone pairs, AB2L2, bent, sp³.
- Non-equivalent hybrids: in TBP the dsp³ set is not equivalent: three strong equatorial sp² orbitals and two weaker axial dp (pzdz²) linear orbitals. Square pyramidal is the square planar dsp² set plus a pz orbital.
- s/p character and angle for equivalent hybrids: $\cos\theta = \dfrac{S}{S-1} = \dfrac{P-1}{P}$ with $S$, $P$ the s and p fractions. CH4: $S = 0.25$, $\cos\theta = -1/3$, $\theta = 109.5^\circ$.
- H2O worked example: bonding hybrids at 104.5°, $\cos 104.5^\circ = -0.25 = S/(S-1)$ gives 20% s, 80% p. Total p over four orbitals is 3 and total s is 1, so each non-bonding hybrid has 70% p and 30% s, and the angle between them is $\cos^{-1}(-0.4285) = 115.37^\circ$.
- AB5 has no unique highly symmetric equivalent set (TBP and square pyramid are inter-linked); calculation is harder when d orbitals participate.
- Take-home exercise: find %s and %p for the bonded and non-bonded subsets of NH3.
- Hypervalence (PCl5, SF6): traditional explanation uses low-lying d orbitals; the slide notes that absence of a 2d shell is not the only reason hypervalence is rare in period 2 (packing more than four atoms around a small atom is hard), and that MO theory describes hypervalent compounds without d orbitals.
- Bent's rule and hybridization energy: hybrid energy is the weighted average of the AO energies. For carbon the promotion energy is independent of the hybridization; for phosphorus there is little promotion energy, and hybridizing costs energy (filled 3s raised, half-filled 3p lowered), about 600 kJ/mol for P, larger for heavier congeners. This is why some lone pairs stay in a spherical, non-stereochemically-active s orbital. With high promotion energy and good overlap the hybrid is s-rich; with poor overlap and low promotion energy it is p-rich.
- In PClxF5−x the more electronegative substituent is axial and the less electronegative equatorial. Bent's rule: more electronegative substituents prefer hybrids with less s character; more electropositive substituents prefer hybrids with more s character. Take-home: explain the bond angles of CH2F2 using VSEPR and Bent's rule. PCl2F3: "which structure is favoured?" is posed on the slide.
