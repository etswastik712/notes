# Acids and Bases: Lecture 2

## Covered
**Brønsted acids: three classes** (Classroom: Acids and Bases Lecture 2)
- Aqua acid: the acidic proton is on a water molecule, e.g. $[\mathrm{Fe(H_2O)_6}]^{3+} + \mathrm{H_2O} \rightleftharpoons [\mathrm{Fe(OH_2)_5(OH)}]^{2+} + \mathrm{H_3O^+}$.
- Hydroxo acid: the acidic proton is on an OH with no neighbouring oxo group, e.g. Si(OH)4.
- Oxo acid: the acidic proton is on an OH with an oxo group on the same atom, e.g. H2SO4, H3PO4.
- Aqua acids: central atoms in low oxidation state (s, d block; less p block). Strength increases with increasing positive charge and decreasing ionic radius of the metal ion. Exception: with increasing covalency (better M–L orbital overlap, left to right and down), aqua acids get stronger.
- Oxo acids: central element in a high oxidation state. Strength depends on the inductive effect of the central atom on the OH group:
  - (a) electronegativity of the central atom: HClO4, HNO3 strongest; H2SO4 slightly weaker; H3PO4 and H2CO3 weak; H3BO3 very weak.
  - (b) inductive effect: CH3COOH very weak; CClH2COOH and CCl2HCOOH intermediate; CCl3COOH strongest.
- Substituted oxo acids: O2SF(OH) (fluorosulfonic) is stronger than O2S(NH2)(OH) (aminosulfonic). F is highly electronegative and gives S a higher effective positive charge; CF3SO3H likewise. NH2 donates lone-pair density to S by π bonding, lowering S's positive charge and weakening the acid.
- The slides pose, unanswered: explain the non-acidic proton in H3PO3 and its anomaly.

**Lewis acids and bases.** A Lewis acid is an e⁻-pair acceptor and a Lewis base an e⁻-pair donor. Five cases: (1) an incomplete octet completes by accepting a pair; (2) a metal cation bonds to a base in a coordination compound; (3) a complete-octet species rearranges its valence electrons to accept more; (4) a species expands its valence shell (or is large enough) to accept a pair; (5) a closed-shell molecule uses an unoccupied antibonding MO (π*) to accept a pair.

**Periodic trends in Lewis acidity**
- BX3, AlX3: incomplete octets; the vacant p orbital perpendicular to the molecular plane accepts a lone pair. Acidity of boron halides: BF3 < BCl3 < BBr3, the order of stability of their complexes with :NMe3. This is opposite to the electronegativity expectation. F⁻ withdraws density, but 2p–2p overlap (orbital matching) gives F→B π bonding that fills the empty B 2p and partly saturates it. BF3 is still a good, convenient Lewis acid (gas, soluble in ether as BF3·OEt2; the slide writes "OEt3").
- Aluminium halides: dimers (Al2Cl6) in the gas phase; each Al acts as acid to a bridging Cl of the other. They are Lewis acid catalysts (alkylation, acylation).
- Si, Sn: valence-shell expansion and hypervalence. SiF4 + 2HF → [SiF6]²⁻ + 2H⁺ (Ge similarly). F⁻ from HF can displace O²⁻ from Si (glass corrosion). Acidity of SiX4: SiI4 < SiBr4 < SiCl4 < SiF4, following halogen electron-withdrawing power. SnCl2 is both acid and base: it adds Cl⁻ to give SnCl3⁻, which can donate to a metal cluster, e.g. (CO)5Mn–SnCl3.
- Group 15: SbF5 is among the most important Lewis acids and gives some of the strongest Brønsted acids. A superacid protonates almost any organic compound; it is made by dissolving SbF5 in HSO3F and SO3.
- SO2 is both acid and base. As a base it donates O or S lone pairs: O-bound to SbF5 (F5Sb–OSO), S-bound to Ru(III). SO3 is a strong acid and weak base; its acidity shows in oleum formation with H2SO4.
- Halogens (I2, Br2, Cl2, F2) are Lewis acids. I2 is violet in solid, gas and non-donor solvents (CHCl3), but brown in H2O, propanol, EtOH, or CHCl3 with added acetone: a 1:1 complex forms. The colour comes from donation into the low-lying σ* (charge-transfer transition). I2 is itself coloured because HOMO electrons are excited into the σ*. I3⁻ is an I2 (acid) + I⁻ (base) complex (iodometric titration).

**Fundamental reaction types**
- Displacement: one Lewis base displaces another. All Brønsted proton transfers are displacements, e.g. $\mathrm{HS^-(aq)} + \mathrm{H_2O} \to \mathrm{S^{2-}(aq)} + \mathrm{H_3O^+(aq)}$; an acid can displace an acid too.
- Metathesis: $\mathrm{Me_3Si{-}I + AgBr \to Me_3Si{-}Br + AgI}$. Here Br⁻ displaces I⁻, assisted by I⁻ as a good leaving group towards salt formation.
