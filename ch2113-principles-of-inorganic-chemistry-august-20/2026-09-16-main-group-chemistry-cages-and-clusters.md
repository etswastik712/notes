# Main Group Chemistry: Cages and Clusters

## Covered
- Periodic trends (Classroom: Main group chemistry cages and clusters): down a group, size increases and ionization energy, electron affinity and electronegativity decrease. Across a period, ionization energy, electron affinity and electronegativity increase and size decreases. Effective nuclear charge changes smoothly within a period, but periods differ in length (8, 18, 32). The first row (H–Ne) differs sharply from Na–Ar (the slide suggests: lighter elements use only 1s, 2s, 2p).
- Group 13 (B, Al, Ga, In, Tl): B is the only non-metal, Al the most abundant; metallic character rises B → Tl. Electronegativity alternates (Ga > Al). B resembles Si diagonally: both give acidic oxides (B2O3, SiO2) while Al2O3 is amphoteric; both form polymeric oxides and glasses (borosilicate glass); both form flammable gaseous hydrides while AlH3 is a solid. Most Group 13 elements are +3; Tl prefers +1 (inert pair effect).
- Topics: cages and clusters (boron hydrides, Zintl compounds, phosphorus oxides and sulfides); rings, macrocycles, polymers (B–N, P–N, S–N); chains and frameworks (Si, silicates); low-valent compounds and main-group organometallics.
- **Boranes.** Alfred Stock (1912) prepared a series including B2H6 with the first vacuum manifold (published 1933). Boiling points: B2H6 −92.5 °C, B4H10 16 °C, B5H9 58 °C, B5H11 65 °C, B6H10 108 °C, B10H14 213 °C (m.p. 99.7 °C). Lower boranes ignite in air with a green flame.
- Diborane is electron deficient: 12 valence electrons (6 from 2 B, 6 from 6 H); the 4 terminal B–H bonds use 8 electrons, leaving 4 for the two bridging B–H–B bonds (which would need 8). Explained by delocalized **3c–2e⁻** bonds: two B orbitals and one H give one bonding, one non-bonding and one antibonding orbital per B–H–B unit, and the bonding orbital takes the 2 electrons. Other bonds in higher boranes: 2c–2e⁻ B–B and 3c–2e⁻ B–B–B. An earlier model took [B2H4]²⁻ as isoelectronic with C2H4 with two H⁺ above and below the plane.
- Higher boranes: made from diborane by controlled pyrolysis or by reaction with hard and soft bases.
- **Wade's rules (1971).** Boranes and carboranes form deltahedra (all faces triangular), classed as closo (complete deltahedron), nido (missing one vertex), arachno (two), hypho (three).

  | Formula | Class | Skeletal electron pairs | Examples |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | $[\mathrm{B}_n\mathrm{H}_n]^{2-}$ or $\mathrm{B}_n\mathrm{H}_{n+2}$ | closo | $n+1$ | B6H6²⁻, B12H12²⁻ |
  | $\mathrm{B}_n\mathrm{H}_{n+4}$ | nido | $n+2$ | B2H6, B5H9, B10H14 |
  | $\mathrm{B}_n\mathrm{H}_{n+6}$ | arachno | $n+3$ | B4H10 |
  | $\mathrm{B}_n\mathrm{H}_{n+8}$ | hypho | $n+4$ | B5H12 (slide) |

  Each BH unit gives 2 skeletal bonding electrons; each additional H gives 1; ionic charges count.
- **Zintl compounds and clusters.** An electropositive element (alkali, alkaline earth, main-group metal) with an anionic element of moderate electronegativity gives polyanions. First evidence: Joannis, 1891, sodium in liquid ammonia with metals (blue solution; with excess Pb new solids NaPb4 or NaPb2). Solid derivatives were only isolated in the last ~40 years. Intact cage anions can be extracted by complexing the cation, e.g. Na⁺ of Na2Sn5 with cryptand as [NaCrypt]2Sn5.
- Wade's rules for ligand-free clusters: put one outward lone pair on each of the $n$ atoms; then $g - 2n$ electrons remain for the skeleton ($g$ = total valence electrons). It also works if some atoms carry ligands and others lone pairs.
- Phosphorus: white phosphorus from phosphate rock, 1200–1500 °C: $2\mathrm{Ca_3(PO_4)_2} + 6\mathrm{SiO_2} + 10\mathrm{C} \to \mathrm{P_4} + 10\mathrm{CO} + 6\mathrm{CaSiO_3}$. The P oxides and sulfides, and the reactions of P4O6 and P4S10, are in image-only slides.
