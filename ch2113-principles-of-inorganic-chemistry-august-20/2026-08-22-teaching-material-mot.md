# Teaching Material: MOT

## Covered
From Molecular Orbital Theory_Part 1.pdf (Classroom): diatomic molecules.
- Order of treatment: homonuclear diatomics, then heteronuclear diatomics, then polyatomics; it can also be applied to molecular fragments.
- Orbital approximation: $\psi = \psi(1)\psi(2)\cdots\psi(N_e)$, each electron in a one-electron molecular orbital; $\psi^2$ gives that electron's probability distribution, which is large where the amplitude is large and zero at nodes.
- LCAO: near a nucleus the wavefunction resembles that atom's orbital, so an MO is a weighted sum of atomic orbitals, $\psi = c_A\chi_A + c_B\chi_B$. $c_A^2$ and $c_B^2$ are the probabilities of finding the electron in $\chi_A$ and $\chi_B$.
- Probability density: $\psi^2 = c_A^2\chi_A^2 + 2c_Ac_B\chi_A\chi_B + c_B^2\chi_B^2$; the middle term is the interference contribution.
- H2: equal contribution from each 1s gives $c_A^2 = c_B^2$, so $c_A = \pm c_B$ and $\psi_\pm = \chi_A \pm \chi_B$ (unnormalised). Relative signs set the orbital energies. Constructive interference gives a bonding orbital; destructive interference gives an antibonding orbital with a nodal surface between the nuclei.
- H2 data: spectroscopic bonding→antibonding gap 11.4 eV; dissociation energy 4.5 eV (434 kJ/mol).
- Li2 to N2 and O2, F2: eight MOs from four atomic orbitals per atom (2s, 2px, 2py, 2pz): 4 σ-type and 4 π-type. The 4 σ orbitals span a range (one strongly bonding, one strongly antibonding, two in between); the 4 π orbitals form two doubly degenerate pairs (bonding and antibonding). The N2 diagram has the order 1σg, 1σu, 1πu, 2σg, 1πg, 2σu; the ordering differs for O2 and F2 (diagrams shown as images).
- Heteronuclear diatomics: unequal AO contributions, $\psi = c_A\chi_A + c_B\chi_B + \dots$. The more electronegative atom contributes more to the bonding MOs, the less electronegative atom more to the antibonding MOs. Energy mismatch of the AOs gives weaker overlap, but A–B bonds are not always weaker than A–A (orbital size and closeness of approach matter): CO bond enthalpy 1070 kJ/mol vs isoelectronic N2 946 kJ/mol.
- HF: three σ orbitals from H1s, F2s and F2pz. F2px and F2py have no matching orbitals and are non-bonding (π symmetry). No inversion centre, so g/u labels are not used. Of 8 valence electrons, two occupy the bonding 2σ and six occupy the largely non-bonding 1σ and 1π orbitals, all mainly on F, so HF is polar with partial negative charge on F.
- CO: 1σ is localised on O (O2s), non-bonding; 2σ is bonding (C2s + C2pz + O2pz); 1π is bonding, mainly O2p; 3σ is largely non-bonding and is the HOMO, with C2s character; 2π is the LUMO, with C2p character. HOMO and LUMO both on C explain coordination to metals through C (σ-donation plus π-back-donation).
- ICl: left as an exercise.
