# Molecular Orbital Theory, Part 2 (polyatomic molecules)

## Covered
- MOT suits triatomics, finite groups of atoms and near-infinite arrays. It resembles the diatomic case; the difference is a larger basis set: from $N$ atomic orbitals, $N$ molecular orbitals can be built. (Classroom: MOT Part 2)
- An MO of a given symmetry is a sum over all overlapping AOs of appropriate symmetry: $\psi = \sum_i c_i \chi_i$.
- Rules from the slides:
  1. The more nodes an MO has, the greater its antibonding character (the slide adds "and higher the symmetry", as written).
  2. Orbitals built from lower-energy AOs lie lower (s below p in a given shell).
  3. Interactions between non-nearest neighbours are weakly bonding or weakly antibonding, depending on the signs of the lobes.
- Hypothetical linear H3 (H_A, H_B, H_C, each $\chi_{1s}$): 1σ is the in-phase combination $\chi_A + \sqrt2\chi_B + \chi_C$ (slide's coefficients), bonding between A–B and B–C. 2σ is $\chi_A - \chi_C$, with negligible interaction with B. 3σ is the alternating combination, antibonding between A–B and B–C. For the triangular H3 (the "a / e" diagram) 1σ is bonding between all pairs. 2σ and 3σ then have the same energy, because 2σ is antibonding for A–C (energy rises) and 3σ is bonding for A–C (energy falls).
- σ and π are not strictly appropriate labels in polyatomic systems, but are convenient for the local shape of an orbital: the language of VBT survives in MOT.
- NH3: N 2s and 2p_z with the three H 1s give three σ MOs along the axis (1a1, 2a1, 3a1). N 2p_x and 2p_y match H 1s combinations of e symmetry (1e, 2e). Eight electrons fill in order of increasing energy.
- NH3 photoelectron spectrum (21 eV radiation): the 11 eV and 16 eV peaks are assigned to 2a1 and 1e. 1a1 is out of range. The HOMO is 2a1, largely confined to N and contributing little to bonding, consistent with the lone pair's role in the shape. The vibrational structure of the 11 eV band comes from the shape change on removing a lone-pair electron.
- Hypervalence in MOT (SF6): VBT invokes d orbitals; MOT does not need them. Six F ligand-group orbitals fall into a, e and t sets (non-degenerate, doubly and triply degenerate). S 3s + ligand a gives one bonding and one non-bonding orbital. S 3p + ligand t gives two triply degenerate sets. The ligand e set has no match, so it is non-bonding. The 12 valence electrons occupy bonding (1a1, 1t1, as labelled on the slide) and non-bonding (1e) orbitals; all antibonding orbitals are empty, so the molecule is stable.
