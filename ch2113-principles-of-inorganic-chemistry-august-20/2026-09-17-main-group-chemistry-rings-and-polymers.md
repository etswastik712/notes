# Main Group Chemistry: Rings and Polymers

## Covered
- Organic rings: benzene (delocalized bonding), cycloalkanes $(\mathrm{CH_2})_n$, heterocycles (about half of known organic compounds contain one). (Classroom: Main group chemistry rings, macrocycles and polymers)
- Inorganic heterocycles: complete replacement of all C atoms. Cyclo-S8 is the quintessential inorganic ring; cyclo-S$_n$ exists for $n \approx 6$–24. Cyclosilanes $(\mathrm{R_2Si})_n$ are known for $n = 3$–35. Salvarsan, an arsenic drug used early in the 20th century for syphilis, is a mixture of 3- and 5-membered rings $(\mathrm{RAs})_n$, $\mathrm{R} = 3\text{-NH_2-4-OHC_6H_3}$.
- History: cyclotriphosphazene $(\mathrm{NPCl_2})_3$ (1834) was the first inorganic heterocycle, and $\mathrm{S_4N_4}$ followed a year later. Their structures (about a century ago) raised questions about the bonding. Borazine $\mathrm{B_3N_3H_6}$ (1926, Stock and Pohland) is "inorganic benzene", with the aromaticity still debated. A long series exists for $(\mathrm{NPX_2})_n$ (X = Cl, F; $n$ = 3–40) but not for borazine.
- **Borazine.** Syntheses: $3\,\mathrm{B_2H_6} + 6\,\mathrm{NH_3} \to 2\,\mathrm{B_3N_3H_6} + 12\,\mathrm{H_2}$; $3\,\mathrm{LiBH_4} + 3\,\mathrm{NH_4Cl} \to \mathrm{B_3N_3H_6} + 3\,\mathrm{LiCl} + 9\,\mathrm{H_2}$ (more efficient); $3\,\mathrm{H_3N{\cdot}BH_3} \to \mathrm{B_3N_3H_6} + 6\,\mathrm{H_2}$; $3(\mathrm{NH_4})_2\mathrm{SO_4} + 6\,\mathrm{NaBH_4} \to 2\,\mathrm{B_3N_3H_6} + 3\,\mathrm{Na_2SO_4} + 18\,\mathrm{H_2}$. Substituted: $3\,\mathrm{BCl_3} + 3\,\mathrm{NH_4Cl} \to \mathrm{Cl_3B_3N_3H_3} + 9\,\mathrm{HCl}$ (the slide also lists 3 LiCl; as written); $\mathrm{Cl_3B_3N_3H_3} + 6\,\mathrm{NaBH_4} \to 2\,\mathrm{B_3N_3H_6} + 2\,\mathrm{B_2H_6} + 6\,\mathrm{NaCl}$; $3\,\mathrm{RBCl_2} + 6\,\mathrm{R'NH_2} \to \mathrm{R_3B_3N_3R'_3} + 3[\mathrm{R'NH_3}]\mathrm{Cl}$. It was first made by thermolysis of the diborane–ammonia adduct $[(\mathrm{BH_2})(\mathrm{NH_3})_2]^+[\mathrm{BH_4}]^-$.
- Borazine is isostructural with benzene. The B–N bond length is 1.436 Å (the slide compares benzene C–C at 1.42 Å), between B–N (1.51 Å) and B=N (1.31 Å), so there is partial delocalization of the N lone pair. It is physically like benzene but chemically very different: the electron density along B–N is uneven because of the electronegativity difference, so borazine is prone to addition reactions.
- Iminoboranes RB≡NR′ are isoelectronic with alkynes but cyclooligomerize; $(\mathrm{RB{=}NR'})_n$ rings are analogues of cyclobutadiene, benzene, cyclooctatetraene, the trimer being the most common.
- B–N polymers: low-temperature thermally induced dehydropolymerization of borazine gives a cyclo-linear polymer ($M_w \approx 7600$); pyrolysis at 1200 °C gives white boron nitride in 85–93% yield. Borazine can also grow B–N thin films. BN fibres: polyaminoborazylenes (borazines substituted with NR2) are melt-extruded then pyrolysed at 1200 °C.
- Cyclophosphazenes: bonding via the Dewar model (pπ–dπ overlap), the reactivity of the cyclotriphosphazenes, and polyphosphazene synthesis. The slide scheme: $(\mathrm{NPCl_2})_3$ heated at 210–250 °C gives $[\mathrm{NPCl_2}]_n$; with RONa (−NaCl) gives $[\mathrm{NP(OR)_2}]_n$; with RNH2 or R2NH (−HCl) gives $[\mathrm{NP(NHR)_2}]_n$ or $[\mathrm{NP(NR_2)_2}]_n$; with an organometallic RM gives P–R substituted polymer.
