## Must know
- Basic counting principle: $r$ experiments with $m_1,\dots,m_r$ outcomes give $m_1m_2\cdots m_r$ outcome $r$-tuples; $n$ distinguishable objects have $n!$ arrangements; with $k_i$ objects of kind $i$ ($\sum k_i=n$) there are $n!/(k_1!\cdots k_r!)$ (Classroom: Class notes 5th August 2026).
- $\binom nr=\dfrac{n!}{(n-r)!\,r!}$; Pascal's identity $\binom nr=\binom{n-1}r+\binom{n-1}{r-1}$; binomial theorem $(x+y)^n=\sum_k\binom nkx^ky^{n-k}$; multinomial coefficient $\dfrac{n!}{m_1!\cdots m_r!}$ for splitting $n$ items into labelled groups of sizes $m_i$, and the multinomial theorem (same notes). If the number of variables $k$ exceeds the exponent $n$, group $y=x_{n+1}+\dots+x_k$, expand $(x_1+\dots+x_n+y)^n$ and then each power of $y$ (Classroom: announcement of 2026-08-06).
- Full house (5 cards): $\binom{13}1\binom43\binom{12}1\binom42=\binom21\binom{13}2\binom43\binom42=3744$; the factor $\binom21$ chooses which of the two denominations appears 3 times. Counting cards one at a time as $\binom{52}1\binom32\binom{48}1\binom31$ overcounts (triple 3 times, pair twice) (Classroom: announcements of 2026-08-07).
- Straight flush: the Ace is low (A-2-3-4-5) or high (10-J-Q-K-A), with no wrap-around, so there are 10 rank sequences and $10\times4=40$ straight flushes (same announcements).
- Round-robin with $n$ players: $\binom n2$ games, so $2^{n(n-1)/2}$ outcomes (Classroom: Video Lecture on Tutorial 1, 2026-08-10).
- Sample space $S$ is the set of all possible outcomes of a random experiment; an event is any subset of $S$; De Morgan: $(\bigcup E_i)^c=\bigcap E_i^c$ and $(\bigcap E_i)^c=\bigcup E_i^c$ (Classroom: Class notes 6th August 2026).
- Naive definition: for finite $S$, $P[A]=|A|/|S|$; the justification is equal probability $1/N$ for each of the $N$ sample points (Classroom: Class notes 6th and 12th August 2026).
- Axioms: $0\le P(E)\le1$; $P(S)=1$; $P(\bigcup E_i)=\sum P(E_i)$ for pairwise disjoint $E_1,E_2,\dots$. Consequences: $P(\emptyset)=0$ and finite additivity (take $E_j=\emptyset$ for $j>n$); $P(E^c)=1-P(E)$ and $E\subset F\Rightarrow P(E)\le P(F)$ (Classroom: Class notes 6th and 12th August 2026).
- $P(E\cup F)=P(E)+P(F)-P(E\cap F)$, and inclusion-exclusion for $n$ events: $P(\bigcup E_i)=\sum P(E_i)-\sum_{i<j}P(E_i\cap E_j)+\cdots+(-1)^{n-1}P(\bigcap E_i)$. Two proofs: induction, or a sample point in exactly $i$ of the events is counted exactly once on the right (Classroom: Class notes 12th August 2026; practice problems 14th August, problems 1-2).
- $P[A\triangle B]=P[A]+P[B]-2P[A\cap B]$, proved by splitting $A\triangle B=(A\cap B^c)\cup(A^c\cap B)$ into disjoint pieces and using $P[A\cap B^c]=P[A]-P[A\cap B]$ (Classroom: Solutions Quiz 1).
- Committee of $k$ from $n$ couples contains exactly $j$ couples with probability $\dbinom nj\dbinom{n-j}{k-2j}2^{k-2j}\Big/\dbinom{2n}k$ (Classroom: Solutions Quiz 1).
- Matching problem: for distinct $i_1,\dots,i_k$, $P[A_{i_1}\cap\cdots\cap A_{i_k}]=(n-k)!/n!$, so $P[\text{no one gets own coat}]=\sum_{i=0}^n(-1)^i\binom ni\frac{(n-i)!}{n!}$ (Classroom: Class notes 12th August 2026, updated version).
- Conditional probability: $P[E\mid F]=P[E\cap F]/P[F]$ for $P[F]>0$; in equally likely settings one may work in the reduced sample space (Classroom: Class notes 13th August 2026). For fixed $F$ with $P[F]>0$, $A\mapsto P[A\mid F]$ satisfies the three axioms of probability (Classroom: Class notes 19th August 2026).
- Multiplication rule: $P[\bigcap_{i=1}^nE_i]=P[E_1]P[E_2\mid E_1]P[E_3\mid E_1\cap E_2]\cdots P[E_n\mid\bigcap_{i<n}E_i]$ (needs $P[E_i]>0$ for each $i$); total probability: $P[E]=P[E\mid F]P[F]+P[E\mid F^c](1-P[F])$ (Classroom: Class notes 13th and 19th August 2026).
- Bayes for a partition: if $E_1,\dots,E_n$ are pairwise disjoint with union $\Omega$, then $P[F]=\sum_jP[F\mid E_j]P[E_j]$ and $P[E_i\mid F]=\dfrac{P[F\mid E_i]P[E_i]}{\sum_jP[F\mid E_j]P[E_j]}$ (Classroom: Class notes 19th August 2026).
- Exchangeability in the urn ($b$ black, $w$ white, draws without replacement): any colour sequence $C_1,\dots,C_k$ with exactly $\ell$ blacks has probability $\dfrac{b(b-1)\cdots(b-\ell+1)\,w(w-1)\cdots(w-k+\ell+1)}{(b+w)(b+w-1)\cdots(b+w-k+1)}$, independent of the order (same notes). So $P[\text{1st black}\mid k\text{ black among }n]$ can be computed by symmetry, or as $P[B\mid B_k]=P[B_k\mid B]P[B]/P[B_k]$ (Classroom: practice problems 21st August, problem 1).
- Independence of two events ($P[F]>0$): $P[E\mid F]=P[E]$, equivalently $P[E\cap F]=P[E]P[F]$; if $E,F$ are independent so are $E,F^c$ (Classroom: Class notes 19th August 2026).
- Independence of $E_1,\dots,E_r$: for every non-empty subset $\{i_1,\dots,i_s\}$, $P[\bigcap_jE_{i_j}]=\prod_jP[E_{i_j}]$; an infinite family is independent if every finite subfamily is. Pairwise independence is not enough (the three dice events $A,B,C$ of 19 August).
- For independent $E_1,\dots,E_n$, $P[\bigcap E_i]=\prod P[E_i]$ (derived via the chain rule; Classroom: practice problems 21st August, problem 6).
- A random variable is a (real-valued) function of the outcome of a random experiment; discrete if it takes at most countably many values; pmf $p(x)=P[X=x]$ and $\sum_ip(x_i)=1$ (Classroom: Class notes 20th August).
- cdf $F(a)=P[X\le a]=\sum_{x\le a}p(x)$; monotone increasing, right-continuous, $F\to0$ at $-\infty$ and $\to1$ at $+\infty$ (proofs of the last two are not in the formal course material).
- $P[X=x]=F(x)-F(x^-)$, where $F(x^-)=\lim_{y\to x^-}F(y)=P[X<x]$ (Classroom: practice sheet of 17 September).
- $E[X]=\sum_ix_ip(x_i)$.
- $\text{Bernoulli}(p)$ is $\text{Binomial}(1,p)$; if $X_i$ are independent Bernoulli($p$) indicators, $\sum_{i=1}^nX_i\sim\text{Binomial}(n,p)$, with $E=np$.
- Binomial arises from sampling with replacement; hypergeometric (urn with $w$ white, $b$ black, $n$ drawn, $X$ = number of white) from sampling without replacement. Discrete uniform on finite $\Omega$: $P[X=x]=1/|\Omega|$ (Classroom: Class notes 20th August).
- Poisson($\lambda$): $P[X=n]=e^{-\lambda}\lambda^n/n!$, $n\in\mathbb N_0$; it is the limit of $\text{Binomial}(n,c/n)$. Moment recursion: $E[X^n]=\lambda E[(X+1)^{n-1}]$ (Classroom: mid-semester solutions, problem 1).
- pmf of $Y=g(X)$: $P[Y=y]=\sum_{i:\,g(x_i)=y}p(x_i)$.
- $E[g(X)]=\sum_ig(x_i)p(x_i)$ (the proof is not examined, the formula is); $E[aX+b]=aE[X]+b$.
- Joint pmf $p(a_i,b_j)=P[X_1=a_i,X_2=b_j]$; $X_1,X_2$ independent iff $P[X_1\le x_1,X_2\le x_2]=P[X_1\le x_1]P[X_2\le x_2]$ for all $x_1,x_2$ (discrete: iff the joint pmf factorises). Equal distribution does not mean equal random variables (two dice rolls $X_1,X_2$).
- Indicator $\mathbf 1_A$ is $1$ if $A$ happens, else $0$.
- Geometric($p$): tosses until the first head, $P[X=i]=(1-p)^{i-1}p$, $P[X>i]=(1-p)^i$; memoryless: $P[X>m+n\mid X>m]=P[X>n]$ (Classroom: Quiz 3 solutions).
- Negative Binomial($k,p$): tosses until the $k$-th head, a sum of $k$ independent Geometric($p$) variables; $P[\text{NegBin}(r,p)>n]=P[\text{Bin}(n,p)<r]$ (Classroom: Quiz 3 solutions).
- Negative Binomial pmf: $P(X=n)=\binom{n-1}{m-1}p^m(1-p)^{n-m}$ for $n\ge m$; with $f=n-m$ failures it is $\binom{m+f-1}{f}p^mq^f$, the coefficient of $q^f$ in $p^m(1-q)^{-m}$ (Classroom: negative_binomial_distribution.pdf).
- Linearity: $E[X_1+X_2]=E[X_1]+E[X_2]$ for any random variables on the same sample space, independent or not (also for continuous or mixed variables).
- $n$-th raw moment $E[X^n]=\sum_ix_i^np(x_i)$; $n$-th central moment $E[(X-E[X])^n]$.
- $\operatorname{Var}[X]=E[(X-E[X])^2]=E[X^2]-(E[X])^2$; $\operatorname{Var}[aX+b]=a^2\operatorname{Var}[X]$; standard deviation $=\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}[X]}$.
- Bayes' rule with total probability: $P[K\mid R]=\dfrac{P[R\mid K]P[K]}{P[R\mid K]P[K]+P[R\mid K^c]P[K^c]}$ (Classroom: mid-semester solutions, problem 4).
- $X$ is continuous if its cdf is absolutely continuous (so continuous); equivalently there is a pdf $f\ge0$ with $P[X\in B]=\int_Bf(x)\,dx$ (Classroom: Class notes 10th September 2026).
- A pdf satisfies $f\ge0$ and $\int_{-\infty}^\infty f(x)\,dx=1$; it is required to be continuous except at finitely many points (Classroom: week1_mt2113.pdf).
- For continuous $X$: $P[X=x]=0$ for every $x$, because $P[X=x]=F(x)-F(x^-)=0$ when $F$ is continuous; so $P[a<X<b]=P[a\le X\le b]=\int_a^bf(x)\,dx$.
- $F(x)=\int_{-\infty}^xf(y)\,dy$ and $F'(x)=f(x)$; to find a distribution, find the cdf first and differentiate.
- Continuous $X$: $E[X]=\int xf(x)\,dx$; $E[g(X)]=\int g(x)f(x)\,dx$ (LOTUS; proved in class for $g\ge0$); $\operatorname{Var}[X]=\int(x-\mu)^2f(x)\,dx=E[X^2]-(E[X])^2$.
- Lemma: for a non-negative random variable $Y$, $E(Y)=\int_0^\infty P(Y>y)\,dy$ (proved by interchanging the order of integration) (Classroom: week1_mt2113.pdf).
- $\text{Unif}(a,b)$: $f=1/(b-a)$ on $[a,b]$, $E=(a+b)/2$, $\operatorname{Var}=(b-a)^2/12$.
- Standard normal $\varphi(x)=e^{-x^2/2}/\sqrt{2\pi}$, with $\int e^{-x^2/2}dx=\sqrt{2\pi}$ (via $I^2$ in polar coordinates); $E(Z)=0$, $\operatorname{Var}(Z)=1$. $X\sim N(\mu,\sigma)$ iff $X=\sigma Z+\mu$; then $E(X)=\mu$, $\operatorname{Var}(X)=\sigma^2$.
- Rayleigh: $f(x)=xe^{-x^2/2}$ for $x>0$, $F(x)=1-e^{-x^2/2}$, $E=\sqrt{\pi/2}$. Logistic: $f(x)=e^x/(1+e^x)^2$, $F(x)=e^x/(1+e^x)$, $E=0$.
- Median: $c$ is a median of $X$ if $P(X\le c)\ge\tfrac12$ and $P(X\ge c)\ge\tfrac12$ (the instructor corrected the board, which had $P(X\ge c)\le\tfrac12$). For a discrete $X$ with values $x_1<\dots<x_n$, if $p_1+\dots+p_{i-1}<\tfrac12\le p_1+\dots+p_i$, then $x_i$ is a median (the smallest that works), since $p_i+\dots+p_n=1-(p_1+\dots+p_{i-1})\ge\tfrac12$ (Classroom: announcement of 2026-10-22).
- Skewness is the standardised third central moment and kurtosis the fourth; higher moments matter e.g. for the Lyapunov condition in central limit theorems (Classroom: announcement of 2026-09-15).
- Order statistics (HW5, stated as a problem to prove): for i.i.d. $X_1,X_2$ with pdf $f$, $Y_1=\min$, $Y_2=\max$, the joint pdf is $f_{Y_1,Y_2}(y_1,y_2)=2f(y_1)f(y_2)$ for $y_1<y_2$; for $n$ i.i.d. variables sorted as $Y_1<\dots<Y_n$, $f_{Y_1,\dots,Y_n}=n!\,f(y_1)\cdots f(y_n)$ (Classroom: hw5.pdf).
- For $X_1,X_2\sim\text{Expo}(\lambda)$ independent: $Y_1=\min\sim\text{Expo}(2\lambda)$ (done in class); HW5 asks to show, via the change of variables $(Y_1,Y_2)\to(Y_1,Y_2-Y_1)$, that $Y_2-Y_1\sim\text{Expo}(\lambda)$ and is independent of $Y_1$ (Classroom: hw5.pdf).
- Multinomial $\text{Mult}_k(n,\vec p)$ via indicators $Y_{ij}$ (outcome $i$ in trial $j$): $E(Y_{ij})=p_i$, $\operatorname{Cov}(Y_{ij},Y_{kj})=-p_ip_k$ ($i\ne k$), $p_i(1-p_i)$ ($i=k$); $X_i=\sum_jY_{ij}$, $E(X_i)=np_i$, $\operatorname{Var}(X_i)=np_i(1-p_i)$, $\operatorname{Cov}(X_i,X_k)=-np_ip_k$; covariance matrix $n\operatorname{diag}(\vec p)-n\vec p\vec p^{\,T}$ (Classroom: hw5.pdf, as problems to show).
- In proofs, use only results fully proved in class, and state them with complete clarity (Quiz 2 solutions marking: a non-rigorous remark earns no points).
- A solution may use mgfs as a concept, but the mgf of a known distribution (e.g. binomial) must be derived before use, or points are lost (Classroom, 2026-09-17).
- $\operatorname{Var}\big[\sum X_i\big]=\sum\operatorname{Var}[X_i]$ for independent $X_i$ is not proved in this course, so it cannot be used in solutions (Classroom: practice sheet, problem 5).
