# Week 5 notes (appended to HW5)

## Covered
Source: Week5_notes.pdf, 9 handwritten pages, transcribed by Kurooto's handwriting reader (not by text extraction), so check formulas against the file. Pages 1-2 repeat the HW5 sheet (see [[introduction-to-probability/2026-11-05-hw5]]); pages 3-9 are "Summary of Week 5 Lectures".

**Covariance matrix.** For a random variable $X$, $\operatorname{Var}(X)=E((X-E X)^2)=E(X^2)-E(X)^2$. For a vector $X=(X_1,\dots,X_n)$ the analogue is the $n\times n$ matrix $\Sigma=(\operatorname{cov}(X_i,X_j))_{ij}$, where $\operatorname{cov}(X,Y)=E((X-E X)(Y-E Y))$. Then
$$\operatorname{Var}(a_1X_1+\dots+a_nX_n)=a^T\Sigma a=\sum_i a_i^2\operatorname{Var}(X_i)+2\sum_{i<j}a_ia_j\operatorname{cov}(X_i,X_j).$$
For $n=2$: $\operatorname{Var}(aX+bY)=a^2\operatorname{Var}X+b^2\operatorname{Var}Y+2ab\operatorname{cov}(X,Y)$.

**$|\operatorname{corr}|\le1$ (first proof).** Put $t=b/a$ and $f(t)=\operatorname{Var}X+t^2\operatorname{Var}Y+2t\operatorname{cov}(X,Y)\ge0$. Then $f'(t)=0$ at $t_0=-\operatorname{cov}(X,Y)/\operatorname{Var}Y$, and $f''=2\operatorname{Var}Y>0$, so $t_0$ is the global minimum. Its value is $f(t_0)=\operatorname{Var}X\,[1-\operatorname{corr}(X,Y)^2]\ge0$, where $\operatorname{corr}(X,Y):=\operatorname{cov}(X,Y)/(\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}X}\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}Y})$. Hence $|\operatorname{corr}(X,Y)|\le1$. (The transcription of the middle line of $f(t_0)$ is garbled; the end result above is what the page states.)

**$|\operatorname{corr}|\le1$ (second proof, via eigenvalues).** A real symmetric $n\times n$ matrix $A$ has an orthogonal $O$ ($O^T=O^{-1}$) with $O^TAO$ diagonal, with real entries $\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_n$; the Hermitian version uses a unitary $U$. The instructor remarks that this underlies the quantum-mechanics axiom that observables are Hermitian operators. Since $a^T\Sigma a\ge0$ for all $a$, putting $b=Oa$ gives $\sum\lambda_ib_i^2\ge0$ for all $b$, so all $\lambda_i\ge0$ (the covariance matrix is positive semidefinite). For $n=2$ the eigenvalues are
$$\frac{(\operatorname{Var}X+\operatorname{Var}Y)\pm\sqrt{(\operatorname{Var}X+\operatorname{Var}Y)^2-4(\operatorname{Var}X\operatorname{Var}Y-\operatorname{cov}(X,Y)^2)}}{2},$$
both $\ge0$ iff $\operatorname{cov}(X,Y)^2\le\operatorname{Var}X\operatorname{Var}Y$, i.e. $|\operatorname{corr}(X,Y)|\le1$.

**Independence and covariance.** If $X,Y$ are independent then $E(XY)=E(X)E(Y)$, so $\operatorname{cov}(X,Y)=0$. The converse is false: $\operatorname{cov}(X,Y)=0$ does not imply independence.

**Lemma (max and min of independent variables).** If $X_1,\dots,X_n$ are independent, $Y_1=\min$, $Y_n=\max$, then
$$P(Y_n\le y)=\prod_iP(X_i\le y),\qquad P(Y_1\ge y)=\prod_iP(X_i\ge y).$$
Proof: $\max\le y$ iff every $X_i\le y$; $\min\ge y$ iff every $X_i\ge y$; then use independence.

**HW5 Q2 (a) and (e).** For $X_i\sim\text{Expo}(\lambda_i)$, $P(Y_1\ge y)=e^{-\lambda_1y}e^{-\lambda_2y}=e^{-(\lambda_1+\lambda_2)y}$, so $Y_1\sim\text{Expo}(\lambda_1+\lambda_2)$ (with equal rates, $\text{Expo}(2\lambda)$). Assuming $Y_2-Y_1\sim\text{Expo}(\lambda)$ and independent of $Y_1$ (parts (c), (d)):
- $\operatorname{Var}(Y_2)=\operatorname{Var}(Y_2-Y_1)+\operatorname{Var}(Y_1)+2\operatorname{cov}(Y_1,Y_2-Y_1)=\dfrac1{\lambda^2}+\dfrac1{4\lambda^2}=\dfrac5{4\lambda^2}$ (the covariance term is $0$ by independence);
- $\operatorname{Var}(Y_1)=\dfrac1{4\lambda^2}$;
- $\operatorname{cov}(Y_1,Y_2)=\operatorname{cov}(Y_1,Y_2-Y_1)+\operatorname{cov}(Y_1,Y_1)=0+\dfrac1{4\lambda^2}$;
- $\operatorname{corr}(Y_1,Y_2)=\dfrac{1/(4\lambda^2)}{\sqrt{5/(4\lambda^2)}\sqrt{1/(4\lambda^2)}}=\dfrac1{\sqrt5}$.

Note on the sheet: Q2(e) is printed as "$\operatorname{var}(Y_1)=5/4\lambda^2$", but the worked solution gives $\operatorname{Var}(Y_1)=1/(4\lambda^2)$ and $\operatorname{Var}(Y_2)=5/(4\lambda^2)$. The value $5/(4\lambda^2)$ belongs to $Y_2$. This resolves the doubt flagged in [[introduction-to-probability/2026-11-05-hw5]]. (Parts (b)-(d), the pdf of $(Y_1,Y_2-Y_1)$ and independence, are assumed here, not derived on these pages.)

**Multinomial distribution.** $X\sim\text{Mult}_k(n,\vec p)$, $p_i\ge0$, $\sum p_i=1$:
$$P(X_1=n_1,\dots,X_k=n_k)=\frac{n!}{n_1!\cdots n_k!}p_1^{n_1}\cdots p_k^{n_k},\quad n_1+\dots+n_k=n.$$
For $k=2$ this is the binomial pmf. With $Y_{i\mu}=1$ if outcome $i$ appears on trial $\mu$ (else $0$), $X_i=\sum_{\mu=1}^nY_{i\mu}$ and $Y_{i\mu}\sim\text{Bern}(p_i)$. Variables from different trials ($\mu\ne\nu$) are independent, so $X_i\sim\text{Bin}(n,p_i)$. Second proof of the marginal: summing the multinomial pmf over $n_1+\dots+n_{k-1}=n-t$ gives
$$P(X_k=t)=\binom nt p_k^t(p_1+\dots+p_{k-1})^{n-t}=\binom nt p_k^t(1-p_k)^{n-t}.$$

**Change of variables for continuous distributions.** For $Y=g(X)$ with $g:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ invertible:
$$P(y\le Y\le y+\delta y)=P(X\in g^{-1}[y,y+\delta y])\ \Rightarrow\ f_Y(y)=f_X(g^{-1}(y))\left|\frac{d}{dy}g^{-1}(y)\right|=f_X(g^{-1}(y))\left|\frac{dg}{dx}\Big|_{x=g^{-1}(y)}\right|^{-1}.$$
For vectors, $f_Y(\vec y)=f_X(\vec g^{-1}(\vec y))\,\left|\det\dfrac{\partial\vec g^{-1}}{\partial\vec y}(\vec y)\right|$, equivalently with $\left|\det\dfrac{\partial\vec g}{\partial\vec x}\right|^{-1}$ evaluated at $\vec x=\vec g^{-1}(\vec y)$.
