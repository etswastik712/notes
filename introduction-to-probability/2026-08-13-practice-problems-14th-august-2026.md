# Practice problems 14 August 2026

## Covered
Six problems "after the second week of class" for the 14 August tutorial (the day of Quiz 1, so the list was kept short and the TAs had little time). The instructor said the Monty Hall problem (his version) was meant as motivation for why Bayes' theorem is studied; Bayes' theorem will be done formally with more examples in class on Wednesday of the following week, with more conditional-probability practice then.

| # | Problem | What it practises |
|---|---------|-------------------|
| 1 | Prove inclusion-exclusion for $n$ events by induction (base cases $n=2,3$ shown in class) | induction on [[introduction-to-probability/2026-08-11-class-notes-12th-august-2026]] (1.1) |
| 2 | For finite equally likely $\Omega$: each sample point $\omega$ contributes exactly once to both sides if $\omega\in\bigcup A_i$, and to neither otherwise | counting proof of inclusion-exclusion |
| 3 | Men who smoke are 23 times more likely to develop lung cancer than non-smokers; 21.6% of men smoke. Find $P[\text{smoker}\mid\text{lung cancer}]$. Hint: $A$ = smoker, $B$ = cancer, $P[A]=0.216$, $P[B\mid A]=23\,P[B\mid A^c]$, find $P[A\mid B]$ | Bayes with a ratio of likelihoods |
| 4 | Monty Hall: write the solution clearly and concisely, defining all events | conditional probability, total probability |
| 5 | Disease affects 1% of the population; test is right 95% of the time in both directions. Find $P[\text{disease}\mid\text{positive}]$. Hint: $P[A]=1/100$, $B$ = positive; translate the givens into $A$, $B$ first | Bayes, base-rate effect |
| 6 | Fair coin and a coin with $P[\text{head}]=3/4$; pick one at random, flip 3 times, all heads. Probability it is the fair coin | Bayes with repeated independent trials |
