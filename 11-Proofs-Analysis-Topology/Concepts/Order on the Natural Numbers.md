# Order on the Natural Numbers

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-beyond">extra · beyond</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** [[L-25.pdf#page=1|Lecture 25]]: the definition $a<b\iff b=a+x$, Theorem 5, the link to PMI $\Leftrightarrow$ WOP, trichotomy in the list of further results.
> 🔵 **extra · beyond:** proofs of exercises (d), (e) and of $a+z\ne a$ (mine).

> [!intuition] In one picture
> $a<b$ means you can walk from $a$ to $b$ by taking at least one step. There is no room between $a$ and its successor, which is exactly why a least element always exists.

> [!key] Definition
> $a<b:\iff b=a+x$ for some $x\in N$; $a\le b:\iff a<b$ or $a=b$. **Theorem 5:** $a<b\Rightarrow a+1\le b$ (write $x=y+1$ by Lemma 1, then $b=(a+1)+y$). Also: no $n<1$; $a<b$ and $b<a$ cannot both hold (since $a+z\ne a$); exactly one of $a<b$, $a=b$, $b<a$; $<$ is transitive and preserved by adding $c$.

Lemma 1 and Theorem 5 are exactly the two facts used to prove the **well-ordering principle from induction**, so PMI $\Leftrightarrow$ WOP rests only on (P1)–(P3) and the definitions of $+$ and $<$.
