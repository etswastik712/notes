# Well-Ordered Set

<span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟡 **extra · book:** Tao *Analysis I* Definition 8.5.8; the special case $\mathbb N$ is the taught [[Well-Ordering Principle]].

> [!intuition] In one picture
> A line of people in which any non-empty group has a first person.

> [!key] Definition
> A total order in which every **non-empty** subset has a **least** element. $\mathbb N$ and its subsets, finite total orders, $\{1,\frac12,\frac13,\dots\}$ with $\ge$: yes. $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q_{>0},\mathbb R$, $\mathbb N$ with $\ge$: no. Equivalent (with a little choice) to "no infinite strictly decreasing sequence". Strong induction and the recursion theorem hold on every well-ordered set.

Used in: [[PA10 Well-Ordering and Well-Ordered Pairs]]. Related: [[Well-Ordering Principle]] · [[Lexicographic Order]] · [[Partially Ordered Set]] · [[Extremal Elements and Bounds]] · [[Zorn's Lemma]].
