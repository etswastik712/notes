# Injective Surjective and Bijective Functions

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** [[Assignment-6.pdf#page=1|Assignment 6, Q3–Q9]].

> [!intuition] In one picture
> No two inputs collide / every output is hit / both.

> [!key] Definition
> **Injective:** $f(a_1)=f(a_2)\Rightarrow a_1=a_2$ (at most one preimage). **Surjective:** $\mathrm{Im}f=B$ (at least one). **Bijective:** both (exactly one); then $f^{-1}$ exists.

$x\mapsto2x$ is a bijection of $\mathbb R$; $n\mapsto2n+1$ is injective on $\mathbb N$ but misses $1,2,4,\dots$; $|x|$ is neither; $x^2$ is a bijection of $\mathbb R_{\ge0}$. Bijection $\mathbb N\to\mathbb Z$: $0,1,-1,2,-2,\dots$. Trace $M_2(\mathbb R)\to\mathbb R$ is onto, not 1-1; transpose is a bijection. For finite sets of equal size injective $\iff$ surjective $\iff$ bijective; **not** for infinite sets.
