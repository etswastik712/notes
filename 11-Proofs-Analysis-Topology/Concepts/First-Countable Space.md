# First-Countable Space

<span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟡 **extra · book:** [[John M. Lee - Introduction to Smooth Manifolds.pdf#page=618|Lee Lemma A.12 (Sequence Lemma), p. 600]]; Munkres §30.

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Near each point countably many shrinking neighbourhoods are enough, as the balls $B(x,1/n)$ in a metric space.

> [!key] Definition
> $X$ is **first-countable** if every point has a countable neighbourhood basis. Then closures and continuity are detected by sequences: $x\in\overline A$ iff some sequence in $A$ converges to $x$.

Metric spaces and manifolds are first-countable. An uncountable discrete space and the lower-limit line $\mathbb R_\ell$ are first-countable but not second-countable.

Used in: [[PA12 Separation, Countability and Local Compactness]]. Related: [[Second-Countable Space]] · [[Basis for a Topology]].
