# Hybridisation

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span> <span class="scope-extra-beyond">extra · beyond</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** [[chem pre-ms .pdf#page=14|slides 14–17]] (hybrid sets, steric number). · 🟡 **extra · book:** Shriver and Atkins ([[Shriver and Atkins Inorganic Chemistry 5th Ed.pdf#page=69|PDF p.69]]). · 🔵 **extra · beyond:** orthonormality check.

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Waves on a pond: mix an $s$ and three $p$ waves and the constructive and destructive interference gives four equivalent lobes pointing to the corners of a tetrahedron.

> [!key] Definition
> $sp^3$: $h_{1\ldots4}=\tfrac12(s\pm p_x\pm p_y\pm p_z)$ (four sign patterns), 25 % $s$, angles $\cos^{-1}(-\tfrac13)=109.47^\circ$; $sp^2$: 33.3 % $s$, 120°; $sp$: 50 % $s$, 180°; $sp^3d$ (TBP): 3 equatorial $sp^2$ + 2 axial $dp$; $sp^3d^2$: octahedron.

## Checks (computed)
$HH^{\mathsf T}=I$ for the $sp^3$ and $sp^2$ sets, all inter-hybrid angles 109.47° and 120.00° resp. A hybrid has directional character and **more amplitude between the nuclei**, so it overlaps better than a pure $s$ or $p$ orbital. The set is chosen from the steric number: $Z=2$: $sp$; 3: $sp^2$; 4: $sp^3$; 5: $sp^3d$; 6: $sp^3d^2$.

> [!pitfall] The name is not the composition
> $\mathrm{H_2O}$ is called $sp^3$ ($Z=4$) but its bonding hybrids are $sp^{3.99}$ (20 % $s$) and its lone pairs $sp^{2.3}$ (30 % $s$) ([[Coulson's Theorem]], [[Equivalent and Non-Equivalent Hybrids]]).
