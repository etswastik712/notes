# Coulson's Theorem

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-beyond">extra · beyond</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** [[chem pre-ms .pdf#page=19|slides 19–22]] (the relation, water, the take-home exercises). · 🔵 **extra · beyond:** derivation, ammonia, heavy hydrides.

> [!intuition] In one picture
> The more $s$ character a pair of equivalent hybrids has, the wider the angle between them, and orthogonality fixes the exchange rate exactly.

> [!key] Definition
> Two equivalent $s$–$p$ hybrids with $s$ fraction $S$: $\ \cos\theta=\dfrac S{S-1}=-\dfrac SP$, $\ P=1-S$. Hybridisation index $sp^n$, $n=(1-S)/S$.

## Derivation
$h_a=\sqrt S\,s+\sqrt P\,p_{\hat a}$, $h_b$ likewise; orthogonality $\langle h_a|h_b\rangle=S+P\cos\theta=0$.

## Values (computed)
| System | $S$ | Angle |
|---|---|---|
| pure $p$ | 0 | 90° |
| $sp^3$ | 0.25 | 109.47° |
| $sp^2$ | 0.333 | 120° |
| $sp$ | 0.5 | 180° |
| $\mathrm{H_2O}$ bonds | 0.2002 | 104.5° |
| $\mathrm{H_2O}$ lone pairs | 0.2998 | 115.35° |
| $\mathrm{NH_3}$ bonds (107°) | 0.2262 | 107° |
| $\mathrm{NH_3}$ lone pair | 0.3214 | n/a |

**Sum rules:** over the four orbitals $\sum S=1$ and $\sum P=3$. Take-home answers in [[CH01 VSEPR and Valence Bond Theory]] §5.

> [!pitfall] Only for $s$–$p$ sets of equivalent orbitals
> With $d$ orbitals, or when no two orbitals are equivalent (trigonal bipyramid), the calculation is harder ([[Equivalent and Non-Equivalent Hybrids]]).
