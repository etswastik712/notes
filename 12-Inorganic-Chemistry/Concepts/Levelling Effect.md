# Levelling Effect

<span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟡 **Extra · book:** S&A Ch.4 ([[Shriver and Atkins Inorganic Chemistry 5th Ed.pdf#page=138|PDF p.138 ff.]]).

> [!intuition] In one picture
> In a given solvent no acid can be stronger than the solvent's own conjugate acid, so all very strong acids look equally strong.

> [!key] Definition and key results
> In water the limit is $\mathrm{H_3O^+}$ ($\mathrm pK_a=0$) and $\mathrm{OH^-}$ ($\mathrm pK_a=14$): HClO$_4$, HCl, H$_2$SO$_4$ (1st step) are all 'completely dissociated'. Ranking them needs a weaker base as solvent (acetic acid) or an acidity function $H_0$.

Anhydrous H$_2$SO$_4$ $H_0\approx-12$; magic acid $\approx-23$ (recalled).

Used in: [[CH04 Acid-Base Concepts]] §3, §6. Related: [[Conjugate Acid-Base Pair]] · [[Superacid]].
