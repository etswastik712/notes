# Arrhenius Acid-Base Definition

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** [[chem pre-ms .pdf#page=55|slides 55–57]].

> [!intuition] In one picture
> An acid makes $\mathrm H^+$ and a base makes $\mathrm{OH^-}$ when dissolved in water; the narrowest definition.

> [!key] Definition and key results
> Acids raise $[\mathrm{H_3O^+}]$, bases raise $[\mathrm{OH^-}]$; $K_w=10^{-14}$ at 25 °C. Fails for $\mathrm{CO_2}$, $\mathrm{NH_3}$, CaO, salts, $\mathrm{AlCl_3}$ (slide 57).

It led Arrhenius to write $\mathrm{NH_4OH}$ for ammonia in water although the base is $\mathrm{NH_3}$.

Used in: [[CH04 Acid-Base Concepts]] §2. Related: [[Bronsted-Lowry Acids and Bases]] · [[Autoprotolysis of Water]].
