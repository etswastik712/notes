# Trigonal Bipyramid Site Preference

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-beyond">extra · beyond</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** [[chem pre-ms .pdf#page=5|slides 5, 26]]. · 🔵 **extra · beyond:** the 90° counts.

> [!intuition] In one picture
> A trigonal bipyramid has two kinds of site; lone pairs, multiple bonds and electropositive groups go equatorial, electronegative groups go axial.

> [!key] Definition
> **Axial** sites have 3 neighbours at 90°, **equatorial** sites 2 (and 120° contacts). Lone pairs take equatorial sites (fewest 90° contacts); more electronegative substituents take axial sites (Bent's rule: the axial $dp$ hybrids have no $s$ character).

## Counted (ll, lb) = 90° lone-pair/lone-pair and lone-pair/bond-pair contacts
1 lone pair: ax (0,3), **eq (0,2)**. 2: eq+eq (0,4), ax+eq (1,3), ax+ax (0,6): **eq+eq** (T-shaped, $\mathrm{ClF_3}$). 3: **eq,eq,eq** (0,6) (linear, $\mathrm{XeF_2}$). Octahedron with 2 lone pairs: trans (0,8) beats cis (1,6) (square planar, $\mathrm{XeF_4}$).

## Bond lengths
Axial bonds are weaker: the three equatorial $sp^2$ bonds are stronger than the two axial $dp$ bonds ([[Equivalent and Non-Equivalent Hybrids]]).
