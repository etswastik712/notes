# Equivalent and Non-Equivalent Hybrids

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** [[chem pre-ms .pdf#page=18|slides 18–22]].

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Equivalent hybrids are identical twins; non-equivalent hybrids are one family with two different jobs (bonding and lone pair, or axial and equatorial).

> [!key] Definition
> **Equivalent:** identical $s$/$p$ composition and orientation (tetrahedral and octahedral molecules). **Non-equivalent:** a molecule such as $\mathrm{H_2O}$ divides its four $sp^3$-derived orbitals into a bonding subset (80 % $p$) and a non-bonding subset (70 % $p$); a trigonal bipyramid has three strong equatorial ($sp^2$) and two weaker axial ($dp$) hybrids.

## Rules
Total $s$ character $=1$ and total $p$ character $=3$ over the four orbitals; within each subset Coulson's relation holds. For $\mathrm{AB_5}$ no unique symmetric set of equivalent orbitals exists, and TBP and square pyramid are interconvertible (slide 22). The square-pyramidal $dsp^3$ set is a square-planar $dsp^2$ set plus a $p_z$ (slide 19).

Used in: [[CH01 VSEPR and Valence Bond Theory]]. Related: [[Coulson's Theorem]] · [[Hybridisation]] · [[Trigonal Bipyramid Site Preference]].
