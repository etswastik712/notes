# Bond Order

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** [[chem pre-ms .pdf#page=35|slide 35]] (implicit) and S&A Ch.2.

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Net number of electron pairs holding the atoms together.

> [!key] Definition and key results
> $\text{bond order}=\tfrac12(n_{\rm bonding}-n_{\rm antibonding})$: $\mathrm{N_2}$ 3, $\mathrm{O_2}$ 2, $\mathrm{F_2}$ 1, $\mathrm{O_2^+}$ 2.5, $\mathrm{O_2^-}$ 1.5. SF$_6$: 8 bonding electrons over 6 bonds, $\tfrac{8}{2\times6}=0.67$ per S–F bond.

Higher bond order means shorter and stronger bonds, except where the electron removed is antibonding ($\mathrm{O_2\to O_2^+}$ strengthens).

Used in: [[CH02 Molecular Orbital Theory Diatomics]] §4, [[CH03 Molecular Orbital Theory Polyatomics]] §5. Related: [[Bonding and Antibonding Orbitals]] · [[sp Mixing]].
