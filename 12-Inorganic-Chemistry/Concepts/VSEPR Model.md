# VSEPR Model

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** [[chem pre-ms .pdf#page=2|slides 2–7]]. · 🟡 **extra · book:** Shriver and Atkins §2.3 ([[Shriver and Atkins Inorganic Chemistry 5th Ed.pdf#page=63|PDF pp.63–66]]).

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Pairs of electrons are like balloons tied to one point: they spread out as far as they can.

> [!key] Definition
> **Regions of enhanced electron density** (bonding pairs, lone pairs, multiple bonds counted as one) arrange themselves to minimise repulsion: $2$ linear, $3$ trigonal planar, $4$ tetrahedral, $5$ trigonal bipyramid, $6$ octahedron, $7$ pentagonal bipyramid. Repulsion order: lone pair/lone pair > lone pair/bond pair > bond pair/bond pair.

## Recipe
Count valence electrons, divide by two, add the regions ([[Steric Number]]), place lone pairs on the larger sites (equatorial in a trigonal bipyramid, trans in an octahedron; [[Trigonal Bipyramid Site Preference]]), then adjust the angles: lone pairs close angles ($\mathrm{CH_4}$ 109.5°, $\mathrm{NH_3}$ 107°, $\mathrm{H_2O}$ 104.5°), multiple bonds open them, electronegative substituents take less room ([[Bent's Rule]]).

## Validity
28 of 29 test species give the right shape by the counting formula; the exception is $[\mathrm{SeF_6}]^{2-}$ whose lone pair is stereochemically inert ([[Lone Pair Stereochemical Activity]]). VSEPR is for main-group $\mathrm{AX_nE_m}$; it says nothing about bond energies.

> [!pitfall] The reason for the larger lone-pair repulsion is a model, not a measurement
> The lecture's reason is that a lone pair is on average closer to the nucleus; the 90° contact counts (ll, lb) are a bookkeeping proxy that reproduces the shapes ([[Trigonal Bipyramid Site Preference]]).
