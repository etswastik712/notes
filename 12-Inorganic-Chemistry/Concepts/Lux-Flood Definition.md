# Lux-Flood Definition

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** [[chem pre-ms .pdf#page=61|slide 61]].

> [!intuition] In one picture
> In a hot melt the thing that moves is the oxide ion: the donor is the base, the acceptor the acid.

> [!key] Definition and key results
> $\mathrm{CaO}$ (base, gives $\mathrm{O^{2-}}$) + $\mathrm{SiO_2}$ (acid) → $\mathrm{CaSiO_3}$ (blast-furnace slag); $\mathrm{MgO+CO_2\rightleftharpoons MgCO_3}$.

Used for molten oxides, glass making, geochemistry; the furnace reduction of phosphate rock ($\mathrm{SiO_2}$ takes oxide from CaO) is a Lux–Flood reaction.

Used in: [[CH04 Acid-Base Concepts]] §4, [[CH07 Main Group Cages and Clusters]] §5. Related: [[Lewis Acids and Bases]] · [[Amphoterism]].
