# Symmetry Labels a b e t

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** [[chem pre-ms .pdf#page=42|slide 42]].

> [!intuition] In one picture
> The letter tells you how many orbitals are tied together by symmetry: one ($a,b$), two ($e$), three ($t$).

> [!key] Definition and key results
> $a,b$: non-degenerate; $e$: doubly degenerate; $t$: triply degenerate; subscripts $1,2$ and $g,u$ record behaviour under other operations. Orbitals of different labels cannot mix.

$\mathrm{NH_3}$ ($C_{3v}$): $a_1,e$; $\mathrm{H_2O}$ ($C_{2v}$): $a_1,a_2,b_1,b_2$; $\mathrm{SF_6}$ ($O_h$): $a_{1g},e_g,t_{1u}$.

Used in: [[CH03 Molecular Orbital Theory Polyatomics]] §1. Related: [[Ligand Group Orbitals]] · [[Reduction of a Representation]].

> [!connection] Group theory used here
> The labels $a,b,e,t$ are names of irreducible representations (1-, 2-, 3-dimensional): [[GT10 Symmetry Representations and Characters]] §2; see also [[Where Groups Appear in the Vault]].

## Across subjects
- [[Irreducible Representation]] (Group Theory): the Mulliken labels $a$, $b$ (1-d), $e$ (2-d), $t$ (3-d) are names of irreducible representations of the molecular point group.
- [[Degeneracy]] (Quantum Mechanics): the dimension of the irreducible representation is the degeneracy symmetry forces: $e$ levels are doubly and $t$ levels triply degenerate; any other degeneracy is accidental.
