## Quizzes (Sep and Nov 2022)

> [!example]- Quiz 1 (07 Sep) and Quiz 1 Set 2 (14 Sep) ([[CH01 VSEPR and Valence Bond Theory]], [[CH02 Molecular Orbital Theory Diatomics]], [[CH03 Molecular Orbital Theory Polyatomics]])
> **Quiz 1 (07 Sep)**
> - Lewis structures and VSEPR shapes:
>   - ClF₃: AX₃E₂, T-shaped.
>   - TeF₆: octahedral.
>   - N₃⁻: [N=N=N]⁻, linear, formal charges −1, +1, −1.
> - Hybridisation: [NH₄]⁺ is sp³; XeF₄ is sp³d² (octahedral parent).
> - Linear [HHeH] and [HHeH]²⁺. Three MOs: σ_g (bonding, He-rich, because He is more electronegative), σ_u (non-bonding, on the H atoms), σ_g* (antibonding).
>   - [HHeH] has 4 e⁻ (σ_g² σ_u²): bond order 1 over two bonds, ½ each. Weak, since He contributes little.
>   - [HHeH]²⁺ has 2 e⁻ (σ_g²): a 3c–2e bond. The more stable of the two.
> - O₂ UPS: as in [[CH2113 Past Papers 2021 - Solved|2021]] Part 3.
> - CCl₂F₂ with F–C–F = 105.5°: $s_{\rm CF}=0.211$ (21.1 % s), $s_{\rm CCl}=0.289$, so **Cl–C–Cl = 114.0°**. By Bent's rule, the more electronegative F gets the p-rich bonds.
>
> **Quiz 1 Set 2 (14 Sep)**
> - Lewis structures and VSEPR shapes:
>   - SO₃²⁻: trigonal pyramidal.
>   - TeF₆: octahedral.
>   - ClO₄⁻: tetrahedral, written with Cl=O bonds to minimise formal charges.
> - SOF₄ isomers. The trigonal bipyramid with **O equatorial** is the most stable, since the double bond needs more room and prefers an equatorial site. The question asks which energy dominates: the **hybridisation energy**, with bonding to the electronegative F concentrating p character, outweighs the promotion energy. [!verify] The wording of that part.
> - BH₃ MO diagram: see 2021 Part 2.
> - CH₄ PES. Two bands, matching the two occupied MO sets:
>   - ~1.4 MJ mol⁻¹ (14.5 eV): **1t₂**, with Jahn–Teller structure;
>   - ~2.2 MJ mol⁻¹ (22.8 eV): **2a₁**.
>
>   Two bands, not one, show the four C–H bonds are not four equivalent sp³ orbitals in MO terms.
> - H₂S with H–S–H = 92.5°:
>   - S–H bonds: **4.2 % s, 95.8 % p** (almost pure p);
>   - each lone pair (if both are equivalent): 45.8 % s;
>   - angle between the lone pairs ≈ 148°.

> [!example]- Quiz 2 (09 Nov) ([[CH07 Main Group Cages and Clusters]], [[CH08 Main Group Rings and Polymers]])
> 1. B₆H₁₀ has $(18+10-12)/2=8$ pairs $=n+2$: **nido**. KH removes a bridging H⁺ to give **A = K[B₆H₉]**, also nido (8 pairs).
> 2. Wade's rules:
>    - B₁₁H₁₅: 13 pairs, nido;
>    - [B₁₁H₁₁]²⁻: 12 pairs $=n+1$, closo;
>    - [B₃H₈]⁻: 6 pairs $=n+3$, arachno.
> 3. Borazine derivatives.
>    - (a) B₃N₃H₆ + 3HBr gives the adduct B₃N₃H₉Br₃. Heating it loses 3H₂ and gives **B-tribromoborazine** Br₃B₃N₃H₃.
>    - (b) 3[MeNH₃]Cl + 3BCl₃ → Cl₃B₃N₃Me₃ + 9HCl. Then Cl₃B₃N₃Me₃ + 3PhMgBr → **Ph₃B₃N₃Me₃**.
> 4. P₃N₃Cl₆ from PCl₅ + NH₄Cl, the stepwise mechanism:
>    - NH₄Cl ⇌ NH₃ + HCl;
>    - PCl₅ ⇌ [PCl₄]⁺[PCl₆]⁻;
>    - NH₃ attacks [PCl₄]⁺, giving Cl₃P=NH plus HCl;
>    - condensation steps give Cl₃P=N–PCl₂=NH and longer chains;
>    - the chain cyclises with loss of HCl to (NPCl₂)₃, along with tetramer and polymer.
> 5. Benzene's pπ–pπ system is fully delocalised with matched phases. In cyclophosphazene, the dπ–pπ overlap gives a **sign mismatch (node) in the odd-membered ring** (Craig–Paddock), so conjugation is interrupted at P. Hence "island" delocalisation and no aromatic ring current.
> 6. [Si₂O₅]²⁻: each tetrahedron shares 3 corners, giving an infinite **sheet** (phyllosilicate, e.g. talc, micas, clays).
> 7. Cyclic silicates [SiO₃]ₙ²ⁿ⁻: (a) 4-ring **[Si₄O₁₂]⁸⁻**; (b) 6-ring **[Si₆O₁₈]¹²⁻**; (c) 8-ring **[Si₈O₂₄]¹⁶⁻**. [!verify] Ring sizes counted from the drawings.

## Mid-semester (26 Sep 2022)

> [!example]- Parts 1–2 (MCQs)
> 1. "A strong acid's strength is levelled by the acidity of the solvent": **False**. It is levelled by the solvent's *basicity*.
> 2. Conjugate acid of HPO₄²⁻: **H₂PO₄⁻ (a)**.
> 3. **[H⁺] = [OH⁻] (d)**.
> 4. NaNH₂ in liquid NH₃ is a **base (b)**.
> 5. $K_w=3.8\times10^{-14}$ gives **pH 6.71 (a)**.
> 6. HCN: **H–C≡N:** with all formal charges zero.
> 7. Same VSEPR geometry: **CH₄ and NH₄⁺ (c)**.
> 8. N₂O: **:N≡N⁺–Ö:⁻**, with −1 on O, the most electronegative atom (the minor form has −1 on the terminal N).
> 9. Incomplete octet: **(d)**, the carbocation form with C⁺ carrying only three bonds.
> 10. Solid PCl₅ is [PCl₄]⁺[PCl₆]⁻: **cation sp³, anion sp³d² (b)**.

> [!example]- Part 3 (3 marks each, any five)
> 11. **BeH₂**:
>     - σ_g from Be 2s + H σ_g;
>     - σ_u from Be 2p_z + H σ_u;
>     - π_u (Be 2pₓ, 2p_y) non-bonding and empty.
>
>     Four electrons fill σ_g²σ_u².
> 12. 1.00 M NH₄NO₃ with $K_a(\mathrm{NH_4^+})=5.6\times10^{-10}$: NH₄⁺ + H₂O ⇌ NH₃ + H₃O⁺, giving **pH 4.63**. NO₃⁻ is a spectator.
> 13. (a) 0.100 mol H₂SO₄ in 2 L is 0.050 M. Taking $K_{a2}=1.2\times10^{-2}$: [H⁺] = 0.0585 M, **pH 1.23**. (pH 1.00 if both protons are counted as fully dissociated.)
>     (b) CH₃NH₂ + H₂O ⇌ CH₃NH₃⁺ + OH⁻. $K_a(\mathrm{CH_3NH_3^+})=K_w/K_b=$ **2.3 × 10⁻¹¹**: a very weak acid.
> 14. Stronger acid in each pair. ([[CH05 Acid Strength Lewis Acidity and Reactions]])
>     - (a) **[Ga(OH₂)₆]³⁺**: poor 3d shielding gives Ga a higher Z* (p$K_a$ 2.6 against 5.0 for Al).
>     - (b) **HMnO₄** ($p=3$, against 2 for H₂CrO₄).
>     - (c) **HClO₄** ($p=3$ against 2).
> 15. In liquid HF all three act as bases (2HF ⇌ H₂F⁺ + HF₂⁻):
>     - EtOH + HF → EtOH₂⁺ + F⁻ (HF₂⁻);
>     - NH₃ + HF → NH₄⁺ + F⁻;
>     - C₆H₅COOH + HF → C₆H₅C(OH)₂⁺ + F⁻.
> 16. CH₃F with H–C–H = 110°: **C–H bonds 25.5 % s; C–F bond 23.5 % s**.

> [!example]- Part 4 (5 marks each, any four)
> 17. ICl UPS bands at ~10, 11, 13 and 14 eV.
>     - **10 and 11 eV**: the 2π (π*) level, split by I spin–orbit coupling into ²Π₃/₂ and ²Π₁/₂.
>     - **~13 eV**: 1π.
>     - **~14 eV**: σ (bonding).
>
>     All the ionisation energies are low because the I 5p and Cl 3p orbitals are high-lying (the IE of I is 10.45 eV). [!verify] Band positions read from the sketch.
> 18. (a) Square cyclic-H₄ group orbitals: **a₁g** (all in phase), **e_u** (a pair), **b₁g** (alternating). (b) XeH₄: see [[CH2113 Past Papers 2021 - Solved|2021]] Part 3 Q3.
> 19. Mixing 0.100 + 0.001 mol HCl in 0.200 L gives 0.505 M: **pH 0.30, pOH 13.70**.
> 20. (a) Me₂N–PF₂ has two donor sites. **BH₃** (soft) binds **P**; **BF₃** (hard) binds **N** (HSAB). (b) DMSO binds H⁺ through **O**: the hard, more basic site (O carries the negative end of the S⁺–O⁻ bond). ([[CH06 Hard and Soft Acids and Bases]])
> 21. 0.534 M formic acid with $K_a=1.8\times10^{-4}$: **[H₃O⁺] = 9.7 × 10⁻³ M, pH 2.01**.

## End-semester (25 Nov 2022)

> [!example]- Parts 1–2
> 1. "ΔG° = 0 for H⁺/H₂ under ambient conditions only": **False**. The convention sets $E^\circ=0$ at every temperature.
> 2. "A metal with a positive standard potential dissolves in acid to give H₂": **False**. Such metals (Cu, Ag) do not reduce H⁺.
> 3. Fe₃(CO)₁₂: 54 − 48 = 6 electrons short, so **3 M–M bonds (b)**.
> 4. Rh–CH(Me)CH₂Me → Rh–H + MeCH=CHMe: **β-hydride elimination (d)**. The H comes off the β carbon. (The answer marked on the scan, (c), is wrong.)
> 5. "At equilibrium a Li-ion battery reads ~4 V": **False**. At equilibrium $E_{\rm cell}=0$; the battery is flat.
> 6. [B₆H₆]⁴⁻: 8 pairs, **nido (b)**.
> 7. [Sn₉]⁴⁻: $(36+4-18)/2=$ 11 pairs $=n+2$, **nido (a)**.
> 8. [Ni(H₂O)₆]²⁺ (d⁸): $\Delta_o=\nu_1=10\,100$ cm⁻¹, so $0.4\Delta_o=$ **48.3 kJ mol⁻¹ (b)**.
> 9. Strong Jahn–Teller distortion: **[Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ (a)**, d⁹ with an e_g³ configuration.
> 10. Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 2FeC₂O₄ → … : Cr₂O₇²⁻ gains **6 e (d)**, balanced by 2 × (Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺ + C₂O₄²⁻ → 2CO₂), i.e. 3 e⁻ per FeC₂O₄. [!verify] The question's "electrons involved".

> [!example]- Part 3 (3 marks each)
> 11. **HClO₂ + 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → HOCl + H₂O, E° = 1.65 V.** Left to right is reduction (HClO₂ is the oxidant); the reverse is oxidation. The H⁺ shows the solution is **acidic**. ([[CH09 Redox]])
> 12. Frost diagram of iron.
>     - (a) Most stable species: the lowest point, **Fe²⁺** in acid ($NE^\circ=-0.88$ V).
>     - (b) **Fe** metal is the reducing agent. It is stronger in **base**, where Fe(OH)₂/Fe is −0.89 V.
>     - (c) **Fe and Fe³⁺ comproportionate** to Fe²⁺, since Fe²⁺ lies below the line joining them. **FeO₄²⁻** (Fe(VI)) can disproportionate in acid. [!verify] Read from a small figure.
> 13. (a) **Dewar–Chatt–Duncanson** model: the olefin's filled π orbital donates σ into an empty metal d orbital, and a filled metal d orbital back-donates into the olefin's π*. The C=C bond lengthens.
>     (b) [(Ph₃P)₃Ru(μ-Cl)₃Ru(PPh₃)₃]⁺: each **Ru(II), d⁶**, 6 + 3 × 2 (P) + 3 × 2 (Cl⁻) = **18 e**.
> 14. Dewar model: see [[CH2113 Past Papers 2021 - Solved|2021]] II-4.
> 15. (a) [Si₆O₁₇]¹⁰⁻: a double chain. (b) N₃P₃Cl₆ substitutions:
>     - **X**: excess allylamine gives **N₃P₃(NHCH₂CH=CH₂)₆** (with HCl removed by the excess amine);
>     - **Y**: 3 eq. 2,2′-biphenol with Et₃N gives the **tris-spiro N₃P₃(O₂C₁₂H₈)₃**.
> 16. B₅H₉ has 7 pairs $=n+2$: **nido**. The adduct B₅H₉(PMe₃)₂ gains 2 × 2 e⁻, giving 9 pairs $=n+4$: **hypho**.

> [!example]- Part 4 (5 marks each)
> 17. Dichromate oxidises oxalic acid:
>     - Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 14H⁺ + 6e⁻ → 2Cr³⁺ + 7H₂O (orange → green);
>     - H₂C₂O₄ → 2CO₂ + 2H⁺ + 2e⁻;
>     - overall: **Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 3H₂C₂O₄ + 8H⁺ → 2Cr³⁺ + 6CO₂ + 7H₂O**.
> 18. [Fe(CN)₆]³⁻ is Fe(III) d⁵, low spin, t₂g⁵: **1 unpaired electron, μ_so = 1.73 BM**. CN⁻ is a strong σ-donor and π-acceptor, so Δ_o is large. MO diagram as in 2021 III-1, plus π back-bonding into ligand π*. ([[CH11 Ligand-Field Theory - Molecular Orbitals of Complexes]])
> 19. Chlorine couples in base.
>     - Cathode: ClO⁻ + H₂O + 2e⁻ → Cl⁻ + 2OH⁻ (0.88 V).
>     - Anode: OCl⁻ + 4OH⁻ → ClO₃⁻ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻ (reverse of the 0.50 V couple).
>     - Full cell: **3ClO⁻ → ClO₃⁻ + 2Cl⁻**, with $E=0.88-0.50=$ **+0.38 V**: spontaneous.
>     - Latimer diagram: ClO₃⁻ →(0.50) ClO⁻ →(0.88) Cl⁻.
>
>     The potential to the right exceeds the one to the left, so **OCl⁻ is unstable to disproportionation**.
> 20. (a) The B–H–B bridge in B₂H₆ is a **3c–2e bond**: each B contributes an sp³ hybrid, the bridging H its 1s. Three MOs form: bonding (occupied), non-bonding and antibonding. The 3c–2e B–B–B bond (in higher boranes) is built the same way from three B hybrids. (b) Borazine → polyborazylene: see 2021 II-2.
> 21. (a) Haematin (μ-oxo dimer) formation:
>     1. P–Fe(II) + O₂ → P–Fe(III)–O₂⁻ (superoxo);
>     2. + P–Fe(II) → P–Fe(III)–O–O–Fe(III)–P (μ-peroxo);
>     3. → 2 P–Fe(IV)=O (ferryl);
>     4. + P–Fe(II) → **P–Fe(III)–O–Fe(III)–P** (the μ-oxo dimer, haematin).
>
>     Proteins prevent this sequence by isolating each heme.
>     (b) **Cooperativity** in haemoglobin. O₂ binding to one subunit pulls the Fe into the porphyrin plane (high-spin Fe(II) is too big to sit in the plane; low-spin oxy-Fe fits). That moves the proximal His and the F-helix and shifts the tetramer from the T to the R state, which raises the O₂ affinity of the other subunits. The result is a **sigmoidal** binding curve, against myoglobin's hyperbolic one.
