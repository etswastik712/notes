## Mid-semester (26 Sep 2023)
> [!example]- Parts 1–2 (1 mark each)
> 1. Formal charges in O=O–O (ozone): **0, +1, −1 (b)**.
> 2. Basic shape of XeF₄: **octahedral, sp³d² (a)**. The parent is octahedral; with the two lone pairs it is square planar.
> 3. Valence-electron counts: NO₂⁺ 16, CO₂ 16, N₃⁻ 16, NO₂⁻ 18. The odd one out is **NO₂⁻ (c)**.
> 4. HNO₃: H–O–N⁺(=O)–O⁻. N is +1 and the singly bonded O is −1, so N keeps its octet.
> 5. SF₆ is stable in MO theory because its **bonding and non-bonding MOs are filled and its antibonding MOs are empty (b)**.
> 6. In the drawing, **(a)** (two S=O bonds) is hypervalent. **(b)** (S²⁺ with four single bonds to O⁻) obeys the octet rule.
> 7. Self-ionisation of N₂O₄: **N₂O₄ ⇌ NO⁺ + NO₃⁻**.
> 8. The anhydride of Fe(OH)₂ is **FeO**.
> 9. **Al(OH)₃ + NaOH → Na[Al(OH)₄]**. Al(OH)₃ is amphoteric.
> 10. [H₃O⁺] = 1.5 × 10⁻⁴ is much larger than [OH⁻], so HOCl is **acidic**, with $K_a=(1.5\times10^{-4})^2/0.75=$ 3.0 × 10⁻⁸.

> [!example]- Part 3 (3 marks each)
> 11. **NO** has 11 valence electrons. The last one sits in π*: 1 unpaired electron, bond order 2.5. **NO⁺** (10 e⁻): BO 3, no unpaired electrons. **NO⁻** (12 e⁻): BO 2, 2 unpaired (triplet, like O₂). Stability follows bond order: **NO⁺ > NO > NO⁻**. ([[CH02 Molecular Orbital Theory Diatomics]])
> 12. (i) HF attacks glass: SiO₂ + 4HF → SiF₄ + 2H₂O, then SiF₄ + 2HF → H₂SiF₆. Si is a Lewis acid that accepts F⁻ (expanding to [SiF₆]²⁻). (ii) SbF₅ + 2HSO₃F → **[H₂SO₃F]⁺** (the acid, a superacid cation) + **[SbF₅(OSO₂F)]⁻** (the base). ([[CH05 Acid Strength Lewis Acidity and Reactions]])
> 13. (i) (CH₃)₂N–PF₂: **BF₃ binds N** (hard–hard) and **BH₃ binds P** (soft–soft). (ii) H₃AsO₄ is (HO)₃AsO, so $p=1$. Pauling's rules give p$K_{a1}$ ≈ 3, p$K_{a2}$ ≈ 8, p$K_{a3}$ ≈ 13. [HAsO₄]²⁻ ionises in the third step, predicted ≈ 13; the observed 11.6 is consistent within the rules' ±1. ([[CH05 Acid Strength Lewis Acidity and Reactions]], [[CH06 Hard and Soft Acids and Bases]])
> 14. N–N bonds:
>     - N₂H₄: **single**, one lone pair on each N.
>     - N₂F₂: **double** (F–N=N–F), one lone pair on each N and three on each F.
>     - [N₂H₅]⁺: **single**. The protonated N has no lone pair; the other N has one.
> 15. **Hard acid + soft base.** The soft base's HOMO is high, the hard acid's LUMO is very high, and the gap is large. Little mixing occurs, so there is little covalent stabilisation and only weak ionic attraction: a poor match. **Hard–hard**: the base's HOMO is low, the acid's LUMO is high, and the interaction is mainly electrostatic (charge-controlled), yet strong. ([[CH06 Hard and Soft Acids and Bases]])
> 16. CHF₃ with F–C–F = 108.8°: **C–F 24.4 % s; C–H 26.9 % s**.

> [!example]- Part 4 (5 marks each)
> 17. (a) [NH₄]⁺ (T_d): the a₁ and t₂ bonding MOs (from N 2s and 2p with the H₄ a₁ and t₂ combinations) hold 8 e⁻. The **UPS shows 2 bands**, t₂ and a₁. (b) **Bent's rule**: atomic s character concentrates in hybrids directed toward electropositive substituents. For example, the F–C–F angle in CH₂F₂ (108.5°) is smaller than its H–C–H angle (110.5°). ([[CH01 VSEPR and Valence Bond Theory]], [[CH03 Molecular Orbital Theory Polyatomics]])
> 18. Cyclic-H₄ group orbitals and XeH₄: see [[CH2113 Past Papers 2021 - Solved|2021]] Part 3 Q3.
> 19. (i) KOH: 25 mL × 0.5 M = 12.5 mmol. Reading the volume as 8.75 × 10⁻² L (87.5 mL), [HCl] = **0.143 M**. [!verify] The exponent on the scan. (ii) pH 8.38 → **pOH 5.62**, [H₃O⁺] = **4.2 × 10⁻⁹ M**.
> 20. SO₄²⁻ = 0.140 × 3 + 0.060 × 1 = **0.48 mol**. HSO₄⁻ is counted because Ba²⁺ precipitation pulls its dissociation to completion. At 0.5 M BaCl₂, **960 mL** is needed.

## End-semester (24 Nov 2023)
> [!example]- Part 1 (1 mark each)
> 1. 2Fe(acac)₃ + 3Fe(py)Cl₂ ⇌ 3Fe(acac)₂ + 2Fe(py)Cl₃, claimed spontaneous: **False**. This would move hard acac⁻ (O-donor) from hard Fe(III) to softer Fe(II), against HSAB.
> 2. "Spontaneous with $E_{cell}<0$": **False**. ΔG = −nFE < 0 requires E > 0.
> 3. "Δ_t is larger than Δ_o": **False**. Δ_t ≈ 4/9 Δ_o.
> 4. Acyl + H on M → RCHO: **reductive elimination (b)**.
> 5. 3Ba + 2Al³⁺ → 3Ba²⁺ + 2Al: **6 e⁻ (d)**.
> 6. C₂B₁₀H₁₂ is equivalent to [B₁₂H₁₂]²⁻ (13 pairs), so **13 electron pairs, closo (b)**.
> 7. 3BCl₃ + 3RNH₂ → (ClBNR)₃. With 3MeLi this gives **X = (MeBNR)₃**: Me on B, R on N, option **(b)**. ([[CH08 Main Group Rings and Polymers]])
> 8. VB picture of [Ni(H₂O)₆]²⁺: **sp³d² (b)** (outer orbital, high spin).
> 9. **[Co(CN)₆]⁴⁻ (c)**.
> 10. "Square-planar d⁸ paramagnetic complexes are very rare": **True**. The large Δ empties d_x²−y².

> [!example]- Part 2 (3 marks each)
> 11. Co + Fe²⁺ (1.94 M) → Co²⁺ (0.15 M) + Fe: $E^\circ=-0.447-(-0.28)=-0.167$ V; $E=-0.167-\frac{0.0592}{2}\log\frac{0.15}{1.94}=$ **−0.134 V**. **Not spontaneous.**
> 12. (a) MnO₄⁻/MnO₂: **1.70 V** ([[CH2113 Past Papers 2021 - Solved|2021]] III-4b). (b) In the M⁺(aq) + ½H₂ → H⁺(aq) + M(s) cycle, ΔG is dominated by **enthalpies**: atomisation, ionisation and hydration, each hundreds of kJ mol⁻¹. The entropy terms are small, and similar for related metals, so they largely cancel when metals are compared (e.g. the gas-phase steps, and hydration entropies of ions of the same charge). ([[CH09 Redox]] §3)
> 13. [Ni(H₂O)₆]²⁺: 48.3 kJ mol⁻¹ ([[CH2113 Past Papers 2022 - Solved|2022]] ES 8).
> 14. B₂H₆ 3c–2e bonds: see 2022 ES 20.
> 15. P₃N₃Cl₆: mechanism in 2022 Quiz 2 Q4. By-product **HCl**; (NPCl₂)₄ and polymer also form.
> 16. B₅H₉ + Et₃N → **[Et₃NH][B₅H₈]**: a bridging H⁺ is removed. Both B₅H₉ and [B₅H₈]⁻ have 7 pairs and are **nido** square pyramids.

> [!example]- Part 3 (5 marks each)
> 17. Sulfur in acid. The Latimer diagram given is SO₄²⁻ →(−0.25) S₂O₆²⁻ →(0.57) SO₂ →(0.51) S₄O₆²⁻ →(0.07) S₂O₃²⁻ →(0.60) S →(0.14) H₂S.
>     - (a) Frost points ($N$, $NE^\circ$ in V):
>
>       | species | $N$ | $NE^\circ$ |
>       |---|---|---|
>       | H₂S | −2 | −0.28 |
>       | S | 0 | 0 |
>       | S₂O₃²⁻ | 2 | 1.20 |
>       | S₄O₆²⁻ | 2.5 | 1.24 |
>       | SO₂ | 4 | 2.00 |
>       | S₂O₆²⁻ | 5 | 2.57 |
>       | SO₄²⁻ | 6 | 2.32 |
>
>     - (b) SO₂ is stable with respect to its immediate neighbours: disproportionation to S₂O₆²⁻ + S₄O₆²⁻ has $E=0.51-0.57<0$. But SO₂ lies **above the line from S to SO₄²⁻** (2.00 against 1.55). Thermodynamically, 3SO₂ + 2H₂O → 2SO₄²⁻ + S + 4H⁺ with $E=E(\mathrm{SO_2/S})-E(\mathrm{SO_4^{2-}/SO_2})=0.50-0.16=$ **+0.34 V**. **Not stable**; the reaction is kinetically slow.
>     - (c) The lowest point, **H₂S**, is the most stable species in acid. S is close above it.
>
>     ([[CH09 Redox]] §11)
> 18. (a) **Cr(CO)₆**: 6 + 12 = 18 e⁻. MO: σ-bonding a₁g, t₁u, e_g filled; t₂g⁶ is stabilised by π back-bonding into CO π*; e_g* is empty. The large Δ_o gives a closed shell, so the complex is stable, diamagnetic and kinetically inert. (b) [(CO)₅Cr(μ-H)Cr(CO)₅]⁻: each **Cr(0), d⁶**, with H counted as hydride. Each Cr: 6 + 10 + 2 (its share of the 3c–2e Cr–H–Cr bond) = **18**. (Total electrons: 2 × 6 + 20 + 2 = 34 = 2 × 17, closed by the 3-centre bond.) ([[CH11 Ligand-Field Theory - Molecular Orbitals of Complexes]])
> 19. Fe Pourbaix diagram, points 1–5. The lines are:
>     - H⁺/H₂: 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂ (slope −0.059 V/pH);
>     - O₂ + 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ → 2H₂O (slope −0.059 V/pH);
>     - Fe³⁺ + e⁻ → Fe²⁺ (0.77 V, pH-independent);
>     - Fe²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Fe (pH-independent);
>     - Fe₂O₃ + 6H⁺ + 2e⁻ → 2Fe²⁺ + 3H₂O (slope −0.177 V/pH);
>     - Fe₂O₃/Fe₃O₄ and Fe₃O₄/Fe lines (each slope −0.059 V/pH);
>     - Fe³⁺ → Fe₂O₃ precipitation (vertical).
>
>     [!verify] Which number marks which line could not be read from the scan.
> 20. (a) **Myoglobin**: a single heme, hyperbolic saturation curve, high affinity (a storage protein). **Haemoglobin**: four subunits, sigmoidal curve, cooperative T→R binding (2022 ES 21b), delivering O₂ efficiently between lungs and tissue. (b) **Deoxy**: high-spin Fe(II) d⁶, 4 unpaired electrons, μ_so = **4.90 BM**, paramagnetic; the Fe sits out of the porphyrin plane. **Oxy**: **diamagnetic**. Either low-spin Fe(III) coupled antiferromagnetically to O₂⁻, or low-spin Fe(II) with singlet O₂; the Fe moves into the plane.
