## Quiz 1 (05 Sep 2024)
> [!example]- Answers ([[CH01 VSEPR and Valence Bond Theory]], [[CH02 Molecular Orbital Theory Diatomics]])
> 1. **XeO₄**: tetrahedral (Xe=O bonds; formal charges 0). **SO₃²⁻**: trigonal pyramidal, AX₃E. **I₃⁻**: linear, AX₂E₃, with −1 formally on the central I.
> 2. **BCl₃**: sp². **XeF₄**: sp³d².
> 3. **BeH₂**: linear. The MO diagram is in [[CH2113 Past Papers 2022 - Solved|2022]] MS 11.
> 4. **CO UPS** (10 valence electrons, 1σ²2σ²1π⁴3σ² in valence numbering):
>    - **~14.0 eV**: 3σ (5σ in full numbering), the C lone pair, nearly non-bonding, so narrow with little vibrational structure;
>    - **~16.9 eV**: 1π (bonding), with a long vibrational progression;
>    - **~19.7 eV**: 2σ (4σ).
>
>    [!verify] Positions read from the figure (axis 11–19 eV).
> 5. **SF₄**, equatorial F–S–F = 102°:
>    - each equatorial S–F bond: **17.2 % s, 82.8 % p**;
>    - taking the axial bonds as pure p (axial angle 173° ≈ 180°), the **lone pair has 65.6 % s**;
>    - the angle between the lone pair and each equatorial F is **129°**, which sums to 360° with the 102° (planar, as required).

## Mid-semester (27 Sep 2024)
> [!example]- Part 1 (2 marks each)
> - **1A/1B** The best structure of [OCN]⁻ is **(b) [:Ö–C≡N:]⁻**, with −1 on O, the most electronegative atom. [:Ö=C=N̈:]⁻ (−1 on N) is the next contributor.
> - **2** ClO₂F₃: 5 bonding domains and no lone pairs (Cl(VII)), so **trigonal bipyramidal**. The two O atoms and one F are equatorial (multiple bonds prefer equatorial sites); two F are axial.
> - **3A/3B** The intermediate is **(ClBNR)₃**, B-trichloro-N-trialkylborazine. With 3MeLi, **X = (MeBNR)₃, option (b)**.
> - **4A** [Sn₉]⁴⁻: 11 pairs, **nido (a)**. **4B** [B₆H₆]⁴⁻: 8 pairs, **nido (b)**.
> - **5** **H₃N–SO₃ has the longer N–S bond.** Me₃N is the stronger σ-donor (inductive effect of the methyls), so Me₃N–SO₃ has the shorter, stronger dative bond. Gas-phase values are about 1.91 Å against 1.96 Å. [!verify] Literature values; general knowledge.

> [!example]- Part 2 (3 marks each)
> 6. Acids and bases. ([[CH04 Acid-Base Concepts]], [[CH05 Acid Strength Lewis Acidity and Reactions]])
>    - HClO₄ + CH₃CN → [CH₃CNH]⁺ + [ClO₄]⁻: HClO₄ is the Brønsted acid and CH₃CN the Brønsted base (also Lewis).
>    - NH₃ + ClF → H₃N···ClF: ClF is the **Lewis acid** (σ-hole on Cl; a halogen bond) and NH₃ the Lewis base.
>    - XeO₃ + OH⁻ → [HXeO₄]⁻: XeO₃ is the **Lewis acid** and OH⁻ the Lewis base.
> 7. (a) Both are R–S(=O)₂–OH: the acidic H is on an O attached to S, which carries oxo groups. That makes them oxoacids. (b) **CF₃SO₃H** (triflic acid) is the stronger acid. The strongly electron-withdrawing CF₃ group stabilises the conjugate base and makes the S centre more electron-poor (stronger Lewis acidity of the S(VI) centre). p-Tolyl donates electron density by resonance and induction.
> 8. Bent's rule.
>    - **CH₃F**, H–C–H = 112°: C–H **27.3 % s**, leaving the C–F bond with only 18.2 % s.
>    - **CHF₃**, F–C–F = 109°: C–F 24.6 % s, C–H **26.3 % s**.
>
>    **CH₃F has the higher %s in its C–H bonds.** In **CH₂F₂** the C–F %p should lie **between** CH₃F's (81.8 %) and CHF₃'s (75.4 %). 2021's 108.49° gives 75.9 %, so CH₂F₂'s C–F %p is *lower* than in CH₃F.
> 9. [H₃O]⁺ (C₃v, like NH₃): 1a₁ and 1e (O–H bonding), then 2a₁, the O lone pair (weakly bonding). 8 electrons; 2e and 3a₁ are antibonding and empty. ([[CH03 Molecular Orbital Theory Polyatomics]])
> 10. B₅H₉ + Et₃N → **[Et₃NH][B₅H₈]**, nido. See [[CH2113 Past Papers 2023 - Solved|2023]] ES 16.
> 11. P₄ reactions. ([[CH08 Main Group Rings and Polymers]])
>     - P₄ + 3O₂ → **X = P₄O₆**.
>     - P₄ + 5O₂ (excess, 323 K) → P₄O₁₀, then + 6H₂O → **Y = 4H₃PO₄**.
>     - P₄ at 250 °C in sunlight → **Z = red phosphorus** (polymeric).

> [!example]- Part 3 (5 marks each)
> 12. (a) [CN]⁻ is isoelectronic with N₂ and CO, configuration … 1π⁴ 3σ². Its **HOMO, 3σ, is concentrated on C** (the less electronegative atom), so H⁺ (via H 1s) bonds to **C**, giving H–C≡N. (b) Cyclic-H₄ group orbitals: **a₁g, e_u, b₁g**, as in [[CH2113 Past Papers 2022 - Solved|2022]] MS 18.
> 13. (a) SbF₅ + 2FSO₃H → [H₂SO₃F]⁺ (the acid) + [SbF₅(SO₃F)]⁻ (the base). (b) HOMO–LUMO diagrams: see [[CH2113 Past Papers 2023 - Solved|2023]] MS 15.
> 14. (a) Dewar model: [[CH2113 Past Papers 2021 - Solved|2021]] II-4. (b) In the **even** 8-membered ring [PNCl₂]₄, the d_π–p_π phases can alternate consistently all the way round, so there is **no sign mismatch**. The odd 6-membered (NPCl₂)₃ ring forces a node (Craig–Paddock).
> 15. (a) **B₄H₄** (closo tetrahedron with only $n=4$ pairs): each BH unit has 2 skeletal electrons, 8 in total. **Four 3c–2e B–B–B bonds**, one on each of the 4 triangular faces. (b) B₂H₆ bridge: see 2022 ES 20.

## End-semester (28 Nov 2024)
> [!example]- Q1 · Redox (2 + 2 + 1 + 1 + 2 + 2) ([[CH09 Redox]])
> - **(A) Oxygen Frost diagram.** pH 0: O₂ (0, 0), H₂O₂ (−1, −0.70), H₂O (−2, −2.46). pH 14 (each couple shifted by −0.83 V per H⁺/e⁻): O₂ (0, 0), HO₂⁻ (−1, +0.13), OH⁻ (−2, −0.80). Both are convex at the peroxide point, so peroxide is unstable to disproportionation at either pH.
> - **(B)** Coordination by bpy raises E(Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺) from 0.77 V to 1.02 V. **bpy binds Fe(II) preferentially**, by a factor of $10^{(1.02-0.77)/0.0592}\approx$ **1.7 × 10⁴** in $K_{\rm II}/K_{\rm III}$. bpy is a π-acceptor and stabilises the electron-rich low-spin d⁶ Fe(II).
> - **(C)** Fe²⁺ disproportionation, 3Fe²⁺ → Fe + 2Fe³⁺: $E=E(\mathrm{Fe^{2+}/Fe})-E(\mathrm{Fe^{3+}/Fe^{2+}})<0$. **No**, it does not occur. [!verify] The sheet prints Fe²⁺/Fe as "+0.41 V"; the true value is −0.44 V. Either gives a negative E (−0.36 or −1.21 V).
> - **(D)** H₂O as an oxidant: 2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂. As a reductant: 2F₂ + 2H₂O → 4HF + O₂.
> - **(E)** Which conditions favour each change:
>   - (a) Mn²⁺ → MnO₄⁻ releases H⁺ (Mn²⁺ + 4H₂O → MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5e⁻): favoured in **basic** solution.
>   - (b) ClO⁻ → ClO₃⁻: favoured in **base**, where ClO⁻ also disproportionates.
>   - (c) H₂O₂ → O₂ + 2H⁺ + 2e⁻: favoured in **base**.
>   - (d) I₂ → 2I⁻: no H⁺ is involved, so it is **pH-independent**. In strong base, I₂ disproportionates to I⁻ + IO₃⁻.

> [!example]- Q2 · Oxidative addition and Wilkinson's catalyst (2 + 2 + 1 + 3)
> (A) H₂ adds through a **concerted three-centre transition state**: H₂ σ → metal d donation and metal d → H₂ σ* back-donation break the H–H bond on one face. The two H atoms therefore end up **cis**. Example: Vaska's complex, trans-IrCl(CO)(PPh₃)₂ + H₂ → cis-IrH₂Cl(CO)(PPh₃)₂ (16e → 18e, Ir(I) → Ir(III)).
> (B) Cycle without a 14-e⁻ intermediate:
> 1. RhCl(PPh₃)₃ (16 e⁻) + H₂ → RhH₂Cl(PPh₃)₃ (18 e⁻): **oxidative addition**.
> 2. − PPh₃ → RhH₂Cl(PPh₃)₂ (16 e⁻).
> 3. + alkene → RhH₂Cl(PPh₃)₂(alkene) (18 e⁻).
> 4. Migratory insertion → RhH(alkyl)Cl(PPh₃)₂ (16 e⁻).
> 5. + PPh₃ → 18 e⁻.
> 6. **Reductive elimination** of the alkane → RhCl(PPh₃)₃ (16 e⁻).
>
> [!verify] Not in this year's vault chapters (organometallic catalysis); general knowledge.

> [!example]- Q4 · ν(CO) and nitrosyls (1 + 2 + 2) ([[CH11 Ligand-Field Theory - Molecular Orbitals of Complexes]])
> (A) **Cr(CO)₆** has the higher ν(CO). The anionic [V(CO)₆]⁻ back-donates more into π*(CO), weakening C–O (about 2000 against 1860 cm⁻¹).
> (B) PEt₃ is a stronger σ-donor and weaker π-acceptor than PPh₃. More electron density reaches the remaining COs in **[Cr(CO)₅(PEt₃)]**, which therefore has the **lower ν(CO)** and the **shorter M–C bond** (stronger back-bonding).
> (C) NO bonding modes:
> - **linear M–N–O** (~180°), NO⁺-like, a 3-electron donor (neutral counting);
> - **bent** (~120–140°), NO⁻-like, a 1-electron donor, with a lone pair on N;
> - **bridging** μ₂ or μ₃ NO.
>
> The modes are told apart by ν(NO): ~1650–1900 cm⁻¹ for linear, ~1520–1720 cm⁻¹ for bent.

> [!example]- Q5 · Octahedral SALCs and π effects (6 + 2) ([[CH11 Ligand-Field Theory - Molecular Orbitals of Complexes]])
> (A) σ SALCs, with σ₁…σ₄ along ±x, ±y and σ₅, σ₆ along ±z:
> - **a₁g**: $\tfrac1{\sqrt6}(\sigma_1+\sigma_2+\sigma_3+\sigma_4+\sigma_5+\sigma_6)$;
> - **t₁u**: $\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(\sigma_1-\sigma_3)$, $\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(\sigma_2-\sigma_4)$, $\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(\sigma_5-\sigma_6)$;
> - **e_g**: $\tfrac12(\sigma_1-\sigma_2+\sigma_3-\sigma_4)$ for d_x²−y², and $\tfrac1{\sqrt{12}}(2\sigma_5+2\sigma_6-\sigma_1-\sigma_2-\sigma_3-\sigma_4)$ for d_z².
>
> (B) **π-acceptor** ligands (CO) have empty π* orbitals *above* t₂g. Mixing **lowers** t₂g, so Δ_o **increases**. **π-donor** ligands (Cl⁻) have filled p orbitals *below* t₂g; mixing raises t₂g and Δ_o **decreases**. σ-only ligands leave t₂g non-bonding.

> [!example]- Q6 · Splittings (2 + 1 + 3) ([[CH10 Crystal Field Theory]])
> (A) **Δ_sp > Δ_t**. Δ_t ≈ 4/9 Δ_o, while Δ_sp (d_x²−y² relative to d_xy) ≈ 1.3 Δ_o. So Δ_sp ≈ **3 Δ_t**.
> (B) z-in (compression along z): d_z² rises above d_x²−y², and d_xz, d_yz rise above d_xy. The barycentre is preserved within each set. (Elongation, z-out, gives the opposite order.)
> (C) Three kinds of M–L π bonding:
> 1. **L → M π-donation** from filled ligand p orbitals: Cl⁻, O²⁻, OH⁻.
> 2. **M → L π back-donation** into ligand π*: CO, CN⁻, bpy.
> 3. **M → L back-donation into σ*** of the ligand: PR₃ (P–R σ*).
