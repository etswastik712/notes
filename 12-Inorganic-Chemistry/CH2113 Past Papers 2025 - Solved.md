## Mid-semester (26 Sep 2025)
> [!example]- Part 1 (1 mark each)
> 1. Same VSEPR geometry: **CH₄ and NH₄⁺ (c)**.
> 2. Largest angle between two bonds: **XeOF₂ (c)**. It is T-shaped (AX₃E₂), with F–Xe–F ≈ 174°. The others are near 105–125°.
> 3. Alkali metals in liquid NH₃: **base (b)**. They give solvated electrons and, catalytically, the amides MNH₂, the solvent's strong base.
> 4. [NO₂]⁻: **1 double bond, 1 single bond, 6 lone pairs, 2 resonance forms (c)**.
> 5. Incomplete octet: **(d)**, the C⁺ form. See [[CH2113 Past Papers 2022 - Solved|2022]] MS 9.

> [!example]- Part 2 (5 marks each, any seven)
> 6. (a) The intermediate is (ClBNR)₃ and **X = (MeBNR)₃** ([[CH2113 Past Papers 2024 - Solved|2024]] MS 3).
>    (b) Wade's rules:
>    - B₅H₁₁: 8 pairs, **arachno**;
>    - [B₅H₅]²⁻: 6 pairs $=n+1$, **closo** (trigonal bipyramid);
>    - B₁₀H₁₄: 12 pairs $=n+2$, **nido**.
> 7. (a) [S₂O₃]²⁻ is tetrahedral, analogous to SO₄²⁻ with one O replaced by S. Central S is sp³. In the preferred resonance structure, formal charges are minimised: S=S and S=O character, with −1 spread over the O atoms. (b) Bent's rule: see [[CH2113 Past Papers 2023 - Solved|2023]] MS 17b.
> 8. As as a Lewis acid or base (oxidation state in brackets):
>    - (i) AsF₃ + F₂ → **AsF₅** (As(V)). AsF₅ is itself a strong Lewis acid: AsF₅ + F⁻ → AsF₆⁻.
>    - (ii) Me₂O + AsCl₃ → Me₂O→AsCl₃: As is the **acid** (As(III)).
>    - (iii) Bu₄NBr + AsCl₃ → Bu₄N⁺[Br–AsCl₃]⁻: As is the **acid** (As(III)).
>    - (iv) NiCl₂ + 4AsF₃ → [Ni(AsF₃)₄]²⁺ 2Cl⁻: As is the **base**, donating its lone pair (As(III)). [!verify] Suitable starting materials are my choice.
> 9. (a) NO, NO⁺ and NO⁻: see 2023 MS 11. (b) **HCl UPS**: two band systems.
>    - **~12.7 eV**: the Cl 3p non-bonding π lone pairs. Sharp, with little vibrational structure, split by spin–orbit coupling.
>    - **~16.3 eV**: the σ bonding MO. Broad, with a vibrational progression, because removing a bonding electron changes the bond length.
> 10. (a) P₃N₃Cl₆ mechanism: see 2022 Quiz 2 Q4. (b) Craig–Paddock: see [[CH2113 Past Papers 2021 - Solved|2021]] II-4.
> 11. (a, b) Cyclic H₄ and XeH₄: see 2021 Part 3 Q3. (c) **(SN)ₓ**: each S–N unit has 11 valence electrons, so the chain's π* band is **half filled**. Delocalisation along the planar chain makes it metallic in one dimension. It even superconducts near 0.3 K.
> 12. (b) HOMO–LUMO diagrams. **Hard–hard**: large gap, little mixing, charge-controlled (ionic) binding. **Soft–soft**: small gap, strong mixing, covalent (orbital-controlled) binding. ([[CH06 Hard and Soft Acids and Bases]])
> 13. (a) **B₄Cl₄**: each B–Cl unit gives 2 skeletal electrons (Cl is univalent), 8 in total, 4 pairs. That makes **four 3c–2e B–B–B bonds, one on each face** of the tetrahedron, as for B₄H₄ (2024 MS 15). (b) B₂H₆: see 2022 ES 20.
> 14. (a) The stronger acids are **[Ga(OH₂)₆]³⁺** and **HMnO₄** (2022 MS 14). (b) **BF₃ < BCl₃ < BBr₃.** The order is the reverse of what halogen electronegativity alone would predict. B–X π back-donation (X p → empty B 2p) is strongest for F, because the 2p–2p overlap is good. That π bond must be broken when BX₃ pyramidalises to accept a base. Larger reorganisation energy means weaker apparent acidity. ([[CH05 Acid Strength Lewis Acidity and Reactions]])

## End-semester (25 Nov 2025)
> [!example]- Q1 · Redox chemistry (2 each) ([[CH09 Redox]])
> - **(A) Li-ion cell.** In discharge, the graphite anode is oxidised, LiC₆ → C₆ + Li⁺ + e⁻ (lithium gives up its electron). The cathode is reduced, Li₁₋ₓCoO₂ + xLi⁺ + xe⁻ → LiCoO₂ (Co(IV) → Co(III)). Charging reverses both.
> - **(B)** Water as an oxidant and as a reductant: see [[CH2113 Past Papers 2024 - Solved|2024]] ES 1D.
> - **(C) Water's stability field.** Between the lines E = −0.059 pH (2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂) and E = 1.23 − 0.059 pH (O₂ + 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ → 2H₂O). Above the upper line, water is oxidised to O₂; below the lower line, it is reduced to H₂. Kinetic overpotentials widen the practical window.
> - **(D) H₂O₂ in acid.** E(right) = 1.78 V > E(left) = 0.68 V, so **yes, it disproportionates**: 2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂, $E=1.10$ V. It is slow without a catalyst.
> - **(E) Ligand effects on E°.**
>   - **Ru**: E rises from aqua (0.25 V) to CN⁻ (0.85 V), so CN⁻ **stabilises Ru(II)**. π back-bonding from the electron-rich 4d⁶ ion into CN⁻ π* is strong.
>   - **Fe**: E falls from aqua (0.77 V) to CN⁻ (0.36 V), so CN⁻ **stabilises Fe(III)** relative to Fe(II). Here the strong σ-donation to the higher-charged ion, and the overall charge of the complexes, dominate.
>
>   Both cyanide complexes are low spin.

> [!example]- Q2 · Crystal-field theory (2 each) ([[CH10 Crystal Field Theory]])
> - **(A) d⁸ square planar.** With a large Δ (strong-field ligands, 4d/5d metals) the high-lying d_x²−y² stays empty. The extra LFSE outweighs the steric cost of square planar over tetrahedral. Examples: [Ni(CN)₄]²⁻, [PtCl₄]²⁻, [Pd(NH₃)₄]²⁺. Weak-field [NiCl₄]²⁻ stays tetrahedral.
> - **(B) Spinels.** **Mn₃O₄** is normal: Mn²⁺ (d⁵ high spin) has no site preference, while Mn³⁺ (d⁴ high spin) gains octahedral CFSE (0.6 Δ_o against 0.27 Δ_o in tetrahedral) and so fills the octahedral sites. **Fe₃O₄** is inverse: Fe³⁺ (d⁵) has no preference, while Fe²⁺ (d⁶ high spin) prefers octahedral (0.4 Δ_o against 0.27 Δ_o). So Fe²⁺ takes octahedral sites and Fe³⁺ splits between tetrahedral and octahedral.
> - **(C)** z-in and z-out splittings: see [[CH2113 Past Papers 2024 - Solved|2024]] ES 6B.
> - **(D)** [Ti(H₂O)₆]³⁺ has a single d–d band (~20 000 cm⁻¹) with a **shoulder** (~17 000 cm⁻¹). The excited state t₂g⁰e_g¹ is Jahn–Teller split, so the transition shows two components.
> - **(E)** (i) [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ → [Co(H₂O)₆]³⁺ + e⁻, E° = −1.83 V (as an oxidation). **Not feasible**: aqua Co(III) is a powerful oxidant. (ii) The cyanide complex is oxidised with E° = +0.83 V, per the handwritten correction. **Feasible**: CN⁻ is a strong field ligand and greatly stabilises low-spin d⁶ Co(III) (CFSE 2.4 Δ_o). [!verify] The sign of the printed potential in (ii) is ambiguous on the scan.

> [!example]- Q3 · Molecular orbital theory (2 + 2 + 4 + 2) ([[CH11 Ligand-Field Theory - Molecular Orbitals of Complexes]])
> (A) SALCs: see 2024 ES 5A. The d-matching ones are the e_g pair.
> (B) **σ-only**: t₂g has no σ SALC partner, so it is **non-bonding**. e_g mixes with the e_g SALCs and becomes the **antibonding e_g*** (the metal-centred upper set). Δ_o is the t₂g–e_g* gap.
> (C) π-acceptors (CO) increase Δ_o; π-donors (Cl⁻) decrease it. See 2024 ES 5B.
> (D) **Synergic M–CO bonding**: σ donation from the CO 5σ (C lone pair, the HOMO) into an empty metal d (or s/p) orbital, plus π back-donation from filled metal d into the CO 2π* (LUMO). Each reinforces the other. Back-donation lowers ν(CO) and strengthens M–C.

> [!example]- Q4 · Organometallic chemistry (8 + 2)
> | complex | oxidation state | count |
> |---|---|---|
> | (i) [IrBr₂(CH₃)(CO)(PPh₃)₂] | **Ir(III), d⁶** | 6 + 4 + 2 + 2 + 4 = **18** |
> | (ii) [Cr(η⁵-C₅H₅)(η⁶-C₆H₆)] | **Cr(I), d⁵** | 5 + 6 + 6 = **17** (a radical, easily oxidised) |
> | (iii) [Mn(CO)₅]⁻ | **Mn(−I), d⁸** | 8 + 10 = **18** |
> | (iv) [HFe₂(CO)₈]⁻ | **Fe(0), d⁸** each (H as hydride) | 2·8 + 16 + 2 = 34; with an Fe–Fe bond (or the 3c–2e Fe–H–Fe), **18** each |
>
> [!verify] The ring in (ii) is read as C₅H₅ from the hand-corrected scan.
> (B) **Oxidative addition**: a reagent X–Y adds to a metal, the X–Y bond breaks and two M–X and M–Y bonds form. The oxidation state and coordination number each rise by 2, and the electron count by 2 (e.g. 16 e⁻ → 18 e⁻). **Reductive elimination** is the reverse: two cis ligands couple and leave as X–Y (e.g. R–H), and the oxidation state falls by 2.
