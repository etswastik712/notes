# Thermometer experiment: temperature of the campus

## Covered
A class-wide field experiment (announced 2026-08-07). The class is split into 15 groups, each with a leader and a thermometer (member limit 19 per group; unbalanced groups are randomly regrouped). Within a group, subgroups of three hold the thermometer for one day each, from Saturday through the following Thursday. Data collection runs until Thursday night; the Friday tutorial analyses the data.

- What and where to measure is open: for example the mean temperature of the campus, or variation over time; collaborating with other groups is allowed.
- Step 1 of the instructions: check that all 15 thermometers agree with each other and give sensible readings for objects of known temperature (for example melting ice, all measured at the same time).
- Data go into one shared Google Sheet, "Temperature of the campus", with columns: entry number, datetime (yyyy-mm-dd hh:mm:ss), latitude, longitude (decimal degrees), temperature (°C), surface, name and roll number, zone and group. The first row is an example. No separate sheets.
- Entries seen include surfaces such as pavement, road, grass, tree, wall in sun and in shade, and ground in sun and in shade; for example, on one morning a ground-in-sun reading was 38 °C against 27.3 °C in shade. Campus coordinates are about 18.54–18.55 N, 73.80–73.81 E.
- Prize for the group with the best sampling strategy.
- Safety: do not fall into wells or enter restricted areas.

This ties to the Lecture 2 themes of representative sampling and area-weighted averaging, and to why Stevenson-screen siting avoids paved surfaces.

## From the post
"IT IS EXPERIMENT TIME!! Please join a group of your choice, if you haven't already ("Groups and leaders" google sheet below). The member limit for a group is 19. If there are groups with too few/ too much members, we will randomly group people. Within each group, a set of three people will have the thermometer for themselves for the each day of the week. What and where you measure, what you want to observe is entirely up to you. You can come up with your own problem. You could observe the mean temperature of the campus, for instance. You could see how temperature varies over time. Feel free to collaborate with other groups and do whatever idea you think is interesting. All groups should enter their data together, into the attached Google Sheet named "Temperature of the campus". The format for data collection is given in the first example row. You can collect data right from now, till Thursday night. Friday morning tutorial we will analyse the collected data. Have a lot of fun!"

## Follow-up (2026-08-14)
Announcement from 2026-08-14: the shared sheet has about 3k data points. The next phase is entirely ungraded and optional. Groups of up to 6 can do anything with the data (or collect more) and summarise their journey (reasons, methods, challenges, findings, significance) as 5 slides (four figures plus bullet points) or a 1-page PDF, mailed to their TA by 2026-08-31 23:59. Each TA picks a best project and gives a prize to the winning team(s). (Classroom)

Sheet columns: entry number, datetime, latitude, longitude, temperature (°C), surface, name and roll number, zone and group. Entries show mixed date formats and some outliers (e.g. readings of ~46–50 °C on cloth or exposed ground), worth cleaning before analysis.
