## Must know
- **Planetary boundaries:** nine boundaries define a "safe operating space"; 6 of 9 are transgressed (climate change, biosphere integrity, land-system change, freshwater change, biogeochemical flows, novel entities); ocean acidification, aerosol loading (breached regionally) and stratospheric ozone are within limits. Climate boundaries: CO$_2$ 350 ppm (now >417 ppm), radiative forcing 1.0 W m$^{-2}$ (now ~2.91 W m$^{-2}$); HANPP <10 % (now ~30 %) (Classroom: Lecture 1 notes, after Richardson et al. 2023).
- **Tipping point:** a critical threshold where a small change in one component can cause a large, potentially irreversible shift in the system; tipping elements are connected (Arctic sea ice → Greenland → AMOC → Amazon and Antarctic ice) (Classroom: Lecture 1 notes, after Lenton et al. 2019).
- **Ice-albedo feedback:** positive; cooling → more ice → higher albedo (snow up to ~90 %, ice 50–70 %) → less absorbed sunlight → more cooling. The IR flux/temperature feedback ($\sigma T^4$) is the basic negative feedback (Classroom: Lecture 1 notes; your notes: [[01-climate/concepts/ice-albedo-feedback]]).
- **Area-weighted global mean:** $\bar T=\sum_i T_iA_i/\sum_i A_i$; box size set by the correlation length (hundreds of km to ~1000 km) (Classroom: Lecture 2 notes).
- **$\delta^{18}$O definition and sign:** $\delta^{18}\mathrm{O}=\left[\frac{(^{18}\mathrm{O}/^{16}\mathrm{O})_{\text{sample}}}{(^{18}\mathrm{O}/^{16}\mathrm{O})_{\text{standard}}}-1\right]\times1000$ ‰. Ice is depleted in $^{18}$O (ice sheets ≈ −50 ‰); colder periods give more negative ice-core $\delta^{18}$O and heavier foraminifera shells (Classroom: Lecture 2 notes; your notes: [[01-climate/concepts/isotopic-thermometers]]).
- **Planck, Wien, Stefan–Boltzmann:** $I(\lambda,T)=\frac{2hc^2}{\lambda^5(e^{hc/\lambda k_BT}-1)}$; $\lambda_{\max}\approx2898/T\ \mu\text{m}$ (Earth ~10 µm at 288 K); $F=\sigma T^4$ with $\sigma=5.67\times10^{-8}$ W m$^{-2}$ K$^{-4}$ (Classroom: Lecture 2 notes; Lecture 3 notes).
- **0-D energy balance and the $S/4$ factor:** the Earth intercepts sunlight over a disk $\pi R^2$ but radiates from $4\pi R^2$, so mean incoming flux is $S/4$ (~340 W m$^{-2}$); equilibrium $\frac{S}{4}(1-\alpha)=\sigma T^4$ gives $T_e=255$ K for $\alpha\approx0.3$, against observed $T_s=288$ K; the 33 K difference is the greenhouse effect. Steady state means $\partial T/\partial t=0$ after averaging over space and time (Classroom: Lecture 3 notes; your notes: [[01-climate/concepts/zero-dimensional-energy-balance-model]]).
- **Stable vs unstable steady state:** stable: a disturbance triggers internal responses returning the system to it (Earth: IR flux/temperature feedback; warmer means $\mathrm{LW_{out}}>\mathrm{SW_{net}}$ and the planet cools). Unstable: stays only if undisturbed; any perturbation moves it away (Snowball threshold at ~30° ice latitude; Venus's runaway greenhouse) (Classroom: Lecture 3 notes).
- **Why planets miss the 1:1 line:** Venus (93-bar CO$_2$ plus water vapour, ≈ 730 K), Mars (thin ~10 mbar atmosphere, greenhouse ~5 K), Mercury (no atmosphere; $\langle T^4\rangle>\langle T\rangle^4$ so the model overestimates its mean temperature), Jupiter and Saturn (internal heat: accretion residue, gravitational contraction, Saturn's helium rain) (Classroom: Lecture 3 notes).
- **Sensitivity by log-differentiation:** from $\frac{S}{4}(1-\alpha)=\sigma T_s^4$, $\frac{\Delta S}{S}-\frac{\Delta\alpha}{1-\alpha}=4\frac{\Delta T_s}{T_s}$; $\Delta\alpha=0.07$ gives ~6 K, a 10 % change in $S$ gives ~6 K (Classroom: Lecture 4 notes).
- **Radiative forcing magnitudes:** sunspot cycle ~1 W m$^{-2}$ (solar forcing since 1750 ≈ 0.06 W m$^{-2}$); total anthropogenic ≈ 3 W m$^{-2}$, CO$_2$ dominant; aerosols and albedo-raising land use cool; observed warming ≈ 1.1 °C vs 1850–1900, with greenhouse gases ≈ +1.5 °C offset by ≈ −0.5 °C from aerosols (Classroom: Lecture 4 notes).
- **Planck feedback (no atmosphere, constant albedo):** $\Lambda_P=\frac{dT_s}{dF}=\frac{1}{4\sigma T_s^3}\approx0.28$ K (W m$^{-2}$)$^{-1}$ at 255 K per the lecture notes, i.e. ≈ 3.8 W m$^{-2}$ per kelvin; negative feedback (Classroom: Lecture 4 notes).
- **Albedo feedback factor:** $f_\alpha=\frac{\partial T_s}{\partial\alpha}\frac{\partial\alpha}{\partial T_s}>0$ for ice-albedo; $\Lambda=\frac{\Lambda_F}{1-f_\alpha}>\Lambda_P$ (feedback amplifies) (Classroom: Lecture 4 notes; your notes: [[01-climate/concepts/climate-feedback-factor]]).
- **Multiple steady states:** with $\alpha=0.7$ below 253 K, linear to 293 K, $0.2$ above, the $\mathrm{SW_{net}}$ and $\mathrm{LW_{out}}$ curves cross three times: stable Snowball, unstable middle, stable ice-free (Classroom: Lecture 4 notes).
- **Snowball Earth (Cryogenian, ~720–635 Ma):** evidence = low-latitude glacial sediments (within 15° of the equator), cap carbonates, $\delta^{13}$C crash; trigger = CO$_2$ drawdown (Rodinia breakup, tropical silicate weathering); runaway when ice reaches ~30°; escape = volcanic CO$_2$ buildup to ~0.1 bar (~300× modern) over ~10 Myr, shutting down weathering meanwhile; hot aftermath 50–60 °C (Classroom: Lecture 4 notes; your notes: [[01-climate/concepts/snowball-earth]]).
- **Barometric law and scale height (Lecture 5):** hydrostatic balance $\frac{dp}{dz}=-\rho g$ with $\rho=\frac{mp}{k_BT}$ at constant $T$ gives $p(z)=p_0e^{-z/H}$, $H=\frac{k_BT}{mg}$ (pressure falls by $1/e$ over $H$); $H\approx7.5$–$8$ km for $T=255$ K, $m=5\times10^{-26}$ kg (Classroom: Lecture 5 notes).
- **Vertical vs horizontal pressure gradient (Lecture 5):** near the surface $\approx1$ hPa per 8 m ($12\,500$ Pa km$^{-1}$); a severe typhoon gives 70 hPa over ~500 km ($0.14$ hPa km$^{-1}$); ratio ≈ $9\times10^4$, i.e. 4–5 orders of magnitude, which justifies hydrostatic balance (Classroom: Lecture 5 notes).
- **Atmospheric layering and mixing (Lecture 5):** troposphere to ~10–15 km (6.5 °C km$^{-1}$ decrease, ~80 % of mass, nearly all water vapour); stratosphere to ~50 km, warming with height because ozone absorbs UV, stable against mixing; homosphere below ~100 km (turbulent mixing keeps N$_2$ 78 %, O$_2$ 21 %, Ar 0.9 % constant), heterosphere above (separation by molecular weight). Long-lived gases outlast the 1–2 year inter-hemispheric mixing time; short-lived gases vary strongly in space and time (Classroom: Lecture 5 notes).
- **Beer's law and optical depth (Lecture 5):** $dI_\lambda=-I_\lambda K_\lambda N\sigma\,ds\Rightarrow I_\lambda=I_{\lambda,0}e^{-\tau_\lambda}$ with $\tau_\lambda=\int\rho\,r\,k_\lambda\,dz$; $\tau_\lambda=1$ leaves 37 % of the intensity; extinction = scattering + absorption (Classroom: Lecture 5 notes; your notes: [[01-climate/concepts/beer-lambert-law-and-optical-depth]]).
- **Rayleigh vs Mie scattering (Lecture 5):** Rayleigh ($r\ll\lambda$) $\propto\lambda^{-4}$, blue sky; Mie ($r\approx\lambda$) weakly wavelength-dependent, white/grey clouds (Classroom: Lecture 5 notes).
- **Shortwave/longwave split and Wien peaks (Lecture 5):** boundary at 4 µm; $\lambda_{\max}\approx2897/T$ µm; Sun (5780 K) peaks ≈ 0.5 µm, Earth (≈ 288 K) ≈ 10 µm (Classroom: Lecture 5 notes).
- **Which gases absorb IR (Lecture 5):** absorption needs a transition dipole moment; symmetric N$_2$, O$_2$ do not absorb; H$_2$O, CO$_2$, O$_3$ do. Rotational transitions are far-IR ($\lambda>12$ µm); vibrational are mid-IR (CO$_2$ bend 15 µm, O$_3$ 9.6 µm) (Classroom: Lecture 5 notes; your notes: [[01-climate/concepts/greenhouse-gases-and-absorption-bands]]).
- **UV and the atmospheric window (Lecture 5):** UVC (200–290 nm) fully absorbed by O$_2$/ozone formation; UVB (290–320 nm) partly absorbed by ozone; UVA (320–400 nm) mostly reaches the surface. The 8–12 µm atmospheric window is transparent (brightness temperature ≈ 288–290 K); CH$_4$, N$_2$O and CFCs absorb inside it, which makes them potent (Classroom: Lecture 5 notes; your notes: [[01-climate/concepts/atmospheric-window]]).
- **One-layer semi-transparent greenhouse model (Lecture 6):** atmosphere transparent to shortwave, longwave emissivity $\varepsilon$ = absorptivity (Kirchhoff); steady-state balances at TOA, atmosphere and surface give $T_s=\left(\frac{2}{2-\varepsilon}\right)^{1/4}T_e$; for $\varepsilon=1$, $T_s=2^{1/4}T_e\approx303$ K, 15 K above the observed 288 K because the real atmosphere has the 8–12 µm window (Classroom: Lecture 6 notes; your notes: [[01-climate/cl08-greenhouse-models]]).
- **Planets in the one-layer model (Lecture 6):** Earth needs an intermediate $\varepsilon$; Mars has a thin CO$_2$ atmosphere and a weak effect; Venus cannot be reproduced even with $\varepsilon=1$ (multi-layer greenhouse, optical depth ~1000 times larger than 1, high albedo from sulfuric-acid clouds); Jupiter and Saturn lie above the curves because of internal heat (Classroom: Lecture 6 notes).
- **Diurnal temperature lag (Lecture 6):** for a bare-rock surface with heat capacity per area (density × 20 cm depth × specific heat), the temperature peaks 2–4 h after noon because absorbed solar still exceeds outgoing longwave; with zero heat capacity it would peak at noon and plunge at sunset. Real swings are smaller because of greenhouse back-radiation, sensible and latent heat fluxes, and deep-soil conduction (Classroom: Lecture 6 notes).
- **Eulerian vs. Lagrangian descriptions:** Eulerian represents atmospheric behavior in terms of field properties (PDEs at fixed space points); Lagrangian tracks the state and composition of individual moving fluid parcels of fixed matter (Classroom: Salby, pp. 1–2).
- **Kinematic constraint of gravity:** Gravity confines $>90\%$ of atmospheric mass to a shallow layer ($<10\text{ km}$, $<0.15\%$ of Earth's radius). Consequently, large-scale motions ($L > 100\text{ km}$) are strictly quasi-horizontal ($w \ll u, v$); vertical displacements equal horizontal scales only in small convective cells and fronts ($L \sim 10\text{ km}$) (Classroom: Salby, p. 2; your notes: [[01-Climate/CL06 Hydrostatic Balance and Vertical Structure]]).
- **Dry air apparent molecular weight:** $M_d = \sum_i \chi_i M_i = 28.97\text{ g mol}^{-1}$ (using $\chi_{\text{N}_2} = 0.7808, \chi_{\text{O}_2} = 0.2095, \chi_{\text{Ar}} = 0.0093, \chi_{\text{CO}_2} = 0.0004$) (Classroom: Wallace & Hobbs, p. 65; Tutorial 2).
- **Specific gas constant for dry air:** $R_d = 1000 \frac{R^*}{M_d} = 1000 \frac{8.3145}{28.97} = 287.0\text{ J K}^{-1}\text{ kg}^{-1}$; equation of state: $p_d = \rho_d R_d T$ (Classroom: Wallace & Hobbs, p. 65).
- **Water vapor specific gas constant and $\epsilon$:** $M_w = 18.016\text{ g mol}^{-1} \implies R_v = 1000 \frac{R^*}{M_w} = 461.51\text{ J K}^{-1}\text{ kg}^{-1}$. The ratio $\epsilon \equiv \frac{R_d}{R_v} = \frac{M_w}{M_d} \approx 0.622$ (Classroom: Wallace & Hobbs, p. 66).
- **Moist air density:** Because $M_w < M_d$, replacing dry air molecules with water vapor at identical $T$ and $p$ lowers mixture density: moist air is less dense than dry air (Classroom: Wallace & Hobbs, p. 66).
- **Virtual temperature exact formula:** $T_v \equiv \frac{T}{1 - \frac{e}{p}(1 - \epsilon)}$. First-order approximation: $T_v \approx T\left(1 + 0.378 \frac{e}{p}\right) \approx T(1 + 0.61 w)$, where $w \approx \epsilon \frac{e}{p}$ is mass mixing ratio. Equation of state for moist air: $p = \rho R_d T_v$. By definition, $T_v \ge T$ always (Classroom: Wallace & Hobbs, p. 67; your notes: [[01-Climate/Concepts/Virtual Temperature]]).
- **Material tracer conservation:** A passive constituent ($\dot{m}_i = 0$) has a materially conserved mass mixing ratio: $\frac{Dr_i}{Dt} = 0$, where $r_i = m_i / m_d$. Molar fraction and volume mixing ratio satisfy $N_i \approx r_i / \epsilon_i$ with $\epsilon_i = M_i / M_d$ (Classroom: Salby, pp. 6–7).
- **Hydrostatic balance equation:** In the absence of vertical acceleration ($dw/dt = 0$), vertical pressure gradient balances gravity: $\frac{\partial p}{\partial z} = -\rho g = -\frac{g}{\alpha}$. Pressure at altitude $z$ is the column weight per unit area: $p(z) = \int_z^\infty \rho g \, dz'$ (Classroom: Wallace & Hobbs, p. 68; Holton & Hakim, p. 7).
- **Geopotential and Geopotential Height:** $d\Phi = g \, dz = -\alpha \, dp$. $\Phi(z) = \int_0^z g(z') \, dz'$ ($\text{J kg}^{-1}$). Geopotential height: $Z \equiv \Phi(z) / g_0$, with $g_0 \equiv 9.80665\text{ m s}^{-2}$ (Classroom: Wallace & Hobbs, pp. 68–69; your notes: [[01-Climate/Concepts/Geopotential]]).
- **Hypsometric equation:** Geopotential thickness $\Delta Z = Z_2 - Z_1 = \frac{R_d \bar{T}_v}{g_0} \ln\left(\frac{p_1}{p_2}\right) = \bar{H} \ln\left(\frac{p_1}{p_2}\right)$. Thickness is strictly proportional to mean layer virtual temperature $\bar{T}_v$ (Classroom: Wallace & Hobbs, p. 69; your notes: [[01-Climate/Concepts/Hypsometric Equation]]).
- **Atmospheric scale height:** $H \equiv \frac{R_d T}{g_0} \approx 29.3 \, T\text{ m}$; for $T \approx 255\text{ K}$, $H \approx 7.5\text{ km} \approx 8\text{ km}$. Isothermal barometric law: $p(Z) = p_1 e^{-(Z - Z_1)/H}$ (Classroom: Wallace & Hobbs, p. 69; Salby, p. 7).
- **Turbopause (~105 km):** Below 105 km, eddy mixing maintains constant mean molecular weight $M_d = 28.97$ and a common scale height $H$. Above 105 km, molecular diffusion separates gases by individual molecular scale heights $H_i = R^* T / (M_i g)$, so heavier gases ($\text{N}_2, \text{O}_2$) decay faster than lighter gases ($\text{He}, \text{H}$) (Classroom: Wallace & Hobbs, pp. 69–70).
- **Pressure gradient force (PGF) per unit mass:** Derived from Taylor expansion on an infinitesimal volume $\delta V = \delta x \delta y \delta z$: $\frac{\mathbf{F}_{pg}}{m} = -\frac{1}{\rho}\nabla p$. PGF is proportional to the gradient of pressure, not pressure magnitude, accelerating fluid from high to low pressure (Classroom: Holton & Hakim, pp. 5–7; your notes: [[01-Climate/Concepts/Pressure Gradient Force]]).
- **Classification of forces:** Body forces act on parcel mass (gravity $\mathbf{g}^* = -\frac{GM}{r^2}\frac{\mathbf{r}}{r}$); surface forces act across parcel boundaries (pressure, viscosity). In rotating frames, apparent inertial forces (Coriolis $-2\boldsymbol{\Omega}\times\mathbf{v}$, centrifugal $\boldsymbol{\Omega}\times(\boldsymbol{\Omega}\times\mathbf{r})$) must be included (Classroom: Holton & Hakim, pp. 5–7).
- **Lorenz convection equations:** $\dot{x} = \sigma(y - x)$, $\dot{y} = x(\rho - z) - y$, $\dot{z} = xy - \beta z$ with standard parameters $\sigma = 10, \rho = 28, \beta = 8/3$. Dissipative volume contraction $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{f} = -(\sigma + 1 + \beta) = -41/3 < 0$. Trajectories are bounded on a strange attractor with sensitive dependence on initial conditions ($\lambda_{\max} > 0$), limiting deterministic prediction (Classroom: announcement 2026-08-27, Tutorial 2).
- **Planetary emission temperature derivation:** At radiative equilibrium, absorbed solar power equals emitted longwave radiation: $(1 - \alpha_p) S_0 \pi a^2 = 4\pi a^2 \sigma T_e^4 \implies T_e = \left[\frac{S_0(1-\alpha_p)}{4\sigma}\right]^{1/4}$. For Earth ($S_0 = 1367\text{ W m}^{-2}, \alpha_p = 0.30$), $T_e \approx 255\text{ K} = -18^\circ\text{C}$ (Classroom: Marshall & Plumb Ch 2; your notes: [[01-climate/concepts/effective-emission-temperature]]).
- **Wien's displacement law and spectral separation:** $\lambda_{\max} T \approx 2898\,\mu\text{m K}$. Solar radiation peaks in the visible at $\sim 0.5–0.6\,\mu\text{m}$ ($T_\odot \approx 6000\text{ K}$); Earth's thermal emission peaks in the infrared at $\sim 14\,\mu\text{m}$ ($T_e \approx 255\text{ K}$). Negligible spectral overlap allows clean separation into shortwave insolation and longwave terrestrial emission (Classroom: Marshall & Plumb Ch 2; your notes: [[03-thermodynamics/concepts/blackbody-radiation]]).
- **Single-layer opaque greenhouse model:** An atmospheric layer transparent to solar shortwave and completely opaque to infrared ($\epsilon = 1$) emits $\sigma T_a^4$ upward and downward. At TOA, $\sigma T_a^4 = \sigma T_e^4 \implies T_a = T_e = 255\text{ K}$. At the surface, $\sigma T_s^4 = \sigma T_e^4 + \sigma T_a^4 = 2\sigma T_e^4 \implies T_s = 2^{1/4} T_e \approx 303\text{ K}$ (Classroom: Marshall & Plumb Ch 2; your notes: [[01-climate/cl08-greenhouse-models]]).
- **Single-layer leaky greenhouse model:** For an atmospheric layer with infrared emissivity/absorptivity $\epsilon < 1$ (by Kirchhoff's law): $T_s = \left(\frac{2}{2 - \epsilon}\right)^{1/4} T_e$ and $T_a = \left(\frac{1}{2 - \epsilon}\right)^{1/4} T_e = 2^{-1/4} T_s$. The atmosphere is always colder than the surface ($T_a < T_s$). For Earth ($T_s = 288\text{ K}, T_e = 255\text{ K}$), effective emissivity is $\epsilon \approx 0.77$ (Classroom: Marshall & Plumb Ch 2; your notes: [[01-climate/cl08-greenhouse-models]]).
- **Multi-layer radiative equilibrium and surface temperature discontinuity:** In continuous radiative equilibrium, downwelling infrared emission from the lowest atmospheric layer is insufficient to warm adjacent air to the ground temperature, producing an inherent finite temperature discontinuity at the surface ($T_s \gg T_{\text{air}}(0^+)$) with superadiabatic lapse rate $-\partial T/\partial z \gg \Gamma_d$, which is dynamically unstable to vertical convection (Classroom: Marshall & Plumb Ch 2, 4; Manabe & Strickler 1964; your notes: [[01-climate/cl09-radiative-convective-equilibrium]]).
- **Radiative-convective equilibrium (RCE) structure:** Vertical convective overturning neutralizes the superadiabatic lower atmosphere up to the tropopause $z_T$, maintaining a moist adiabatic lapse rate $\Gamma \approx 6.5\text{ K km}^{-1}$, while pure radiative equilibrium governs the stratosphere above $z_T$. In RCE, net radiative cooling of the troposphere is balanced by vertical convective heat convergence (Classroom: Marshall & Plumb Ch 4; Manabe & Strickler 1964; your notes: [[01-climate/concepts/convective-adjustment]]).
- **Buoyancy frequency formula in temperature coordinates:** $N^2 = \frac{g}{T_E}\left(\frac{dT_E}{dz} + \Gamma_d\right)$, derived from $N^2 \equiv \frac{g}{\theta_E}\frac{d\theta_E}{dz}$ with hydrostatic balance $\frac{dp}{dz} = -\rho g$ and ideal gas law. Atmospheric stability requires $\frac{dT_E}{dz} > -\Gamma_d$ ($N^2 > 0$) (Classroom: Marshall & Plumb Ch 4; your notes: [[01-climate/concepts/atmospheric-stability]]).
- **Laboratory convective layer growth law:** Under constant bottom heat flux $H$ into a linearly stratified fluid $T(z, 0) = T_0 + T_z z$, the well-mixed layer depth $h(t)$ satisfies $\frac{1}{2}\rho c_p T_z \frac{d(h^2)}{dt} = H$, giving encroachment depth $h(t) = \sqrt{\frac{2 H t}{\rho c_p T_z}} \propto \sqrt{t}$ and layer temperature $T(t) - T_0 \propto \sqrt{t}$ (Classroom: Marshall & Plumb Ch 4).
- **Climate sensitivity and feedbacks:** Planck blackbody negative feedback gives baseline sensitivity $\left(\frac{\partial T_s}{\partial Q}\right)_{\text{BB}} = \frac{1}{4\sigma T_e^3} \approx 0.26\text{ K}/(\text{W m}^{-2})$. Clausius–Clapeyron relation $\frac{de_s}{e_s} \approx 0.067\, dT$ (~$7\%$ vapor increase per $^\circ\text{C}$) provides a strong positive water vapor feedback, doubling sensitivity to $\approx 0.5\text{ K}/(\text{W m}^{-2})$ (Classroom: Marshall & Plumb Ch 2).
- **Insolation on a non-tilted Earth:** Diurnal mean incident solar flux per unit area is $\bar{S}(\phi) = \frac{S_0}{\pi}\cos\phi$, maximizing at $S_{\max} = S_0/\pi \approx 435\text{ W m}^{-2}$ at the equator and falling monotonically to zero at the poles (Classroom: Marshall & Plumb Ch 5; your notes: [[01-climate/cl10-global-energy-balance-and-meridional-structure]]).
- **Summer solstice polar insolation peak:** Continuous 24-hour polar day illumination produces a daily averaged TOA insolation peak at the summer pole exceeding $500\text{ W m}^{-2}$ (higher than the equator), but high ice/snow albedo ($\alpha \approx 0.60–0.90$) maintains very low net absorbed solar radiation (Classroom: Marshall & Plumb Ch 5, Basics of atmosphere doc; your notes: [[01-climate/cl10-global-energy-balance-and-meridional-structure]]).
- **Isentropic surfaces in the troposphere:** Potential temperature increases with altitude everywhere ($\partial\bar{\theta}/\partial z > 0$). Surfaces of constant $\theta$ slope upward from the tropics toward the poles throughout the troposphere. Contours are widely spaced in the troposphere and tightly packed in the stratosphere (Classroom: Marshall & Plumb Ch 5; your notes: [[01-climate/cl10-global-energy-balance-and-meridional-structure]]).
- **Tropopause structure:** Slopes downward from an altitude of $\sim 16–17\text{ km}$ ($\sim 100\text{ mbar}$) at the equator to $\sim 8–9\text{ km}$ ($\sim 300\text{ mbar}$) over the poles, causing the tropical tropopause to be much colder ($T \sim 190–200\text{ K}$) than the polar tropopause ($T \sim 220–230\text{ K}$) (Classroom: Marshall & Plumb Ch 5; your notes: [[01-climate/cl10-global-energy-balance-and-meridional-structure]]).
- **TOA Net Radiation formulation:** $R_{\text{TOA}} = Q_{\text{abs}} - \text{OLR} = S_{\text{TOA}}(1 - \alpha) - \text{OLR}$. Global mean planetary albedo is $\alpha_p \approx 0.29$ ($29\%$), with minimum albedo ($8–10\%$) over clear oceans and maximum ($>60\%$) over polar snow and ice (Classroom: Hartmann Ch 2; your notes: [[01-climate/cl10-global-energy-balance-and-meridional-structure]]).
- **Subtropical desert radiative deficit:** Arid regions like the Sahara experience an annual-mean net radiative loss ($R_{\text{TOA}} < 0$) due to high sand albedo ($\alpha \approx 0.35–0.45$) and massive OLR emission through dry cloudless skies. The deficit is balanced by lateral atmospheric heat convergence and adiabatic subsidence warming (Classroom: Hartmann Ch 2; also Hartmann Ch 6: import of high-energy air aloft, subsidence with radiative cooling, low-level moisture export).
- **Polar cap energy transport integral:** Total northward energy transport $F(\phi)$ across latitude $\phi$ is given by $F(\phi) = 2\pi a^2 \int_{-\pi/2}^\phi \bar{R}_{\text{TOA}}(\phi') \cos\phi' \, d\phi'$. Net transport peaks in midlatitudes ($35^\circ–40^\circ\text{N/S}$) at $\approx 5.8\text{ PW}$ ($5.8 \times 10^{15}\text{ W}$), with $F(\pi/2) \equiv 0$ by global energy conservation (Classroom: Hartmann Ch 2; Marshall & Plumb Ch 5; your notes: [[01-climate/concepts/poleward-energy-transport]]).
- **Atmosphere vs. Ocean heat transport partition:** At $20^\circ\text{N}$, atmosphere and ocean transport equal amounts ($\approx 2.0\text{ PW}$ each). Oceanic transport peaks in the subtropics ($\sim 20^\circ\text{N}$) driven by wind-driven gyres, while atmospheric transport dominates mid- and high latitudes, peaking broadly near $40^\circ\text{N/S}$ via baroclinic eddies (Classroom: Hartmann Ch 2; your notes: [[01-climate/concepts/poleward-energy-transport]]).
- **Tropospheric vertical aspect ratio and velocity scale:** Synoptic scale ratio $H/L \sim 10\text{ km}/1000\text{ km} \sim 10^{-2}$. By mass continuity, large-scale vertical velocity is $w \sim (H/L) U \sim 1–10\text{ cm s}^{-1}$, which is $10^2$ to $10^3$ times smaller than horizontal winds ($u, v \sim 10–30\text{ m s}^{-1}$) (Classroom: Marshall & Plumb Ch 5).
- **Subtropical jet core characteristics:** Maximum zonal westerly wind occurs near $30^\circ$ latitude at $\sim 200\text{ mbar}$ in the winter hemisphere, with climatological mean speed $\approx 30\text{ m s}^{-1}$ and local synoptic speeds up to $50\text{ m s}^{-1}$ (Classroom: Marshall & Plumb Ch 5).
- **Seasonal Hadley cell asymmetry:** In DJF, air rises south of the equator and subsides strongly in the northern subtropics ($\sim 30^\circ\text{N}$); in JJA, air rises north of the equator and subsides in the southern subtropics ($\sim 30^\circ\text{S}$). The winter cell is much stronger and crosses the equator (Classroom: Marshall & Plumb Ch 5; your notes: [[01-climate/cl11-mean-meridional-circulation-and-angular-momentum]]).
- **Extratropical heat transport by eddies:** In midlatitudes, mean meridional wind is negligible ($\bar{v} < 1\text{ m s}^{-1}$). Heat and moisture transport is carried virtually entirely by the eddy covariance terms: $\overline{v' T'} > 0$ and $\overline{v' q'} > 0$ in synoptic cyclones and anticyclones (Classroom: Marshall & Plumb Ch 5; your notes: [[01-climate/cl10-global-energy-balance-and-meridional-structure]]).
- **Global surface torque balance:** In steady state, net surface frictional torque over the globe must vanish ($\int \tau_{\text{drag}} dA = 0$). Easterly trade winds in the tropics provide an eastward torque (atmosphere gains westerly angular momentum), while midlatitude surface westerlies provide a westward torque (atmosphere loses westerly angular momentum), necessitating poleward atmospheric angular momentum transport (Classroom: Marshall & Plumb Ch 8; your notes: [[01-climate/cl11-mean-meridional-circulation-and-angular-momentum]]).
- **Absolute angular momentum on a sphere:** $A = \Omega a^2 \cos^2\phi + u a \cos\phi$. For an axisymmetric parcel ascending at the equator with $u=0$, conservation of $A$ along the upper Hadley branch yields $u(\phi) = \Omega a \frac{\sin^2\phi}{\cos\phi}$. This requires $u = 14\text{ m s}^{-1}$ at $10^\circ$, $58\text{ m s}^{-1}$ at $20^\circ$, $130\text{ m s}^{-1}$ at $30^\circ$, and $\to \infty$ at the pole, demonstrating that a single equator-to-pole cell is dynamically impossible on a rapidly rotating planet (Classroom: Marshall & Plumb Ch 8; your notes: [[01-climate/cl11-mean-meridional-circulation-and-angular-momentum]]).
- **General vector transformation rule between frames:** For any vector $\mathbf{A}$, $\left(\frac{D\mathbf{A}}{Dt}\right)_{\text{in}} = \left(\frac{D\mathbf{A}}{Dt}\right)_{\text{rot}} + \boldsymbol{\Omega} \times \mathbf{A}$, derived from rotating unit basis vectors $(D\hat{\mathbf{x}}_i/Dt)_{\text{in}} = \boldsymbol{\Omega} \times \hat{\mathbf{x}}_i$ (Classroom: Marshall & Plumb Ch 6; your notes: [[01-climate/cl12-rotating-frames-and-the-coriolis-force]]).
- **Apparent accelerations in a rotating frame:** $\left(\frac{D\mathbf{u}_{\text{in}}}{Dt}\right)_{\text{in}} = \left(\frac{D\mathbf{u}}{Dt}\right)_{\text{rot}} + 2\boldsymbol{\Omega} \times \mathbf{u} + \boldsymbol{\Omega} \times (\boldsymbol{\Omega} \times \mathbf{r})$. Centrifugal acceleration is $-\boldsymbol{\Omega} \times (\boldsymbol{\Omega} \times \mathbf{r}) = \nabla(\frac{1}{2}\Omega^2 r^2)$; Coriolis acceleration is $-2\boldsymbol{\Omega} \times \mathbf{u}$ (Classroom: Marshall & Plumb Ch 6; your notes: [[01-climate/cl12-rotating-frames-and-the-coriolis-force]]).
- **Workless property of Coriolis force:** $(-2\boldsymbol{\Omega} \times \mathbf{u}) \cdot \mathbf{u} \equiv 0$. The Coriolis force acts strictly perpendicular to parcel velocity, doing zero work and altering only the direction of flow without changing kinetic energy (Classroom: Marshall & Plumb Ch 6; your notes: [[01-climate/concepts/coriolis-force]]).
- **Modified gravitational potential (geopotential):** Combining Newtonian gravity with centrifugal potential gives $\Phi = gz - \frac{1}{2}\Omega^2 r^2$, such that effective gravity is $-\nabla\Phi$. In solid-body rotation ($\mathbf{u} = 0$), the free surface of an incompressible fluid deforms into a paraboloid: $z(r) = h(0) + \frac{\Omega^2 r^2}{2g}$ (Classroom: Marshall & Plumb Ch 6).
- **Rossby number and geostrophic scaling:** $R_o=\mathcal U/(fL)$, the ratio of the acceleration terms ($\sim\mathcal U^2/L$) to the Coriolis term ($f\mathcal U$); $\simeq0.1$ for mid-latitude atmospheric flow, $\simeq10^{-3}$ for large-scale ocean flow; fails near the equator where $f\to0$ (Classroom: Marshall & Plumb Ch 7, Study material 13).
- **Geostrophic balance and wind:** for small $R_o$, $f\hat{\mathbf z}\times\mathbf u+\frac1\rho\nabla p=0$, i.e. $\mathbf u_g=\frac{1}{f\rho}\hat{\mathbf z}\times\nabla p$, $(u_g,v_g)=\left(-\frac{1}{f\rho}\frac{\partial p}{\partial y},\frac{1}{f\rho}\frac{\partial p}{\partial x}\right)$. The flow is along isobars, stronger where isobars are closer; clockwise around highs and anticlockwise around lows for $f>0$ (Buys-Ballot: back to the wind in the Northern Hemisphere, low pressure on your left) (Classroom: Marshall & Plumb Ch 7, Study material 13).
- **Geostrophic wind in pressure coordinates:** $\mathbf u_g=\frac gf\hat{\mathbf z}_p\times\nabla_pz$, $(u_g,v_g)=\left(-\frac gf\frac{\partial z}{\partial y},\frac gf\frac{\partial z}{\partial x}\right)$, with no explicit density; $\nabla_p\cdot\mathbf u_g=0$ for constant $f$, streamfunction $\psi_g=gz/f$, so height contours are streamlines. Example: $\Delta z=800$ m over 5000 km gives $u=g\Delta z/(fL)\approx15$ m s$^{-1}$ (Classroom: Marshall & Plumb Ch 7, Study material 13).
- **Land–sea contrast and monsoon basics:** land heats and cools much more than ocean because of small heat capacity and limited evaporation (ocean puts the heat into a deep layer and evaporates). Lows lie over warm regions and highs over cool ones; in July the dominant NH low is over Asia (~30° N), in January there is a high over Asia and the Aleutian and Icelandic lows. The summer low-level flow of moist air onto heated land (Tibetan Plateau heating) with latent heating gives monsoon rain; winter reverses it; onset is abrupt (~mid-June) (Classroom: Hartmann Ch 6, Study material 12).
- **Causes of deserts:** Hadley subsidence (~10°–40° latitude); lee-side subsidence behind mountains (North American deserts, Patagonia, Gobi); cold coastal SST stabilising the air (Peru/Chile); blocked humid air; deserts show a large diurnal temperature range (dry, cloudless nights) (Classroom: Hartmann Ch 6, Study material 12).
- **Wet climates:** the three equatorial OLR minima are South America, Africa and the Indonesian–western Pacific; the Maritime Continent is the largest tropical convective region and the SPCZ extends southeast from it in SH summer; the Amazon is fed by north-easterly trades, blocked by the Andes, with rainfall recycling; rainy seasons on opposite sides of the equator are 6 months apart (Classroom: Hartmann Ch 6, Study material 12).
- **Monsoon pressure-gradient reversal (advanced reading):** $\Delta\ln p(z_1)=\frac gRz_1\left(\frac1{T_c}-\frac1{T_w}\right)+\Delta\ln p(0)$; with equal surface pressure the upper-level gradient reverses if $T_w>T_c$, but with a surface low over the continent the temperature difference must exceed a threshold, $T_w>\frac{gz_1T_c}{gz_1+R\,\Delta\ln p(0)\,T_c}$, which may explain the sudden onset of the South Asian monsoon; land warms faster than ocean (specific heat of water 4218 vs ≈ 1300 J kg$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$ for dry land, and a shallow active layer) (Classroom: Webster and Fasullo, advanced reading; not necessarily examined).
