# Wien Displacement Law

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span> <span class="scope-extra-beyond">extra · beyond</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** $\lambda_mT=2898$ µm K (Argha L3, Suhas) · 🔵 **extra:** derivation, conventions

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Hotter ⇒ bluer. A candle is orange, the Sun is yellow-white, a welding arc is blue-white: the peak colour slides to shorter wavelength in inverse proportion to $T$.

> [!key] Statement
> The wavelength of maximum emission scales inversely with temperature: $\lambda_mT=b=2897.8\ \mu\mathrm{m\,K}$.

Sun (5772 K): 0.50 µm (visible). Earth (288 K): 10.1 µm; (255 K): 11.4 µm (thermal infrared). Human skin (305 K): 9.5 µm.

> [!extra-beyond]- Extra (beyond): derivation and conventions
> Put $x=hc/\lambda k_BT$ in $B_\lambda\propto x^5/(e^x-1)$ (times $T^5$). Setting the derivative to zero gives $5(1-e^{-x})=x$, whose root is $x=4.9651$, so $b=hc/(4.9651\,k_B)$. The peak depends on what is plotted: per unit **frequency** $\nu_m/T=5.879\times10^{10}$ Hz K⁻¹ (a different wavelength, 5.1 mm K / T), and per unit **log wavelength** ($\lambda B_\lambda$) $\lambda T=3670\ \mu\mathrm{m\,K}$ (14.4 µm for 255 K). See the box in [[CL03 Radiation and Planetary Energy Balance]].

See [[Planck Law]], [[Blackbody Radiation]].
