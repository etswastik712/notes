# Kelvin and Clausius Statements

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** [[1a131e225246d9749a298f49675c5fe5_MIT8_333F13_Lec2.pdf#page=1|MIT L2 §I.D]].

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Two ways of saying you cannot get something for nothing; each can be turned into the other.

> [!key] Definition
> **Kelvin:** no process whose sole result is complete conversion of heat to work. **Clausius:** none whose sole result is heat flow from cold to hot. If one is violated the other is too (proof by combining the machine with an ordinary engine or refrigerator).

## Equivalence
Clausius-violator + engine with $Q_C=Q$ gives a 100 % engine. Kelvin-violator driving a refrigerator gives a cold-to-hot transfer at no cost.

> [!pitfall] "Sole result"
> An isothermal expansion converts heat completely to work but changes the gas; a refrigerator moves heat to a hotter body but needs work. The statements forbid only processes whose *only* effect is the conversion or transfer.
