# Carnot Engine

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** [[1a131e225246d9749a298f49675c5fe5_MIT8_333F13_Lec2.pdf#page=2|MIT L2 §I.E]].

> [!intuition] In one picture
> The best possible engine: reversible, with all its heat exchanged at just two temperatures.

> [!key] Definition
> A **Carnot engine** is reversible, cyclic, with heat exchanged only at $T_H$ and $T_C$. For an ideal gas: two isotherms joined by two adiabats ($V_2/V_1=V_3/V_4$), $Q_H/Q_C=T_H/T_C$, $\eta=1-T_C/T_H$.

## Numbers
1 mol monatomic, $500$/$300$ K, $1\to2$ L: $V_3=4.303$ L, $V_4=2.152$ L, $Q_H=2882$ J, $Q_C=1729$ J, $W=1153$ J. In the $TS$ plane the cycle is a rectangle of width $R\ln2=5.76$ J K$^{-1}$.

> [!pitfall] Reversible means unusably slow
> A real engine delivering power is irreversible and below $\eta_{\rm Carnot}$ (endoreversible bounds are beyond this course).
