# Empirical Temperature

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** [[0d0ec730e8e02ed0399d39ff05054a72_MIT8_333F13_Lec1.pdf#page=2|MIT L1 §I.B]]. · 🟡 **extra · book:** thermometers and scales (Zemansky Ch. 1).

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Any number a thermometer assigns that is the same for bodies in equilibrium.

> [!key] Definition
> $\Theta_A(A_1,A_2,\dots)$: a function of the coordinates of a system whose value is common to all systems in mutual thermal equilibrium. Any monotonic function of it is equally valid; scales differ between thermometers.

## Examples
For a wire, a paramagnet and a van der Waals gas the same $\Theta\propto(P+a/V^2)(V-b)=c[F/(L-L_0)-K]=dB/M$ ([[TH01 Temperature and the Zeroth Law]] §3). Mercury and platinum-resistance thermometers agree at two fixed points but not at 50 °C.

> [!pitfall] Thermometer-dependent
> Equal numbers between fixed points mean different true temperatures for different substances; the dilute-gas and thermodynamic scales remove the dependence.
