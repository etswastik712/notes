# Equation of State

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** [[0d0ec730e8e02ed0399d39ff05054a72_MIT8_333F13_Lec1.pdf#page=3|MIT L1 eqs. I.7–I.9]].

> [!intuition] In one picture
> The recipe that ties the coordinates of one substance together at a given temperature.

> [!key] Definition
> A relation among the thermodynamic coordinates of a system in equilibrium, e.g. ideal gas $PV=Nk_BT$, van der Waals $(P+a/V^2)(V-b)=Nk_BT$, Curie paramagnet $M=N\mu^2B/3k_BT$, rubber $F=(K+DT)(L-L_0)$.

## Use
Differentiating the equation of state gives the response functions: ideal gas $\kappa_T=1/P$, $\alpha_P=1/T$, $(\partial P/\partial T)_V=P/T$. Van der Waals CO$_2$: $T_c=304.0$ K, $P_c=7.40$ MPa, $V_c=128$ cm$^3$/mol from $\partial_VP=\partial_V^2P=0$ ([[TH05 Potentials Maxwell Relations Stability and the Third Law]]).

> [!pitfall] An equation of state is not a law of thermodynamics
> It is empirical (or comes from statistical mechanics) and substance-specific; thermodynamics supplies the *relations between* its derivatives (Maxwell relations).
