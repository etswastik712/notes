# Third Law of Thermodynamics

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** [[97dcd8dee3d3301cd7c27745b17b10b5_MIT8_333F13_Lec4.pdf#page=4|MIT L4 §I.J]].

> [!intuition] In one picture
> At absolute zero the system is in its single lowest state, so there is no disorder left to count.

> [!key] Definition
> $\lim_{T\to0}S(X,T)=0$. Consequences: $\partial S/\partial X\to0$; $C_X\to0$; thermal expansivities $\to0$; $T=0$ unattainable in a finite number of steps. Microscopically $S=k_B\ln g$, $g=1$.

## Notes
Verified for metastable allotropes (sulphur): $S_A(0)=S_B(0)$. With $C=aT^n$, finite $S$ requires $n>0$ (Debye: $S=C/3$). Two-level system: $S\to0$ at $T\to0$, $\to\ln2$ at high $T$. Fails for glasses and for classical degenerate ground states.

> [!pitfall] A limit, not an attainable state
> It does not say a system *reaches* $T=0$; it constrains the approach.
