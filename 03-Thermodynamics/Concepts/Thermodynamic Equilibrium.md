# Thermodynamic Equilibrium

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** [[0d0ec730e8e02ed0399d39ff05054a72_MIT8_333F13_Lec1.pdf#page=1|MIT L1 §I.A]].

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Nothing changes on the timescale you care about: pressure, temperature and the rest have settled.

> [!key] Definition
> A system is in **equilibrium** when its thermodynamic coordinates do not change appreciably over the observation time. It is described by a few state functions; closed systems are bounded by **adiabatic** walls, **diathermic** walls allow heat exchange.

## Notes
Equilibrium is **timescale-relative**: window glass is in equilibrium as a solid for decades but flows over millennia; matter and radiation in the first minutes of the Big Bang were in equilibrium. Mechanical, thermal and chemical equilibrium are separate conditions (equal $P$, equal $T$, equal $\mu$).

> [!pitfall] Steady is not the same as equilibrium
> A pipe carrying a constant flow of heat has constant temperatures but is **not** in equilibrium: there is a net flux and entropy is being produced ([[Entropy]]). Equilibrium also requires no net fluxes.
