# Problem Set I: solutions (2026-08-13)

## Covered
Worked results from the posted solutions (Classroom: PH2113_Problem_Set_1_Solutions); problem statements in [[ph2113/2026-08-11-problem-set-1]].

**1. Amplitudes.** (a) Exclusive alternatives: $P_{AB}=P_A+P_B=0.40$. (b),(c) $P=|\psi_A+\psi_B|^2=|\psi_A|^2+|\psi_B|^2+2\,\mathrm{Re}(\psi_A\psi_B^*)=0.40+0.40\cos\varphi$; $\varphi=0$: 0.80, $\varphi=\pi$: 0; range $0\le P\le0.80$. (d) $\varphi=\varphi(x)$ tracks path difference, so $0.40\cos\varphi(x)$ integrates to about 0 over the screen and $N_{AB}=N_A+N_B$: interference redistributes particles, it does not create or destroy them.

**2. Photoelectric ($W=2.0$ eV, 400 nm).** $E_\gamma=1240/400=3.10$ eV; $K_{\max}=E_\gamma-W=1.10$ eV; $eV_s=K_{\max}\Rightarrow V_s=1.10$ V. Doubling intensity doubles the photocurrent; $K_{\max}$ and $V_s$ unchanged. Threshold: $\lambda_0=hc/W=1240/2.0=620$ nm. Classically energy arrives continuously, so any frequency should eventually free an electron; a hard cutoff has no classical explanation.

**3. Compton ($\lambda=50$ pm, 90°).** $\lambda'=50+2.426=52.43$ pm; $E_i=24.80$ keV; $E_f\approx23.65$ keV; $K_e\approx1.15$ keV. Visible light: $\Delta\lambda/\lambda=2.426/500000\approx4.85\times10^{-6}$ vs $2.426/50\approx4.9\%$ for the X-ray; $\Delta\lambda$ is set by $\theta$ and $m_ec$ only, so only the comparison with $\lambda$ differs. Thomson: the driven electron oscillates at the wave's frequency and an oscillating charge radiates at the frequency it oscillates, so $\nu'=\nu$ at every angle; the shift is a quantum (photon recoil) effect.

**4. Baseball.** $\lambda_{dB}=h/mv=6.626\times10^{-34}/(0.145\times40)\approx1.14\times10^{-34}$ m; ratios $\sim10^{-24}$ (atomic spacing $10^{-10}$ m) and $\sim10^{-19}$ (nucleus $10^{-15}$ m). For $\lambda=10^{-10}$ m: $v=h/m\lambda\approx4.6\times10^{-23}$ m/s; 1 mm takes $\approx2.2\times10^{19}$ s, about 50 times the age of the universe. Criterion: the dimensionless ratio $\lambda_{dB}/d$ (wavelength to the apparatus length scale). Electron 0.17 nm vs 0.1 nm: order 1, clear diffraction; baseball: $10^{-24}$-$10^{-19}$, none.

**5. Davisson-Germer.** $\lambda=12.27/\sqrt{54}=1.67$ Å $=0.167$ nm, same order as $d=0.215$ nm. $\sin\theta=0.167/0.215=0.777\Rightarrow\theta\approx51^\circ$ vs observed about 50° (within about 2%), with no adjustable parameter. At 100 V: $\lambda=0.123$ nm, $\sin\theta=0.571$, $\theta\approx35^\circ$; the maximum moves to smaller $\theta$ since $\lambda\propto1/\sqrt V$. This $\theta(V)$ dependence is what excludes a classical explanation.

**6. Plane wave.** $|\psi|^2=|A|^2$, independent of $x$ and $t$. $\int|\psi|^2dx=|A|^2\cdot\infty$ diverges for every $A\ne0$, so it is not normalisable. $\lambda_{dB}=h/p$ is a statement about momentum, not spatial extent: wavelength is not size. Definite $p=\hbar k$ means completely undetermined position; the plane wave is an idealisation and building block, not a preparable state.

**7. Box ($0<x<L$).** $\int_0^L\sin^2(\pi x/L)dx=L/2\Rightarrow A=\sqrt{2/L}$, $|\psi|^2=(2/L)\sin^2(\pi x/L)$. Maximum at $x=L/2$; zero at walls. $P(0<x<L/4)=(2/\pi)\int_0^{\pi/4}\sin^2u\,du=(2/\pi)(\pi/8-1/4)=\tfrac14-\tfrac1{2\pi}\approx0.091$, against the classical 0.25. $|\psi|^2$ is ensemble statistics; one position measurement returns one sharp value.

**8. Conceptual.** $\psi$ is a complex probability amplitude; its phase lets amplitudes add constructively or cancel, but it is never the outcome of a single measurement. $|\psi|^2$ is real and non-negative; by the Born rule (a postulate) it is the probability density for position and the quantity tied to the observed distribution. Each detection is localised; only the accumulated histogram shows the fringes. The "wave" belongs to the ensemble of outcomes.
