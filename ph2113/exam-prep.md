## Must know
- Acceptable wavefunction on the whole line with a finite nonsingular potential: finite, single-valued, continuous, normalisable ($\int|\psi|^2dx=1$). $\psi\to0$ is necessary but not sufficient: $1/\sqrt{1+|x|}$ is not normalisable (log divergence) (midsem Q6a).
- Admissible as a state is not the same as a stationary solution for a given $V$; $e^{-a|x|}$ is normalisable but has a cusp (fine for a $\delta$-potential only); $A/(1+|x|)$ is normalisable, $A/x$ across $0$ is not; a constant is normalisable on $0\le x\le L$ ($A=1/\sqrt L$) but not on the line (Classroom: Lecture 8 §3-4).
- Overall phase changes no probability; relative phase can (e.g. $|\psi|^2=2+2\cos(2kx-\varphi)$).
- Ring: $\psi(x+L)=\psi(x)$, $\psi_n=e^{ik_nx}/\sqrt L$, $k_n=2\pi n/L$, $E_n=2\pi^2\hbar^2n^2/(mL^2)$; $E_n=E_{-n}$ (degenerate), $p_{-n}=-p_n$. Single-valuedness (e.g. $e^{i\varphi/2}$ fails) forces integer $m$, $L_z=m\hbar$. On a ring of radius $R$: $\psi_n=Ae^{in\theta}$, $E_n=\hbar^2n^2/2mR^2$ (Classroom: Lecture 9 §5).
- Free particle on a line: $\psi=Ae^{ikx}+Be^{-ikx}$, $k^2=2mE/\hbar^2$, any real $k$ allowed, so a continuous spectrum $E\ge0$; discreteness comes from boundary conditions, not from the Schrödinger equation itself (Classroom: Lectures 8-9).
- $\hat x=x$, $\hat p=-i\hbar\,d/dx$, $\hat H=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{d^2}{dx^2}+V$; $\hat E=i\hbar\,\partial_t$; operators are linear. They arise because $\partial_x$ extracts $k$ and $\partial_t$ extracts $\omega$ from a plane wave (Classroom: Lectures 6-7, 8).
- The TDSE is a postulate of non-relativistic QM, not derived; the plane-wave substitution only checks consistency with $E=p^2/2m+V$.
- Eigenstate: $\hat A\psi=a\psi$; a measurement of $A$ gives $a$ with certainty; $\Delta A=0$.
- Born rule: probability of finding the particle in $[x,x+dx]$ is $|\psi|^2dx$ with $\int|\psi|^2dx=1$; for $\Psi=\sum c_a\phi_a$, $P(A=a)=|c_a|^2$, $\sum|c_a|^2=1$ (Classroom: wavefunction lecture note).
- $\langle A\rangle=\sum|c_a|^2a=\int\Psi^*\hat A\Psi\,dx$ (an average; need not be a possible outcome).
- $\Delta A=\sqrt{\langle A^2\rangle-\langle A\rangle^2}$; for $a\psi_n+b\psi_{-n}$, $\Delta p=2|a||b||p_n|$.
- $[\hat A,\hat B]=\hat A\hat B-\hat B\hat A$; $[\hat x,\hat p]=i\hbar$; $\Delta A\Delta B\ge\frac12|\langle[\hat A,\hat B]\rangle|$, so $\Delta x\Delta p\ge\hbar/2$.
- $\Delta x$, $\Delta p$ are standard deviations over identically prepared systems; the bound is a property of the state, not of instrument imperfection. Fourier bound $\Delta x\Delta k\ge\frac12$ (Gaussian saturates) plus $p=\hbar k$ gives $\Delta x\Delta p\ge\hbar/2$ (Classroom: Lectures 6-8).
- Compton-scale limit: $\Delta x\sim\hbar/(mc)$ gives $\Delta p\sim mc$, where the non-relativistic theory fails; $\hbar/mc$ is the reduced Compton wavelength ($\lambda_C=h/mc=2\pi\hbar/mc$), $\approx3.9\times10^{-13}$ m for the electron. Dimensional analysis: $\ell=\hbar^ac^bm^d$ forces $a=1,b=-1,d=-1$ (kg: $a+d=0$; m: $2a+b=1$; s: $-a-b=0$). A validity threshold, not a minimum size (Classroom: Lecture 8 §2).
- $[\hat A,\hat B]=0$ means compatible (common eigenstates), not that both uncertainties vanish in every state (degeneracy example: $\sqrt{2/L}\cos kx$ on the ring). Conversely $[A,B]\neq0$: an eigenvector of $A$ is in general not one of $B$ (midsem Q5d).
- $\langle p\rangle=0$ is not "momentum definitely zero": for $(\psi_++\psi_-)/\sqrt2$, $p=\pm\hbar k$ with probability $\tfrac12$ each, $\Delta p=\hbar k$.
- Classical vs quantum alternatives: exclusive classical routes add count densities, $R_{AB}=R_A+R_B$; quantum indistinguishable alternatives add amplitudes, $p_{AB}=|\psi_A+\psi_B|^2=|\psi_A|^2+|\psi_B|^2+2\mathrm{Re}(\psi_A^*\psi_B)$; distinguishable (physically recorded path) alternatives add probabilities (Classroom: Lecture 1).
- Two-path superposition: $|\psi_1+\psi_2|^2=4A^2\cos^2\frac{\varphi_2-\varphi_1}{2}$; fringe spacing $\lambda D/d$; incoherent addition has no interference term. Interference redistributes particles; it does not change the total count (Problem Set I Q1d).
- Plane wave $Ae^{i(kx-\omega t)}$: $|\psi|^2=|A|^2$ constant, not normalisable, $\Delta p=0$, $\Delta x=\infty$; wavelength $h/p$ is not a size. $e^{ikx}$ moves toward $+x$ ($p=+\hbar k$), $e^{-ikx}$ toward $-x$.
- Gaussian packet: $\psi=(2\pi\sigma^2)^{-1/4}e^{-x^2/4\sigma^2}$, $\Delta x=\sigma$, $\Delta p=\hbar/2\sigma$, $\Delta x\Delta p=\hbar/2$; its Fourier transform is again Gaussian, $\varphi(k)=(2\sigma^2/\pi)^{1/4}e^{-\sigma^2k^2}$; narrow in $x$ means broad in $k$.
- Single slit of width $a$: first minimum $\sin\theta=\lambda/a$, $\Delta x\Delta p_x\approx h$. Hydrogen from uncertainty: $E(r)=\hbar^2/2m_er^2-e^2/4\pi\varepsilon_0r$ gives $r_{\min}=a_0$, $E_{\min}=-13.6$ eV (scaling is reliable, prefactor depends on $\Delta p\approx\hbar/r$ vs $\hbar/2r$) (Classroom: Lectures 6-7 §6, wavefunction lecture note §10).
- Matching conditions: finite jump in $V$ gives continuous $\psi'$; for $V=\lambda\delta(x-x_0)$, $\psi'(x_0^+)-\psi'(x_0^-)=\frac{2m\lambda}{\hbar^2}\psi(x_0)$.
- TDSE $i\hbar\partial_t\Psi=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\partial_x^2\Psi+V\Psi$; for $V(x)$, separation gives $T(t)=e^{-iEt/\hbar}$ and the TISE $-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\psi''+V\psi=E\psi$; stationary state $\Psi=\psi e^{-iEt/\hbar}$ has time-independent $|\Psi|^2$ (Classroom: Lecture 9).
- The Schrödinger equation plus $V$ gives a set of possible states; the preparation selects which one. Linearity: $c_1\Psi_1+c_2\Psi_2$ solves the TDSE; same energy gives a stationary state, different energies do not (Classroom: Lecture 9 §7-8).
- Stationary vs general: $\Psi=\sum c_ne^{-iE_nt/\hbar}\psi_n$; $|c_n|$, $\langle E\rangle$, $\Delta E$ constant; $|\Psi|^2$ and off-diagonal observables oscillate at $(E_2-E_1)/\hbar$.
- Infinite well (width $a$, $0<x<a$): $\psi=A\sin kx+B\cos kx$, $\psi(0)=\psi(a)=0\Rightarrow B=0$, $ka=n\pi$, $\psi_n\propto\sin(n\pi x/a)$, $E_n=n^2\pi^2\hbar^2/2ma^2$, $n=1,2,\dots$; $\int\psi_m^*\psi_n=\delta_{mn}$; normalisation $\sqrt{2/a}$; $P(0<x<L/4)=\tfrac14-\tfrac1{2\pi}\approx0.091$ in the ground state (Problem Set I Q7).
- Two-state box superposition $(\phi_1+\phi_2)/\sqrt2$: $T=2\pi\hbar/(E_2-E_1)=4mL^2/3\pi\hbar$, $\langle E\rangle=5\pi^2\hbar^2/4mL^2$, $\Delta E=3\pi^2\hbar^2/4mL^2$, $\Delta E\,T=\pi\hbar$ (midsem Q3).
- Free particle: $\omega=\hbar k^2/2m$, $v_{ph}=\hbar k/2m=\tfrac12v_{gr}$, $v_{gr}=\hbar k/m$ (physical). Current $j=\frac{\hbar}{2mi}(\Psi^*\Psi'-\Psi\Psi'^*)$; standing wave $\Rightarrow j=0$.
- Continuity: $\partial_t\rho+\partial_xj=0$; a complex potential $V=i\Gamma$ gives $P(t)=P(0)e^{2\Gamma t/\hbar}$, lifetime $\tau=\hbar/2|\Gamma|$ for $\Gamma<0$; $\hat H$ non-Hermitian means probability is not conserved.
- Local conservation of probability from the TDSE and its conjugate: $\partial_t(\Psi^*\Psi)=\frac{i\hbar}{2m}\partial_x(\Psi^*\Psi'-\Psi\Psi^{*\prime})$; the terms proportional to (real) $V$ cancel, which gives $\partial_t\rho+\partial_xj=0$ with the $j$ above (Classroom: Ehrenfest.pdf; algebra reconstructed from a garbled scan).
- First Ehrenfest equation: $\frac{d\langle x\rangle}{dt}=\frac{\langle p\rangle}{m}$ (from $\frac{d\langle x\rangle}{dt}=\int x\,\partial_t\rho\,dx=\int j\,dx$ by parts, boundary terms vanish) (Classroom: Ehrenfest.pdf, "First theorem").
- Superposition of solutions is a solution (linearity) but not an eigenstate unless the energies are equal.
- de Broglie: $\lambda=h/p=h/\sqrt{2m_eeV}\approx150.4/\sqrt V$ Å; non-relativistic error $\approx K/4mc^2$ (1% at about 20 keV). Davisson-Germer 54 V: $\lambda=0.167$ nm, $d\sin\theta=\lambda$ gives about 51° (observed 50°); raising $V$ shrinks $\lambda\propto1/\sqrt V$ and moves the maximum to smaller $\theta$. Wave behaviour is observable when $\lambda_{dB}/d$ (apparatus length scale) is of order 1 or larger.
- Electron length scales: $r_e=2.8$ fm, $\bar\lambda_C=\hbar/mc=386$ fm, $a_0=\bar\lambda_C/\alpha=0.053$ nm (ratio $\alpha:1:1/\alpha$); $E=-\tfrac12\alpha^2m_ec^2=-13.6$ eV. Confinement to about 1 fm gives $\Delta pc\approx98.5$ MeV, above pair production, so QFT is needed.
- Oscillator ladder operators: $\hat a_\pm=\frac1{\sqrt{2\hbar m\omega}}(\mp i\hat p+m\omega\hat x)$, $[\hat a_-,\hat a_+]=1$, $\hat H=\hbar\omega(\hat a_+\hat a_-+\tfrac12)=\hbar\omega(\hat a_-\hat a_+-\tfrac12)$ (Classroom: Tutorial III, oscillator notes).
- $\hat x=\sqrt{\hbar/2m\omega}\,(\hat a_++\hat a_-)$, $\hat p=i\sqrt{\hbar m\omega/2}\,(\hat a_+-\hat a_-)$.
- Ladder property: $\hat H(\hat a_\pm\psi)=(E\pm\hbar\omega)(\hat a_\pm\psi)$; eigenvalues are spaced by $\hbar\omega$.
- Ground state: $\hat a_-\psi_0=0$, $\psi_0=(m\omega/\pi\hbar)^{1/4}e^{-m\omega x^2/2\hbar}$, $E_0=\hbar\omega/2$; $E_n=\hbar\omega(n+\tfrac12)$.
- $\hat a_+\psi_n=\sqrt{n+1}\,\psi_{n+1}$, $\hat a_-\psi_n=\sqrt n\,\psi_{n-1}$, $\psi_n=\frac1{\sqrt{n!}}(\hat a_+)^n\psi_0$; $\hat a_+\hat a_-\psi_n=n\psi_n$.
- Hermitian conjugates: $\int f^*(\hat a_\pm g)\,dx=\int(\hat a_\mp f)^*g\,dx$.
- Eigenfunctions of a Hermitian $\hat H$ with different eigenvalues are orthogonal: $(E_n-E_m)\int\psi_m^*\psi_n\,dx=0$ (Tutorial III Problem 3).
- Oscillator expectation values by ladder operators: $\langle x\rangle=\langle p\rangle=0$ in $\psi_n$; $\langle p^2\rangle=\hbar m\omega(n+\tfrac12)$ (derived from $\langle\hat a_+\hat a_-\rangle=n$, $\langle\hat a_-\hat a_+\rangle=n+1$; check against your own notes).
- Finite well ($V=0$ for $|x|<a$, $V_0$ outside), bound states $E<V_0$: $k_0=\sqrt{2mE}/\hbar$ inside, $k=\sqrt{2m(V_0-E)}/\hbar$ outside (decay constant, written $\kappa$ in the lecture note); $k^2+k_0^2=2mV_0/\hbar^2$. Symmetric $V$ gives even or odd $\psi$. Even: $\psi=G\cos k_0x$ inside, $Ae^{-kx}$ outside, matching $\psi,\psi'$ at $x=a$ gives $\tan(k_0a)=k/k_0$ (Classroom: Tutorial IV, Finite_well.pdf). Odd: $\cot(k_0a)=-k/k_0$ (reconstructed from a garbled scan; check).
- Finite well limits: deep/wide well gives many states and $k_na\approx n\pi/2$, $E_n\approx n^2\pi^2\hbar^2/8ma^2$ (box of width $2a$), slightly lower; shallow/narrow well has few bound states, always at least one even state, possibly no odd one (Classroom: Finite_well.pdf).
- Finite well, $E>V_0$ (scattering): $Ae^{ikx}+Be^{-ikx}$ for $x<-a$, $Ce^{ik_0x}+De^{-ik_0x}$ inside, $Fe^{ikx}$ for $x>a$ (here $k=\sqrt{2m(E-V_0)}/\hbar$ outside, $k_0=\sqrt{2mE}/\hbar$ inside); continuity of $\psi,\psi'$ at $\pm a$ fixes $B,C,D,F$ in terms of $A$; $T=|F/A|^2$, $R=|B/A|^2$, $T+R=1$.
- Tutorial V result: $\dfrac FA=\dfrac{4kk_0e^{-2ika}}{(k+k_0)^2e^{-2ik_0a}-(k-k_0)^2e^{2ik_0a}}$ and $T=\Big[1+\dfrac{V_0^2}{4E(E-V_0)}\sin^2\!\big(\tfrac{2a}{\hbar}\sqrt{2mE}\big)\Big]^{-1}$, equal to $[1+\frac{(k^2-k_0^2)^2}{4k^2k_0^2}\sin^2(2k_0a)]^{-1}$ because $k^2-k_0^2=-2mV_0/\hbar^2$ (Classroom: Tutorial5.pdf, eqs. 8-9). $T=1$ when $2k_0a=n\pi$ (Finite_well.pdf).
- Tutorial V bound-state question: a third bound state (even, odd, even) needs $z_0=\sqrt{2mV_0a^2}/\hbar>\pi$, i.e. $V_0a^2>\pi^2\hbar^2/2m$ (derived from the quantisation conditions, not posted; check).
- Finite barrier ($V=V_0$ for $|x|<a$, $0$ outside), $E<V_0$: classically the particle is reflected; quantum mechanically $\psi=Ae^{ikx}+Be^{-ikx}$ ($x<-a$), $Ce^{\kappa x}+De^{-\kappa x}$ inside, $Fe^{ikx}$ ($x>a$), with $k=\sqrt{2mE}/\hbar$, $\kappa=\sqrt{2m(V_0-E)}/\hbar$; matching $\psi,\psi'$ at $\pm a$ gives $T=|F/A|^2=\Big[1+\dfrac{V_0^2\sinh^2(2\kappa a)}{4E(V_0-E)}\Big]^{-1}\neq0$: tunnelling through a classically forbidden region (Classroom: Barrier.pdf; the exact formula is reconstructed from a garbled scan, check; it is the well formula with $k_0\to i\kappa$). For $\kappa a\gg1$, $T\approx\frac{16E(V_0-E)}{V_0^2}e^{-4\kappa a}$ (derived, not posted).
- Second Ehrenfest equation: $\frac{d}{dt}\langle p\rangle=-\langle dV/dx\rangle$, from the TDSE and its conjugate; the kinetic terms combine into a boundary term $[\psi^{*\prime}\psi'-\psi^*\psi'']_{-\infty}^{\infty}=0$ and the potential terms give $-\int V'|\psi|^2dx$. With $\frac{d}{dt}\langle x\rangle=\langle p\rangle/m$, for $V=kx^2/2$ this gives $m\frac{d^2\langle x\rangle}{dt^2}=-k\langle x\rangle$; for any quadratic potential $V'$ is linear so $\langle V'(x)\rangle=V'(\langle x\rangle)$ and the mean follows Newton's law exactly; in general $\langle V'(x)\rangle\ne V'(\langle x\rangle)$ (Classroom: Tutorial6.pdf and Ehrenfest.pdf; the derivation is worked out in the notes, partly reconstructed).
- Barrier with $E>V_0$ (Tutorial VI): $k_0=\sqrt{2mE}/\hbar$ outside, $k=\sqrt{2m(E-V_0)}/\hbar$ inside, so $k<k_0$ and the wavelength inside $2\pi/k$ exceeds the one outside $2\pi/k_0$; as $E\to V_0^+$, $k\to0$ and the wavelength inside $\to\infty$; as $E\gg V_0$, $k/k_0\to1$, $T\to1$. Four matching conditions at $x=\pm a$ (continuity of $\psi$ and $\psi'$ at each); from $x=a$: $C=\tfrac12(1+k_0/k)Fe^{i(k_0-k)a}$, $D=\tfrac12(1-k_0/k)Fe^{i(k_0+k)a}$ (Classroom: Tutorial6.pdf; the solved forms are derived in the note, not posted).
- 2-d Schrödinger equation in polar coordinates ($r=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$, $\theta=\tan^{-1}(y/x)$): $-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\big[\partial_r^2\psi+\frac1r\partial_r\psi+\frac1{r^2}\partial_\theta^2\psi\big]+V\psi=E\psi$ (Classroom: Tutorial7.pdf eq. 5; Angular_momentum.pdf).
- Circular symmetry, $V=V(r)$: $[\hat H,\hat L_z]=0$, so $\hat H$ and $\hat L_z$ have common eigenfunctions; $\Theta(\theta)=e^{il\theta}$, eigenvalue $\hbar l$; single-valuedness $\theta\to\theta+2\pi$ forces integer $l$; the radial equation depends on $l^2$ only, so $\pm l$ are degenerate for any $V(r)$ (Classroom: Angular_momentum.pdf, both files; the handwritten one is partly reconstructed).
- 2-d radial equation: $R''+\frac1rR'+\big[\frac{2m}{\hbar^2}(E-V)-\frac{l^2}{r^2}\big]R=0$; for the 2-d oscillator $V=\tfrac12m\omega^2r^2$ the solutions are Laguerre polynomials, no solving required (Classroom: Tutorial7.pdf eq. 8).
- Circular infinite well (radius $a$): $R=AJ_l(kr)+BY_l(kr)$, $B=0$ for finiteness at $r=0$, $J_l(ka)=0$ so $k_{n,l}=j_{l,n}/a$, $E_{n,l}=\hbar^2k_{n,l}^2/2m$, $\psi=J_l(k_{n,l}r)e^{il\theta}$; $|N_{n,l}|^2=1/[\pi a^2J_{l+1}^2(j_{l,n})]$; every $l\neq0$ level is doubly degenerate; $e^{il\theta}$ states have ring-shaped densities, $\cos l\theta$ combinations have $2l$ lobes (Classroom: Angular_momentum.pdf, circular_well_density.ipynb).
- Radial probability density: 2-d $P(r)=\int_0^{2\pi}|\psi|^2r\,d\theta$; 3-d $P(r)=\int_0^\pi\int_0^{2\pi}|\psi|^2r^2\sin\theta\,d\theta\,d\varphi$; angle-independent $\psi$: $2\pi r|\psi|^2$ (2-d), $4\pi r^2|\psi|^2$ (3-d) (Classroom: Tutorial7.pdf eqs. 9-12). For hydrogen 1s, $|R_{1,0}|^2$ peaks at $r=0$ but $P(r)$ does not; maximum at $r=a$ (derived, not stated in the sheet).
- 3-d: $\hat L^2=-\hbar^2\big[\frac1{\sin\theta}\partial_\theta(\sin\theta\,\partial_\theta)+\frac1{\sin^2\theta}\partial_\varphi^2\big]$, $\hat L_z=-i\hbar\partial_\varphi$, both commute with $\hat H$; $\Phi=e^{im\varphi}$, eigenvalues $\hbar^2l(l+1)$ and $\hbar m$ with $l\ge0$, $m=-l,\dots,l$; simultaneous eigenfunctions $Y_{l,m}\propto P_l^m(\cos\theta)e^{im\varphi}$ (Classroom: Hydrogen_atom.pdf).
- Hydrogen: $E_n=-\dfrac{me^4}{2(4\pi\epsilon_0)^2\hbar^2}\dfrac1{n^2}$ ($=-13.6$ eV$/n^2$), $n=1,2,\dots$; $l=0,\dots,n-1$; degeneracy $n^2$ (without spin); $E$ is independent of $l$ and $m$; $\psi_{n,l,m}=N_{n,l}e^{-r/na}(2r/na)^lL^{2l+1}_{n-l-1}(2r/na)Y_l^m$; natural scales: energy $2\times13.6$ eV, length $\hbar^2(4\pi\epsilon_0)/(me^2)=0.529\times10^{-10}$ m (Bohr radius) (Classroom: Hydrogen_atom.pdf). Radial equation: $\big[-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}(\frac{d^2}{dr^2}+\frac2r\frac d{dr})+\frac{\hbar^2l(l+1)}{2mr^2}-\frac{e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0r}-E\big]R=0$.
- Radial functions: $R_{1,0}=2a^{-3/2}e^{-r/a}$, $R_{2,1}=\frac1{\sqrt{24}}a^{-3/2}\frac rae^{-r/2a}$, $R_{2,0}=\frac1{\sqrt2}a^{-3/2}(1-\frac r{2a})e^{-r/2a}$ (normalised form; the sheet prints $(2-r/a)$ with $1/\sqrt2$, see Gaps).
- Photoelectric effect: $E_\gamma=h\nu$, $K_{\max}=h\nu-\Phi$, $\nu_0=\Phi/h$; $eV_s=K_{\max}$; intensity changes photon number (current), not energy per photon; classical wave theory predicts no threshold. $hc\simeq1240$ eV nm.
- Compton: $\Delta\lambda=\lambda'-\lambda=\frac h{m_ec}(1-\cos\theta)$, $\lambda_C=h/m_ec=2.426$ pm, $0\le\Delta\lambda\le2\lambda_C=4.853$ pm; $p_\gamma=h/\lambda$; $K_e=E_\gamma-E'_\gamma$; $\mathbf p_\gamma=\mathbf p'_\gamma+\mathbf p_e$; derived from $m_ec^2(1/E'-1/E)=1-\cos\theta$. The shift is independent of $\lambda$, so the effect is visible only when $\Delta\lambda/\lambda$ is non-negligible (X-rays: percent; 500 nm: $\sim5\times10^{-6}$). Thomson: $\nu'=\nu$ for $h\nu\ll m_ec^2$ (Classroom: Lectures 2-3).
- Time is a parameter, not an operator.
- Two-state matrices: $X|{\pm y}\rangle=\pm i|{\mp y}\rangle$; $[X,Y]=2iZ$.
