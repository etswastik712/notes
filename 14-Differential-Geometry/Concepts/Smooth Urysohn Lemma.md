# Smooth Urysohn Lemma

<span class="scope-extra-book">extra · book</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟡 **extra · book:** [[John M. Lee - Introduction to Smooth Manifolds.pdf#page=65|Lee Thm 2.29, p. 47]] (every closed set is a zero set of a smooth function); a consequence of [[Partition of Unity]].

> [!intuition] In one picture
> Smooth functions are rich enough to separate any two disjoint closed sets, like the topological Urysohn lemma but with $C^\infty$.

> [!key] Definition
> For disjoint closed $A,B\subseteq M$ there is smooth $f:M\to[0,1]$ with $f|_A=0$, $f|_B=1$ (from a partition of unity subordinate to $\{M\setminus B,M\setminus A\}$).

Smooth analogue of [[Separation Axioms]]; on $\mathbb R$, $f=h$ separates $\{x\le0\}$ from $\{x\ge1\}$.

Used in: [[DM02 Smooth Maps and Partitions of Unity]]. Related: [[Partition of Unity]] · [[Bump Function]] · [[Separation Axioms]].
