# Hairy Ball Theorem

<span class="scope-taught">taught · in syllabus</span>

> [!syllabus] In the syllabus vs extra
> 🟢 **taught:** slide DG8 (stated: $S^n$ has a nowhere-vanishing vector field iff $n$ is odd); [[mt3184-differential-geometry-and-lie-groups/2026-08-20-hw3|HW3]] Q3.

> [!intuition] In one picture
> You cannot comb a hairy ball flat: some point always has a cowlick (on even-dimensional spheres).

> [!key] Definition
> $S^n$ has a nowhere-vanishing smooth vector field iff $n$ is odd. $S^1$: the rotation field; $S^3$: left-invariant fields of $SU(2)$; $S^2$: none, but the HW3 Q3 field $\widetilde Y=(\tilde u_2^2-\tilde u_1^2,-2\tilde u_1\tilde u_2)$ has exactly one zero.

Stated, not proved (needs degree theory / Poincaré–Hopf). $S^2$ is not parallelizable; Lie groups always are ([[DM11 The Lie Algebra of a Lie Group]]).

Used in: [[DM04 The Tangent Bundle and the Rank Theorem]]. Related: [[Local Frame]] · [[Tangent Bundle]].
