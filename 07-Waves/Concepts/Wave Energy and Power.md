# Wave Energy and Power

> [!intuition] In one picture
> A water wave carries energy in the motion of its water; the energy per square metre goes as the square of the height, and the power as height squared times period.

> [!key] Definition and key results
> $E=\rho gH^2/8$ ($\rho gH^2/16$ potential, equal kinetic); flux $P=EC_g$, deep water $P=\rho g^2H^2T/32\pi$: $H=2$ m, $T=10$ s: 39 kW per metre of crest; 8 m, 14 s: 879 kW/m. For a Rayleigh sea $H_{1/3}=2\sigma=1.416H_{rms}$ and the largest fraction $q$ of waves carries $q(1+\ln1/q)$ of the energy (10% $\to$ 33%).

> [!pitfall] Pitfall
> Power per metre of crest is not power per metre of coast unless the crest is parallel to the shore.
