# Wave Shoaling and Refraction

> [!intuition] In one picture
> A wave slows as the water shoals, its crests turn to face the shore, its height rises until it breaks.

> [!key] Definition and key results
> Refraction: $\sin\alpha/C=$ const along a ray. Shoaling: $H/H_0=K_sK_r$, $K_s=\sqrt{C_{g0}/C_g}$, $K_r=\sqrt{b_0/b}$; shallow-water Green's law $H\propto h^{-1/4}$. Breaking at $H\approx0.78h$. Example: $T=12$ s, 2 m, $30^\circ$: $H$ dips to 1.8 m at 20-50 m depth then grows; breaks at $h_b=3.2$ m, $H_b=2.5$ m, $\alpha_b=8.5^\circ$. Rays focus on headlands, defocus over canyons.

> [!pitfall] Pitfall
> Shoaling coefficients below 1 occur at intermediate depths where $C_g$ exceeds its deep-water value.
